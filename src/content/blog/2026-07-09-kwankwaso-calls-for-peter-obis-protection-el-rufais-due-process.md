**ABUJA, Nigeria** — In a refreshing display of political statesmanship that transcends party lines, Senator Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso, Vice Presidential Candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has called on the Federal Government to provide adequate protection for Peter Obi and ensure due process in the case of former Kaduna Governor Nasir El-Rufai.

Kwankwaso made the remarks on Thursday, July 9, 2026, in response to recent developments that have raised concerns about political tolerance, personal safety, and the administration of justice in Africa’s most populous nation. 

His statement has been widely welcomed by many as a positive step toward fostering democratic values and reducing political tension.

Referring to Peter Obi’s recent public expression of safety fears during an interview with Chude Jideonwo, Kwankwaso emphasized that every Nigerian leader, regardless of affiliation, deserves full state protection. 

He criticized what he described as a dismissive official response and stressed the need for genuine security measures rather than ridicule.



On the detention of Nasir El-Rufai, Kwankwaso advocated for fair trial, presumption of innocence, and reasonable bail conditions. *“Regardless of political affiliation, Malam El-Rufai, like every Nigerian, is entitled to due process,” h*e stated, highlighting the importance of transparent justice to maintain public confidence in institutions.

Kwankwaso’s intervention comes at a critical time as Nigeria prepares for the 2027 general elections. 

Analysts see it as an encouraging sign of potential collaboration among opposition figures and a reminder that healthy democracy thrives on mutual respect, protection of critics, and adherence to constitutional principles.

The move has drawn praise from various quarters, with many Nigerians expressing relief that prominent voices are prioritizing fairness over partisan rivalry. 

It also underscores growing demands for de-escalation of political hostilities and focus on issues that truly affect ordinary citizens, such as security, economy, and justice.

While challenges remain, Kwankwaso’s principled stance offers a glimmer of hope. 

By publicly championing the rights of fellow opposition leaders like Peter Obi and calling for fairness in El-Rufai’s case, he has modeled the kind of mature political engagement many believe is essential for Nigeria’s progress.

As the nation watches closely, this development reinforces the message that true leadership involves defending democratic norms even when it benefits political rivals. 

In a country often divided by ethnicity, religion, and party loyalty, such cross-aisle advocacy for protection and due process stands out as genuinely good news.

For Nigerians yearning for a more inclusive and just political environment ahead of 2027, Kwankwaso’s words serve as both a timely reminder and a call to action for all stakeholders.
