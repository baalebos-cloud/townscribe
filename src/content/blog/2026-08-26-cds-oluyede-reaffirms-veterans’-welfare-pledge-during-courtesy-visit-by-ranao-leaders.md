**Abuja, Nigeria – August 26, 2026** — In a clear demonstration of institutional respect for those who once wore the uniform, the Chief of Defence Staff, General Olufemi Oluyede, on Wednesday reaffirmed the Defence Headquarters’ unwavering commitment to the welfare, dignity and continued relevance of Nigeria’s military veterans.

The assurance came during a courtesy visit by the leadership of the Retired Army, Navy and Air Force Officers Association (RANAO), led by its National President, Major General Hussaini Abayomi Salihu (Rtd), at Defence Headquarters in Abuja. 

General Oluyede, who also serves as Chief Patron of the association, received the new executives shortly after their assumption of office.



**Strengthening Old Bonds**

Major General Salihu explained that the visit was primarily to familiarise the new executive committee with the CDS and to deepen engagement between the association and the Defence Headquarters. 

He thanked General Oluyede for the warm reception and praised the CDS’s deliberate outreach to veterans across Nigeria’s six geopolitical zones.

The RANAO president also expressed gratitude to President Bola Ahmed Tinubu for recent improvements in the welfare of Armed Forces personnel, particularly the ongoing salary increases for serving members and the consequential adjustments expected for retirees. 

Among the key requests presented was the creation of a comprehensive database of association members to help companies and organisations easily identify experienced retired officers for professional engagements.



**Assurances on Pensions and Economic Empowerment**

Responding, General Oluyede thanked the veterans for their years of service and sacrifice, stressing that their contributions to national security and development remain deeply valued. 

He pledged to discharge his responsibilities as Chief Patron diligently and to work with the association within existing policy frameworks to address welfare concerns.

On the sensitive issue of salary increments and their impact on veterans, the CDS was categorical: the ongoing exercise is comprehensive and inclusive. 

Implementation is already underway, and under government policy, adjustments to the emoluments of serving personnel carry consequential implications for the pensions and benefits of retired personnel.

Beyond pensions, General Oluyede highlighted practical initiatives already in motion. Chief among them is a collaboration between the Defence Headquarters and the Bank of Industry aimed at providing low-interest loans to both serving and retired military personnel and their families. 

The scheme is designed to help veterans establish or expand sustainable businesses, promoting economic self-reliance and improving quality of life after active service.

**A Continuing Priority**

The CDS made it clear that the Defence Headquarters will keep exploring practical partnerships and measures to support veterans, recognising that those who served the nation deserve sustained institutional backing and dignity in retirement.

The engagement forms part of a broader pattern under General Oluyede’s leadership. 

In recent weeks, the CDS has held similar interactive sessions with veterans in different zones, repeatedly emphasising that the bond between the Armed Forces and its retirees remains a core institutional priority.

As Major General Samaila Uba, Director of Defence Information, noted in the official statement, the meeting underscores DHQ’s determination to maintain a strong and enduring relationship with Nigeria’s veterans while ensuring their welfare stays firmly on the military’s agenda.

For the men and women who once stood watch over the nation, the message from the Defence Headquarters on Wednesday was simple and reassuring: service does not end with retirement, and neither does the nation’s appreciation.
