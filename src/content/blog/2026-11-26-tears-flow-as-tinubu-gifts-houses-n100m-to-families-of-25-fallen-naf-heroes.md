**Abuja | November 26, 2026—It** was a morning of tears, salutes, and solemn silence as President Bola Ahmed Tinubu, on Thursday, led a grieving nation to honour the 25 personnel who died in the Nigerian Air Force NAF 931 aircraft accident in Ondo State on October 5, 2026, describing their death as a painful national loss.

Speaking during a memorial service and wreath-laying ceremony at the National Military Cemetery in Abuja, the President announced a massive welfare package for the bereaved families—a three-bedroom flat at the Renewed Hope Housing Estate, Karsana, Abuja, for each family, plus N100 million financial support for each of the deceased families.

According to the Statehouse Press Release issued by Special Adviser on Information and Strategy Bayo Onanuga on November 26, 2026, the victims were aboard the NAF ATR-42 aircraft, coded NAF 931, which crashed near Igbokoda, Ondo State. All 25 people on board—five crew members and 20 passengers, including gallant officers, airmen, airwomen, and civilians—were killed.

#### “Nigeria Will Give Their Families A Home”

In an emotional tribute, Tinubu said the nation owes the fallen heroes and their families an unpayable debt.

“It places upon us a sacred duty to safeguard their well-being, strengthen their capabilities, and honor their service. As Commander-in-Chief, I reaffirm my administration's commitment to a professional, well-equipped, and mission-ready Armed Forces,” Tinubu said.

*“A nation that asks its sons and daughters to fly into danger must give them the best wing it can build. We must also stand by the families who bear the deepest cost of national service.”*

He continued: *“As a tangible expression of the nation's gratitude, I approve the allocation of a three-bedroom flat in the Renewed Hope housing project in Abuja to the family of each of the military personnel who lost their lives in this accident.* 

*No home can ever replace the one you have lost. But they gave their lives to Nigeria, and Nigeria will give their families a home. This honour is extended to the immediate families of the civilians on board.”*

*“In addition to the flats, those officers will receive from this government 100 million each to settle their families. I will commit their soul into the massive hands of Almighty God.”*

#### “The Sky Did Not Take Their Honour”

The President described the victims as parents who will not see their children grow, newlyweds whose marriages had just begun, and sons, daughters, brothers, sisters and friends who carried dreams in their hearts and the Nigerian flag on their shoulders.

“Your loss is our nation's loss, and your grief is our grief. They were not merely passengers aboard the aircraft,” he said. “We could not bring them home to you, and our inability to recover their remains has made this harder still. But hear me, the sky did not take their honour.”

He assured a thorough investigation to unravel the cause of the crash, pledging to strengthen military and civilian aviation safety standards, operational procedures and institutional accountability. “We owe them the truth.”

Dignitaries present included Senate President Godswill Akpabio, NSA Nuhu Ribadu, Defence Minister Gen. Christopher Musa (rtd), Ondo Governor Lucky Aiyedatiwa, service chiefs Lt-Gen. Waidi Shaibu, Air Marshal Sunday Aneke, Vice Adm. Idi Abbas, IGP Tunde Disu, NIA DG Amb. Mohammed Mohammed, DSS DG Tosin Ajayi and NEMA DG Zubaida Umar Abubakar.

The Chief of Air Staff, Air Marshal Sunday Kelvin Aneke, pledged a comprehensive, transparent investigation and presented national flags to next-of-kin, assuring welfare of families remains top priority.
