**ASABA, Delta State** — In a disturbing case that has sparked outrage across Nigeria’s Delta State, a commercial motorcyclist (okada rider) has been arrested for allegedly kidnapping a three-year-old girl placed in his care for daily school transportation.

The Delta State Police Command confirmed the arrest of Kelvin Ogaga, who had been hired by the child’s parents for a monthly fee of N10,000 to ferry the toddler to and from school. 

After faithfully performing the duty for about three weeks, Ogaga allegedly deviated from the routine one morning, abducting the child instead of taking her to school.

According to the Command’s spokesperson, SP Bright Edafe, the suspect switched off his personal phone to avoid detection but contacted the family using another number. 

He initially demanded N2 million, threatening to harm the child if police were involved. 

The ransom was later negotiated down to N500,000, which the desperate family paid, leading to the toddler’s safe release.

Acting on credible intelligence, police tracked and arrested Ogaga, recovering part of the ransom money. 

During interrogation, the suspect confessed to the crime, claiming he was influenced by a friend identified only as ‘Omo’ due to financial difficulties. 

He also implicated his girlfriend as being aware of the plan. Efforts are ongoing to apprehend other accomplices.

Edafe reassured the public that the child is safe and healthy, emphasizing that the suspect is in custody and part of the ransom has been recovered. 

He urged parents and guardians to thoroughly verify the backgrounds and trustworthiness of anyone they entrust with their children’s care and transportation.

This incident adds to growing worries about child safety and the risks associated with informal transportation arrangements in Nigeria. 

While okada riders provide essential mobility in many communities, cases of betrayal of trust highlight the need for greater vigilance, proper vetting, and possibly formal regulation of child transport services.

Delta State, like many parts of the country, continues to battle various security challenges, including kidnapping for ransom. 

Police authorities have reiterated their commitment to swift response and prosecution in such matters to deter potential offenders.

The quick resolution — with the child unharmed and the suspect apprehended — offers some relief to the victim’s family and serves as a reminder that community vigilance and prompt reporting remain critical tools in combating crime. 

As investigations continue, this case underscores the importance of trust, responsibility, and robust child protection mechanisms in everyday Nigerian life.
