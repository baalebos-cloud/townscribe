**Lagos, Nigeria** — In a passionate street-side interview that quickly went viral, prominent activist Aisha Yesufu and Peter Akah, a vocal supporter associated with the "Peter for Nigeria" movement, delivered a resolute message amid fresh legal turbulence for former Anambra Governor Peter Obi: he will contest the 2027 presidential election regardless of setbacks to his newly adopted political platform.

The video, shared widely by Obi grassroots accounts on Saturday, June 27, 2026, captures the duo responding directly to a Federal High Court ruling in Lokoja, Kogi State. 

Justice Isah Dashen set aside an earlier December 2025 judgment that had compelled the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) to register the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) as a political party. 

The reversal came after an application by the Peace Movement Party (PMP), which claimed it had prior rights to a similar symbol.

Speaking with visible determination while surrounded by microphones and supporters, Yesufu — a well-known #EndSARS advocate and consistent Obi ally — and Akah struck a defiant tone. 

"Peter Obi will surely be on the ballot," they emphasized, framing the court decision as a temporary hurdle rather than a fatal blow. 

They warned against political desperation that could undermine Nigeria's democracy and explicitly stated that President Bola Tinubu would not run unopposed in 2027.

Peter Obi himself has expressed disappointment with the ruling but maintained optimism. He described it as a setback for democratic institutions while reaffirming his commitment to the broader movement for a "New Nigeria." 

Supporters view the NDC as a vehicle for his ambitions after his third-place finish in the 2023 election under the Labour Party banner.

The NDC saga highlights the fluid and often contentious nature of Nigeria's political party registration process ahead of the 2027 polls. 

An initial court victory in late 2025 had positioned the NDC as a potential platform for Obi, reportedly alongside figures like Rabiu Kwankwaso. 

The latest reversal has left the status of its candidates in limbo, prompting speculation about alternative vehicles such as the African Democratic Congress (ADC) or others.

Obi's camp has consistently portrayed such legal maneuvers as attempts by the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) to stifle opposition. 

Yet, analysts note that Nigerian electoral politics frequently involves multi-layered court battles over party symbols, registrations, and candidacies — a pattern seen across parties in past cycles.

Yesufu's involvement adds significant symbolic weight. Known for her fearless activism, she has been a bridge between civil society and the "Obidient" movement that propelled Obi to national prominence in 2023. 

Akah, wearing attire associated with the movement, echoed the sentiment with energetic gestures, underscoring grassroots determination.

Their message resonates with many Nigerians frustrated by economic hardships, governance challenges, and perceived elite maneuvering. 

"*The fight for the soul of Nigeria continues,"* the video implicitly conveys, positioning 2027 as a pivotal battle against entrenched power structures.

With over two years until the election, this episode underscores several realities:
**Legal and Institutional Volatility**:  Court interventions in party affairs remain a double-edged sword, capable of both enabling and derailing ambitions.
**Obi's Enduring Appeal**: Despite party registration woes, his personal brand — built on perceived integrity, governance track record in Anambra (including education improvements and fiscal prudence), and anti-corruption stance — continues to mobilize supporters.
**Opposition Dynamics**: The insistence that Tinubu "will not run unopposed" signals active coalition-building efforts across opposition figures, even as platforms shift.
**Youth and Civil Society Role**: Figures like Yesufu highlight how activism is evolving into direct political engagement.

Critics may dismiss the video as motivational rhetoric amid legal uncertainty, while supporters see it as proof of unyielding resolve. Peter Obi has faced similar obstacles before and emerged as a formidable force. 

Whether the NDC survives further challenges or another platform is adopted, the activists' core assertion remains: the ballot will feature Peter Gregory Obi as a candidate.

As Nigeria grapples with its democratic journey, such public declarations serve as both rallying cries and reminders of the high stakes. 

The coming months will test whether institutional processes or popular will shape the eventual ticket. For now, the Obidient movement signals it is far from deterred.
