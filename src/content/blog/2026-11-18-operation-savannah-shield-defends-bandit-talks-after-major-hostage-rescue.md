**Abuja, Nigeria—November 18, 2026—The** Headquarters of the Joint Task Force Operation Savannah Shield (JTF OPSS) has moved to clarify a widely circulated social media video showing Department of State Services (DSS) personnel and traditional leaders interacting with armed bandits in Kwara State, describing the encounter as a deliberate security initiative rather than fraternization.

In a statement signed by Lieutenant Colonel Ibrahim Shittu, Acting Deputy Director of Army Public Relations for 9 Division and Operation Savannah Shield, the military said the video captured part of an engagement with armed elements who had indicated their willingness to surrender weapons and facilitate the release of kidnapped victims.

The development followed successful military strangulation operations that left the bandits with limited options: surrender or face neutralization.

During the engagement, security forces recovered a total of 12 AK-47 rifles and secured the safe release of 10 kidnapped victims.

*“Contrary to the misleading interpretations accompanying the video, the interaction was neither an act of fraternization nor an abandonment of the military’s constitutional responsibilities,”* the statement emphasized. 

*“Rather, it was a calculated engagement aimed at achieving tangible security outcomes, particularly the recovery of weapons and the safe release of innocent citizens held hostage.”*



**Context of the Operation**



Reports indicate the video was recorded around 2:00 p.m. on November 13 in the Gbugbu Forest area of Edu Local Government Area, Kwara State. It involved troops of the 22 Brigade, DSS operatives, and community leaders engaging three prominent bandit figures identified as Auta Ilori, Dan Karemi, and Ori Gambali.

The process formed part of broader efforts under Operation Savannah Shield, which has intensified pressure on criminal networks operating in parts of the North-Central region. 

Security sources noted that sustained military activity restricted the group’s freedom of movement, creating conditions for non-kinetic engagement aimed at encouraging voluntary surrender.

The military stressed that acceptance of surrender remains an acceptable outcome of conflict resolution and aligns with the Law of Armed Conflict, international best practices, and operational rules of engagement. 

Surrendered combatants are not to be harmed at the point of surrender and will undergo necessary security screening and applicable legal procedures.



**Broader Strategy and Public Appeal**



JTF OPSS reaffirmed its commitment to a comprehensive approach that combines robust kinetic operations with intelligence-led engagements and other non-kinetic measures to address terrorism, banditry and related security threats.

The task force said such initiatives provide opportunities for those willing to abandon violence while ensuring accountability.

The statement urged members of the public to disregard misleading interpretations of the circulating video and avoid sharing unverified narratives that could undermine ongoing security efforts. 

Operation Savannah Shield, it added, remains committed to keeping Nigerians informed through official communication channels.

The clarification comes amid heightened public sensitivity to images of security personnel interacting with armed groups, a sensitivity shaped by years of banditry and kidnapping challenges across several states. 

While some online reactions questioned the optics of the meeting, the military presented the recovery of weapons and release of captives as concrete evidence of operational success.

The rescued victims and recovered rifles were taken into military custody as the surrender process continued. Analysts familiar with the region have noted that non-kinetic approaches, when backed by sustained pressure, can yield results without exposing hostages or troops to the full risks of direct confrontation.

The Armed Forces, the statement concluded, remain resolute, professional, and focused on their constitutional mandate of protecting the territorial integrity of the country and safeguarding lives and property.



***This report is based on the official statement issued by Joint Task Force Operation Savannah Shield on November 17, 2026, and corroborated by multiple contemporary security reports.***
