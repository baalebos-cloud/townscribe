**Minna, Niger State—November 1, 2026**—Amnesty International Nigeria has kicked off the 2026 edition of its Secondary Schools Human Rights Debate Programme, bringing together young minds to discuss children’s rights and pressing social issues in a vibrant competition held in Minna.

The event, which commenced on Wednesday, 21 October 2026, drew students from eight public and private secondary schools for the main competition, while four additional schools attended as observers. Altogether, 176 participants, including students and their coaches, represented 12 secondary schools across the state.

The debate offered a rare platform for teenagers to engage critically with real-life challenges facing children in Nigeria and beyond. 

Participants demonstrated strong knowledge of human rights principles while showcasing critical thinking, public speaking, and structured argumentation skills over three intense rounds of debate.

At the end of the competition, Muhammad Mudasir of the Niger State Teacher Professional Development Institute emerged as the overall winner. 

Adamu Muhammad Ja’agi from Father O’Connell Science College took first runner-up position, while Hauwa Jibril of Aisha Audi School claimed second runner-up.

Amnesty International Nigeria described the programme as a deliberate effort to equip the next generation with the tools needed to understand and defend human rights. 

Organizers emphasized that creating such platforms helps young people value evidence-based debate, strengthen their advocacy abilities, and take an active part in conversations that shape their communities and futures.

The initiative reflects a broader commitment by the organization to nurture civic awareness from the classroom level. 

In a country where children’s rights often face threats ranging from insecurity and abduction to limited access to quality education, events like this aim to plant seeds of awareness and responsibility early.

Students tackled topics that connected classroom knowledge to everyday realities, learning not only how to argue effectively but also why human rights matter in practical terms. 

Coaches and school representatives praised the opportunity for their wards to compete in a structured, respectful environment that rewarded clarity of thought over mere volume.

By the close of the day, the atmosphere remained one of encouragement and shared learning. Winners received recognition, but every participant left with sharper skills and a deeper appreciation of the power of informed advocacy.

Amnesty International Nigeria has continued to invest in youth-focused programmes as part of its wider work on freedom, justice and equality. 

The Minna debate stands as a practical demonstration that meaningful change often begins when young people are given the space and confidence to speak up.

The organization expressed hope that similar platforms will expand to more states, ensuring more secondary school students across Nigeria gain the opportunity to develop into informed, articulate defenders of human rights.
