**ILORIN, Nigeria — November 27, 2026** — Hopes of a sacred journey to Mecca turned into heartbreak for several Kwara State intending pilgrims, as the Ilorin Zonal Directorate of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) arraigned a self-styled visa agent over an alleged N62.5 million Hajj fraud.

The defendant, Hauwa Mohammed Abdulkadir, was docked on Thursday, November 26, 2026, before Justice Haleema Saleeman of the Kwara State High Court, Ilorin, on a seven-count charge bordering on obtaining money by false pretence.

The charges, brought under Section 1(1)(a) and punishable under Section 1(3) of the Advance Fee Fraud and Other Fraud Related Offences Act, 2006, accuse Abdulkadir of posing as a competent travel agent capable of processing Saudi Arabian visas and flight tickets.

According to the EFCC, the alleged fraud occurred in March 2026, when Abdulkadir collected various sums from victims under the pretence of securing travel documents for the 2026 Muslim pilgrimage to the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia.

Details of the charge sheet reveal the scale of the alleged deception. Count six accuses her of obtaining N39,570,000 from one Saka Rauf Ajagbe between January and February 2026, purportedly to procure visas for him and others he represented.

Count seven further alleges that between December 2025 and April 2026, she obtained N14,705,000 from Abdulmehumeen Abdulsalam for the same purpose—representations the Commission says she knew to be false.

When the charges were read, Abdulkadir pleaded not guilty.

Following her plea, EFCC counsel, Rashidat Alao, urged the court to fix a date for trial and remand the defendant in prison custody. However, defence counsel, O. A. Teni, prayed the court to admit her to bail to enable her to source funds for restitution to the victims.

Justice Saleeman granted bail in the sum of N5 million with three sureties in like sum and adjourned the case until December 3, 2026, for commencement of trial.

The case underscores growing concerns over Hajj and visa-related fraud in Nigeria, where desperate pilgrims are often exploited by unlicensed agents. The EFCC reiterated its warning to the public to verify agents through approved authorities.
