**Port Harcourt, Rivers State – July 3, 2026** – In a striking display of international military solidarity, Nigeria’s Chief of Army Staff, Lieutenant General Waidi Shaibu, hosted a distinguished gathering of army chiefs and senior officers from across Africa and beyond at an exclusive cocktail reception this evening. 

The event, held in Port Harcourt as part of the ongoing 163rd Nigerian Army Day Celebration (NADCEL 2026), underscored Nigeria’s growing role as a pivotal player in regional and global defense cooperation.

The reception brought together military leaders in an atmosphere of camaraderie and strategic dialogue. 

Photographs from the event capture Lieutenant General Shaibu, dressed in elegant traditional attire, warmly engaging with counterparts—shaking hands, exchanging ideas over refreshments, and fostering personal connections that often form the bedrock of enduring partnerships. 

Attendees included senior figures from various nations, reflecting a broad spectrum of international military representation.

In his welcome address, Lt. Gen. Shaibu framed the anniversary not merely as a celebration of the Nigerian Army’s storied history but as a vital platform for *“strengthening military diplomacy, fostering professional engagement, and promoting the exchange of operational experiences among partner armies.*” 

He emphasized that such interactions build mutual understanding and deepen cooperation essential for tackling contemporary security challenges.

The COAS highlighted the realities facing armed forces worldwide: evolving and transnational threats that no single nation can confront in isolation. 

“*Sustained collaboration, knowledge sharing, and interoperability*,” he noted, *are critical to enhancing collective security and operational effectiveness.* 

Partnerships rooted in trust and shared professional values, Shaibu stressed, remain indispensable for safeguarding peace and regional stability.

This year’s celebrations, hosted primarily in Rivers State by the 6 Division Nigerian Army, commemorate 163 years of the Nigerian Army’s service since its formal establishment. 

The theme—“**Protecting the Nation and Serving the People: A Way Forward for the Nigerian Army”**—encapsulates a dual commitment to national defense and public welfare. Activities have included interdenominational services honoring fallen heroes, prayers for veterans and the wounded, public engagement with students on patriotism and career opportunities, and various community initiatives.

The cocktail reception serves as a prelude to the 3rd Africa Land Forces Forum (ALFF 2026), scheduled for July 4–6 at the EUI Event Center in Port Harcourt. 

This strategic defense forum, held under the patronage of the Nigerian Army, will delve deeper into land force priorities, operational collaboration, and emerging defense technologies. The grand finale is set for July 6 at Liberation Stadium, featuring parades and awards.

Lieutenant General Waidi Shaibu, who assumed office as COAS in late 2025 after a distinguished career that included commanding Operation Hadin Kai in the Northeast, has consistently championed intelligence-driven operations, troop welfare under the “**Soldiers First**” policy, and enhanced inter-service and international partnerships. 

His leadership comes at a time when Nigeria grapples with multifaceted security issues, including insurgency, banditry, and other transnational threats.

Beyond the pomp and pageantry, events like this carry profound implications. In an era of hybrid warfare, climate-induced conflicts, and cross-border extremism, military diplomacy serves as a force multiplier. 

Nigeria, with its large, battle-tested army and strategic position in West Africa, is positioning itself as a hub for collaborative solutions. 

Such gatherings enhance interoperability—vital for joint operations—and facilitate the sharing of best practices in counter-terrorism, peacekeeping, and humanitarian assistance.

For host communities in Rivers State, the celebrations also highlight the Army’s civil-military cooperation (CIMIC) efforts, including project commissions and outreach, reinforcing the bond between the military and civilians it serves.

As the week unfolds toward the grand finale, the message from Port Harcourt is clear: in facing shared threats, professional armies stand stronger together. 

The Nigerian Army’s proactive engagement signals not just celebration of the past, but a forward-looking commitment to a more secure future for the nation and the continent.
