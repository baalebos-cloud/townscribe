**Washington—August 17, 2026—**President Donald Trump said Monday that Iran is unwilling to accept the kind of agreement he considers essential to permanently prevent it from obtaining a nuclear weapon, underscoring the central red line of U.S. policy as a key negotiating window expired.

Speaking to reporters in the Oval Office, Trump stated, "*They're not going to make the kind of deal that I feel is necessary. Look, we’re in there for one reason: Iran cannot have a nuclear weapon."*

He repeated the point for emphasis, adding that Iran *“cannot have a nuclear weapon, and they won’t have a nuclear weapon.”*

The president also argued that any effort by Tehran to rebuild nuclear capabilities damaged by earlier U.S. strikes involving B-2 bombers would require a significant amount of time.



**End of the 60-Day Window**

Monday marked the end of the maximum 60-day period set out in a June memorandum of understanding (MOU) between the United States and Iran. 

That interim arrangement had called for an immediate halt to military operations and committed both sides to negotiate a broader final deal covering Iran’s nuclear program.

The MOU quickly frayed. Trump declared it *“over”* in early July, while Iran’s foreign ministry later described it as suspended. Asked whether the United States was seeking to extend the interim deal, Trump replied simply: *“No.”*

Earlier the same day, Trump posted on Truth Social that preventing Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon *“in any way, shape, or form*” remains the administration’s “*number one goal.”* 

He also told a Fox News reporter that Iran “should put up the white flag of surrender.”



**Broader Context of the Standoff**

The remarks come amid a prolonged U.S.-Iran conflict that began earlier this year, involving strikes on Iranian nuclear and military facilities, a naval blockade of Iranian ports, and disruptions to shipping through the strategically vital Strait of Hormuz. 

Talks aimed at converting the temporary understanding into a lasting settlement have remained stalled, with each side accusing the other of failing to meet core demands.

Trump has consistently framed the prevention of an Iranian nuclear weapon as non-negotiable, a position he has repeated dozens of times over the years. 

Iranian officials have long maintained that their nuclear program is for peaceful purposes and have rejected what they view as excessive restrictions on their sovereign rights.



**Regional and Diplomatic Ripples**

Separately, Oman has been engaged in discussions with Iran aimed at restoring commercial traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. 

Trump warned that interference by third parties would be met firmly, telling Fox News that if Oman *“gets in the way,*” the United States would respond forcefully, though he later said in the Oval Office that he was not out of patience and that any issues could be handled readily.

The prolonged confrontation has kept pressure on global energy markets and remains politically sensitive in the United States ahead of the November midterm elections. 

Trump insisted the midterms were not influencing his approach: “*Midterms have nothing to do with my thinking.”*

As the interim negotiating window closed without a comprehensive agreement, the path forward remained uncertain. 

Trump’s latest comments reinforce that any future diplomatic progress will hinge on ironclad, verifiable guarantees that Iran cannot develop, acquire, or possess a nuclear weapon—a demand Tehran has so far shown little willingness to fully meet on the terms Washington requires.
