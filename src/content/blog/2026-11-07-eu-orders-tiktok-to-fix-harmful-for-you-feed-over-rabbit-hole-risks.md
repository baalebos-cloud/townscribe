**Brussels, Belgium—November 7, 2026**—The European Commission has preliminarily found that TikTok is in breach of the European Union’s Digital Services Act over the design of its highly popular “*For You*” feed, accusing the platform of creating harmful “rabbit holes” that repeatedly expose users to content about sadness, isolation, and body image.

In a statement released on Friday, 6 November, the Commission said TikTok’s recommendation algorithm continuously serves users more of the same type of video whenever they scroll, like, or watch content for longer periods. 

This design, officials warned, can pull people—especially minors and vulnerable adults—into prolonged exposure to material that risks damaging their mental health.

*“Every time a user scrolls or likes a video on TikTok, the algorithm serves more of the same, leading users down rabbit holes they cannot easily escape from,”* said Henna Virkkunen, the European Commission’s Executive Vice-President for Tech Sovereignty, Security and Democracy. 

“*Today’s preliminary findings show again that TikTok needs to step up its efforts to protect users’ well-being, including those of minors.”*

The investigation forms part of a broader, years-long probe into TikTok under the Digital Services Act (DSA), the EU’s landmark rulebook that requires large online platforms to assess and mitigate systemic risks to users. 

This is the first time the Commission has specifically targeted a social media platform’s recommender system as a potential DSA breach.

According to the Commission’s preliminary findings, TikTok failed to adequately assess how its algorithm could lead users into these rabbit holes. 

The platform has introduced some mitigation tools, including a “not interested” button and content diversification measures. 

However, regulators concluded these are not effective enough. The algorithm continues to prioritize viewing time over a user’s explicit choice to see less of certain content, meaning the “not interested” feature often fails to meaningfully alter the feed.

The Commission has instructed TikTok to redesign the For You feed. Suggested changes include giving greater weight to users’ explicit preferences, disabling viewing time as a key indicator for recommendations, making user controls more accessible and easy to use, and regularly testing the algorithm for harmful effects.

TikTok has rejected the findings. A company spokesperson said the Commission’s statements were *“fundamentally inaccurate”* and distorted the platform’s architecture and commitment to user safety. 

*“We have a strong record of protecting our community while giving users direct and intuitive control over their content experience,”* the spokesperson added.

If the preliminary findings are confirmed after TikTok’s response and further proceedings, the Chinese-owned platform could face a fine of up to six percent of its total worldwide annual turnover. 

The exact amount would depend on the nature, gravity, duration and recurrence of any confirmed infringement.

The case comes amid growing global scrutiny of short-form video platforms and their impact on mental health, particularly among young people. It also follows earlier EU actions against TikTok over addictive design features such as infinite scroll, autoplay, and push notifications.

TikTok now has the opportunity to examine the Commission’s investigation file and submit a written defence. The European Board for Digital Services will also be consulted before any final decision is reached. 

For now, the message from Brussels is clear: the era of engagement-at-all-costs algorithms is under serious regulatory pressure in Europe.
