**KYIV, Ukraine —** In the pale light of a July morning, rescue workers sifted through mounds of concrete and twisted rebar where apartment blocks once stood. 

Families huddled in shelters or emerged dazed from the night's terror, their lives upended once more by the relentless rhythm of war. 

Russian forces unleashed hundreds of drones and dozens of missiles in one of the most intense attacks on Kyiv in recent memory, claiming at least 22 lives and injuring well over 90 others, according to Ukrainian officials.

The assault, which stretched for hours into Thursday, July 2, 2026, damaged around 130 buildings, including residential structures, and sent shockwaves through a city long hardened by conflict. 

Explosions reverberated across districts as air defenses engaged the incoming barrage, but the sheer volume — with reports of ballistic missiles arriving mere minutes after alerts — overwhelmed protections amid reported shortages of interceptors.

Al Jazeera correspondent Audrey MacAlpine captured the human toll on the ground. Residents described altering daily rhythms — rushing to basements, avoiding routines, and living with constant vigilance — to survive. 

One man, standing amid the debris of his damaged home, surveyed shattered walls and scattered belongings. 

An elderly woman recounted the fear gripping her neighborhood. Even pets appeared unsettled in the footage, symbols of disrupted lives in a city under siege.

### Scenes of Devastation and Resilience

Video from the scene shows orange excavators clawing through piles of rubble at collapsed multi-story buildings, dust rising against a clear blue sky. 

Firefighters and volunteers worked frantically as sirens wailed. In one poignant frame, a resident carefully tends to a birdcage in a partially destroyed kitchen, a quiet act of normalcy amid chaos.

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko and other officials reported widespread damage across more than 20 sites. 

Strikes hit civilian infrastructure, with reports of impacts near logistics centers, warehouses, and residential zones. 

Ukrainian authorities described it as retaliation linked to Ukrainian operations against Russian oil facilities, which have strained Moscow's resources.

This attack stands out for its scale and intensity. Officials and analysts noted it as among the heaviest on the capital this year, echoing earlier deadly barrages but with fresh urgency as the broader war grinds on into its fifth year. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and Ukrainian leaders have repeatedly called for stronger international support, particularly air defenses, to counter such waves.

### Broader Context and Human Cost

The war's toll on civilians remains staggering. Beyond the immediate casualties in Kyiv, similar strikes have hit other cities like Dnipro in recent waves, compounding a humanitarian crisis marked by displacement, infrastructure ruin, and psychological strain. 

Families in Kyiv now face power outages, disrupted services, and the grim task of burying loved ones while wondering what the next night will bring.

For many residents, adaptation has become survival. Shelters that once seemed temporary fixtures have turned into extensions of daily life. 

Parents shield children from the sounds of explosions; workers navigate debris-strewn streets. The attack underscores the war's grinding attrition: even as diplomatic murmurs persist — with some optimism from U.S. officials under President Trump about potential peace talks — the violence continues unabated.

Russia has framed such operations as responses to Ukrainian strikes, while Ukraine and its allies condemn them as indiscriminate terror against civilians. 

Independent verification remains challenging amid the fog of war, but footage and official tallies paint a consistent picture of widespread destruction in urban areas.

### Implications for a Protracted Conflict

This latest escalation raises pressing questions about the sustainability of Ukraine's defenses and the international response. 

With energy infrastructure and logistics repeatedly targeted on both sides, the conflict risks deeper economic pain and prolonged suffering. 

Aid organizations warn of growing needs for medical support, reconstruction, and psychological care in affected communities.

As Kyiv buries its dead and begins clearing the rubble, the message from its people is one of weary defiance mixed with exhaustion. 

*"We change our routines to stay safe," one survivor told reporters, "but we refuse to surrender our lives to fear."*

The world watches as another chapter unfolds in Europe's longest-running major conflict since World War II—a reminder that behind every headline statistic are individual stories of loss, resilience, and the unyielding human spirit in the face of adversity. 

Recovery will be long and arduous, but for now, the focus remains on survival and solidarity in a city that has endured far too much.
