Vice President Kashim Shettima on Tuesday made a pointed appeal to northern voters, declaring that ***“this is payback time” as*** he pressed the case for President Bola Tinubu’s re-election in 2027. Speaking in Borno State at the distribution and flag-off of special intervention projects by the North-East Development Commission (NEDC), Shettima framed the forthcoming presidential contest as an opportunity for the region to return a political favour granted more than a decade earlier.

Shettima reminded his audience of Tinubu’s critical intervention ahead of the 2015 general election. He said the then-opposition leader deliberately shifted the All Progressives Congress (APC) presidential primary to Lagos and mobilized the South-West, actions that helped former President Muhammadu Buhari clinch the party’s ticket. 

*“Those of us that were active in the political scene can bear testimony to the fact that without the efforts of President Bola Tinubu, Muhammadu Buhari couldn’t have clinched the ticket of the APC in 2014 in Lagos,”* the Vice President stated. 

*“He deliberately shifted the base of the primaries to Lagos, and he worked assiduously to deliver the South-West. And one good turn always deserves another.”*

Turning to the present, Shettima urged northerners to exercise informed judgment. *“And for us in the North, this is payback time. I urge you, I beseech all of us here, let us use our informed judgment and make decisions that are in the best interest of our nation,”* he said. 

The remarks come as the APC intensifies its campaign machinery across the country ahead of the January 2027 polls, with Shettima, a former Borno governor, serving as a key mobilizer in the Northeast.

Beyond historical reciprocity, the Vice President pointed to recent economic indicators as further justification for continuity. 

He noted that headline inflation has declined and that Nigeria’s foreign reserves have climbed to their highest level in 17 years, now hovering around $55 billion. 

Defending the removal of the petrol subsidy, Shettima insisted the decision was taken to avert economic collapse rather than to impose hardship. “The President has a great deal of human empathy for the Nigerian people. 

He withdrew the subsidy not to inflict pain on Nigerians, no. He removed the subsidy to save the Nigerian economy from collapse,” he argued.

The appearance in Borno forms part of a broader working visit that also included the inauguration of digital skills facilities and other development projects. 

With the 2027 election drawing closer, Shettima’s message blends personal political history with claims of economic stabilisation, seeking to convert regional gratitude into electoral support for the Tinubu-Shettima ticket.
