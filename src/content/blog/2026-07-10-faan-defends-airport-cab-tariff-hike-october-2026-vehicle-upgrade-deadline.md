**LAGOS, Nigeria** — The Federal Airports Authority of Nigeria (FAAN) has robustly defended its controversial airport cab vehicle upgrade policy and revised operational tariff, describing the measures as essential steps toward enhancing passenger safety, comfort, and overall service standards at the nation’s airports.

In an official statement issued on Friday, July 10, 2026, FAAN emphasized that the policy — which includes a tariff adjustment and a mandatory vehicle upgrade deadline of October 2026 — was not designed to punish operators but to address longstanding issues affecting millions of air travelers across Nigeria. 

The authority noted that stakeholders had been notified of the requirements since July 2024, giving operators ample time to prepare.

FAAN highlighted persistent challenges with aging cabs operating at airports, including poor vehicle conditions that compromise passenger safety and the overall travel experience. 

The new policy mandates upgrades to modern, roadworthy vehicles equipped with necessary safety features, air conditioning, and professional standards befitting international gateways like Murtala Muhammed International Airport in Lagos and others nationwide.

The tariff hike is intended to support operators in achieving these upgrades while ensuring fair compensation for improved services. 

FAAN maintained that the changes align with global best practices in airport ground transportation and respond to frequent passenger complaints about substandard cabs.

The announcement comes amid reported pushback from some cab operators concerned about the financial implications of vehicle upgrades and the revised tariffs.

FAAN urged operators to view the policy as an investment in their long-term viability and Nigeria’s aviation industry reputation. 

The authority reiterated its commitment to dialogue with stakeholders to ensure smooth implementation.

This development forms part of broader efforts to modernize Nigeria’s aviation infrastructure and improve user experience. In recent years, FAAN and other agencies under the Ministry of Aviation have pursued reforms aimed at positioning Nigerian airports as competitive regional hubs, addressing everything from terminal upgrades to ground handling services.

For millions of Nigerians who rely on airport taxis for seamless connections between flights and destinations, the policy promises safer and more reliable transportation. 

Frequent travelers have long voiced frustrations over inflated fares paired with poor vehicle conditions, especially during peak periods or inclement weather.

Industry observers note that successful implementation could set a positive precedent for regulated transport services at other transport hubs. 

However, critics worry about potential short-term disruptions or cost pass-throughs to passengers if operators face genuine financial strain.

As the October 2026 deadline approaches, FAAN’s firm stance signals determination to enforce standards, while leaving room for collaboration. 

The coming months will test the policy’s practicality and its ability to deliver tangible improvements without undue burden on operators or travelers.

In Nigeria’s evolving aviation sector, initiatives like this underscore the tension between regulation for public good and the economic realities of service providers. 

FAAN’s defense of the airport cab tariff hike and upgrade policy reflects a clear priority: elevating service quality to match the aspirations of a modernizing air travel industry.
