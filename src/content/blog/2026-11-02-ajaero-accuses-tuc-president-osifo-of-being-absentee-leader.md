**Abuja, Nigeria—November 2, 2026**—Nigerian Labour Congress (NLC) President Joe Ajaero has accused Trade Union Congress (TUC) President Festus Osifo of being an “absentee president” who does not live in Nigeria and only comes into the country from the United States to attend meetings before rushing back.

Speaking on Channels Television’s Morning Brief on Monday, Ajaero escalated the ongoing public dispute between the two labour centres over the Federal Government’s N70,000 monthly transport allowance for workers.

*“He (Festus Osifo) is an absentee president of the TUC; he doesn’t live in Nigeria. He comes in from the United States to attend meetings and rush back,”* Ajaero said.

He further challenged Osifo and the Nigeria Immigration Service to prove that the TUC leader has spent four to five months in the country this year, arguing that no one living abroad should hold a central leadership position in a national labor organization.



**Background to the Clash**

The personal attacks come against the backdrop of a sharp disagreement that began late last week after the Federal Government approved an N70,000 monthly transport support for public sector workers for six months, beginning November 2026. 

The package was reached following a conciliation meeting between government officials and the NLC.

The TUC promptly rejected the allowance, insisting that temporary interventions are unsustainable and calling instead for an immediate and permanent salary increase. 

Osifo maintained that the TUC had already communicated its position to the government before the NLC meeting and that Ajaero had no mandate to negotiate on behalf of TUC members.

Ajaero responded by saying the TUC could not reject an agreement it did not negotiate or sign, and suggested both centres should have pushed for better terms rather than opposing each other.



**Growing Rift in Organized Labor**

What started as a policy disagreement has now turned personal, with both leaders trading accusations. Osifo had earlier claimed that Ajaero short-changed workers during previous negotiations and could not speak for organized labor as a whole. Ajaero has now countered with questions about Osifo’s residency and commitment to the job.

Observers note that the public feud risks weakening the collective strength of organized labor at a time when Nigerian workers continue to face severe cost-of-living pressures.

Neither Osifo nor the TUC had issued an immediate response to Ajaero’s latest comments as of Monday morning.



***This report is based on statements made by NLC President Joe Ajaero on Channels Television on November 2, 2026, and related coverage of the ongoing NLC-TUC dispute.***
