**Abuja—November 25, 2026—Nigeria's** long-running $1.6 billion oil fraud trial took a dramatic turn on Wednesday as the Court of Appeal, Abuja Division, jailed oil businessman Olajide Jones Omokore and four others to seven years' imprisonment each.

The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission, EFCC, which secured the conviction, described the judgment as a landmark victory for accountability in the oil sector.

In a unanimous judgment, a three-member panel comprising Justices James Gambo Abundaga, Yusuf Alhaji Bashir, and Kenneth Ikechukwu Amadi convicted them on counts 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 10, 11, and 12 of the charge while discharging them on the remaining counts.

The court sentenced Omokore, Victor Briggs, former Managing Director of the Nigerian Petroleum Development Company, NPDC, and Abiye Membere, former Group Executive Director, Exploration and Production of the Nigerian National Petroleum Corporation, NNPC, to seven years imprisonment each, with the sentences running concurrently.

In a devastating blow to the business empire at the centre of the scam, the court also ordered the winding up of the two companies involved—Atlantic Energy Brass Development Limited and Atlantic Energy Drilling Concept Limited.

The convicts were prosecuted on a 15-count charge bordering on obtaining under false pretence, conspiracy, money laundering, and procurement fraud arising from a controversial Strategic Alliance Agreement (SAA) between the NPDC and Atlantic Energy for the exploitation of Oil Mining Licences (OMLs) 60, 61, 62, and 63, otherwise known as the Brass Assets.

The prosecution’s case was that Omokore and his companies lifted over 7.5 million barrels of crude oil valued at $823,075,189.95 without paying the mandatory entry fee of $123.19 million nor fulfilling their financial obligations under the agreement.

Justice Nnamdi Dimgba of the Federal High Court, Abuja, had on February 7, 2023, discharged and acquitted the defendants on counts 1 to 9 and 12 to 15, discharging Briggs and Membere of money laundering in counts 10 and 11 but convicting them of the lesser offence of official corruption with a fine of N2 million each.

Dissatisfied, lead prosecutor Rotimi Jacobs, SAN, filed a Notice of Appeal on March 6, 2023, anchored on 34 grounds. Omokore countered with a preliminary objection challenging the competence of the appeal.

In its Composite Reply Brief, the EFCC argued the appeal was competent, the lower court wrongly treated a clear fraud as a mere commercial dispute, and cited a March 5, 2019 arbitral award which held the companies were “unjustly enriched by the payments that they received from the sale of crude oil.”

The Commission also tendered evidence of luxury vehicles, including a N23 million car bought through Omokore’s account and delivered to Briggs through his wife, stressing that a contract or arbitration, which started August 15, 2016, could not erase a criminal charge filed June 2, 2016.

The ruling is seen as one of the EFCC’s biggest appellate victories under Chairman Ola Olukoyede.
