**Uyo—November 25, 2026—The** Akwa Ibom State Police Command has smashed a suspected criminal fortress in Obot Akara Local Government Area, arresting six men and recovering a human skull, a firearm and suspected ritual materials.

The breakthrough operation, carried out at about 1:00 p.m. on Monday, November 24, 2026, at Abiakpo Village, followed what police described as credible intelligence that members of a dangerous gang were gathering and *“fortifying”* themselves for major criminal operations.

Police Public Relations Officer DSP Timfon John disclosed the feat in a statement issued in Uyo on Wednesday on behalf of the Commissioner of Police, CP Baba Mohammed Azare.

According to John, the gang allegedly comprised suspected members of the Indigenous People of Biafra, IPOB, armed robbers and kidnappers who had turned the remote hideout into a launch pad.

“When operatives stormed the location, six suspects were arrested, while other members of the gang fled upon sighting the police,” she said.

Those in custody were identified as Victor Effiong, 30, described as the alleged leader from Abiakpo Ibor Village in Obot Akara; Samuel Alexander, 29, and Joshua Alexander, 26, both from Obingwa community in Abia State; John Samuel, 29, and Inyele Jesse, 22, from Eleme and Abia Village in Rivers State; and Iboro Udoudo, 43, from Abiakpo Village.

What shocked even battle-hardened officers was the haul recovered inside the den: one locally made revolver pistol, three rounds of 7.65mm ammunition, two live cartridges, one human skull, one red candle, four black candles, one antelope horn, one native pot tied with red cloth, one penny, one bottle of Danduala incense and other suspected fetish objects.

Security analysts say the combination of guns and ritual items points to a disturbing trend where criminals blend spiritual fortification with violent crime to instil fear and loyalty.

CP Azare, while commending the operatives for swift action on intelligence, reiterated the command’s unwavering resolve to identify, dismantle, and eliminate criminal hideouts and black spots across the state in line with the directives of Inspector-General of Police, Tunji Disu.

He appealed to residents of Obot Akara, a border council sharing boundaries with Abia and Rivers States, to volunteer timely and actionable information, assuring utmost confidentiality.

Police say investigations are ongoing to track down fleeing members, unravel the source of the human skull, determine the full activities of the gang and prosecute anyone found culpable.

For residents of Abiakpo, the raid brings cautious relief—and a haunting question: what evil rituals were being perfected in their backyard?
