**Abuja, Nigeria—In** a significant push to empower small businesses and formalize the informal economy, President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has approved free Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) registration for 250,000 Micro, Small, and Medium Enterprises (MSMEs) across Nigeria. The landmark initiative was announced on Saturday, June 27, 2026, during the 8th National MSME Awards at the State House in Abuja.

This move, framed under the administration’s Renewed Hope Agenda, aims to dismantle financial barriers that often discourage entrepreneurs from registering their businesses. 

Normally, business name registration with the CAC costs around ₦10,000 to ₦11,000 in statutory fees. 

Under this programme, selected MSMEs will receive full formalization and corporate registration at no cost, enabling them to operate legally, access credit, build credibility, and scale more effectively.

Beyond waiving registration fees, the initiative includes additional layers of support such as moral encouragement for business owners, technical training for beneficiaries, and efforts to integrate them into the formal economy. 

The partnership between the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC) and the Small and Medium Enterprises Development Agency of Nigeria (SMEDAN) is central to implementation, building on earlier collaborations launched in late 2025.

SMEDAN and CAC officials have emphasized seamless processes, with certificates to be issued promptly to approved applicants. 

Interested MSMEs are encouraged to engage through SMEDAN channels, including their official website and programmes, to access this opportunity.

Nigeria’s MSME sector is the backbone of the economy, employing millions and driving innovation, yet many operate informally due to high registration costs, bureaucratic hurdles, and limited awareness. 

Formal registration unlocks benefits like access to government contracts, bank loans, grants, and legal protections — advantages critical in an economy grappling with inflation, youth unemployment, and post-subsidy adjustment challenges.

President Tinubu’s approval signals a continued focus on entrepreneurship as a pathway to job creation and inclusive growth. 

By targeting 250,000 businesses nationwide, the programme spans urban centers and rural communities, potentially benefiting artisans, traders, tech startups, agripreneurs, and service providers alike.

Public response on social media has been mixed but largely positive. Many entrepreneurs welcomed the relief, describing it as a “practical step” toward empowerment and a boost for formalization. 

Supporters highlighted how lowered barriers could spur business growth, job creation, and economic diversification.

Skeptics, however, raised valid concerns: registration alone may not suffice without addressing deeper issues such as unreliable power supply, high operating costs, fuel prices, access to affordable credit, and market challenges. 

Some noted past initiatives that faced implementation hurdles or “hidden costs,” urging transparency in beneficiary selection and efficient rollout. 

Others pointed out that while free registration helps startups, sustaining businesses in Nigeria’s tough economic climate remains the real test.

Veteran observers view this as part of ongoing CAC reforms under the Tinubu administration, aimed at making business formalization more accessible and business-friendly. 

Earlier phases of similar programmes had registered thousands, but the current scale—250,000—represents an ambitious expansion.

As details on application processes, selection criteria, and timelines emerge, the success of this initiative will hinge on transparent execution, widespread awareness campaigns, and complementary policies that tackle electricity, infrastructure, and financing gaps. 

For many aspiring and existing entrepreneurs, this could mark the beginning of a more supportive ecosystem.

President Tinubu’s administration has positioned MSME development as a cornerstone of economic recovery. 

Whether this free registration drive translates into tangible growth will depend on effective implementation and the resilience of Nigeria’s small business owners. 

In the meantime, the announcement offers a timely morale boost and practical incentive for thousands to take the formal leap.
