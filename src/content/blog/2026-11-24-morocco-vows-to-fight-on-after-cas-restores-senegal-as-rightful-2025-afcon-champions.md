**Lausanne, Switzerland—November 24, 2026**—The Court of Arbitration for Sport has restored Senegal as champions of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations, ruling that the original 1-0 extra-time victory over hosts Morocco must stand. 

The unanimous decision ends months of uncertainty but has already sparked a fresh legal response from the Royal Moroccan Football Federation.

The drama began on 18 January 2026 at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat. With the final goalless deep into stoppage time, Congolese referee Jean-Jacques Ndala awarded Morocco a penalty. Senegalese players walked off the pitch in protest for around 14 minutes. 

They eventually returned. Morocco’s Brahim Diaz saw his spot-kick saved, and Pape Gueye scored the only goal of the match in extra time. Senegal lifted the trophy on the night.

In March, the Confederation of African Football’s Appeal Board overturned the result. Citing tournament regulations on teams that leave the field, the board declared Senegal had forfeited the match and awarded Morocco a 3-0 victory. The Senegalese Football Federation appealed to CAS.

On Tuesday the Swiss-based court set aside the CAF Appeal Board’s decision and reinstated the earlier finding of the CAF Disciplinary Board. 

The panel held that the referee’s choice to restart the match fell within his sole authority under the “*field of play”* doctrine. Forfeiture rules, the arbitrators said, apply only when a referee permanently abandons a game. That did not occur.

CAS secretary general Matthieu Reeb confirmed the referee “did not commit any error of law.” The original 1-0 scoreline now stands, confirming Senegal’s second AFCON title.

Morocco has refused to accept the outcome. The Royal Moroccan Football Federation stated it will ***“pursue all necessary legal avenues,***” including a possible appeal to Switzerland’s Federal Supreme Court on procedural grounds. 

The federation also raised questions about alleged contact between one of the CAS arbitrators and the Senegal federation president shortly before the ruling.

The case has exposed deep tensions between the laws of the game, which treat the referee’s decisions as final, and competition regulations. 

For now, Senegal stands as champions once more. Morocco’s next legal steps will determine whether the dispute finally ends or continues into another chapter of African football’s most contentious final in recent memory.
