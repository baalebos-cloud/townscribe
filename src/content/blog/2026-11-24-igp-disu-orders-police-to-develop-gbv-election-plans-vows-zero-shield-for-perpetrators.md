**Abuja, Nigeria—November 24, 2026—**In a firm move to protect women’s participation in Nigeria’s democracy, Inspector-General of Police Olatunji Rilwan Disu has directed all Police Commands to draw up dedicated Gender-Based Violence (GBV) Election Communication Plans before the 2027 General Elections. 

The directive came on Tuesday as the IGP opened a two-day regional capacity-building workshop for Police Public Relations Officers organized by CLEEN Foundation at the Nigeria Police Force Public Relations School in Abuja.

Disu described election-related gender-based violence as a clear electoral security threat. He stressed that no perpetrator would be shielded because of political connections or status. 

The Force, he said, remains fully committed to ensuring women can safely take part as voters, candidates, observers and journalists.

The IGP charged Public Relations Officers to communicate promptly, accurately and with empathy. They must protect the identities and dignity of those who report GBV, avoid victim-blaming or sensational language, and refrain from any statement that could prejudice ongoing investigations. 

Officers were also urged to educate the public on the criminal nature of GBV, encourage reporting, and share verified updates on police action and available support services.

Closer coordination with Gender Desk Officers, investigators, the Independent National Electoral Commission, election observers and civil society groups was ordered to guarantee timely response. 

Disu issued a clear warning: Police platforms must never be used to mock, dismiss or politicise complaints. Any officer who mishandles cases, demands payment before investigation, or compromises the safety or confidentiality of complainants will face disciplinary action.

Dr Salaudeen Hashim, Director of Programmes at CLEEN Foundation, highlighted the power of behavioural change communication in preventing sexual and gender-based violence. 

He called on participants to craft messages that challenge harmful social norms, reduce stigma and promote safe reporting, especially during election periods.

The IGP praised CLEEN Foundation for its long-standing partnership and urged officers to apply the training in their respective commands. 

He reaffirmed the Force’s resolve to strengthen Gender Desks and Family Support Units, improve personnel capacity for sensitive handling of GBV cases, and ensure prompt investigation and prosecution.

The workshop forms part of broader efforts to professionalize police communication and protect vulnerable groups as Nigeria prepares for another high-stakes electoral cycle. 

By placing clear communication plans, designated spokespersons, hotlines and referral pathways at the centre of election security, the Nigeria Police Force signals that safeguarding women is now treated as a core operational priority rather than an afterthought.

***CSP Ani Iniedu, Force Public Relations Officer, issued the statement from Force Headquarters, Abuja, on 24 November 2026.***
