**Abuja, Nigeria – July 24, 2026—Tensions** within Nigeria’s main opposition party, the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP), have resurfaced as a senior chieftain publicly faulted the Federal Capital Territory (FCT) Minister, Nyesom Wike, over his vocal support for President Bola Tinubu’s re-election bid.

In a candid interview on ARISE News, Dan Ulasi, a notable PDP stakeholder, described Wike’s declaration of support for Tinubu—made while claiming leadership of the PDP—as "*demoralizing"* to party faithful. 

Ulasi argued that such a move, especially months before the election cycle intensifies, undermines the party’s ability to mount a credible challenge.

Ulasi recalled raising the issue directly with Wike earlier, stressing that while individuals have the right to support any candidate, a national leader of the PDP should not prematurely align with the ruling party’s candidate. 

*“He would have waited till the election is close and give reasons why he’s supporting him,”* Ulasi stated, highlighting the timing and optics of the endorsement.

Wike, a powerful figure in Rivers State politics and a key minister in the Tinubu administration, has repeatedly affirmed his backing for the President, citing developmental projects and appointments benefiting his state. 

He has positioned this support as pragmatic political realism, even as he maintains influence within PDP structures.

The remarks come amid ongoing divisions within the PDP, which continues to grapple with internal factions and leadership questions. 

Critics like Ulasi warn that contradictions at the leadership level weaken the party’s prospects against the All Progressives Congress (APC) in the next general elections. 

Some observers note that such rifts could push more members toward alternative platforms or coalitions.

Ulasi’s comments reflect a broader frustration among some PDP members who believe the party must resolve its internal inconsistencies to present a united front. 

He emphasized that Wike cannot effectively lead a national opposition party while openly championing the incumbent President.

The controversy has ignited discussions across political circles, with some viewing Wike’s stance as strategic bridge-building and others seeing it as a betrayal of PDP ideals. 

Supporters of the minister point to his delivery of federal projects in the FCT and Rivers State as justification for cross-party collaboration.

As Nigeria inches closer to the 2027 polls, questions about opposition cohesion, party loyalty, and individual political calculations are expected to dominate conversations. 

The PDP faces the challenge of reconciling influential voices like Wike with the expectations of its core base.

For now, Dan Ulasi’s pointed critique adds another layer to the complex dynamics shaping Nigeria’s political landscape, underscoring the delicate balance between personal convictions and party obligations.
