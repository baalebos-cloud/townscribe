Mauricio Pochettino’s first afternoon as Manchester United manager lasted barely eight minutes before the scale of the task ahead became painfully clear. By full-time at Anfield on Sunday, the Argentine had suffered a 4-0 humiliation at the hands of Liverpool — the joint-heaviest defeat endured by any United manager on his debut and the worst in 100 years.

The decisive moment arrived early. Leny Yoro was shown a straight red card in the eighth minute for a foul on Alexander Isak as the last defender. Dominik Szoboszlai immediately punished the decision, curling the resulting free-kick into the bottom corner. 

Three minutes later Florian Wirtz produced a moment of brilliance, swiveling to volley home from Rio Ngumoha’s cross. The contest was effectively over before the quarter-hour mark.

Liverpool, under Andoni Iraola, remained ruthless after the interval. Ngumoha, the standout performer, added a third in the 66th minute from Bradley Barcola’s low cross to become the youngest scorer in the history of this fixture. Substitute Víctor Muñoz completed the rout eight minutes from time, finishing a slick combination with Wirtz.

United, already enduring their worst start to a Premier League season in four decades, finished the afternoon 19th in the table with just eight points from 11 games. Liverpool climbed to fourth.

In his post-match press conference, Pochettino did not shy away from the result but insisted the red card had fundamentally altered the contest. 

*“One centre-back made a mistake and wasn’t punished and us, we made one mistake and we were punished,*” he said, referring to an earlier challenge by Liverpool’s Jeremy Jacquet on Bryan Mbeumo that he believed should have been a penalty. He maintained that Isak had lost control of the ball when Yoro made contact and that the decision was harsh.

The new manager accepted full responsibility for the defeat while stressing the circumstances. “It is painful because it is 4-0 but we play more than 80 minutes with 10 men. I take responsibility for that, no worries,” he told reporters. 

He also defended the selection of Matthijs de Ligt, who was making his first Premier League start in nearly a year and was involved in the buildup to the red card.

The result leaves Pochettino facing an enormous rebuilding job. United’s defensive fragility, lack of intensity and inability to respond under pressure were laid bare in front of a raucous Anfield crowd that at one point chanted ***“sacked in the morning.”*** 

For Liverpool, the emphatic victory provided a timely boost and reinforced their credentials as genuine top-four contenders.

Pochettino arrived at Old Trafford only days earlier after leaving the United States national team. Few expected an immediate transformation, yet few anticipated a collapse quite this swift or this comprehensive. 

The Argentine now has a clear, if unwelcome, understanding of the depth of the problems he has inherited. The road back for Manchester United looks longer than ever.
