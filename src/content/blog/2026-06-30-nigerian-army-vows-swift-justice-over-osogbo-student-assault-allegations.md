**Osogbo, Osun State** – In a firm display of accountability, the Nigerian Army has launched a full investigation into disturbing allegations of misconduct by some of its recruits against students of Osun State University (UNIOSUN), signaling a commitment to upholding discipline and protecting civilians amid rising public concern.

The response comes hours after the National Association of Nigerian Students (NANS) Southwest Zone D issued a strongly worded press release detailing an alleged invasion of student hostels in the Oke Baale area of Osogbo on Monday night. 

According to student accounts and NANS, newly recruited personnel from the Depot Nigerian Army reportedly broke into halls of residence, physically assaulted male students, sexually harassed and stripped female students, and made away with valuables including over 60 mobile phones and other personal items.

Students took to the streets in protest, barricading the popular Obokun Road and carrying placards that read *“Students are not safe,” “Our girls are not animals,” and “We demand justice.”* 

The Vice Chancellor of UNIOSUN also addressed the protesters, condemning the alleged actions.

In a detailed statement issued on Tuesday, June 30, 2026, and shared via the official @HQNigerianArmy X account, the Commandant of the Depot Nigerian Army, Osogbo, expressed deep concern over the claims. 

Major Yahaya Ibrahim, Army Public Relations Officer for the Depot, emphasized that such behavior contradicts the Army’s core mandate to protect citizens—especially the youth, described as “the future of the nation.”

*“No student or law-abiding citizen should ever feel unsafe or be subjected to harassment, intimidation or assault by those entrusted with the security of lives and property,” the statement read. It further assured that any personnel found guilty would face “the full weight of military law.”*

The Depot has formally requested that NANS Southwest Coordinator, Comrade Adeyemo Josiah Kayode, provide all available evidence, witness statements, and supporting materials to facilitate a thorough probe. 

The Commandant also extended an invitation for an early meeting with NANS leadership to chart a path forward.

The Depot Nigerian Army in Osogbo serves as a key training facility for recruits, with its insignia featuring a fierce black panther symbolizing resolve (*“No Going Back”).* 

This incident, involving fresh recruits, raises questions about training standards, discipline during off-duty hours, and oversight in the recruitment and integration process.

Nigeria has a complex history of tensions between security forces and civilians, including past #EndSARS protests that highlighted issues of excessive force and impunity. 

This swift response from the Army stands in contrast to some earlier episodes and could help rebuild trust if the investigation is transparent and conclusive.

Public reactions on social media have been mixed: many Nigerians praised the Army’s professionalism and prompt action, while others demanded swift punishment for any “bad eggs” to deter future occurrences. 

Some called for broader reforms in how recruits are monitored post-training.

As the investigation unfolds, stakeholders—including university authorities, student bodies, civil society, and the military—face a critical test. 

A transparent process that delivers justice could reinforce the Nigerian Army’s image as a disciplined force committed to national values. Conversely, any perceived delay or cover-up risks further eroding public confidence in institutions meant to safeguard citizens.

The Army’s statement underscores a key principle: accountability is not optional. For students in Osogbo and across Nigeria, the coming days will determine whether this remains an isolated aberration or prompts lasting improvements in civil-military relations.

The Nigerian Army has reiterated its readiness to act decisively. All eyes now turn to the outcome of the probe and the implementation of any recommended reforms. 

In a nation where the youth represent both hope and vulnerability, restoring faith in those sworn to protect them is paramount.
