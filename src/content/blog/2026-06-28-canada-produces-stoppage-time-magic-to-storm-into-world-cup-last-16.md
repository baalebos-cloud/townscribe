**Los Angeles —** In a match that tested nerves, resilience, and belief until the final seconds, Canada delivered one of the most dramatic results in its soccer history. 

Stephen Eustaquio’s thunderous volley in the 92nd minute shattered South Africa’s heroic resistance, securing a 1-0 victory in the Round of 32 at SoFi Stadium and propelling the co-hosts into the knockout stage for the first time.

The scenes that followed were pure pandemonium. Canadian players piled on Eustaquio as substitutes sprinted from the bench. Fans in red and white erupted across the stands in Inglewood, California. 

For a nation that had never before won a knockout match at the men’s World Cup — and had only just begun making deep runs on the global stage — this was more than a result. It was a declaration.

The game itself was a tense, attritional affair. South Africa, in their first-ever World Cup knockout appearance, came with a clear plan: frustrate Canada’s press, control possession through patient build-up (they held 58% on the day), and rely on a rock-solid defensive structure.

Hugo Broos, the 74-year-old Belgian coach possibly in his final game, orchestrated a display of defiance. Defenders Mbekezeli Mbokazi and Aubrey Modiba produced heroic clearances, including one that denied Jonathan David an open goal. 

Goalkeeper Ronwen Williams made several key saves. South Africa created little in attack but made life miserable for Canada’s forwards.

Canada, managed by the fiery Jesse Marsch, dominated chances (1.32 xG to South Africa’s 0.13) and showed the spirit that has defined this tournament run. 

They pressed high, won duels, and created danger from set pieces. Yet the breakthrough refused to come — until the dying moments.

A major flashpoint arrived just before halftime. Richie Laryea burst into the box and went down under a challenge from Khuliso Mudau. 

Replays suggested contact from the defender first, but Portuguese referee Joao Pinheiro waved play on, and VAR did not intervene. 

Marsch had to be restrained by Moise Bombito from confronting officials. Experts were divided, but many, including former Premier League referee Graham Scott, believed it should have been a penalty.

Alphonso Davies, Canada’s superstar left-back, made his long-awaited tournament debut as a 75th-minute substitute after a hamstring injury. His introduction immediately lifted the intensity. 

He delivered a line-breaking pass leading to a chance for Promise David and threaded another to Jonathan David, whose shot was saved. While not directly involved in the winner, Davies’ presence signaled a shift in momentum.

The decisive moment came deep in stoppage time. Alistair Johnston got down the right and delivered a cross that was headed out to the edge of the box. 

Eustaquio, the composed midfielder (and current captain), controlled the ball with his chest, let it drop, and unleashed a clean, low strike into the bottom corner past a stranded Williams. Bedlam.

This victory marks Canada’s first-ever progression beyond the group stage in the men’s World Cup as co-hosts. 

It builds on their group performances and cements a growing belief in the program under Marsch, who recently extended his contract through 2030.

For South Africa, the end was bittersweet. They fought valiantly and earned respect, but exit in the Round of 32 after a campaign that included advancing from Group A as runners-up. 

Broos’ future remains uncertain, though his leadership in reaching this stage is a milestone.

Canada now heads to Houston for a July 4 Round of 16 clash against the winner of Netherlands vs. Morocco. 

A sterner test awaits — France or Germany potentially loom in the quarterfinals — but momentum is a powerful force, especially on home soil for the co-hosts.

This could be remembered as one of Canada’s greatest sporting moments: a team built on spirit that willed its way into history. 

The eyes of the world are now fixed on a squad that refuses to be overlooked.

What a night for Canadian soccer. The dream continues.
