**Washington, D.C.—October 9, 2026**—President Donald Trump declared Friday that a newly struck agreement with Russian President Vladimir Putin will flood global markets with millions of tons of diesel fuel, pairing the move with assertions of complete American control over the Strait of Hormuz to drive prices sharply lower for U.S. consumers.

In a White House post featuring Trump’s own statement, the president said he concluded a ***“highly successful discussion”*** with Putin under which Russia will immediately supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel to American and global markets, followed by 500,000 tons in November and 1 million tons shortly thereafter. 

An additional 3 million tons would follow based on the condition of Russian refineries.

*“Between our TOTAL CONTROL of the Strait of Hormuz and this great announcement on Russian Energy, Diesel Prices for Americans and, indeed, the World, will be COMING DOWN, IN RECORD NUMBERS, AND FAST!”* Trump stated. He emphasized that lowering costs for farmers, ranchers, and truckers remains his ***“Greatest Priority.”***



**Background on Surging Diesel Costs**

U.S. diesel prices have hovered near record highs, averaging about $6.28 per gallon on Friday according to AAA data, after climbing more than 70% since the start of the U.S.-Israeli conflict with Iran in late February. 

The war has severely disrupted oil and refined product flows through the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for global energy supplies. 

Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries have further constrained diesel availability, prompting Moscow to restrict exports earlier this year.

Analysts have long noted that Middle Eastern crude is particularly suited for diesel production, making Hormuz disruptions especially painful for the fuel that powers trucking, agriculture, and industry. 

While some tanker traffic has resumed under U.S. military escort, flows remain volatile, with recent Iranian attacks continuing to pressure the market.



**The Deal and Immediate Steps**

Shortly after Trump’s announcement, the U.S. Treasury issued a temporary license permitting Russian diesel into the market. A Russian envoy, Kirill Dmitriev, publicly welcomed the cooperation, saying it would benefit the world.

The agreement marks a notable shift. Just weeks earlier, Trump signed a sweeping sanctions package targeting Russia’s energy sector. 

The new arrangement effectively eases pressure on Moscow’s diesel exports in an effort to ease domestic price pressures ahead of the November midterm elections.

White House messaging has repeatedly stressed U.S. dominance in the Strait of Hormuz, with military officials asserting that American forces and partners control shipping lanes and have facilitated the movement of substantial volumes of non-Iranian crude. Iran continues to contest those claims and has conducted attacks on vessels.



**Political and Market Context**

The announcement arrives as high diesel costs weigh on American agriculture and logistics sectors, key constituencies for Republicans. 

Farmers and truckers have felt the pinch acutely, with elevated fuel expenses feeding into broader inflation concerns. 

Trump has previously attributed much of the price surge to the Russia-Ukraine conflict rather than the Iran war, a view disputed by many energy analysts who cite Hormuz disruptions as the larger factor.

Whether the Russian supplies and claimed Hormuz control will deliver the rapid, “record number” price declines Trump predicts remains to be seen. 

Global refining capacity remains tight, and geopolitical risks in both the Middle East and Eastern Europe persist. Markets will closely watch actual delivery volumes and any impact on retail prices in the coming weeks.

Trump closed his statement by reiterating that Iran “will not have a Nuclear Weapon,” framing the energy moves within his broader foreign policy agenda.
