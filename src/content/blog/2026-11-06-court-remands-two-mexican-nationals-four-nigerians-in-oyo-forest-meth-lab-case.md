The Federal High Court in Ibadan on Friday ordered the remand of two Mexican nationals and four Nigerian men accused of running a sophisticated clandestine methamphetamine laboratory hidden deep in a forest in Oyo State. 

Justice Nkeonye Evelyn Maha directed that the six defendants be held at the Agodi Correctional Centre pending the start of their trial in late January 2027.

The suspects are Mexican nationals Jose Villa Ochoa, 56, and Carrera Loaiza Arturo, 40, along with Nigerians Maxwell Uche Nevoh, 30; Olatunji Yusuf, 37; Bankole Akeem Owolabi, 45; and Ganiu Monsiu, 43. All six pleaded not guilty to a four-count charge brought by the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA). 

The charges include conspiracy to operate a drug trafficking organization, establishing an illegal laboratory for methamphetamine production, and the unlawful transportation and distribution of chemical substances used to manufacture the highly addictive psychotropic drug.

According to the NDLEA, operatives arrested Ochoa and the four Nigerians on 17 June 2026 during a raid on a fortified, industrial-scale laboratory located in a forested area of Tapa village in Ibarapa North Local Government Area. 

Investigators described the facility as a sophisticated operation linked to a Nigerian-Mexican cartel, equipped with reactor pots, distillation units, mixers, condensers, and large quantities of precursor chemicals including phenylacetic acid. 

Field tests confirmed the presence of methamphetamine, with the charge sheet referencing the manufacture of approximately 1.26 kilogrammes of the substance.

Arturo, identified by investigators as a key figure who allegedly recruited Ochoa for his technical expertise, was tracked down two months later. 

He was arrested on 19 August 2026 at Murtala Muhammed International Airport in Lagos while allegedly attempting to leave the country. 

The agency said the suspects had conspired between December 2025 and June 2026 at various locations in Lagos and Oyo states to set up and run the production site.

Prosecuting counsel Adekunle Adebajo told the court the defendants acted without lawful authority and were members of a drug trafficking organization. 

Defence counsel raised no objection to the remand order. Justice Maha ordered the prosecution to serve the proof of evidence on all defence lawyers and fixed the matter for trial on 26, 27, 28 and 29 January 2027.

The case forms part of a broader pattern of NDLEA operations targeting transnational synthetic drug production in southwestern Nigeria. 

Only weeks before the Oyo raid, the agency had dismantled a similar industrial methamphetamine laboratory in a forest hideout in Ogun State, arresting other Mexican nationals and Nigerian collaborators. 

Officials have repeatedly highlighted the growing involvement of foreign specialists in large-scale meth production within Nigeria, describing the Tapa facility as far more advanced than typical local operations.

Methamphetamine, often called ***“ice” or “crystal meth,”*** is a potent stimulant with severe health and social consequences. Its production involves hazardous chemicals and poses significant environmental and community risks, particularly when laboratories are concealed in rural forest areas. 

The NDLEA has framed the successive forest raids as evidence of improved intelligence capabilities and international cooperation in disrupting these networks.

As the six men remain in custody, the coming trial will test the strength of the agency’s evidence against an alleged cross-border cartel that sought to turn a quiet Oyo forest into a multi-billion-naira drug factory. 

The outcome is expected to send a clear signal about Nigeria’s resolve to confront the rising threat of industrial-scale synthetic drug manufacturing.
