**London, September 6, 2026** — Arsenal produced a statement performance in the Premier League’s first major clash of the season, recovering from an early shock to defeat Chelsea 2-1 at the Emirates Stadium and maintain their perfect start.

Morgan Rogers, the forward Arsenal tried and failed to sign in the summer, stunned the home crowd by putting Chelsea ahead after just 77 seconds with a crisp volley from the edge of the box. 

It was the first goal the Gunners had conceded all season and handed the visitors an unexpected foothold in a red-hot London derby.

Mikel Arteta’s side responded with character and quality. Kai Havertz, starting ahead of summer arrival Viktor Gyökeres, levelled in the 25th minute with a low drive that beat former Arsenal goalkeeper Emiliano Martínez at his near post. 

Five minutes after the break, captain Martin Ødegaard finished a slick move — helped by a clever dummy from Havertz — to seal the victory.



**A True Test of Champions**

This was no routine afternoon. Chelsea, under Xabi Alonso, arrived with an attacking threat that had already produced high-scoring wins. 

Arsenal had to show composure after falling behind so early against one of their main rivals.

They did exactly that. The Gunners controlled large periods of the contest, created the clearer chances, and defended resolutely when required. 

David Raya produced a crucial late save to deny substitute Estevão a dramatic equaliser, underlining the side’s resolve.



**Havertz Settles the Striker Debate**

The performance of Kai Havertz stood out. Once again preferred to Gyökeres, the German delivered the all-round contribution Arsenal need. 

He pressed high, linked play, drifted intelligently, and scored the equaliser. His dummy for Ødegaard’s winner was pure football intelligence.

Gyökeres came on late for his first league minutes of the season but looked rusty, struggling to hold the ball as Arsenal saw the game out. The message was clear: for now, there is no genuine debate over who should lead the line.



**Ødegaard Leads from the Front**

Captain Martin Ødegaard was at the heart of Arsenal’s recovery. His energy, vision and decisive finish — his second goal in three league games — provided the leadership the occasion demanded. 

With two goals already, he has already matched or surpassed previous seasonal tallies at a similar stage.

The result leaves Arsenal level on nine points with Manchester City at the top of the table after three matches. 

More importantly, it demonstrated that Arteta’s champions can dig deep when tested and still produce the quality required to win the biggest games.

For Chelsea, there were encouraging signs in attack and organisation, but a first defeat under Alonso will sting. For Arsenal, this was more than three points — it was early proof that the title defence remains firmly on track.
