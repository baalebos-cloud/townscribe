**Lagos, Nigeria—November 18, 2026**—Drama unfolded at the Lagos State Special Offences Court sitting in Ikeja on Wednesday when the court dismissed a fresh bail application by Immanuel Joseph Ufoma, who is standing trial over an alleged $1.5 million fraud, and his defence counsel abruptly walked out of the courtroom.

Justice Mojisola Dada rejected the application after the prosecution presented a letter from the Lagos State University Teaching Hospital (LASUTH) that formally disowned the medical report Ufoma had relied upon. 

The judge ruled that the court could not exercise discretion in favour of the defendant in light of the hospital’s position that the report was not authentic.



**Why the Bail Application Failed**



The defence had submitted a medical report addressed to the Nigerian Correctional Service (NCoS). However, the court noted that the defence failed to provide any confirmation that the document was received by the correctional authorities. The report also lacked the official stamp of the NCoS.



Prosecuting counsel Ekele Iheanacho, SAN, opposed the application, arguing that Ufoma remained a flight risk and could abscond if released while the trial was still ongoing. 

The prosecution further relied on the LASUTH letter to challenge the authenticity of the medical document.



**Dramatic Walkout Stalls Proceedings**



After the bail application was dismissed, Iheanacho informed the court that the prosecution was ready to proceed and that its witnesses were already present. 

Defence counsel Oluseun Awonuga, SAN, then told the court that he had filed a motion seeking to restrain the prosecution from continuing with the trial.

Upon checking its records, the court found that no such application was before it. Justice Dada directed the fourth prosecution witness (PW4) to return to the witness box for cross-examination.

It was at this point that proceedings took a dramatic turn. Rather than proceed with the cross-examination, Awonuga informed the court that he could no longer continue and abruptly walked out of the courtroom. 

He gestured to his junior counsel, who was still seated, to follow him.

The prosecution immediately objected, with Iheanacho citing Section 234 of the Administration of Criminal Justice Law (ACJL), which requires counsel seeking to disengage from a case to give at least three days’ notice. Despite the objection, Awonuga left the courtroom.

With the defence unable to continue, Justice Dada discharged PW4 from the witness box. The development stalled further proceedings even though the prosecution had other witnesses ready in court.



**Case Adjourned for New Representation**



To give Ufoma sufficient time to engage another legal representative, the court adjourned the matter until January 25, 2027, for continuation of trial. The earlier adjourned date of November 19, 2026, was vacated.

Before moving on to the next matter, Iheanacho apologized to the judge over the conduct of the senior counsel, who had walked out during the proceedings.



**Background of the Allegations**



Ufoma and his company, Intermediate Investment Holdings Limited, are facing allegations of fraudulently inducing Adebisi Adebutu of R28 Holdings Limited to part with $2.25 million and shares in the company. 

One of the charges alleges that between April 2022 and October 2023 in Lagos, the defendants, with intent to defraud, induced Adebutu to deposit a total of $2.25 million in exchange for 22.4 percent shares in Intermediate Investment Holdings Limited through a representation they allegedly knew to be false.

The case continues to attract attention as one of the high-profile fraud trials being prosecuted by the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) in Lagos.
