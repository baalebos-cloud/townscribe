**Beirut / Southern Lebanon – August 17, 2026** — Civilians in southern Lebanon are once again shouldering the heaviest burden of rising military activity, according to the United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon (UNIFIL), which reported a sharp increase in Israeli projectiles fired into the region over the past two weeks.

In a statement issued Monday, UNIFIL said it recorded an average of 137 projectiles per day between August 5 and 16. The figures climbed higher over the weekend, with 208 projectiles registered on Saturday and 185 on Sunday.

*“As tensions rise, civilians are once again paying the highest price, with many families displaced or forced to change their daily routines because of insecurity and uncertainty,*” the peacekeeping mission stated.



**Ceasefire Under Strain**

The escalation comes despite the June 26 ceasefire framework agreement between Lebanon and Israel, brokered by the United States. 

That deal is linked to the eventual disarmament of Hezbollah but did not require an immediate full Israeli withdrawal from areas of southern Lebanon still under occupation. 

Hezbollah has rejected the arrangement, insisting Israel must leave Lebanese territory first.

UNIFIL stressed that UN Security Council Resolution 1701, adopted in 2006, remains the foundational framework for restoring stability. 

The resolution calls for a cessation of hostilities, the strengthening of the Lebanese Armed Forces, and the continued presence of UN peacekeepers south of the Litani River.

Peacekeepers have maintained their operational presence through regular patrols, monitoring, clearing road obstacles, reducing risks from unexploded ordnance, and facilitating humanitarian access, the mission said.



**Mounting Human Cost**

The human toll continues to climb. Lebanese authorities reported that the death toll from Israeli operations in southern Lebanon since March 2 rose to 4,346 after 11 people were killed in two separate airstrikes on Saturday.

Lebanese President Joseph Aoun on Monday expressed support for extending UNIFIL’s mandate, which is due to expire at the end of 2026, and for maintaining an international force presence in the south. Beirut views the mission as a critical element of stability.



**Competing Perspectives**

Israel maintains that its forces are operating in southern Lebanon to eliminate threats posed by Hezbollah to Israeli civilians and troops, in accordance with international law. 

The Israeli military has stated it is not targeting the Lebanese state or its citizens and has linked any full withdrawal to the disarmament of Hezbollah.

Analysts note that the persistent activity reflects deeper unresolved issues: the incomplete implementation of the June framework, ongoing occupation of parts of the south, and the absence of a durable security arrangement acceptable to all parties.

As negotiations continue and UNIFIL’s future hangs in the balance, ordinary residents of southern Lebanon remain caught between intermittent fire, restricted movement, and the uncertainty of whether the fragile calm can hold. 

The peacekeepers’ latest figures serve as a stark reminder that, for many families, the promised return to normal life remains elusive.
