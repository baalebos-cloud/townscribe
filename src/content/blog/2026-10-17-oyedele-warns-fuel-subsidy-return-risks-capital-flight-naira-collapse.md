**Bangkok/Abuja—October 17, 2026—The** Federal Government has firmly ruled out any return to fuel subsidy, warning that such a move could trigger capital flight, put severe pressure on the naira, and undermine the hard-won gains of recent economic reforms.

Finance Minister and Coordinating Minister of the Economy, Taiwo Oyedele, delivered the clear message on Saturday during a press briefing on the sidelines of the 2026 International Monetary Fund (IMF) and World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok, Thailand.

*“Let me be clear, there will be no return to fuel subsidies or multiple exchange rates,”* Oyedele stated.



**Investor Confidence at Stake**

Oyedele explained that questions about a possible policy reversal had come up repeatedly in engagements with investors, credit rating agencies, and multilateral institutions. He argued that even the mere suggestion of returning to subsidies could prompt investors to withdraw capital, forcing pressure on the foreign exchange market and the naira.

The minister noted that ordinary Nigerians, including the elite and middle class, could also begin converting their savings into dollars, creating a dangerous cycle of capital outflows and currency instability.

Addressing speculation around the recent 30-day retail margin discount offered by the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPC), Oyedele stressed that the measure is a temporary commercial decision by the company to ease pressure on consumers. It is not a government-funded subsidy drawn from public funds.



**Broader Reform Stance**

The strong position comes amid rising petrol prices and growing political debate ahead of the 2027 general elections, with some opposition figures floating the idea of restoring some form of subsidy. 

Oyedele maintained that the Tinubu administration remains committed to the reforms introduced since 2023, which include subsidy removal and the liberalization of the foreign exchange market.

He said the government is instead exploring targeted measures to cushion the impact of higher energy costs without returning to the costly and distortionary subsidy regime of the past.

The Federal Government’s message is unambiguous: the reforms stay, and any talk of reversal carries serious risks for Nigeria’s economy and currency.
