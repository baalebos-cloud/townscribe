**ABUJA, Nigeria—November 27, 2026—Oyo** State Governor and Allied Peoples Movement (APM) presidential candidate, Engr. Seyi Makinde, has sparked fresh political curiosity after revealing a closed-door meeting with the British High Commissioner to Nigeria, His Excellency Peter Vowles, at the High Commission in Abuja.

Makinde disclosed the high-level engagement himself on Friday night via his verified X handle, @seyimakinde, writing:

> "We held a closed-door meeting with the British High Commissioner, His Excellency Peter Vowles and his team at the High Commission in Abuja today. It was a productive discussion and we look forward to further engagements in the coming days."

Though details of the discussion were not disclosed, the timing is politically weighty. Makinde, who declared his presidential ambition in May, has in recent days dominated national headlines with his bold prediction that President Bola Tinubu has no pathway to victory in the January 16, 2027 presidential election.

Speaking on the WithChude podcast published Wednesday, Makinde said he was "*100 per cent certain that an arrangement will emerge that will eventually dislodge this government."* 

He stressed that opposition leaders must think outside Tinubu's 2015 playbook and build a sustainable governance framework, not merely share offices.

The Oyo governor confirmed he has held separate engagements with former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar and NDC candidate Peter Obi over a possible opposition coalition. 

His Makinde-Daura Presidential Campaign Organisation (MDPCO) on Thursday also dismissed rumors that he plans to step down, insisting he remains fully in the race to lead a four-year transitional government of national unity.

Observers say Friday's meeting with Vowles, the UK's top diplomat who champions a stronger UK-Nigeria partnership for inclusive growth and investment, signals Makinde's push for international credibility. The UK Mission in Nigeria closely watches pre-election diplomacy, economic reforms, and democratic stability.

Makinde has also recently criticized the Tinubu administration over the proposed Sokoto-Badagry superhighway, alleged judicial interference in PDP cases, and failure to honour a post-2023 unity government agreement he said he signed on behalf of the G-5.

With further engagements promised, many Nigerians are now asking: is this routine courtesy or the quiet laying of a 2027 foreign-policy foundation?
