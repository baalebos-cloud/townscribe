**Lagos, Nigeria – September 14, 2026**—Africa's richest man, Aliko Dangote, saw his fortune surge dramatically on Monday after Forbes updated its Real-Time Billionaires List, valuing him at $51.3 billion. The 63% jump from about $31.4 billion the previous day propelled him from 76th to 36th place among the world’s wealthiest people.

The sharp rise follows the official launch of the initial public offering (IPO) for Dangote Petroleum Refinery and Petrochemicals, widely expected to become the largest share sale in African history.



**A Landmark “People’s IPO”**

The offer, which opened on the Nigerian Exchange (NGX) on Monday, is selling 4.1 billion new ordinary shares at ₦525 each. If fully subscribed, it will raise approximately ₦2.15 trillion (about $1.6 billion). A greenshoe option could push proceeds higher if demand exceeds expectations.

Dangote has deliberately framed the deal as a “people’s IPO,” setting the minimum subscription at just 10 shares (₦5,250, or roughly $4). 

The low entry point aims to open ownership of the continent’s largest oil refinery to ordinary Nigerians and retail investors across Africa. The subscription window runs until October 13, with trading expected to begin in November.

Early interest was strong. Reports indicated more than $7 million poured in within the first hour through digital channels spanning banks, fintechs, and mobile money operators.



**How the Valuation Boost Happened**

The wealth increase is largely tied to a highly successful private placement completed in July. That round, which was several times oversubscribed, raised about $2.5–3.7 billion and implied a valuation for the refinery in the range of $40–42 billion. The public offering has pushed the implied valuation closer to $47–50 billion.

Dangote retains a dominant stake—previously around 87%—which is expected to dilute modestly after the IPO while still leaving him firmly in control. 

The refinery, which began operations in 2024 and reached full 700,000 barrels-per-day capacity earlier this year, now accounts for the majority of his wealth, surpassing his longstanding cement, fertilizer, and sugar interests.



**Broader Ambitions and Market Impact**

Proceeds from the IPO will help fund plans to double the Lekki facility’s capacity to 1.4 million barrels per day by around 2029. 

Dangote has also signaled interest in a secondary listing, potentially in the United States, and continues exploring a new refinery project in Kenya.

Market observers note that a successful listing could significantly deepen Nigeria’s capital market, with the refinery potentially representing a substantial portion of the NGX’s total market capitalization once trading begins.

Speaking at the launch ceremony on the NGX floor, Dangote described the moment as an opportunity to share the refinery’s success more widely. 

The plant has already transformed Nigeria’s refined products landscape, reducing reliance on imports and benefiting from stronger regional and international demand.



**What It Means Going Forward**

At $51.3 billion, Dangote remains firmly Africa’s richest individual and has now overtaken several prominent global figures on the Forbes ranking. 

Analysts will be watching both the final subscription levels and the eventual trading performance closely, as the true market valuation of the refinery will further shape his fortune in the months ahead.

The IPO marks a rare large-scale opportunity for everyday investors to participate in one of Africa’s most significant industrial assets—an outcome Dangote has repeatedly said was intentional.
