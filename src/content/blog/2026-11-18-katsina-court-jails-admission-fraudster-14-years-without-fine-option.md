**Katsina, Nigeria—November 18, 2026—A** Federal High Court sitting in Katsina has sentenced a fraudster, Imrana Yusuf Tsauri, to 14 years imprisonment without an option of fine for obtaining money by false pretence and money laundering.

Justice Daden Garba delivered the judgment after Tsauri pleaded guilty to a two-count charge filed by the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC). 

The convict had posed as a facilitator who could secure university admissions and nursing licenses for the children and wards of unsuspecting parents and guardians.



**Details of the Fraud**



According to the EFCC, Tsauri obtained a total of ₦9,316,500 (Nine Million, Three Hundred and Sixteen Thousand, Five Hundred Naira) from Francis Madaki Joshua and 52 other victims in 2022. 

He collected the funds under the false pretence that he would process admissions and professional licences, a claim he knew was untrue.

On the second count, he was charged with dishonestly concealing and disguising the origin of the same sum, knowing it represented proceeds of an unlawful act. 

The offences contravened Sections 1(1) and 15(2)(a) of the Advance Fee Fraud and Other Fraud Related Offences Act, 2006, as well as the Money Laundering (Prohibition) Act, 2011 (as amended).

Tsauri was arraigned on November 12, 2026. He pleaded guilty to both counts. Prosecution counsel Bright C. Ogbonna thereafter urged the court to convict him based on his plea.



**Sentence and Restitution Order**

Justice Garba consequently convicted Tsauri and sentenced him to seven years imprisonment on each count. The sentences are to run concurrently, resulting in an effective 14-year term without any option of fine.

In addition, the court ordered the convict to make full restitution of ₦9,316,500 to the lead petitioner, Joshua Madaki Francis.

The EFCC described the case as another example of the growing trend of admission-related scams in which fraudsters exploit the desperation of parents seeking educational and professional opportunities for their children. 

Many victims in such cases lose life savings after being promised placements in nursing schools or tertiary institutions that never materialize.



**Broader Context of Anti-Fraud Efforts**



Admission and licence-related fraud remains a persistent challenge across Nigeria, with numerous reports of individuals posing as agents of universities, polytechnics, or regulatory bodies. 

The EFCC has repeatedly warned the public against making payments to unverified individuals promising guaranteed admissions or professional certifications.

The agency’s continued prosecution of such cases underscores its commitment to protecting citizens from advance-fee fraud and money-laundering schemes that prey on educational aspirations. 

Officials have urged parents and guardians to verify claims directly with institutions and regulatory agencies rather than relying on middlemen.

Tsauri’s conviction and the restitution order are expected to offer some measure of relief to the 53 victims who lost significant sums.

The concurrent sentencing without an option of fine also sends a strong signal that courts are prepared to impose stiff penalties on those who exploit public trust for personal gain.

The case was investigated and prosecuted by the EFCC, reflecting the commission’s ongoing efforts to tackle economic and financial crimes at both individual and organised levels.
