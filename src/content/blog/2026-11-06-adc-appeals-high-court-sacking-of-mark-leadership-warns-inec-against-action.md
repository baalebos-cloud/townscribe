The African Democratic Congress has moved swiftly to challenge a Federal High Court judgment that removed the David Mark-led leadership of the party and voided the nomination of former Vice President Atiku Abubakar as its presidential candidate for the 2027 general elections. 

In a statement issued on Friday, the opposition party confirmed it had filed an appeal at the Court of Appeal and formally notified the Independent National Electoral Commission, urging the electoral body to refrain from any consequential steps while the legal challenge remains pending.

The judgment, delivered on Thursday, November 5, 2026, by Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court in Abuja, declared the Mark-led caretaker committee illegal. 

The court held that the process through which former Senate President David Mark and former Osun State Governor Rauf Aregbesola assumed the positions of national chairman and national secretary violated the ADC constitution, the Electoral Act, and the 1999 Constitution. 

Justice Lifu consequently nullified all actions taken under that leadership, including the presidential primary that produced Atiku Abubakar as the party’s flag bearer and the nominations of candidates for other elective positions in the 2027 polls. 

The court also directed INEC not to recognize decisions reached at the party’s 99th National Executive Committee meeting held on July 27, 2025.

The suit, marked FHC/ABJ/CS/1819/2025, was instituted by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former National Deputy Chairman (North-East) of the ADC. 

Gombe argued that following the resignation of the party’s former national chairman, Chief Ralph Nwosu, he was constitutionally entitled to assume the leadership. 

He sought orders restraining Mark and Aregbesola from parading themselves as national officers and nullifying the decisions of the caretaker committee. The court upheld his claims in full.

Atiku’s name had already been published by INEC as the ADC presidential candidate, and he had selected former Rivers State Governor Rotimi Amaechi as his running mate. 

The ruling therefore places the party’s entire slate of candidates in limbo and raises questions about its participation in the elections scheduled to begin in January 2027. 

The judgment came barely a day after Atiku launched his campaign with a rally in Sokoto State.

The ADC has rejected the decision as legally indefensible, pointing to an earlier ruling by Justice Musa Liman of the same Federal High Court on July 2, 2026, which affirmed the legitimacy of the Mark-led National Working Committee. 

Party officials argued that a court of coordinate jurisdiction cannot sit on appeal over or nullify a subsisting judgment of another court of equal standing. 

The National Welfare Secretary, Nkemakolam Ukandu, and the Director of Strategic Communication of the Presidential Campaign Council, Phrank Shaibu, confirmed that the party had filed both the appeal and an application for a stay of execution. 

The party formally notified INEC of these steps on November 6 and asked the commission to take no further action that could prejudice the outcome of the appeal.

This is not the first chapter in the ADC leadership dispute. The crisis has already travelled through the Court of Appeal and the Supreme Court. 

In April 2026 the apex court set aside a Court of Appeal order directing parties to maintain the status quo and restored recognition of the Mark-led executive after INEC had temporarily removed their names from its portal. 

The latest High Court decision returns the matter to the appellate courts once more, with the final outcome expected to shape the opposition landscape ahead of the 2027 elections.

Political observers note that the ruling intensifies pressure on the opposition at a critical moment. The ADC has positioned itself as a major challenger to the ruling All Progressives Congress, attracting high-profile figures including Atiku and Amaechi. 

Party officials insist the campaign will continue uninterrupted while the legal process runs its course, expressing confidence that the Court of Appeal will give the facts and arguments full and fair consideration. 

For now, the ball rests with the appellate court and with INEC, which has been asked to hold the line until the appeal is determined.
