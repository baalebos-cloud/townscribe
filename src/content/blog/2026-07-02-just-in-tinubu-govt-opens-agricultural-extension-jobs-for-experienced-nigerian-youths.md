**Abuja, Nigeria** – In a proactive move to strengthen Nigeria’s agricultural sector and create meaningful opportunities for young professionals, the Office of the Special Assistant to the President on Youth Initiatives (Monitoring & Delivery) has issued a formal Call for Expressions of Interest.

Dr. Titilope Gbadamosi, PhD, the Special Assistant leading the initiative, announced the opportunity on Wednesday, July 1, 2026, via her official X (formerly Twitter) account. 

The drive specifically targets Nigerian youths with practical backgrounds in agricultural extension, aiming to deploy experienced hands directly into roles that support farmers across the country.

 **Who Qualifies?**

Eligible candidates must meet clear, merit-based criteria designed to ensure readiness for field work:

\- At least two (2) years of hands-on experience in agricultural extension, either as an Agricultural Extension Agent or as an Input Dealer working directly with farmers.

\- A valid and functional email address and phone number.

\- A functional bank account registered in a name that exactly matches their National Identification Number (NIN).

\- The ability to read and write.

The initiative emphasizes transparency and direct engagement, encouraging qualified young Nigerians to submit their details through the dedicated portal for consideration in what appears to be part of broader efforts to revitalize extension services—a critical but often under-resourced link between research, policy, and smallholder farmers.

Speaking to the broader context of her office’s work, Dr. Gbadamosi has consistently championed youth inclusion in national development. 

As founder of “*Women Too, Africa”* and a key figure in the Renewed Hope Agenda, she brings both academic rigor (recently earning a PhD in English Studies from the University of Ibadan) and grassroots passion to her role.

This latest call reflects a practical focus: bridging the gap between policy ambitions for food security and the realities on Nigeria’s farms. 

Agricultural extension agents play a vital role in disseminating best practices, improving yields, and supporting rural economies — areas where Nigeria continues to face challenges amid population growth and climate pressures.

The announcement quickly gained traction online, with over 250,000 views and hundreds of engagements within hours. 

Many users expressed appreciation and began sharing it with their networks, though some reported temporary technical difficulties accessing the application portal (linked via** [https://yid.fmyd.gov.ng](https://yid.fmyd.gov.ng/)).**

Dr. Gbadamosi and supporters urged patience and direct outreach where needed, underscoring the administration’s commitment to accessible opportunities. 

One reply highlighted the value for offline communities, suggesting wider dissemination through radio and local channels.

This is not framed as a massive nationwide recruitment but a targeted expression of interest—a smart, focused step toward building a stronger cadre of youth professionals in agriculture. 

For many with the right experience, it represents a chance to turn field expertise into stable, impactful public service roles.

**How to Apply**: Qualified youths are encouraged to visit the Opportunity Corner on the Youth Initiatives portal and submit details promptly. 

Only those meeting the exact criteria should apply to avoid disappointment.

As Nigeria grapples with youth unemployment and the urgent need for agricultural transformation, initiatives like this offer a glimmer of structured hope—connecting capable young people directly with the soil of national progress.
