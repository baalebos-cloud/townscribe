**Abuja, Nigeria—November 24, 2026—**The Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) on Tuesday described the deadly attack on a Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) campaign gathering in Uduere, Ughelli North Local Government Area of Delta State, as the direct fruit of what it called the All Progressives Congress (APC)’s sustained “war rhetoric” and violent communication ahead of the 2027 general elections.

In a strongly worded press statement signed by its National Publicity Secretary, Comrade Ini Ememobong, the PDP said gunmen opened fire while Senator Ovie Omo-Agege, the NDC candidate for Delta Central Senatorial District and former Deputy Senate President, was addressing supporters. 

The assault left at least four people dead, including Moses Anaughe and Akima Efemena Umukoro, and several others injured. The party described the incident as a clear attempt on Omo-Agege’s life.

*“We mourn those killed… and we stand with their families and with all those injured,*” the statement said. It linked the violence to earlier comments by President Bola Ahmed Tinubu, noting that the President, as APC leader, had in 2022 spoken of power that must be *“grabbed, snatched, and run away with,*” and in 2026 declared that ***“all is fair in politics.”*** 

According to the PDP, such remarks provided the “adrenaline” that emboldened supporters to unleash mayhem on the opposition.

The party further alleged that APC figures across the country, including in Osun, the Federal Capital Territory and Edo State, had issued direct threats against citizens unwilling to support the ruling party. 

In Delta alone, it claimed, at least two deadly attacks on opposition elements had already occurred in November, creating a pattern of intimidation.

The PDP issued four specific demands. First, it called on President Tinubu to immediately disavow the “war and anti-opposition rhetoric” previously deployed. 

Second, it urged security agencies to ensure that the investigation ordered by the Inspector-General of Police is thorough, transparent, and publicly reported, and that similar political attacks elsewhere receive the same treatment. 

Third, it asked the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), the National Peace Committee, and election observers to name and shame perpetrators of political violence on an international scale.

 Fourth, it pressed the police to maintain neutrality and professionalism, citing accusations that officers present at the Uduere scene were “unhelpful.”

Independent reports confirm that the attack took place on Monday, 23 November, while Omo-Agege was speaking to supporters. 

Police later stated that six people died and several others were injured. The Delta State Government imposed a night-time curfew in Ughelli North. 

Conflicting accounts have emerged: Omo-Agege’s campaign and eyewitnesses described a targeted assassination attempt, while the Ughelli North Local Government Chairman, an APC chieftain, offered a rival narrative blaming members of the senator’s team.

The PDP concluded by insisting that Nigerians need leadership that does not sacrifice peace and unity for political domination. It called on citizens to reject what it termed a “warmongering party and its government” at the polls in January 2027.



***This report is based on the official PDP press statement of 24 November 2026 and contemporaneous reporting on the Uduere incident.***
