**ABUJA, Nigeria—November 27, 2026—The** battle for 2027 turned brutally numerical on Friday night as Minister of Aviation and Aerospace Development, Festus Keyamo, SAN, declared that the opposition's dream of forcing a presidential run-off against President Bola Tinubu is a "complete pipe-dream."

In a lengthy post on X (formerly Twitter), monitored by our correspondent, Keyamo—back-to-back Chief Spokesperson for the All Progressives Congress in the last two presidential elections—said he stumbled on an analysis by @Oh_Analytics on how to force a run-off and felt compelled to respond.

> *"I just stumbled upon this analysis and I must say that if the opposition’s hope is to force a run-off (like our intel tells us), then it is a complete pipe-dream,"*
>
>  
>
> Keyamo wrote.

He challenged both major opposition candidates to ignore social media noise and face demography.

*"If both major opposition candidates forget the social media noise for a moment and look calmly at the numbers, they will both stop campaigning and step down immediately,"* he said.

Keyamo, who prides himself on correctly calling the minutest details of the last two elections, promised a full demographic breakdown a week to voting day, but released one teaser *"so as to demoralise the opposition a bit."*

His first bombshell: the Atiku Abubakar-Rotimi Amaechi ticket of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) is *"dead on arrival"* — a full analysis he promised next time.

His second: the much-hyped Peter Obi-Rabiu Kwankwaso ticket of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) is *"also going nowhere."*

Keyamo questioned what Kwankwaso, former Kano governor and 2023 NNPP presidential candidate, truly brings as a running mate.

*"Let me shock everyone: EVEN AS A PRESIDENTIAL CANDIDATE HIMSELF, Kwankwaso was able to secure 25 percent in ONLY ONE NORTHERN STATE IN 2023 (Kano), talk less as a VP candidate now. So, what really does Kwankwaso bring to that ticket?"* he asked.

Using 2023 results as a baseline, he argued that even combined, Obi and Kwankwaso only crossed 25 percent in three of 13 North-West and North-East states—Taraba, Kaduna, and Kano. Since Obi already made 25 percent in Taraba alone, Kwankwaso's addition delivers only Kano and Kaduna.

*"Peter Obi had 25 percent in only 16 States before. That takes him to 18 States, still short of the constitutional requirement of 24 States. And that is only one hurdle. The real monster of the requirement of greater number of votes is still staring at them,"* Keyamo wrote.

He dismissed claims that 2027 will be different: *"Times may change, but the demography of a country like Nigeria does not change DRASTICALLY within a short period. And why would the slightest change be in your favour and not against you?"*

*"To the largely uninformed internet trolls, your leaders know these numbers like us, but they won’t tell you in order not to demoralise you. Goodluck,"* he added.

The referenced @Oh_Analytics chart argued Tinubu must be held under 25 percent in 13 states to force a run-off. In 2023, he was under 25 percent in only seven, meaning the opposition needs six more—a mountain Keyamo calls impossible.

With the January 16, 2027 poll barely seven weeks away, Keyamo's salvo sets the tone for a data-driven war of numbers.
