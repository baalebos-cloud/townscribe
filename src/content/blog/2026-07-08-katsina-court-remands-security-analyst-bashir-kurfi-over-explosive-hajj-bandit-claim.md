**KATSINA, Nigeria** — In a development that has ignited fierce debate on freedom of expression and government accountability, a Chief Magistrates’ Court in Katsina State on Wednesday remanded prominent security analyst and Ahmadu Bello University lecturer, Dr. Bashir Kurfi, in police custody.

Kurfi was arraigned over comments made during a Trust TV interview in which he alleged that the Katsina State Government sponsored former bandits—individuals widely linked to terrorism and insecurity in the North-West—to participate in the 2026 Hajj pilgrimage using taxpayers’ money, reportedly to the tune of about ₦10 million per person. 

The state government has vehemently denied the claims, describing them as baseless and defamatory.

The court, presided over by Chief Magistrate Zaharaddeen Kofar Soro, ordered Kurfi’s remand pending the hearing of his bail application, now adjourned to Monday, July 14, 2026. 

He faces charges of defamation of character and injurious falsehood.

Dr. Bashir Kurfi, convener of the Katsina Security Community Initiative, is well-known for his outspoken analysis of banditry, kidnapping, and governance failures in Katsina and the broader North-West region. 

His supporters view him as a courageous whistleblower highlighting alleged complicity or poor handling of the security crisis that has plagued the state for years.

Kurfi’s legal team, led by Barrister A.D. Umar, argued that he pleaded not guilty and that continued detention was unjustified. 

However, prosecutors maintained that investigations were ongoing and that releasing him could interfere with the process. 

The defence noted that Kurfi declined to make a statement to police without his lawyer present.

The Katsina State Government has vowed to pursue legal action against what it calls false and damaging accusations. Officials insist no such sponsorship of former bandits occurred and have demanded evidence from Kurfi’s claims.

The case has drawn widespread attention online and in traditional media, with many Nigerians questioning whether the arrest represents a clampdown on critics amid persistent insecurity challenges. 

Others argue that unsubstantiated allegations, especially those accusing governments of aiding terrorists, warrant scrutiny to prevent misinformation that could inflame tensions.

Katsina, like much of North-West Nigeria, continues to grapple with banditry, farmer-herder clashes, and terrorism-related issues. 

Kurfi’s earlier public commentaries have often criticized both state and federal responses, including so-called peace deals with armed groups. 

His detention raises important questions about the balance between protecting public officials’ reputations and safeguarding citizens’ right to discuss governance and security matters openly.

As the matter returns to court next week, observers will be watching closely to see how the judiciary navigates this high-profile case. 

For many in a region weary of violence, the outcome could signal whether bold commentary on insecurity will be encouraged or chilled.

Dr. Kurfi’s remand adds another layer to ongoing national conversations about transparency, press freedom, and the fight against banditry in Nigeria. 

Developments in this case are expected to generate continued interest from civil society, media, and security stakeholders.
