**ABUJA, Nigeria** — In a firm directive aimed at accelerating Nigeria’s upstream oil and gas sector, the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission (NUPRC) has called on newly awarded Petroleum Prospecting Licence (PPL) holders to prioritize rapid asset development and responsible engagement with host communities.

The charge was delivered by NUPRC Chief Executive, Mrs. Oritsemeyiwa Eyesan, during the signing ceremony for the second batch of winners from the 2022/2023 Mini Bid Round and the 2024 Licensing Round, held in Abuja on Friday, July 17, 2026.

Eyesan emphasized that success for these licensees hinges on strict adherence to host community obligations under the Petroleum Industry Act (PIA). 

She stressed the need for timely exploration and production activities to unlock Nigeria’s hydrocarbon potential and boost economic growth.

The new awardees are expected to contribute meaningfully to the Federal Government’s ambitious targets: increasing crude oil production to 2 million barrels per day by 2027 and reaching 3 million barrels per day by 2030. 

These goals align with broader efforts to enhance energy security and attract foreign and local investment.

The Commission issued a stern warning: licensees must develop their assets or risk losing them under the PIA’s *“drill or drop”* provision. 

This policy is designed to prevent speculative holding of licences and ensure active investment in Nigeria’s oil blocks.

The event featured formal signing of concession contracts and issuance of PPLs, marking a significant milestone in the ongoing licensing rounds. 

Photographs from the ceremony showed key stakeholders, including Eyesan and other officials, formalizing agreements in a professional setting.

This development comes at a critical time for Nigeria’s oil sector. Decades of underinvestment, infrastructure challenges, and community issues have hampered production. 

By enforcing community development alongside aggressive exploration, NUPRC aims to create a more sustainable and inclusive industry that benefits both investors and host communities.

For the winners — a mix of established players and new entrants — the message is clear: licences are not trophies but responsibilities. Failure to deliver could see blocks returned to the state for reallocation to more committed operators.

As Nigeria pushes to reclaim its position as a major global oil producer, initiatives like this underscore a shift toward transparency, accountability, and results-driven governance in the petroleum sector. 

The coming months will test whether these new licence holders can translate regulatory directives into tangible production gains and community prosperity.

The NUPRC continues to position itself as a proactive regulator driving energy reforms under the PIA framework.
