**KANO, Nigeria** — In a significant political realignment shaking Kano State’s political landscape, several members of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) have defected to the relatively new Nigerian Democratic Congress (NDC), dealing a fresh setback to President Bola Tinubu’s camp ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The defectors, predominantly youths from Kumbotso Local Government Area, were formally welcomed into the NDC on Sunday by the party’s Kano State Publicity Secretary, Alhaji Yusuf Rabiu Fasaha, and Captain Nafe, the NDC’s House of Assembly candidate for the area.

Fasaha described the development as evidence of the NDC’s rising appeal, especially among young people disillusioned with the status quo. 

He promised the new members equal opportunities, inclusiveness, and a commitment to internal democracy. Captain Nafe urged them to mobilize support at the grassroots level, emphasizing the NDC’s focus on youth empowerment and people-centered governance.

This latest wave of defections adds to a pattern of political movement in Kano and across northern Nigeria. 

The NDC, founded in 2026 and positioning itself as a credible alternative, has been attracting notable figures and grassroots support. 

Analysts suggest such shifts could reshape the 2027 electoral battleground, particularly in key states like Kano.

The defections occur amid heightened pre-election activities nationwide. Opposition parties and emerging platforms are intensifying membership drives and coalition talks, capitalizing on public frustrations over economic challenges, governance issues, and unfulfilled promises. 

The NDC has positioned itself around themes of disciplined governance, transparency, and long-term national planning.

Political observers note that continued realignments and cross-party movements may significantly influence the upcoming presidential and legislative races. 

In Kano, a traditional political powerhouse, such shifts carry symbolic and strategic weight.

While the APC has not yet issued an official response to this specific defection, the trend underscores the fluid and competitive nature of Nigerian politics as parties gear up for 2027. 

For the defectors, the move represents hope for fresh representation; for the ruling party, it serves as a reminder of the need to reconnect with grassroots supporters.

As the countdown to 2027 intensifies, developments like these in Kano highlight the dynamic forces shaping Nigeria’s democratic future. 

Whether this signals a broader wave of dissatisfaction or isolated local dynamics remains to be seen — but the political temperature is clearly rising.
