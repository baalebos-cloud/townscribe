**Ado-Ekiti, August 17, 2026—**Ekiti State Governor Biodun Abayomi Oyebanji has struck a reflective yet forward-looking note, declaring that his administration has laid solid foundations across key sectors and now urging citizens to take personal ownership in building the Ekiti they desire.

In a series of posts on X on Monday evening, the governor wrote: ***“We have built the foundation. Now, let us build ourselves, and together, build the Ekiti we want and deserve.”*** 

The message was accompanied by a detailed graphic on healthcare infrastructure achievements under his administration.



**Healthcare as a Clear Foundation Stone**

The accompanying visual outlined major progress in the health sector. 

Key completed and ongoing projects include the construction or renovation of 106 primary healthcare centres and general hospitals across the state, alongside the provision of free medical services directly to communities.

Short-term benefits listed for citizens include immediate free medical consultations, maternal care, and general treatment. 

Long-term strategic gains pointed to a drastic reduction in neonatal and infant mortality and improved productive capacity of the state’s workforce. 

The graphic also highlighted opportunities for health workers and the elderly through better access to advanced diagnostic equipment and proximity-based care.

These claims align with earlier reports of the revitalization of over 100 primary health centers, the renovation of nine general hospitals, upgrades at Ekiti State University Teaching Hospital (EKSUTH), and the expansion of the Ulerawa Health Insurance Scheme that has enabled hundreds of thousands of residents to access free or subsidized care.



**A Broader Call for Citizen Participation**

Oyebanji’s message formed part of a short thread emphasizing personal responsibility. 

He stressed that Ekiti has never lacked intellect or talent, and the task now is to convert that human capital into productivity, enterprise, and shared prosperity. 

He urged residents to acquire digital skills, develop crafts, join farming clusters, build businesses, and keep learning while the government continues creating the enabling environment.

The governor’s remarks come as he prepares for a second term following his re-election earlier this year. 

His first term has been marked by significant infrastructure delivery, including dozens of road projects that earned him the informal title *“Construction King,*” progress on the agro-cargo airport, the Special Agro-Industrial Processing Zone, the Ekiti Knowledge Zone, and steady work on healthcare and human capital.

Officials and supporters describe the current phase as one of consolidation. With physical and institutional foundations in place, the emphasis is shifting toward industrialization, private investment, job creation, and deeper citizen engagement so that development reaches every community.

Oyebanji’s latest appeal frames governance as a partnership. Government provides the platform and opportunities; citizens must prepare themselves to seize them and contribute meaningfully. In the governor’s words, the next step belongs to the people of Ekiti.

As the state moves into the next chapter of the BAO administration, the success of this shared-responsibility model will likely determine how fully the foundations already laid translate into lasting prosperity for residents.
