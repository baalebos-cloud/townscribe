**Abuja, Nigeria—November 20, 2026**—The Federal Government on Friday broke ground for a state-of-the-art National Passport Manufacturing Centre at the Nigeria Immigration Service headquarters, marking a major step toward local production of e-passport booklets and greater national self-reliance.

Minister of Interior Dr. Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo, Comptroller-General of Immigration Kemi Nanna Nandap, Senate Committee Chairman on Interior Senator Adams Oshiomhole, and House of Representatives counterpart Hon. Abdullahi Aliyu Ahmed performed the symbolic turning of the sod.



**Project Details and Targets**



The $70 million facility, developed in partnership with Iris Smart Technologies without direct federal expenditure, will sit on 1.1 hectares and operate as an off-grid, AI-driven plant. Local production is expected to begin in May 2027, when President Bola Tinubu is scheduled to commission the centre.

Tunji-Ojo said Nigeria currently needs about 1.8 to 2 million passport booklets annually. The new plant will start with a capacity of six million booklets a year and scale to 10 million, positioning the country to meet domestic demand and potentially supply ECOWAS and other African nations that currently source passports from Europe and the Americas.

*“Nigeria will no longer import the sovereign document that carries our identity, and the price of the passport will no longer be dictated by foreign exchange rates,”* the minister stated.



**Strengthening Security and Service Delivery**



Nandap described the project as the final link in a chain of reforms that began with process automation in 2023, contactless biometric applications in 2024, and the Centralized Passport Personalization Center in 2025. 

She said past booklet shortages and long waiting times highlighted the need for full control over the supply chain.

Local manufacturing is expected to enhance document security, reduce external vulnerabilities, transfer specialized skills to immigration officers, and restore public confidence in passport administration. 

Officials noted that Nigeria will become the first country in West Africa—and only the second on the African continent—with full e-passport production capacity.

The ceremony underscores ongoing efforts to modernize immigration services, eliminate bottlenecks, and deliver more efficient passport processes for Nigerians at home and in the diaspora.



***This report is based on official statements from the Nigeria Immigration Service and coverage of the groundbreaking ceremony held on November 20, 2026.***
