**Eruwa, Oyo State—September 6, 2026—Oyo** State Governor Seyi Makinde has issued a firm warning to traditional rulers across the state, urging them to stay clear of partisan politics and warning that those who openly back political parties or candidates risk being treated as ordinary politicians and exposed to public attacks.

The governor spoke on Saturday at the grand finale of the week-long first coronation anniversary of His Royal Majesty Oba Samuel Adebayo Adegbola Ajobo Olurin I, the Eleruwa of Eruwa. 

The colourful event also featured the commissioning of the newly renovated Eleruwa Palace and the conferment of traditional titles on Makinde and his wife, Engr. Tamunominini Makinde.



**“Fathers to All, Not Campaign Tools”**

Makinde told the gathering of traditional rulers, political leaders, and dignitaries that the traditional institution occupies a sensitive place in society and should not be dragged into partisan contests. 

Monarchs, he said, are expected to serve as fathers to every resident and political actor in their domains, regardless of party affiliation.

*“If you want to play politics, then you should be ready to be treated as a politician. But if you want to remain a traditional ruler, then stay away from politics,”* he declared.

In stronger language reported across multiple outlets, the governor added that any traditional institution that interferes in politics *“we will insult them." If they can cross into politics, we will abuse them.”* 

He stressed that rulers who choose open endorsement risk avoidable political attacks and the loss of the respect that comes with the throne.

Makinde urged Kabiyesis to remain accessible to all who seek royal blessings rather than publicly endorsing any candidate. 

He asked them to focus on their core responsibilities: preserving peace, fostering unity, and driving development in their communities.



**Honour from Ibarapaland and 2027 Ambition**

The governor expressed deep appreciation for the titles of Ààrẹ Asọ̀lúdẹ̀rọ̀ (Aare Asoludero of Ibarapaland) and Yèyé Ààrẹ Asọ̀lúdẹ̀rọ̀ conferred on him and his wife. 

He noted that it was the first chieftaincy title he had accepted in Oyo State—and fittingly so, since his political journey began in Ibarapaland.

The Eleruwa and traditional rulers of Ibarapaland said the honor recognized Makinde’s achievements and pledged continued support for his administration.

Makinde, who is the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM) for the 2027 general elections, also used the platform to reaffirm his nationwide ambition. 

He said he would intensify consultations across the length and breadth of Nigeria to seek the support of citizens.



**Why the Warning Matters**

The caution comes as Nigeria edges closer to the 2027 polls and political realignments intensify. Traditional rulers in many states have long faced pressure to endorse candidates, often turning palaces into informal campaign stops. 

Makinde’s message underscores a growing debate about the proper boundary between the throne and partisan politics.

By insisting that monarchs remain neutral, the governor argued that the traditional institution itself is protected. Politics, he implied, is temporary; the throne endures.

As the event closed, the renovated palace stood as a symbol of continuity. Whether Oyo’s traditional rulers will fully heed the call remains to be seen, but Makinde has drawn a clear line: stay above the fray, or be prepared to be treated as just another player in the political arena.
