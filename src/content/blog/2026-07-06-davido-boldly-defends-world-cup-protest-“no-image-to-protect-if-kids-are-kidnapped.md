**LOS ANGELES / LAGOS** — In a powerful display of social conscience that has ignited passionate discussions across Nigeria and beyond, Afrobeats megastar David Adeleke, popularly known as Davido, has passionately defended his decision to highlight the ongoing plight of abducted schoolchildren during a high-profile performance at the FIFA World Cup 2026 Countdown Concert.

The singer took to the international stage wearing a jacket emblazoned with the message “*Bring Them Home”* alongside the names of 39 pupils and 7 teachers abducted from Oyo State. 

Rather than shying away from criticism that he was tarnishing Nigeria’s image abroad, Davido doubled down in a candid interview with **BBC’s Focus on Africa**.

“*A lot of people do not want to talk about what goes on in my country simply because they want to protect the image. But there is no image to protect if little kids are being kidnapped,”* he stated firmly.

Davido emphasized that while performing for Nigeria on the world stage filled him with pride, silence in the face of insecurity was not an option. 

He views his music and global reach as tools to shine a light on pressing national issues, particularly the persistent kidnapping of innocent schoolchildren that continues to traumatize families and communities.

The abduction in Oyo State, one of several tragic incidents in recent times, has left families in anguish and fueled public frustration over the government’s handling of security challenges. 

By naming the victims on his jacket, Davido transformed a celebratory performance into a poignant call for their safe return.

The move has drawn both widespread support and sharp criticism. Many Nigerians, especially parents and youth, praised Davido for using his influence responsibly and refusing to sugarcoat the country’s realities. 

Supporters see it as genuine patriotism — putting people before image.

Others, however, accused him of politicizing the event or damaging Nigeria’s international reputation at a time when the country is co-hosting aspects of the 2026 FIFA World Cup. 

Critics argue that such protests should be handled domestically rather than on global platforms.

This episode underscores a growing trend of celebrity activism in Nigeria, where stars like Davido leverage their massive followings to address governance and security failures. It also reflects deeper societal tensions: a young population demanding accountability amid recurring abductions, banditry, and insecurity that have plagued various regions.

Davido’s stance comes at a moment when Nigeria continues to battle multifaceted security challenges despite government assurances of improvement. 

His words resonate with many who feel that true national pride cannot coexist with the suffering of innocent children held in captivity.

As the 2026 FIFA World Cup approaches, with Nigeria expected to showcase its talents and culture on the global stage, Davido’s protest raises important questions: Should celebrities prioritize “protecting the image” or using their voices to demand change? For the families of the abducted Oyo pupils and teachers, the answer seems clear—bring them home.

Davido has once again proven that his influence extends far beyond music charts. Whether this sparks meaningful action on insecurity or remains a powerful symbolic gesture, his message is unmistakable: Nigeria’s children deserve safety more than any polished national image.
