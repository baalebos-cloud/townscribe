**Glasgow, Scotland—**In a night of pride and power at the 2026 Commonwealth Games in Glasgow, Nigeria’s track stars once again lit up Scotstoun Stadium. 

Twenty-three-year-old Ezekiel Nathaniel produced a commanding performance to win gold in the men’s 400-metre hurdles, while Udodi Onwuzurike fought bravely to secure silver in the men’s 200 metres.

Nathaniel, the Nigerian record holder, stopped the clock at 48.47 seconds to claim his first Commonwealth Games title. 

The young hurdler dominated the final, leaving England’s Jake Minshull in second place and Jamaica’s Assinie Wilson with bronze. 

His victory makes him the first Nigerian athlete to win gold in the 400m hurdles at the Games, surpassing the previous best of silver won by Kingsley Agbabokha in 1996.

Just hours later, attention shifted to the straight. Udodi Onwuzurike, also 23, delivered a season’s best of 20.09 seconds to take silver in the 200m final. 

South Africa’s Sinesipho Dambile edged him for gold in 19.96 seconds, while Jamaica’s Christopher Taylor finished third in 20.11 seconds. 

Running a strong bend and holding his form down the home straight, Onwuzurike narrowly held off Taylor to add another valuable medal to Nigeria’s growing tally.

The double success continues Team Nigeria’s strong athletics campaign in Glasgow. Earlier performances by para-athletes and field stars had already lifted the nation’s medal count, and Friday’s results on the track reinforced the country’s resurgence. 

Nathaniel’s gold was particularly emotional — a long-awaited breakthrough after previous near-misses and steady improvement on the global stage.

For Nigerian sports fans, these victories carry deeper meaning. They reflect years of quiet dedication by young athletes training across continents, often balancing university commitments with the demands of elite competition. 

Nathaniel and Onwuzurike both represent a new generation determined to rewrite the country’s athletics story.

As the Games continue, Team Nigeria’s athletes remain focused. The medals won in Glasgow will serve as both celebration and motivation, proving that consistent excellence and belief can still deliver golden moments on the biggest stages.
