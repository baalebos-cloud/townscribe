**ABUJA/IBADAN, Nigeria—Fresh** outrage over the abduction and rescue of schoolchildren and teachers in Oyo State has ignited sharp calls for accountability at the highest levels, with Senator Abdul Ningi urging President Bola Tinubu to ensure the swift public parading and prosecution of arrested suspects to deter future criminality.

Speaking during Senate plenary on Wednesday, the Bauchi Central lawmaker warned that any delay in bringing the perpetrators to justice would erode public confidence in Nigeria’s security and judicial systems. 

*“What is important going forward is to take a cue. Now we have people arrested, they must be paraded, prosecuted and let the law take its full course,”* Ningi declared.

The abduction occurred on May 15, 2026, when gunmen attacked three schools in Orire Local Government Area of Oyo State, seizing around 44 pupils (some as young as two years old) and seven teachers. 

The victims endured weeks in captivity, during which two teachers were reportedly killed. 

Security forces — involving the DSS, military, and other agencies — secured their release on July 10 in what the federal government described as an intelligence-led operation.

Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde has added fuel to the debate by calling on international bodies, including the United Nations and human rights organizations, to investigate the circumstances of the abduction, identify security lapses, and recommend preventive measures. 

The move has sparked mixed reactions, with some senators rejecting it as an unnecessary internationalization of a domestic matter that could undermine national sovereignty.

Senator Ningi commended the security operatives for the rescue but stressed the need for psychological support for the traumatized children and teachers. 

He also appealed for adequate compensation for security personnel who lost their lives during the operation, describing their sacrifice as a true display of patriotism. 

The senator emphasized that the crime was driven purely by criminal intent and financial gain, not religious or ethnic factors.

This high-profile case highlights the persistent vulnerability of schools in parts of Nigeria to banditry and kidnapping for ransom—a crisis that has disrupted education and instilled fear in communities. 

The eventual rescue without reported ransom payment (according to official accounts) has been hailed by some, but lingering questions about how the abduction occurred in the first place, the conditions in captivity, and the speed of justice continue to dominate public discourse.

Ningi’s demand for visible prosecution aligns with widespread frustration among Nigerians who feel that high-profile arrests often fail to translate into convictions, sending the wrong signal to criminals. 

As one of the most disturbing incidents in recent memory involving very young children, the Oyo school abduction has united calls across political lines for stronger security architecture and decisive judicial action.

For the affected families and the broader Oyo community, the ordeal is far from over. Healing, justice, and systemic reforms to protect schools remain urgent priorities. 

The federal and state governments now face a critical test: will this case mark a turning point in the fight against banditry, or will it fade into another unresolved statistic?

As investigations continue and political tempers flare over the proposed international probe, one thing remains clear — Nigerians are demanding not just rescues, but real deterrence through swift and transparent justice.
