**Benin City – November 25, 2026 —** Edo State Governor, Senator Monday Okpebholo, on Wednesday took President Bola Tinubu’s 2027 re-election campaign to the heart of the Arewa community in Aduwawa, pleading for a massive 2.5 million votes from the state.

Speaking at a colorful campaign rally, Okpebholo told Hausa traders, cattle dealers, and community leaders that their interests remain central to his administration, promising a better business environment and sustained government support for their commercial activities.

*“Today, I have come to my brothers and sisters in the Arewa community to say thank you for the love you have shown me by accepting me as your own. You called me your brother, and I also take you as my own,*” the governor said to loud applause.

He said the personal visit was deliberate, stressing he could not prosecute the election without face-to-face engagement.

*“I cannot do this election without coming to meet you to tell you that I need 2.5 million votes for Mr President,”* Okpebholo declared.

The governor recalled his earlier campaign visit to the Eyaen cattle market, where he promised to rehabilitate the deplorable access road to the market, assuring the traders the project will be completed before the end of the current dry season.

He listed his administration’s investments in roads, hospitals, and schools, alongside regular payment of workers’ salaries, urging the crowd: “Vote for Tinubu so that the good work will continue.”

Responding, Mansur Ibrahim, chairman of the Arewa community in Edo, assured the Hausa neighborhood of the government’s continued support and pledged to mobilize for the target.

The 2.5 million-vote target, which Okpebholo has consistently projected alongside allies like ex-militant leader Government Ekpemupolo, alias Tompolo, who donated 25 campaign vehicles, is widely seen as audacious in a state with about 2.7 million registered voters, requiring near-total turnout.

The Aduwawa outing is part of a broader strategy in which Okpebholo is courting non-indigene blocs, including the Igbo and Arewa communities, to shore up Tinubu and the APC ahead of the January 16, 2027 presidential poll.

It came on the same day rival Nigeria Democratic Congress candidate Peter Obi, with running mate Rabiu Kwankwaso, held a town hall in Benin City amid alleged attacks on his supporters — underscoring Edo’s emergence as a fierce 2027 battleground.

For the Arewa traders who closed shops to welcome “their brother,” the promise is simple: votes for roads, peace for business.
