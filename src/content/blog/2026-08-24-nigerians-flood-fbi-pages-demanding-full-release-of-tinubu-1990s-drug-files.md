**Lagos/Washington—August 24, 2026** — Thousands of Nigerians have taken to the official social media accounts of the U.S. Federal Bureau of Investigation, flooding Instagram, Facebook, X, and other platforms with demands for the immediate and full release of records linked to a decades-old drug trafficking investigation involving President Bola Ahmed Tinubu.

The coordinated online pressure campaign gained fresh momentum over the weekend following the FBI’s recent court filing in which the agency asked a U.S. federal judge for permission to explain, in private, why it is withholding certain documents. 

The bureau argued that public disclosure of some information could reveal law-enforcement techniques or endanger individuals.



**The Heart of the Dispute**

The records at issue stem from Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) requests filed by American transparency activist Aaron Greenspan between 2022 and 2023. 

They seek the FBI’s full file on Tinubu and Form 302 interview records from Case No. 245-IP-71386 covering 1992–1993. 

Those years coincide with a U.S. investigation into a Chicago-based heroin trafficking network and a related civil forfeiture action in which approximately $460,000 linked to accounts associated with Tinubu was forfeited to the American government.

Importantly, the historical investigation produced no criminal conviction against Tinubu. The current legal fight centres solely on whether and how much of the investigative material should be made public.

U.S. District Judge Beryl Howell of the District of Columbia has set tight deadlines. The FBI, DEA, Department of Justice and Tinubu (who has intervened in the case) must file any opposition to the release of the records by August 28, 2026.



**Public Reaction in Nigeria**

Many of the posts directed at the FBI accuse the agency of double standards and call for transparency. 

Hashtags and direct appeals to release *“Tinubu drug files”* have proliferated, with some users framing the demand as a matter of accountability for Nigeria’s highest office. 

Others have cautioned that an investigation or civil forfeiture does not equal a proven criminal case.

The renewed online activism coincides with heightened political tensions ahead of Nigeria’s 2027 elections, with opposition voices and online commentators keeping the issue alive.



**What Happens Next**

The court’s forthcoming rulings on the FBI’s request for confidential review and the broader summary-judgment motions will determine how much, if any, of the withheld material becomes public. 

Until then, the social-media campaign shows no sign of slowing, ensuring the long-running American FOIA dispute remains a live topic in Nigerian public discourse.
