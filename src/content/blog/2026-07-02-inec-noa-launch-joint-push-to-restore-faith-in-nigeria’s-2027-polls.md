**Abuja, Nigeria – July 2, 2026** – In a timely bid to counter voter apathy, rampant misinformation, and eroding public confidence, Nigeria’s Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) and the National Orientation Agency (NOA) have forged a strategic partnership to ramp up nationwide civic and voter education ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The collaboration was sealed during a high-level meeting in Abuja on Wednesday between INEC Chairman Prof. Joash Amupitan and NOA Director-General Mallam Issa Onilu. 

Both leaders described the alliance as essential for deepening democratic participation and safeguarding the integrity of future polls.

Prof. Amupitan was blunt about the challenges. While INEC continues to invest in technology, logistics, and administrative reforms, he warned that such efforts risk falling short without an informed and trusting electorate. 

Voter apathy, he noted, represents one of the greatest threats to Nigeria’s democracy, fueled by a widening trust deficit between citizens and the electoral system.

“*Elections are not just about processes and results — they are about people who believe their votes matter,”* Amupitan emphasized. 

He highlighted how misinformation during recent polls, including false claims of pre-determined outcomes and fabricated videos, had sown unnecessary anxiety and discouraged participation.

The INEC chief advocated for a decentralized, grassroots-focused campaign delivered in indigenous languages. 

This would clarify electoral procedures, debunk myths, and underscore the legal protections around the voting process.

Mallam Issa Onilu welcomed the partnership, stressing that civic education must be continuous rather than limited to election seasons. 

He pointed to gaps exposed during the 2023 polls, particularly among young first-time voters heavily influenced by digital misinformation.

NOA’s extensive network — spanning 818 offices across local governments, states, and zones — positions it ideally to drive community-level sensitization and relay public feedback to institutions like INEC. 

Onilu underscored that public trust cannot be imposed but must be earned through transparency, consistent engagement, and visible commitment to citizens’ welfare.

The two agencies agreed to jointly design and implement a robust program leveraging NOA’s field presence and INEC’s technical expertise. Key focus areas include:



\- Intensified voter education

\- Combating fake news

\- Correcting misconceptions about electoral rules

\- Encouraging broader citizen participation

\- Rebuilding confidence in the democratic process



This initiative comes against a backdrop of declining voter turnout and growing skepticism following previous elections marred by logistical issues, violence claims, and disputed results. 

Analysts view it as a proactive step to prevent 2027 from becoming another low-participation exercise that weakens democratic legitimacy.

With political activities expected to intensify soon, the partnership reflects recognition that credible elections require more than efficient administration — they demand an engaged populace that trusts the system. 

By starting early, INEC and NOA aim to transform passive citizens into active stakeholders, potentially reversing the trend of voter disillusionment.

As Nigeria approaches another pivotal electoral cycle, this collaboration offers a glimmer of institutional responsiveness. 

Whether it successfully rebuilds trust will depend on sustained implementation, measurable outcomes, and tangible improvements in how elections are perceived and conducted.

For millions of Nigerians who have grown cynical about the democratic process, this joint effort represents a critical test: Can electoral bodies and orientation agencies together restore faith that every vote truly counts? The coming months will provide the first answers.
