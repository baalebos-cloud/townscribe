**Umuahia, Abia State – July 24, 2026** — In a bold legal move that underscores the escalating tensions between the ruling Labour Party administration and the opposition All Progressives Congress (APC) in Abia State, Governor Dr. Alex Chioma Otti has filed a ₦5 billion defamation lawsuit against Uche Aguoru, the APC’s State Publicity Secretary.

The suit, filed at the High Court of Abia State in the Umuahia Judicial Division, stems from a series of Facebook publications in which Aguoru allegedly accused the governor of corruption, diversion of public funds, abuse of office, political inducement, interference in another political party’s affairs, and judicial intimidation.

Governor Otti, through his legal team, maintains that these allegations are “*false, malicious, and defamatory,”* aimed at tarnishing his hard-earned reputation as a reform-minded leader focused on accountable governance and infrastructure development in Abia State.

In the court papers, Otti is seeking several reliefs, including:

\- A judicial declaration that the publications are defamatory.

\- An order compelling Aguoru to retract the statements and publish an unreserved public apology.

\- A perpetual injunction restraining further similar publications.

\- Award of ₦5 billion in general damages plus costs of the suit.

The matter is now before the court for full determination. As is standard in ongoing litigation, all allegations remain unproven until judicially resolved.

This latest legal battle comes against the backdrop of persistent opposition criticism of the Otti administration. 

The Abia APC has recently raised concerns over workers’ welfare, implementation of the national minimum wage, pension payments, and other governance issues. 

Aguoru has been vocal in highlighting these matters on behalf of the party.

Governor Otti, who assumed office in 2023 under the Labour Party platform, has built a public image centered on fiscal prudence, road infrastructure revival, and transparent public fund management. 

Supporters view the suit as a necessary step to protect his record against what they describe as politically motivated attacks.

This is not Otti’s first high-profile defamation case. In late 2025, he pursued a separate ₦100 billion suit against a former commissioner, demonstrating his willingness to use legal avenues to defend his reputation.

As Abia State continues to navigate post-election realities and preparations for future cycles, the suit highlights the intense rivalry between the Labour Party-led government and the APC opposition. 

Political observers note that such high-stakes defamation cases often serve both to clear the air and to send strong signals in Nigeria’s polarized political environment.

Governor Otti’s administration has prioritized capital projects and institutional reforms since taking office. 

How this legal showdown unfolds could influence public perception and the tone of political discourse in the state ahead of upcoming electoral activities.

The High Court is expected to provide further directions as the case progresses. Both parties and the general public await developments that could shape the narrative around governance and accountability in Abia State.
