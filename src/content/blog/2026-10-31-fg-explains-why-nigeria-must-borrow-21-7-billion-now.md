**Abuja, Nigeria—October 31, 2026**—The Federal Ministry of Finance has defended the proposed US$21.7 billion External Borrowing Plan for 2026–2027, insisting that borrowing is not merely about raising funds but about financing transformative investments that will strengthen infrastructure, create jobs and promote inclusive growth.

In a post shared on its official X handle on Friday, the Ministry stated, "*Borrowing is not just about raising funds; it is about financing critical investments that transform lives, strengthen infrastructure, create jobs and drive inclusive economic growth.”*



**Breakdown of the Plan**

The plan, which has been submitted to the National Assembly, covers 46 projects across 11 sectors. Transportation will take the largest share—about $10.6 billion (49 percent)—for major highways, including sections of the Lagos–Calabar Coastal Highway and Sokoto–Badagry Superhighway, as well as the Kano–Kaduna Metro Rail.

Defense and security projects are allocated $2.4 billion, while agriculture, energy, social protection, climate, and education will receive a combined $4 billion, mostly through concessional financing.

About $7.9 billion is expected from multilateral and bilateral partners, including $5 billion from the World Bank at an average interest rate of 2.1 per cent. 

Commercial facilities, which make up 64 per cent of the package, carry an indicative average rate of 7.8 per cent.

Notably, $4.8 billion of the total is earmarked for states, the Federal Capital Territory, and government agencies.



**Official Position**

Minister of Finance and Coordinating Minister of the Economy, Taiwo Oyedele, has repeatedly described the proposal as ***“investment, not borrowing.”*** 

He argued that the funds should be judged by the productive assets they will create and the long-term economic returns, rather than simply as an addition to the debt stock.

The average project lifespan under the plan is five years. Officials emphasize that the request does not mean the entire amount will necessarily be drawn down.

The Ministry’s public messaging aims to explain the rationale behind the borrowing amid public debate and criticism from opposition figures.
