**Abuja, August 15, 2026**— The Federal Government has intensified efforts to tighten homeland security coordination across agencies, institutions, communities and citizens, framing peace and security as the indispensable foundation for national development.

The renewed push was highlighted in an official Presidency release on Saturday, following a high-level engagement between the Minister of Information and National Orientation, Mohammed Idris, and the Special Adviser to the President on Homeland Security, Major-General Adeyinka Famadewa (rtd.).



**Coordination Beyond Guns and Uniforms**

Speaking during the courtesy visit by Famadewa to the Ministry, Idris stressed that Nigeria’s security challenges cannot be solved by military action alone. 

He called for deeper collaboration among the armed forces, security agencies, all tiers of government, communities, the media and ordinary citizens.

*“Peace and security are essential elements; in fact, they are the building blocks of any development that can happen anywhere,”* the minister said. 

He particularly flagged misinformation and disinformation as emerging security threats that must be confronted with timely, accurate official communication.



**Mandate of the New Homeland Security Office**

Major-General Famadewa, appointed by President Bola Tinubu in May 2026, outlined the core focus of his office: unified intelligence and surveillance, stronger border security, better threat identification, and effective crisis reporting and response protocols.

*“Information is key in asymmetric warfare. The first person to speak wins, and we cannot sit back and allow others to shape perception for us,”* Famadewa noted. 

The office is designed to complement existing institutions rather than replace them, concentrating on intelligence fusion, technology-driven surveillance and inter-agency synergy.

Senior officials present at related engagements have included Dr. Binyerem C. Ukaire, Charles Ebuebu, Salihu Abdulhamid Dembos, Jibrin Baba Ndace, and Ali Muhammad Ali, underscoring the whole-of-government approach.



**Four Strategic Pillars**

Earlier stakeholders’ meetings convened by Famadewa prioritised four practical pillars:



\- A Unified Surveillance Programme for nationwide situational awareness  

\- Stronger identity management to reduce anonymity exploited by criminals  

\- Enhanced border security through technology and intelligence-led operations  

\- Robust early-warning, crisis reporting and emergency response systems



These priorities form part of President Tinubu’s broader effort to build a resilient, technology-driven homeland security ecosystem capable of addressing both traditional and emerging threats.



**Why the Push Matters Now**

With insecurity remaining a major constraint on investment, agriculture, education, and daily life across parts of the country, the government is signaling that coordination, intelligence sharing, and public communication will sit at the centre of its strategy. 

Officials argue that only a joined-up approach—linking kinetic operations with community engagement and rapid narrative control—can deliver the safer environment needed for economic recovery and development.

As the Office of the Special Adviser on Homeland Security continues to roll out its framework, the coming months will test whether stronger coordination translates into measurable improvements in public safety and public confidence.

The Federal Government maintains that under President Tinubu’s leadership, the goal remains clear: a safer, more peaceful, and prosperous Nigeria built on the twin pillars of security and development.
