**Washington, D.C.—October 3, 2026**—President Donald Trump has issued a direct call to Congress for tougher legislation targeting fraud in America’s major benefit programs, declaring that *“the days of fraudsters stealing from the American people are over.”*

In a nearly two-minute video released by the White House on Saturday, Trump outlined a legislative proposal aimed at Medicare, Medicaid, and Social Security. 

He said the measures would impose mandatory minimum prison sentences on those who steal from programs designed to care for the sick, feed the hungry, support children, and protect seniors’ retirements.

*“Under our proposal, probation is no longer an option for the fraudsters,”* Trump stated. *“If you steal from the American people, you will go to prison for a long period of time.  And the more you steal, the longer you’ll stay in that prison.”* Repeat offenders, he added, would face doubled sentences.

The president also called for real consequences for “*crooked and corrupt politicians who willfully turn a blind eye to fraud,”* arguing the package would force both criminals and officials to think twice before allowing theft to continue.



**Part of a Broader Crackdown**

The announcement builds on the administration’s ongoing effort against government fraud, led by the White House Task Force to Eliminate Fraud under Vice President JD Vance. 

Officials have repeatedly highlighted large-scale recoveries and enforcement actions across health care, pandemic-era loans, and other federal programs.

Administration statements have pointed to hundreds of billions of dollars in fraud identified or halted since early 2025, including major health care takedowns and referrals of fraudulent loans for collection. 

The Department of Justice has expanded its National Fraud Enforcement Division and announced numerous charges and convictions in recent months.

Critics, including some independent analyses, have questioned the precision of certain headline figures, noting that some totals may include paused payments, pre-existing cases, or estimates that are difficult to fully verify.

Medicare, Medicaid, and Social Security represent some of the largest federal expenditures and have long been targets for sophisticated fraud schemes. 

Lawmakers on both sides of the aisle have previously expressed concern about losses to these programs, though agreement on solutions has often been elusive.

Trump framed the proposal as both a deterrent and a signal of restored accountability. Whether Congress advances the specific mandatory-minimum and political-accountability measures remains to be seen, but the White House message was unmistakable: benefit fraud will face harsher consequences under the current administration.
