In a passionate defense of President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s administration, Dr. Oke Idawene, a prominent voice and supporter, has highlighted what he describes as unprecedented financial support extended to state governors and local government structures. 

Speaking in a recent interview that quickly gained traction online, Idawene praised the president for prioritizing structural reforms, economic stabilization, and grassroots development through substantial resource allocation.

"President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has given so much to the governors, excess money to develop our local government, our state structures," Idawene declared emphatically, gesturing to underscore his point. 

He framed these transfers as part of a broader strategy that includes creating development commissions, adjusting security architecture, and empowering sub-national governments to deliver tangible results to citizens.

According to Idawene, Tinubu’s approach marks a departure from previous eras by providing governors with significant fiscal breathing room. 

This, he argued, enables states to strengthen institutions, invest in critical infrastructure, and address local challenges more effectively. 

The comments come amid ongoing national conversations about federal revenue sharing, the impact of fuel subsidy removal, and the need for coordinated governance in tackling Nigeria’s multifaceted problems.

Supporters of the administration see this as evidence of collaborative federalism — a president willing to empower states rather than centralize control. 

Idawene positioned these moves alongside other reforms aimed at long-term economic resilience, suggesting that the "**excess money"** is intended to translate into visible development at the grassroots level.

As with many defenses of the current government, the remarks have elicited sharply divided responses. 

While some viewers appreciated the acknowledgment of federal support, others expressed skepticism on social media. 

Critics questioned whether the funds are truly yielding results for ordinary citizens or primarily serving political loyalty ahead of future elections.

One user (@SegzyTel) captured a common sentiment: "*My problem with this type of argument... Are the citizens feeling this support?"* 

Another (@PhilipAdanu) asked pointedly if the allocations were "*for development or to buy loyalty and votes ahead of 2027?"*

Skeptics point to persistent hardships — inflation, high living costs, and uneven development across states — arguing that increased allocations to governors have not always translated into improved public services or accountability at the state level. 

Questions about transparency in how these funds are utilized remain a recurring theme in public discourse.

Idawene’s intervention reflects a larger effort by Tinubu’s allies to reframe the administration’s narrative around empowerment and reform rather than solely focusing on short-term pains from economic adjustments. 

It also underscores the delicate balance in Nigeria’s federal system: the tension between providing states with resources and ensuring those resources deliver democratic dividends to the people.

As Nigeria navigates its third year under President Tinubu, voices like Dr. Idawene’s contribute to an intensifying debate. 

Are generous federal disbursements laying the foundation for genuine transformation, or do they risk becoming tools for political patronage? 

The answer, many observers note, will ultimately be measured not in billions allocated, but in tangible improvements felt by everyday Nigerians — from better local infrastructure to enhanced security and economic opportunities.

The conversation continues, with citizens closely watching whether "excess money" to governors translates into excess progress for the nation. 

In a democracy where trust in leadership is often tested by outcomes on the ground, this defense adds another layer to the complex story of Tinubu’s Renewed Hope agenda.
