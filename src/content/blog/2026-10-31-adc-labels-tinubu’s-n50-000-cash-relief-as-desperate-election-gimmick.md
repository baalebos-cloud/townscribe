**Abuja, Nigeria—October 31, 2026**—The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has dismissed the Federal Government’s announcement of an N50,000 monthly cash relief as an election gimmick driven by President Bola Tinubu’s desperation, rather than genuine concern for Nigerians struggling under the weight of economic hardship.

In a strongly worded statement signed by National Publicity Secretary Mallam Bolaji Abdullahi, the party described the move as a panic reaction to the opposition’s campaign focus on reducing the cost of living ahead of the 2027 general elections.



**“Too Little, Too Late”**

Abdullahi questioned the real value of the payment in today’s economy.

*“What exactly is N50,000 supposed to buy in Tinubu’s Nigeria today? How much food can a family purchase with that amount? How much transport can it cover? And for how long?”* he asked. 

*“Petrol prices have risen roughly sevenfold. Food and transportation costs have followed… It is just a desperate and cynical attempt to buy back the goodwill of the people. But it is too little, too late.”*

The ADC listed a series of previous announcements it claimed had failed to materialize, including a $1 billion Renewed Hope Social Intervention Fund, a 30-day fuel discount, cheaper transportation by October 1, and a N70,000 transport allowance for workers.

*“They are just thrashing around, hoping that something would stick,”* Abdullahi said.



**Accusations of Mismanagement and Lack of Accountability**

The party accused the Tinubu administration of mismanaging its economic reforms and forcing Nigerians to *“endure”* hardship for three years. It argued that if such cash support was possible, it should have been factored into the reform plans from the beginning.

The ADC also raised concerns about transparency, pointing to a recent Auditor-General’s report on unaccounted billions in previous cash transfer programmes.

*“How can a government that has failed to explain what happened to its previous intervention funds expect Nigerians to trust it with another programme involving billions of naira?”* the statement asked.

It warned that the latest initiative could become another opportunity for diversion of public funds under the guise of poverty alleviation.



**Call for Real Solutions**

The party maintained that the lasting solution to Nigeria’s cost-of-living crisis lies in restoring purchasing power and reducing the cost of food, energy, and transportation—not seasonal handouts.



The ADC’s presidential candidate for the 2027 election is former Vice President Atiku Abubakar.



***This report is based on the official press statement released by the African Democratic Congress on October 31, 2026.***
