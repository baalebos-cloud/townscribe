**Kaduna, Nigeria—November 22, 2026**—In a tense early-morning drama that has once again highlighted the fragile security of rural communities, Nigerian Army troops foiled a mass abduction attempt targeting young members of the Boys’ and Girls’ Brigade in Kaduna State, successfully rescuing 18 of the 22 children seized by suspected terrorists.

The incident unfolded in the early hours of Sunday, November 22, in the Mother-Cat Community within the Kachia/Kajuru general area. 

The children had gathered for an early morning group road walk when armed men intercepted them and whisked them away into the surrounding bush.

Security sources revealed that troops of Sub-Sector 5, 1 Division Nigerian Army, operating under Sector 1 of Operation FANSAN YAMMA, received a distress call and mobilized without delay. The soldiers launched an aggressive pursuit of the fleeing abductors, pressing them towards the Rehina riverside.

There, under mounting military pressure, the criminals abandoned several of the children, who were immediately secured by the troops. Undeterred, the soldiers continued the chase across the river line, recovering more victims and bringing the total number of children freed to 18.

The sustained operation forced the remaining abductors to abandon their captives and flee deeper into the forests. 

Troops have since extended the search along the Ankwa–Kabode–Pachi link road and towards the Awon and Akwondo general areas in a determined effort to locate the four children still unaccounted for and to apprehend those responsible.

The successful intervention underscores the growing importance of rapid response and inter-sector coordination in the face of persistent kidnapping threats across parts of northern Nigeria. 

Parents and community leaders in the affected area have expressed both relief at the rescue of the majority of the children and deep anxiety over the four who remain missing.

Military authorities have assured that search-and-rescue operations will continue relentlessly until every child is safely returned and the perpetrators are brought to justice. 

The incident serves as a stark reminder of the vulnerability of civilian activities, even routine ones such as a morning walk by young people, in regions still grappling with armed criminal networks.
