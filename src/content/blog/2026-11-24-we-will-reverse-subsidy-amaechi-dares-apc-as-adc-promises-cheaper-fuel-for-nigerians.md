**ABUJA —** Nigeria’s 2027 presidential race took a fiery new turn on Tuesday as the African Democratic Congress, ADC, threw down the gauntlet on the most painful economic question facing ordinary families: fuel price.

Speaking in a clip aired by ARISE News and amplified by ADC Vanguard on X, the party’s vice-presidential candidate, former Rivers State Governor Rotimi Amaechi, declared in plain, defiant words:

> *“APC said you cannot reverse subsidy. We can reverse subsidy. We will reverse subsidy.”*

It was not just a slogan. Amaechi went further to explain the detail that, analysts say, matters more than the headline.

According to him, the ADC will make fuel affordable again, but not through the old, corruption-ridden subsidy regime. To ensure that “those people who were stealing in the last subsidy do not hijack it,” he said, the new subsidy will be “*only for local producers in Nigeria.”*

Those local producers, he argued, “will create employment,” keep foreign exchange at home, and build an industry instead of a racket.

Presidential candidate, former Vice President Atiku Abubakar, made the same commitment on Tuesday at the ADC’s massive campaign rally in Lokoja, Kogi State. 

Atiku promised a targeted, production-based subsidy on petrol refined in Nigerian refineries, including the Dangote Refinery and state-owned plants, and not on imported cargoes by middlemen.

Atiku, who is contesting for the fourth time after 2007, 2019 and 2023, told the mammoth crowd chanting *“Tinubu Must Go*” that his ‘Affordable Life’ campaign would bring down the cost of fuel, strengthen local production, and restore the purchasing power of the naira.

*“I have already promised Nigerians that I will return subsidy. I mean it. I am serious about it. This will bring about affordability of your life,”* Atiku said, while also pledging to revive the Ajaokuta Steel Mill with foreign investment and dredge the River Niger for the Baro Port.

The ADC proposal is a direct challenge to the ruling All Progressives Congress, APC. Vice President Kashim Shettima said on Tuesday that President Bola Tinubu *“removed the subsidy to save the Nigerian economy from collapse.”*

That defence comes three and a half years after Tinubu’s May 29, 2023 inaugural declaration of *“subsidy is gone,*” which tripled transport fares and food prices and left millions counting their last naira.

Amaechi saved his sharpest jab for the incumbents: “If you are running an election and you are saying what you will do in your second tenure, you did not do well in your first tenure.”

For voters—the mother choosing between food and school fees, the trader watching customers disappear, the worker trekking home—the ADC says relief can be targeted so thieves cannot return. 

But serious questions remain: what will it cost, how will it be funded, how will smuggling be stopped, and what audit trail will prove the money reaches the pump?
