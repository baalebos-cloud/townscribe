**IBADAN, Oyo State** — In a moment of profound relief for families and the entire state, Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde on Saturday visited the rescued victims of the Oriire abduction, offering words of encouragement and assurances of full government support as they begin the road to recovery.

The governor, accompanied by senior military officials, met with the freed pupils, teachers, and other victims at a medical facility where they are undergoing observation. 

According to Makinde, the rescued individuals will remain under close medical monitoring for the next 48 hours before a thorough assessment paves the way for their formal handover to the Oyo State Government for comprehensive rehabilitation and reintegration.

The abduction saga began on May 15, 2026, when armed gunmen stormed three schools in the Yawota and Ahoro-Esiele communities of Oriire Local Government Area, near Ogbomoso. 

The attackers seized dozens of pupils — some as young as two years old — along with seven teachers and a school principal. The victims endured 56 harrowing days in captivity within nearby forests.

Tragedy marked the ordeal, including the reported beheading of one teacher, Michael Oyedokun, which sparked widespread outrage, protests, and a teachers’ strike demanding swift action. 

Governor Makinde had earlier vowed that Oyo State would not negotiate from a position of weakness, while also expressing personal willingness to make extreme sacrifices for the victims’ safe return.

The Presidency announced the successful rescue on July 10, 2026, crediting coordinated efforts by Nigerian security forces, including the military, police, intelligence agencies, and local vigilantes like Amotekun. 

Several kidnappers were neutralized during the operation, with eight others arrested and now in custody of the Department of State Services (DSS). Authorities confirmed no ransom was paid.

Makinde described the rescue as “a *big relief”* for the affected families and the people of Oyo State, ending weeks of anxiety and uncertainty. 

His hospital visit, captured in images showing him interacting compassionately with victims and security personnel, underscored a message of solidarity and commitment to their well-being.

Medical teams are prioritizing physical and psychological care for the survivors, many of whom endured significant trauma. 

The state government is expected to provide ongoing support, including counseling, educational continuity for the pupils, and assistance for the teachers as they rebuild their lives.

The Oriire incident, which occurred shortly after Makinde’s declaration of interest in the 2027 presidential race, has reignited conversations about school safety, rural insecurity, and the effectiveness of multi-agency security operations in Nigeria. 

While the rescue brings closure to one painful chapter, it also highlights persistent vulnerabilities in parts of the Southwest.

President Bola Tinubu and other national leaders have welcomed the development, with calls for sustained vigilance against banditry and abduction threats nationwide. For Oyo residents, the safe return of the victims represents not just a security success but a testament to resilience in the face of adversity.

As the rescued pupils and teachers begin their healing process under state care, Governor Makinde’s hands-on engagement sends a clear signal: the welfare of citizens remains paramount. 

In a country where such incidents too often test public confidence, stories of successful rescues and compassionate follow-through offer a glimmer of hope and a reminder of what coordinated action can achieve.
