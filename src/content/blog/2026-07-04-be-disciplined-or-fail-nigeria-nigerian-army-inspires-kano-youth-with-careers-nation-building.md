**Kano, Nigeria – July 4, 2026** — In a heartening display of civil-military partnership, the Nigerian Army’s 3 Brigade has reached out to the next generation, inspiring hundreds of students and teachers at Tony Cheta International Group of Schools in Kano through a vibrant public speaking engagement. 

The event forms part of the broader 2026 Nigerian Army Day Celebration (NADCEL), themed “Protecting the Nation and Serving the People: A Way Forward for the Nigerian Army.”

Brigadier General Saka Jimoh, Commander of 3 Brigade, delivered a passionate address urging young Nigerians to prioritize discipline, hard work, and academic excellence as cornerstones of personal growth and national development. 

He framed the initiative as a direct reflection of the Army’s commitment to mentorship, patriotism, and stronger bonds between the military and civilian communities.

The Brigade Assistant Chief of Staff (Administration), Lieutenant Colonel Gbenga Adekunle, and Brigade Education Officer, Major Hassan Abdullahi, reinforced these messages in separate presentations. 

They emphasized core values — honesty, integrity, and responsible citizenship — while warning students against social vices such as drug abuse, internet fraud (often called “yahoo yahoo”), cultism, and other criminal behaviors that derail futures and harm society.

Officers highlighted diverse career pathways within the Nigerian Army, spanning engineering, medical services, logistics, intelligence, communications, education, and more. 

They encouraged academically driven students to consider military service as a noble and rewarding profession dedicated to protecting Nigeria’s sovereignty while serving its people.

Mr. Tony Emecheta, Director of Tony Cheta International Group of Schools, praised the Army’s proactive engagement. 

He described the program as a powerful demonstration of the military’s investment in youth development and nation-building, noting how it strengthens trust and collaboration between soldiers and civilians.

Students Andrew Success and Confidence George, speaking on behalf of their peers, expressed gratitude for the enlightening session. 

They said it dispelled misconceptions about the military, broadened their horizons, and motivated them to contribute positively to Nigeria’s future.

The event featured interactive discussions, the distribution of souvenirs and educational materials, and group photographs that captured the spirit of unity. 

Images from the day show enthusiastic students in school uniforms mingling with officers in camouflage, teachers, and school leaders against the backdrop of school buildings and official banners.

This Kano outreach is one of many community-focused activities marking the Nigerian Army’s 163rd anniversary. 

Across formations, the Army has organized prayers for fallen heroes, thanksgiving services, and public engagements that underscore its dual role in security and societal development. 

The theme chosen for 2026 highlights a forward-looking vision: balancing robust national defense with tangible service to citizens through education, mentorship, and partnership.

In a country where youth empowerment and security challenges often dominate national discourse, initiatives like this send a clear message. 

The Army is not only a fighting force but also an institution invested in shaping disciplined, patriotic leaders. 

By engaging students directly, 3 Brigade is planting seeds of civic responsibility that could yield long-term dividends for peace and progress, particularly in northern Nigeria.

As NADCEL 2026 activities continue toward the grand finale, programs like the one in Kano humanize the military’s image and reinforce the idea that protecting the nation begins with investing in its youth. 

For these Kano students, a day with the Army may well become a turning point — inspiring dreams of service, excellence, and a brighter collective future.
