**Abuja, August 22, 2026** — A potential alliance between former Vice President Atiku Abubakar and Peter Obi ahead of the 2027 presidential election faces significant obstacles rooted in the candidates’ personalities and their supporters’ expectations, according to political strategist Umar Ardo.

Ardo, Convener of the League of Northern Democrats, made the assessment during an appearance on Arise Television. 

He argued that while talks of opposition unity against President Bola Tinubu continue, the personal dispositions of the two leading figures present the biggest barriers to a workable merger.



**The Twin Challenges Blocking a Deal**

*“My view is that Atiku does not have the propensity by himself to say, ‘I will step down.’ If he says so, he will not have a problem with his support base. But the issue is that he is highly unlikely to say that,”* Ardo stated.

He contrasted this with Obi’s position: *“Then Peter Obi has the propensity to say, ‘I will step down.’ But if he says so, he will not have his support base.* 

*He will not be able to carry his support base. His support base will not go with him. So, now, these are the two problems.”*

Unless these issues are resolved, Ardo concluded, “*I don’t see the fusing together being possible.”*

Atiku currently flies the flag of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), while Obi is the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC). 

Both men remain central to opposition calculations after their strong showings in previous contests and ongoing efforts to present a united front.



**Individual Strength Still Matters**

Ardo was careful to note that the absence of a formal coalition would not automatically hand the advantage to the ruling party. 

He maintained that either Atiku or Obi, campaigning independently with the right strategy, still has the capacity to “*upturn the order”* and pose a serious challenge to the incumbent.

The comments come amid persistent speculation about opposition realignment, previous coalition attempts, and the complex dynamics of Nigeria’s multi-party landscape. 

Public discussions about one candidate stepping aside for the other have circulated repeatedly, though both camps have largely stayed focused on their individual paths.

Political observers say Ardo’s analysis reflects a widely held view within certain opposition circles: that personal ambition and the loyalty of core supporters often outweigh strategic calculations for unity. 

Whether the two camps can find a creative solution before the election cycle intensifies remains one of the key questions shaping Nigeria’s 2027 contest.

For now, the conversation continues, with strategists, party leaders and voters watching closely to see if the obstacles Ardo identified can be overcome—or whether the opposition will once again face the ballot divided.
