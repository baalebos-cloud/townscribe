**Abuja, Nigeria—November 6, 2026**—The Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) has secured a final forfeiture order for the sum of $163,592 fraudulently obtained by one Akpasi Michael Oziegbe in an elaborate international impersonation and fraud scheme involving a United States-based law firm.

Justice M. S. Liman of the Federal High Court in Abuja granted the non-conviction-based forfeiture order on Friday, November 6, 2026, following an application filed by the EFCC. Prosecution counsel Ekele Iheanacho, SAN, led the Commission’s legal team in the proceedings.

The court held that Oziegbe failed to establish legitimate ownership of the funds. Justice Liman ruled that the accused had illegally procured the services of the American law firm to secure a settlement sum from a company he was never employed by.



**Details of the Fraudulent Scheme**

Investigations revealed that Oziegbe impersonated Edward Campbell, a former employee of the United States-based company Applied Industrial Technologies who had sustained an accident while on duty. 

Using this false identity, he deceived the Law Firm of Steven J. Mandel & Co. into facilitating the release of settlement funds.

According to information obtained from the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI), the law firm received a request for legal representation on June 27, 2022, from an individual claiming to be Edward Campbell. 

The impersonator alleged that Applied Industrial Technologies had failed to honour a settlement agreement reached over the workplace accident.

The firm was subsequently informed that a cheque of $165,157 had been issued by the company on behalf of Campbell. 

Acting on instructions from the impersonator, the law firm deducted its fees and transferred the balance of $163,592 to a bank account provided on July 20, 2022. 

The funds were credited to the domiciliary account of Micoz Bluelink Enterprise, operated by Oziegbe, at United Bank for Africa (UBA) Plc.

A day later, on July 21, 2022, JP Morgan Chase Bank alerted the law firm that the cheque was altered and fictitious. 

The firm contacted Applied Industrial Technologies and confirmed the entire transaction was fraudulent. The matter was promptly reported to the United States Secret Service and the FBI.



**Banking Red Flags and EFCC Intervention**

UBA had flagged the incoming funds as suspicious and initiated a reconfirmation process through its correspondent bank, Citi Bank. 

Confirmation of the fraud and a refund request from JP Morgan Chase Bank on grounds of unjust enrichment led UBA to place a lien on the account.

Oziegbe later petitioned the EFCC in May 2024 through his lawyers, GAIA & Montgomery Legal, alleging misconduct by some UBA officials and claiming the money represented payment for materials supplied to the United States. 

The Commission’s Extractive Industry Fraud Section (EIFS) investigated the petition and established that the funds originated from the fraudulent US transaction. Oziegbe could not produce documents to support his claims.

The EFCC obtained a freezing order on the account on September 9, 2024, and an interim forfeiture order on August 15, 2025. The Commission had also earlier declared Oziegbe wanted in connection with the case.

With the final forfeiture order now secured, the $163,592 will be returned to the Law Firm of Steven J. Mandel & Co. through the Federal Government of Nigeria.

The successful recovery underscores the EFCC’s continued collaboration with international partners such as the FBI in tackling cross-border financial crimes and the effectiveness of non-conviction-based asset recovery mechanisms under Nigerian law.
