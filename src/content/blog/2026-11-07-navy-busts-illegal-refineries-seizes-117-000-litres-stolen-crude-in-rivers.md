**Abuja/Port Harcourt, Nigeria—November 7, 2026**—The Nigerian Navy has dismantled three illegal refining sites and recovered approximately 117,000 litres of suspected stolen crude oil in Awokiri Community, Ogu-Bolo Local Government Area of Rivers State.

The operation, carried out on November 5 by personnel of the Forward Operating Base (FOB) Bonny, followed credible intelligence about the activation of new illegal refining sites in the area. 

Director of Naval Information, Navy Captain Abiodun Folorunsho, disclosed the details in an official statement on Saturday.



**Details of the Raid**

During the raid, the naval team discovered 16 dugout pits. Nine of the pits held about 115,000 litres of suspected stolen crude oil, while an additional storage tank contained roughly 2,000 litres, bringing the total volume to approximately 117,000 litres. 

Four other pits contained water, and three were freshly excavated, indicating ongoing activity by the operators.

The identified sites, pits and recovered petroleum products were handled in accordance with existing regulations. No arrests were immediately reported in connection with this specific operation.

Captain Folorunsho said the action forms part of the Navy’s sustained, intelligence-driven campaign against crude oil theft and illegal bunkering under Operation Delta Sentinel. 

He emphasized that deactivating such facilities aims to disrupt criminal networks, protect critical oil and gas infrastructure, and safeguard Nigeria’s economic interests.



**Broader Context of Anti-Theft Efforts**

This latest success comes against the backdrop of significant gains recorded by the Nigerian Navy under Chief of the Naval Staff, Vice Admiral Idi Abbas. 

In the first year of his leadership (from late October 2025 to early November 2026), the Service recovered more than 7.48 million litres of stolen crude oil and illegally refined products. 

It also deactivated 168 illegal refining sites, destroyed 363 dugout pits, arrested 259 suspects, and secured nine convictions related to crude oil theft and pipeline vandalism.

Operation Delta Sentinel, launched in January 2026, has been central to these results. The Navy has intensified patrols, surveillance, and community engagement across the Niger Delta to deny oil thieves operational space. 

Recent months have seen multiple recoveries in Rivers State alone, including earlier discoveries of tens of thousands of litres in Bonny and other communities.

Oil theft continues to pose a major challenge to Nigeria’s economy, reducing revenue, damaging the environment, and funding criminal networks. 

The Navy’s consistent pressure has contributed to improved crude oil production figures and the country’s sustained zero-piracy status in its maritime domain.



**Call for Public Support**

The Navy reiterated its appeal to community leaders, residents, traditional institutions, and other stakeholders to promptly report suspicious activities, including crude oil theft, illegal refining, and unauthorized movement of petroleum products, to the nearest security agency. 

Timely information, it said, enables swift intervention and prevents the expansion of illicit operations.

*“Safeguarding the nation’s critical petroleum assets requires vigilance and active cooperation between security agencies and the communities they serve,”* the statement noted.

The Nigerian Navy reaffirmed its commitment to maintaining momentum against maritime crimes and protecting national resources for the benefit of all Nigerians.
