**SAGAMU, Ogun State** — In a heartwarming display of compassion and cross-border collaboration, the wife of Ogun State Governor, Mrs. Bamidele Abiodun, has once again stepped forward to bridge healthcare gaps by flagging off a major free medical outreach programme.

The initiative, organized through her Ajose Foundation in partnership with a team of US-based Nigerian medical professionals, aims to provide free surgeries and medical interventions to over 300 vulnerable residents. 

The event took place at the Olabisi Onabanjo University Teaching Hospital (OOUTH) in Sagamu, offering hope to many who might otherwise lack access to specialized care.

This year’s outreach focuses on a range of conditions, including breast lumps, fibroids, hernias, and other surgical needs. 

Mrs. Abiodun, through her foundation, has sustained this impactful project for several years, consistently partnering with diaspora medical experts to deliver quality healthcare at no cost to beneficiaries.

Dr. Bimpe Adenusi, a key member of the US-based team, joined the flag-off ceremony alongside hospital officials and other dignitaries. 

The First Lady commended the volunteers for their dedication and urged greater support for initiatives that uplift the less privileged in society.

For residents in Ogun State and surrounding communities, such outreaches represent more than medical procedures — they restore dignity, alleviate suffering, and demonstrate that leadership can be both visionary and deeply compassionate. 

Many beneficiaries are indigent citizens who would struggle to afford even basic consultations, let alone complex surgeries.

Mrs. Abiodun’s consistent efforts highlight the power of public-private and international partnerships in addressing Nigeria’s healthcare challenges. 

By leveraging the expertise of Nigerian doctors practicing abroad, the programme not only provides immediate relief but also fosters knowledge transfer and strengthens local capacity at institutions like OOUTH.

This 2026 edition continues a tradition of service that has positively impacted thousands over the years. 

As the surgeries commence, stories of transformed lives are expected to emerge — from a mother relieved of debilitating fibroids to a child given a chance at normalcy through corrective procedures.

In a country where healthcare access remains a pressing issue for many, initiatives like this serve as powerful reminders of what collective goodwill and strategic partnerships can achieve. 

Mrs. Bamidele Abiodun’s Ajose Foundation continues to set a commendable example of using influence for tangible grassroots impact.

The programme is ongoing, bringing renewed hope to families across Ogun State and reinforcing the message that no one should be left behind in the journey toward better health and wellbeing.
