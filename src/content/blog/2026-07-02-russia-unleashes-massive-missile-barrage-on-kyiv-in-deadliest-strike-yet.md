**Kyiv, Ukraine – July 2, 2026** – In one of the most intense assaults on Ukraine’s capital in recent months, Russia launched a ferocious overnight barrage of missiles and drones, killing at least a dozen civilians and injuring scores more as explosions rocked residential districts and civilian infrastructure across Kyiv.

Ukrainian officials reported that Russia fired dozens of missiles — including ballistic variants — and hundreds of attack drones in coordinated waves, targeting multiple districts of the city. 

Rescue workers continue sifting through rubble of collapsed buildings, with fears that the death toll could rise as operations uncover more victims trapped beneath debris, including reports of children among the missing.

President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had warned just hours earlier of an impending “*massive*” Russian strike, a grim prophecy that materialized as air raid sirens wailed through the night and residents sought shelter in underground metro stations — with over 40,000 crowding into Kyiv’s subway system at the peak of the alert.

Ukraine’s air force said Russia unleashed more than 70 missiles and nearly 500 drones nationwide, with Kyiv bearing the brunt. 

While defenses intercepted many, numerous strikes penetrated, hitting residential blocks, causing fires, and damaging civilian sites. 

Black smoke billowed over the skyline as emergency services battled blazes and evacuated the wounded.

Russian authorities described the operation as a “massive strike” using high-precision long-range weapons aimed at military and energy targets. 

Ukrainian officials, however, insist the barrage deliberately hit civilian areas, a pattern that has drawn repeated international condemnation.

This latest escalation comes as the grinding war — now in its fourth year — shows no signs of abating. 

Families in Kyiv awoke to the familiar terror of explosions, a reality that has become tragically routine for millions. 

Emergency responders, working through the night, pulled survivors from ruined apartment buildings while hospitals treated the injured, including young children.

The attack underscores the vulnerability of urban centers even as both sides claim advances elsewhere on the front. 

It also highlights the limitations of air defenses against saturation barrages combining drones with sophisticated missiles.

As smoke still rises over parts of the capital, the assault serves as a stark reminder of the human toll of this conflict. 

Zelenskyy and Ukrainian leaders have repeatedly called for stronger international support, including enhanced air defense systems, to protect civilians. 

For ordinary Ukrainians, nights like this erode any sense of normalcy, turning homes into potential targets in a war that began with Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022.

While diplomatic efforts flicker intermittently, the immediate focus remains on recovery: accounting for the dead, treating the wounded, and restoring power and services to affected neighborhoods. 

In the words of weary residents emerging at dawn, the resilience of Kyiv’s people endures — but so does the unrelenting violence.

This tragedy adds to the mounting civilian casualties that continue to define one of Europe’s deadliest conflicts since World War II. 

Global attention, once intense, must not waver as the war grinds on with no clear end in sight.
