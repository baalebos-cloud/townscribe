**Abuja, Nigeria—November 2, 2026**—Dino Melaye, Deputy Director General of the African Democratic Congress (ADC) Presidential Campaign Council, has issued a strong warning that his party will not tolerate continued intimidation by the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) ahead of the 2027 general elections.

Speaking in a recent interview, the former Kogi West senator declared that the ADC is closely monitoring the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) *“back to back”* and is prepared to defend itself if necessary.

*“We are following INEC back to back!! All Die Na Die. We will defend ourselves in 2027. The ADC will not allow the APC to continue intimidation,”* Melaye said.



**Reference to Past Violence and Street Action**

Melaye recalled the violence that marred the Osun State election, stating that over 50 people died during that process. He used the moment to send a direct message to President Bola Tinubu and the APC.

*“In the Osun election, over 50 people died. We are announcing to Tinubu and the APC that this time around, we will defend ourselves. We will not go to court; we will settle the matter on the streets,”* he declared.

The comments reflect growing tension within opposition ranks as preparations for the 2027 polls intensify. Melaye, a former Peoples Democratic Party senator who later joined the ADC, has become one of the party’s most vocal critics of both the ruling party and the electoral umpire.



**Criticism of INEC and Voter Register Concerns**

In the same interview, Melaye raised serious concerns about the integrity of INEC’s voter registration process. He claimed the register failed an integrity test, with an alleged 89 percent indication of artificial intelligence (AI) imputation through repeated photographs.

He cited a specific example from Edo State, where a polling unit that previously recorded just over 400 voters during the election that produced the current governor now allegedly lists more than 4,000 voters—an increase of over 1,000 percent without any corresponding population explosion in the area.

These remarks come against a backdrop of wider controversy surrounding AI-generated photographs discovered in INEC’s preliminary voters’ register and complaints from political parties about the omission of some candidates from the commission’s final list.



**Broader Political Context**

Melaye’s hardline stance fits into a pattern of increasingly confrontational rhetoric from opposition figures as the 2027 election cycle approaches. 

The ADC has previously accused INEC of self-sabotage and laying the groundwork for possible postponement of the polls through repeated administrative failures.

Analysts note that Melaye’s language—particularly the phrase *“All Die Na Die”* and the threat of street-level confrontation—signals a willingness by some opposition elements to escalate beyond legal challenges if they perceive the process as compromised.

The ruling APC has not yet issued a formal response to Melaye’s latest comments. However, the party has consistently maintained that it is committed to free, fair and credible elections under the supervision of INEC.

As political temperatures rise, the coming months are expected to feature sharper exchanges between the ruling party and opposition groups, with the credibility of the electoral process remaining a central flashpoint.
