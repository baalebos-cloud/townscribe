**Kaduna, Nigeria – July 8, 2026** — In a powerful demonstration of unity and determination, Governor Uba Sani of Kaduna State is today hosting an extraordinary joint meeting of the Northern States Governors’ Forum (NSGF) and Northern Traditional Leaders Council at the Sir Kashim Ibrahim House, the Kaduna State Government House.

Chaired by Gombe State Governor and NSGF Chairman Muhammadu Inuwa Yahaya, the high-level session brings together governors, eminent traditional rulers led by the Sultan of Sokoto, and senior representatives from Nigeria’s security architecture. 

Attendees include delegates from the National Security Adviser’s office, the Minister of Defence, the Inspector-General of Police, the Director-General of the Department of State Services (DSS), former Inspectors-General, retired military and paramilitary officers of Northern extraction, and former Secretaries to the Government of the Federation.
This gathering stands out for its focus on practical solutions to the region’s persistent security threats—banditry, terrorism, kidnappings, and related crimes that have long tested communities across Northern Nigeria. 

Past joint meetings have emphasized the need for state police, enhanced intelligence sharing, and community-led initiatives; today’s extraordinary session builds on that momentum with broader stakeholder involvement.

Governor Uba Sani, as chief host, has positioned the meeting as a platform for decisive action rather than mere deliberation. 

By convening both political and traditional leadership alongside federal security operatives and seasoned retirees, the forum leverages diverse expertise and cultural influence—key assets in addressing root causes that go beyond military responses alone.

Traditional rulers, whose influence resonates deeply in Northern communities, play a pivotal role in intelligence gathering, community mobilization, and fostering trust between citizens and authorities. 

Their participation underscores a holistic approach: combining governance, cultural authority, and professional security strategies.

Northern Nigeria has faced escalating insecurity for years, affecting agriculture, education, economic development, and daily life. 

Previous forums have advocated for state policing reforms and a Northern Security Framework, highlighting slow implementation as a hurdle. 

Today’s meeting signals renewed urgency and potential breakthroughs, including discussions around the Northern Nigeria Security Fund.

For residents, this collaboration offers tangible hope. Successful outcomes could lead to improved coordination, faster response times to threats, and long-term investments in human security—factors that strengthen livelihoods and reduce vulnerability.

- Unity Across Tiers: Federal, state, and traditional institutions aligning demonstrates a whole-of-society commitment rarely seen at this scale.
- Experienced Voices: Inclusion of retired security leaders brings institutional memory and pragmatic insights often missing in high-stakes discussions.
- Regional Stability: Progress in the North ripples across Nigeria, supporting national goals under President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s administration for peace and development.

As the meeting unfolds, expectations are high for concrete deliverables—actionable resolutions, strengthened partnerships, and a clear roadmap forward. In a region yearning for relief, this gathering represents more than protocol: it embodies leadership willing to confront challenges collectively for the greater good.

Stakeholders and citizens alike will watch closely for follow-through. In an era where security underpins every facet of progress, today’s deliberations in Kaduna could mark a turning point toward a more secure and prosperous Northern Nigeria.
