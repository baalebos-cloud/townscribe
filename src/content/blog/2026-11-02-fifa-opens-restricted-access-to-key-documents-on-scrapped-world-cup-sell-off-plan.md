**Zurich/Paris—November 2, 2026**—FIFA has agreed to grant its Council members limited access to key documents relating to the abandoned FIFA Forward Enterprise (FFE) project, the controversial plan that sought to create a $20 billion commercial vehicle for the World Cup and other major tournaments.

In a letter sent on Monday by Secretary General Mattias Grafström, Council members were invited to register their interest by November 16 if they wish to examine the materials in person at FIFA’s headquarters in Zurich. Access will be tightly controlled under “*strict review protocols to preserve confidentiality.”* 

There is no indication yet of which specific documents will be made available, and members will not be permitted to remove copies.

The move comes on the same day a UEFA deadline expired for FIFA to answer more than 50 questions about governance, the FFE scheme, the FIFA Peace Prize, and other recent controversies surrounding President Gianni Infantino’s leadership.



**Background to the FFE Controversy**

The FIFA Forward Enterprise proposal, first made public in late July, aimed to move FIFA’s commercial and event operations into a new subsidiary. Private investors would have taken a minority stake—valued at approximately $20 billion—while FIFA retained majority control. 

The plan promised significantly increased development funding for member associations but triggered an immediate and fierce backlash.

Three continental confederations rejected the idea, and UEFA threatened a boycott of FIFA competitions. Within days, Infantino was forced to scrap the project entirely, describing it as having created divisions that no longer served the original purpose of uniting and strengthening football worldwide.

Critics questioned the valuation process, the decision to grant exclusivity to Thrive Capital (an investment firm founded by Joshua Kushner, brother of Jared Kushner), the speed of the proposal’s development, and whether Infantino stood to gain personally. 

UEFA has pursued legal avenues in the United States seeking documents that could support a potential criminal complaint in Switzerland alleging financial mismanagement.



**UEFA’s Push and the Election Clock**

European football’s governing body continues to seek a candidate to challenge Infantino in the FIFA presidential election scheduled for March 2027 in Morocco. 

The deadline for candidates to declare is November 18—just two days after the final registration date for viewing the FFE documents.

UEFA and its allies are understood to be close to identifying a challenger, though no name has been confirmed. Infantino has repeatedly stated his intention to stand for a further term, and he retains support from several confederations, including CAF and CONMEBOL, as well as key nations such as Saudi Arabia.

FIFA has described UEFA’s two-week deadline for answers as unfeasible, citing ongoing work on a new youth World Cup in Azerbaijan, preparations for an extraordinary congress on 23 November to decide hosts for the 2031 and 2035 Women’s World Cups, and legal constraints arising from U.S. court proceedings.



**What Happens Next**

Council members who travel to Zurich will view the documents under conditions designed to protect confidentiality. 

Whether the limited access satisfies those demanding full transparency remains to be seen. FIFA has indicated that a broader response to the Council on the full range of questions raised by UEFA will be provided “in due course.”

The episode has intensified scrutiny of Infantino’s leadership style and decision-making processes at a critical moment, less than five months before the election that will determine who guides world football into the 2030 World Cup cycle and beyond.

For now, the restricted document viewing represents a partial concession in a high-stakes governance battle that shows little sign of cooling.
