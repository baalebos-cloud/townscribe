**MAIDUGURI, Borno State** — In a significant breakthrough for security forces battling insurgency in Nigeria’s Northeast, troops of Operation Hadin Kai have arrested a suspected logistics supplier to the Islamic State West Africa Province (ISWAP). 

The suspect was caught attempting to move a substantial quantity of pharmaceutical drugs into a volatile area of Borno State.

According to security reports, the arrest occurred around 11:00 a.m. on Thursday, July 10, 2026, during a routine security screening by intelligence personnel from the 18 Battalion at the Molai Main Entrance Gate on the outskirts of Maiduguri. 

The suspect, identified as Mohammed Goni, aged 31, was traveling in a vehicle heading towards Damboa when troops conducted a search.

The search uncovered a large consignment of assorted pharmaceutical drugs. Goni could not provide any valid clearance or documentation authorizing the movement of medical supplies into the conflict-prone region, raising immediate suspicions. 

He was promptly detained for further interrogation.

Military sources confirmed that the suspect has been handed over to the Military Intelligence Brigade. Investigators are working to trace the source of the drugs, their intended destination, and any direct connections to ISWAP’s logistics and supply networks operating in the Lake Chad region.

Terrorist groups like ISWAP increasingly rely on clandestine supply chains to obtain pharmaceuticals and other essentials for treating wounded fighters and sustaining operations in remote camps. 

This latest arrest underscores the military’s proactive, intelligence-led strategy to disrupt these networks before supplies reach insurgent enclaves.

Photographs released in connection with the incident show the suspect in custody holding boxes of medical supplies, highlighting the tangible results of heightened vigilance at key entry points around Maiduguri.

This development comes amid ongoing efforts by the Nigerian military’s Operation Hadin Kai to stabilize Borno and surrounding states. 

The operation continues to record successes in neutralizing threats, intercepting supplies, and degrading the operational capabilities of ISWAP and other terrorist factions in the Northeast.

Security experts note that intercepting logistics — whether weapons, fuel, food, or medical items — is often as critical as direct combat engagements. 

Pharmaceutical drugs, in particular, can prolong the fighting capacity of insurgents by enabling treatment of battlefield injuries far from formal medical facilities.

While investigations are still ongoing, the arrest of Mohammed Goni sends a clear message: movement into and around active conflict zones is under close scrutiny, and attempts to support terrorist networks carry severe consequences.

As Nigerian troops maintain pressure on ISWAP remnants, incidents like this reinforce public confidence in the armed forces’ commitment to dismantling support systems that keep insurgency alive. 

Further updates on the suspect’s interrogation and any recoveries or linkages are expected as military intelligence deepens its probe.

The people of Borno and the wider Northeast continue to hope that sustained operations, combined with community vigilance and international partnerships, will eventually restore lasting peace to a region long scarred by violence.
