**Abuja, Nigeria – September 1, 2026** — Presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), Mr. Peter Obi, CON, on Tuesday held discussions with officials of the Canadian High Commission in Nigeria, focusing on the forthcoming 2027 general elections, democratic institutions, trade, investment, education and greater women’s participation in politics.

The meeting, held at the invitation of the Canadian mission, brought Obi face-to-face with the head of the commission and his team. 

According to Obi’s account, talks centred on Canada’s continued support for strengthening Nigeria’s democratic processes, alongside practical areas of bilateral cooperation.

*“I reiterated my conviction that a well-governed, peaceful, stable and prosperous Nigeria is firmly in Canada’s strategic interest,”* Obi stated. 

He added that a Nigeria that works for its people would unlock the country’s potential and open deeper partnership opportunities between Nigeria, Canada and the wider African continent.

Among those who accompanied Obi was young Nigerian Zekeri Idris Jnr, who later described the engagement as an honour and used the moment to underscore Obi’s longstanding message on youth leadership. 

In a detailed post, Idris Jnr said Obi has consistently called for young Nigerians to step forward, take responsibility and begin assuming leadership roles now rather than in some distant future.

He pointed to Obi’s familiar mantra — *“A child of nobody will become somebody without knowing anybody*” — as the guiding principle for the kind of Nigeria the candidate seeks to build: one where competence outweighs connections and young people can rise without the need for political godfathers.

The presence of young aides at such diplomatic engagements reflects a recurring theme in Obi’s public messaging: the deliberate elevation of competent young Nigerians into meaningful roles. 

Supporters argue this approach signals a break from traditional patronage politics and aligns with broader calls for generational renewal ahead of the 2027 polls.

Official reports confirm the discussions also touched on economic development and affirmative steps to boost women’s representation in governance — issues that have featured regularly in Obi’s recent international and domestic engagements.

As Nigeria moves closer to another high-stakes electoral cycle, meetings of this nature between opposition figures and foreign missions continue to attract attention. 

For Obi’s supporters, the Canadian High Commission visit offers both diplomatic substance and a symbolic reminder of the candidate’s emphasis on inclusion, competence and a politics that prioritises results over connections.

A new Nigeria, they insist, remains possible.
