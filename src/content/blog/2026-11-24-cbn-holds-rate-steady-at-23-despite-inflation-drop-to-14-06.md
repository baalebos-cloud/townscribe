The Central Bank of Nigeria has retained its benchmark Monetary Policy Rate at 23 percent, even as headline inflation continued its downward path to 14.06 percent in October.

The decision emerged from the 308th meeting of the Monetary Policy Committee, held on 23 and 24 November 2026 in Abuja and announced by Governor Olayemi Cardoso.

In its communiqué, the committee stressed that the hold was necessary *“to sustain the progress made in reducing inflation and keep inflation expectations under control.”* Headline inflation has now moderated for five consecutive months, falling from 14.63 per cent in September.

Core inflation eased more sharply to 10.78 per cent from 12.95 per cent, while food inflation declined to 19.13 per cent from 19.59 per cent, helped by the harvest season and lower prices for processed foods and farm produce.

The committee left other key parameters unchanged. The asymmetric corridor around the MPR stays at +50/-300 basis points. The Cash Reserve Requirement remains 45 per cent for deposit money banks, 16 per cent for merchant banks, and 75 per cent for non-Treasury Single Account public-sector deposits. 

These settings continue the relatively tight stance adopted after the September cut that brought the rate down from 26.5 per cent.

Cardoso and his colleagues acknowledged the improving backdrop. The composite Purchasing Managers’ Index rose slightly to 53.2 in October, signalling ongoing expansion across industry, agriculture, and services. Gross external reserves stood at $54.02 billion as of 17 November, covering more than ten months of imports. 

A new memorandum of understanding between the CBN and the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited is expected to bolster foreign-exchange inflows and support energy-related projects.

Yet the committee flagged clear upside risks. Ongoing conflict in the Middle East could keep global energy prices elevated. 

Seasonal demand from the festive period and spending linked to the approaching election cycle may also rekindle price pressures. 

Members judged that the current policy stance remains appropriate to protect the gains already achieved and to prevent a reversal in inflation expectations.

Looking ahead, the CBN expressed cautious optimism for the rest of 2026 and into 2027, pointing to improved crude-oil production and resilience in key sectors. It pledged to keep future decisions data-driven and focused on price stability. The next MPC meeting is scheduled for 22 and 23 February 2027.

For households and businesses, the hold means borrowing costs are unlikely to ease in the near term. 

For policymakers, it signals a preference for consolidating hard-won progress on inflation rather than rushing further rate reductions while external and domestic uncertainties persist.
