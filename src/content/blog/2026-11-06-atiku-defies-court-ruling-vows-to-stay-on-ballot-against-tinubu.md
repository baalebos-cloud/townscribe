Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has rejected a Federal High Court judgment that nullified his nomination as the African Democratic Congress presidential candidate, declaring that no court ruling can erase the will of the Nigerian people or remove him from the 2027 ballot.

In a strongly worded statement issued on Friday, Atiku said the latest court decision should not be mistaken for the people’s verdict. 

*“Let no one mistake the latest court ruling for the will of the Nigerian people. No manipulation, conjecture, or concoction can erase our place in this contest, silence our campaign, or deny Nigerians the right to choose their future. We are on the ballot, and the people must be allowed to decide,”* he stated.

The judgment, delivered on Thursday by Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court in Abuja, sacked the Senator David Mark-led caretaker leadership of the ADC and nullified all actions taken under it, including the primary that produced Atiku as the party’s flag bearer. 

The suit was filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former National Deputy Chairman of the party. The ruling has created fresh uncertainty for the opposition party less than three months before the presidential election scheduled for 16 January 2027.

Atiku accused the ruling All Progressives Congress of using political pressure, manufactured narratives and courtroom tactics to weaken the opposition and push Nigeria towards a one-party state. 

He claimed the government was also attempting to compromise the voters’ register with AI-generated photographs, false names and fictitious identities. 

*“These are not the actions of people confident in their record,” he said. “They are calculated attempts to make genuine competition impossible.”*

The former Vice President launched a blistering attack on the Tinubu administration’s economic record. He blamed the government for removing the fuel subsidy overnight without adequate protection for citizens, overseeing the steep decline of the naira, and allowing food prices to rise beyond the reach of ordinary families. 

Factories and small businesses have shut down, young people are leaving the country in search of hope, and bandits and kidnappers continue to hold communities hostage, he argued.

*“Look around you. Market stalls stand empty. Feeding your family has become a daily struggle. Fuel prices keep rising, and silent pain fills homes across the country,”* Atiku said. 

*“Those in power may want you to believe that your suffering does not matter. They may want you to surrender your future before you even reach the ballot box. Do not lose heart. Do not retreat. Your vote is your power, and together, we will take our country back.”*

He reaffirmed his commitment to the race. *“I am in this race. I am not going anywhere. We will be on the ballot. No threat, blackmail, or court maneuver will make me abandon you or the cause we share.”* 

Atiku vowed to resist every attempt to turn Nigeria into a one-party state and insisted that democracy must remain open to more than one party and more than one voice.

The statement comes just days after Atiku flagged off his presidential campaign in Sokoto with a large crowd, followed by activities in Kebbi State. 

His campaign has focused heavily on the cost-of-living crisis under the banner of making life affordable again. 

The ADC has already indicated it will appeal the judgment and seek a stay of execution while the legal battle continues.

Atiku ended on a defiant note: “Bola cannot use the courts to escape the judgment of Nigerians at the ballot box.”
