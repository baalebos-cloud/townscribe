**Lagos, Nigeria – July 8, 2026** — Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) for the 2027 election and former Anambra State governor, has raised serious alarms about alleged targeted harassment by the current administration, suggesting it could even endanger his life and derail his political future.

In a wide-ranging, emotionally charged interview with media personality Chude Jideonwo on the popular **WithChude** podcast, Obi painted a picture of daily frustrations that extend beyond politics into his personal and business life. 

“*The way they are going now, I might not even be alive,” he stated bluntly, adding that “every single thing I do for a living, this government is frustrating it, deliberately so.”*

Obi described what he sees as subtle but pervasive interference, claiming the government avoids overt actions while leaving visible fingerprints across opposition activities. 

He alleged personal targeting, including denial of entitlements and broader attacks on anyone associated with opposition parties.

A striking example he shared involved a recent incident at an airport, where workers allegedly locked his car despite his identification. “***I said it’s me. The person said he doesn’t care,***” Obi recounted. 

He contrasted this with other vehicles left unhindered and observed staff whispering as if questioning his identity. Such episodes, he suggested, reflect deeper societal division and fear.

He further noted that acquaintances at the airport now avoid greeting him out of fear of becoming targets themselves, and even wedding invitations come with pleas for him not to attend. *“We have divided the country, breeding hatred among u*s,” Obi lamented.


Despite the frustrations, Obi struck a hopeful tone, emphasizing his vision for a reunited Nigeria. He reiterated his commitment to fostering unity where division exists, promoting love, compassion, and care — values he believes have eroded under current pressures.

This interview comes months after Obi was officially named the NDC’s presidential flagbearer at the party’s special convention in May 2026. 

He is positioning himself as a strong contender in what is shaping up to be a rematch of key 2023 figures, facing incumbent President Bola Tinubu of the APC and others in the opposition space.

Peter Gregory Obi, born July 19, 1961, in Onitsha, Anambra State, built a successful career in banking before entering politics. 

As Anambra governor (2006–2014, with interruptions due to impeachment and reinstatement), he gained recognition for prudent fiscal management, education reforms, and security improvements, often cited as a model of efficient governance.

His 2023 presidential run under the Labour Party mobilized a massive youth-driven “Obidient” movement, securing strong support in key regions despite the final outcome. 

Now with the NDC, Obi continues to advocate for issue-based politics focused on security, economic revival, youth empowerment, and anti-corruption.

The latest remarks have sparked widespread reactions across Nigerian media and social platforms, with supporters viewing them as evidence of political persecution and critics questioning the claims amid ongoing governance debates. 

As the 2027 election cycle heats up, such personal testimonies highlight the intense pressures on opposition figures and the polarized state of Nigerian politics.

Obi’s message remains one of resilience: a call to transcend division and rebuild a compassionate, unified nation.
