**ABUJA, Nigeria**— In a bold and timely intervention that has sent ripples across Nigeria’s political landscape, Peter Obi, the 2023 Labour Party Presidential Candidate and current 2027 Presidential Candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has publicly called on President Bola Tinubu to either resign or abandon any plans for re-election in 2027.

Obi’s strong remarks, widely reported on Monday, July 6, 2026, center on the administration’s failure to curb the country’s escalating insecurity and what he described as a troubling lack of empathy toward victims of violence and their families. 

The statement comes as banditry, kidnappings, and other violent crimes continue to claim lives and displace communities across multiple states.

Nigeria has grappled with complex security challenges for years, but many analysts and citizens argue the situation has deteriorated under the current government despite promises of improved safety. 

Obi’s intervention highlights the human cost — families mourning loved ones, communities living in fear, and a pervasive sense that leadership has not matched the urgency of the crisis.

As the NDC flagbearer for 2027, Obi has positioned himself as a consistent voice for good governance, accountability, and people-centered leadership. 

His latest call underscores a central theme in his political messaging: leaders must demonstrate both competence and compassion, especially during times of national distress.

The statement has ignited passionate debate online and in political circles. Supporters praise Obi for speaking truth to power and amplifying the suffering of ordinary Nigerians. 

Critics, however, accuse him of politicizing security issues and question whether his own proposed solutions would deliver faster results in a complex federation.

With the 2027 presidential race already heating up, Obi’s intervention sets a sharp tone. 

As the NDC candidate (often aligned with broader opposition sentiments), he continues to challenge the ruling APC on key performance indicators — security, economy, and governance transparency.

Insecurity remains one of Nigeria’s most pressing challenges, affecting investment, agriculture, education, and daily life. 

Obi’s demand for accountability reflects growing public frustration and the expectation that leaders prioritize citizens’ safety over political survival.

As a veteran journalist who has covered Nigeria’s turbulent political journey for decades, one thing is clear: such high-stakes calls rarely go unanswered. 

They test institutional resilience, opposition strength, and the ruling party’s ability to deliver tangible results before voters head to the polls in 2027.

Whether President Tinubu’s team responds with concrete security reforms or dismisses the criticism as electioneering will shape the narrative in the months ahead. 

For millions of Nigerians yearning for peace and progress, the conversation Obi has reignited is long overdue.
