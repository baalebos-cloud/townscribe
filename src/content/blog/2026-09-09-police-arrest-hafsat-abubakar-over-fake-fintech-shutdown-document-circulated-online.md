**Abuja, Nigeria—September 9, 2026**—The Nigeria Police Force National Cybercrime Centre (NPF-NCCC) has arrested a suspect identified as Hafsat Abubakar for allegedly creating and circulating a forged document that falsely claimed a fintech company was shutting down its operations.

Force Public Relations Officer Anietie Iniedu disclosed the development during a news conference in Abuja. 

He explained that investigators deployed digital forensic tools to trace the publication of the fabricated document to an X (formerly Twitter) handle operated by the suspect. Abubakar was arrested on September 4, 2026.



**Threat of Digitally Fabricated Information**

Iniedu stressed that the case underscores the growing danger posed by digitally manufactured content. Such false reports, he noted, are capable of triggering public panic, disrupting financial activities, and causing serious reputational harm to legitimate businesses in Nigeria’s fast-growing fintech sector.

The forged document reportedly suggested the unnamed fintech company was ceasing operations. Authorities say the material was designed and shared in a manner that could mislead customers, investors, and the general public.



**How the Investigation Unfolded**

According to the police, the NPF-NCCC received information about the circulating false report and immediately activated its technical capabilities. 

Through digital forensics and open-source intelligence methods, officers linked the content to Abubakar’s social media activity. The arrest followed the successful tracing of the online publication.

The centre has not released further personal details about the suspect or named the specific fintech firm involved, citing ongoing investigations. 

Officials indicated that the matter will proceed through the appropriate legal channels once inquiries are completed.



**Broader Context of Cybercrime Enforcement**

This arrest forms part of the NPF-NCCC’s wider efforts to combat various forms of cyber-enabled crime, including the spread of harmful false information that can undermine public confidence in digital financial services. 

Nigeria’s fintech industry has expanded rapidly in recent years, making it particularly vulnerable to reputational attacks and coordinated disinformation campaigns.

Police leadership has repeatedly urged members of the public to verify information before sharing content online, especially materials that could affect financial institutions or market stability. 

The Force also continues to encourage citizens to report suspicious online activities through official channels.

As the investigation continues, authorities are expected to provide additional updates on the case and any potential charges against the suspect. 

The development serves as a timely reminder of the real-world consequences of circulating unverified or fabricated digital content in an increasingly connected economy.
