**IBADAN, Oyo State** — Calm has returned to the Tudun Wuli area of Ojoo in Ibadan following a volatile communal clash between indigenes and settlers that claimed lives, destroyed property, and temporarily shut down a major federal highway. 

Security operatives acted swiftly to restore order in this bustling part of Oyo State.

The incident erupted on the evening of July 8, 2026, after a disagreement reportedly escalated into full-blown violence. Irate youths allegedly attacked a settlement occupied by northern residents, setting several makeshift structures ablaze. 

The unrest forced the temporary closure of the busy Ibadan-Oyo Federal Highway, disrupting vehicular movement and commercial activities in the area.

Multiple accounts indicate the violence was triggered by the alleged killing of a commercial tricycle (Keke) rider during a dispute over a Point-of-Sale (POS) transaction the previous night. 

This incident quickly snowballed, drawing in rival groups and leading to further casualties, with reports confirming between two and four deaths and several injuries. 

Some descriptions frame it as a clash involving Yoruba traders and Hausa scavengers or settlers.

Security forces, including troops from the Nigerian Army, Nigeria Police Force (NPF), Department of State Services (DSS), Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC), and the Oyo State Amotekun network, were rapidly deployed. 

They dispersed the hoodlums, restored law and order, and prevented further escalation.

Officials from the Federal and Oyo State Fire Services responded promptly to extinguish the fires, stopping them from spreading to nearby properties. 

As of the latest updates, normal vehicular and commercial activities have resumed along the highway, with a visible security presence maintained to deter any recurrence.

Oyo State Government officials, including the Deputy Governor, visited the scene for an on-the-spot assessment. 

The state has appealed for calm, launched a full-scale investigation into the root causes, and vowed to bring perpetrators to justice while emphasizing the need to prevent the incident from snowballing into broader ethnic or tribal conflict.

In a positive development, leaders from both Yoruba and Hausa communities in Ibadan have pledged to work together to restore lasting peace. 

They expressed support for government efforts to de-escalate tensions and address underlying grievances in the diverse Ojoo area.

Communal clashes of this nature, often rooted in minor disputes that spiral due to underlying socio-economic tensions, highlight the fragile balance in mixed communities across Nigeria. 

Ojoo, a key commercial and transit hub in Ibadan, serves as a melting pot of cultures and economic activities. 

Incidents like this not only cause immediate loss of life and property but also risk damaging the state’s reputation as a relatively stable commercial center under Governor Seyi Makinde’s administration.

Security analysts note that proactive multi-agency collaboration, as demonstrated here, remains crucial in containing such flare-ups. 

Ongoing investigations will likely focus on the initial POS-related killing, the role of criminal elements, and any ethnic dimensions to ensure justice and reconciliation.

As residents return to their daily routines, the emphasis now shifts to addressing root causes—from youth unemployment and petty disputes to stronger community mediation mechanisms—to safeguard peace in Ibadan and beyond.

The swift restoration of normalcy offers relief, but the human cost serves as a sobering reminder of the need for vigilance, dialogue, and inclusive governance in Nigeria’s diverse urban centers. Authorities continue to monitor the situation closely.
