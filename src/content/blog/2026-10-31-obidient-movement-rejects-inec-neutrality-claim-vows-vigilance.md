**Abuja, Nigeria—October 30, 2026**—The official handle of the Obidient Movement has dismissed assurances by the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) of its neutrality and commitment to free and fair elections, declaring that *“no Nigerian believes you”* and pledging to remain vigilant ahead of the 2027 general elections.

The reaction came after INEC Chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan, SAN, received the ECOWAS Pre-Election Fact-Finding Mission led by former Sierra Leonean Vice-President Victor Bockarie Foh at the Commission’s headquarters in Abuja.



**INEC’s Assurances to ECOWAS**

Amupitan told the regional delegation that Nigeria’s National Register of Voters has surpassed 103 million. He said the Commission had completed nine of the 14 major statutory milestones in its revised timetable, including three phases of Continuous Voter Registration and the nationwide physical display of the Preliminary Register of Voters.

He reaffirmed plans to deploy the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System (BVAS) for biometric accreditation, electronic result management systems, and the new eEC8A electronic verification platform to complement the INEC Result Viewing Portal (IReV). 

The Chairman stressed that INEC has *“no candidate and no political party,”* insisting its sole allegiance is to Nigerians and the rule of law.

The ECOWAS mission is assessing electoral preparedness in line with regional protocols on democracy and good governance.



**Obidient Movement’s Sharp Response**

In a strongly worded post, the Obidient Movement HQ stated: *“INEC has never been committed to free and fair elections. Never. So, dear @inecnigeria, no Nigerian believes you. We will all stay vigilant to ensure you do the right thing. Nothing will be taken for granted this time.”*

The group specifically questioned the Commission’s comfort with introducing artificial intelligence tools not recognized by the Electoral Act while “**vehemently refusing”** electronic or instant transmission of results. 

It also asked for an update on “***AI voters”*** and warned that Nigerians would resist any perceived plans to undermine the polls.

The statement reflects lingering distrust among sections of the opposition and civil society over electoral integrity, particularly following controversies around previous elections and recent concerns about the accuracy of the expanded voter register.

INEC has maintained that AI tools will only support logistics, result verification, and cybersecurity under strict human oversight and will not determine election outcomes.

As preparations intensify for the January 16, 2027 presidential and National Assembly elections, the exchange underscores the high stakes and deep polarization surrounding Nigeria’s next democratic contest.



***This report is based on the official INEC statement and the response published by the Obidient Movement HQ on October 30, 2026.***
