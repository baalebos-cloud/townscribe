### **Abuja | November 26, 2026—Grief** gave way to solemn national pride on Thursday as President Bola Ahmed Tinubu led the Armed Forces, lawmakers and bereaved families in honoring the 25 gallant souls lost in the Nigerian Air Force NAF 931 aircraft accident in Ondo State.

As captured by **ARISE NEWS** in its live coverage of the Air Force Memorial, the President announced: *“I approve the allocation of 3 bedroom flats in the Renewed Hope Housing Project in Abuja and N100 Million to the family of each of the military personnel who lost their lives.”*

The memorial service and wreath-laying ceremony, held at the National Military Cemetery, Abuja, was in honour of the victims of the NAF ATR-42 aircraft, call sign NAF 931, which crashed near Igbokoda in the Ilaje Local Government Area of Ondo State on October 5, 2026. 

The Nigerian Air Force confirmed all 25 people on board—five crew members and 20 passengers, including officers, airmen, airwomen, and civilians—died in the tragedy.

#### A Nation Must Give Its Best Wings

In his address quoted by ARISE TV, Tinubu, as Commander-in-Chief, reaffirmed his administration’s commitment to a professional, well-equipped, and mission-ready Armed Forces.

*“As Commander-in-Chief, I reaffirm my administration’s commitment to professional, well-equipped, and mission-ready armed forces. A nation that asks its sons and daughters to fly into danger must give them the best wings it can build,”* the President declared.

According to the Statehouse Press Release by Special Adviser Bayo Onanuga, Tinubu said the three-bedroom flats in Karsana, Abuja, and N100 million per family are a tangible expression of national gratitude. “*No home can ever replace the one you have lost.* 

*But they gave their lives to Nigeria, and Nigeria will give their families a home,”* he said, extending the gesture to families of civilians on board.

The President described the loss as a painful national wound—parents who will not see their children grow, newlyweds whose unions had just begun, and patriots who carried dreams in their hearts and the Nigerian flag on their shoulders.

He assured a thorough, transparent investigation to unravel the crash circumstances and strengthen military and civilian aviation safety, operational procedures, and institutional accountability. “We owe them the truth,” he vowed.

The solemn event drew Senate President Godswill Akpabio, NSA Nuhu Ribadu, Defence Minister Gen. Christopher Musa (rtd), Ondo Governor Lucky Aiyedatiwa, Chief of Army Staff Lt-Gen. Waidi Shaibu, Chief of Air Staff Air Marshal Sunday Aneke, Naval Chief Vice Adm. Idi Abbas, IGP Tunde Disu, and heads of NIA, DSS, and NEMA.

Air Marshal Aneke pledged a comprehensive probe and presented national flags to next-of-kin, assuring families the Air Force will stand firmly by them.
