**Abuja, Nigeria—November 18, 2026**—The administration of President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has recorded a major milestone in the recovery of stolen public assets, with the Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC) facilitating the return of land capable of supporting more than 900 housing units. 

The achievement was highlighted on Wednesday during the ongoing inaugural General Assembly of the African Asset Recovery Practitioners’ Forum (AARP) in Abuja.

The four-day continental gathering, running from 17 to 20 November 2026 at the Abuja Continental Hotel, is convened under the auspices of the African Union Advisory Board Against Corruption (AUABC) and the African Union Commission. Nigeria is hosting the event through the ICPC. 

The forum, themed ***“From Commitment to Action — Enhancing Africa’s Asset Recovery Architecture through Effective Implementation of the Common African Position on Asset Recovery (CAPAR),”*** brings together practitioners from across the 55 African Union Member States. Participants are focused on translating policy commitments into practical steps for detecting, recovering, managing and monitoring illicit assets.

ICPC Chairman and Chair of the AARP Forum Interim Steering Council, Dr. Musa Adamu Aliyu, SAN, told delegates that every resource lost to corruption represents far more than a financial setback. 

It is a development opportunity denied to ordinary people who need education, healthcare, infrastructure, and housing. 

He stressed that asset recovery must serve as a tool of accountability, national development, and the protection of public resources, with recovered proceeds returned and managed strictly in the public interest.

A flagship example of this approach under the Tinubu administration is the recovery and formal handover of forfeited land in Kaba District, Abuja—the site originally earmarked for the Goodluck Jonathan Estate. Following a final forfeiture order by the Federal High Court, the ICPC returned the property to the Federal Mortgage Bank of Nigeria (FMBN). 

The project had been planned to deliver 962 housing units on approximately 27.92 hectares, financed by a $65 million loan that investigators found was completely diverted, with no houses built. Some of the funds were allegedly channelled through bureaux de change and taken out of the country. 

The court directed that the land be used to complete the housing units and allocate them to the intended beneficiaries, demonstrating how recovered assets can be restored to productive public use.

The AARP Forum itself was established in Nairobi in April 2025 as a practitioner-led platform to strengthen cooperation among African anti-corruption and asset recovery institutions. 

Key expected outcomes from the Abuja assembly include the adoption of a CAPAR Reporting Tool, validation of work plans, and stronger continental commitments to implementation. 

Nigeria’s hosting of the event underscores the country’s growing role in shaping Africa’s collective response to illicit financial flows, which cost the continent an estimated $150 billion annually.

The Tinubu administration, working through the ICPC and sister agencies, continues to prioritize the transparent recovery and productive redeployment of stolen assets. 

Officials say such recoveries directly support national priorities in housing, infrastructure, and broader development for ordinary Nigerians. 

By converting forfeited land into homes for citizens, the government is seeking to turn the proceeds of past wrongdoing into tangible public good.

The recovery of the Kaba District land and its linkage to over 900 planned housing units stands as a concrete illustration of how determined enforcement, judicial cooperation, and inter-agency collaboration can deliver results that improve lives while strengthening the rule of law across Africa.



***This report is based on the official statement issued by the Presidency on 18 November 2026 and related coverage of the African Asset Recovery Practitioners’ Forum.***
