**LAGOS, Nigeria** — In a bold and unsparing interview that has ignited fresh national debate, award-winning rapper, actor, and social commentator Folarin Falana, popularly known as Falz, has delivered one of the sharpest public takedowns of President Bola Tinubu’s administration to date. 

Falz declared that Tinubu *“has nothing to offer” Nigeria and described his famous campaign slogan “Emi L’okan” (“It is my turn”) as the “weirdest*” he has ever heard.

Speaking on Arise Television, the outspoken entertainer questioned the administration’s spending priorities, particularly the acquisition of official vehicles and aircraft at a time when insecurity continues to plague large parts of the country. 

“*If you look at what has been spent on acquiring new vehicles, acquiring jets... and look at what has actually gone on with insecurity... what are your priorities?”* he asked pointedly.

Falz also pushed back against comments attributed to First Lady Oluremi Tinubu suggesting that pressure on the government was excessive and that younger Nigerians and private individuals should step in to help. 

“*What do you mean pressure on the government? Did anyone beg you to take up this role?”* he fired back, emphasizing that leadership comes with the responsibility to deliver without shifting blame.

The rapper reserved special criticism for the **“Emi L’okan**” slogan that became central to Tinubu’s 2023 presidential campaign. 

He found the message not only strange but incompatible with the demands of governance. *“I’ve never seen anything more absurd than the ‘Emi L’okan’ slogan in my entire life,”* Falz stated. 

He argued that anyone who campaigned on the basis of “it is my turn” should not later complain about the pressures of office, as they had actively sought the role.

Falz’s remarks come against the backdrop of persistent economic challenges, including high inflation, fuel price hikes, and widespread insecurity—issues that have dominated public discourse since Tinubu assumed office. 

His comments echo sentiments shared by many young Nigerians who feel disconnected from the current leadership’s direction.

Falz, known for his socially conscious music and consistent advocacy on governance, corruption, and youth issues, has built a reputation for speaking truth to power. 

His latest intervention adds to a growing chorus of voices from the creative industry questioning the government’s performance and priorities.

While supporters of the administration may dismiss the criticism as typical opposition rhetoric from an entertainer, Falz’s influence among young Nigerians makes his words particularly impactful. 

The interview has already generated thousands of reactions online, with many praising his boldness and others accusing him of being overly critical without offering solutions.

As Nigeria navigates complex economic reforms and security threats, voices like Falz’s serve as a mirror to power—reflecting public frustrations and demanding better governance, transparency, and results. 

Whether the administration will engage constructively with such critiques or continue on its current path remains to be seen.

In a democracy, robust public discourse — even when uncomfortable — remains essential for accountability and progress. Falz has thrown down the gauntlet; the ball is now in the court of those in power to respond not just with words, but with visible improvements in the lives of ordinary citizens.
