**Osogbo, Osun State – June 3, 2026,** Governor Ademola Adeleke has launched a sweeping, multi-layered security operation to shield Osun State from rising threats of banditry, kidnapping, and political violence. 

In a proactive move that blends grassroots vigilance with high-level coordination, the governor is leaving no stone unturned to protect lives, schools, and communities across the state. 

In a strongly worded statement issued via his official X account, Governor Adeleke assured residents of his administration’s unwavering commitment. 

“*Securing our schools, our communities and our people is non-negotiable,”* he declared. 

He revealed that over the past few days, he held extensive consultations with both formal and informal security stakeholders to build a robust defense against emerging dangers.

The governor began at the roots of society. He engaged hunters, the Oodua Peoples Congress (OPC), Agbekoya farmers’ group, Amotekun operatives, and various community-based security networks. 

Transitioning swiftly to formal structures, he convened an expanded State Security Council meeting with service commanders from the police, military, DSS, and other agencies. 

This comprehensive activation ensures every layer of Osun’s security architecture now operates in seamless coordination.



**Partnership with Traditional Rulers Strengthened**



Governor Adeleke has also reached out personally to the Osun State Traditional Council of Obas through the Ooni of Ife, His Imperial Majesty Oba Enitan Ogunwusi. 

A major meeting with all traditional rulers is imminent. 

The governor emphasized their vital role: “*They are not just custodians of our culture, they are also the eyes and ears of their domains, and critical partners in community security response.”*

This renewed push comes amid genuine concerns. Recent school abductions in neighboring Oyo State triggered panic, while brutal massacres in Kwara earlier this year forced Osun to tighten border surveillance. 

Porous boundaries and forest routes linking Osun with Kwara, Ekiti, and Oyo remain vulnerable entry points for criminals. 

Reports of suspected bandit threat letters have already caused temporary school closures in some communities, heightening public anxiety.



**Reassuring a Worried Citizenry**



Addressing the people directly, Governor Adeleke delivered a message of vigilance and hope: “To the good people of Osun: we are watching. We are working. And we will not relent. If you see something, say something.”

Analysts view these steps as timely. With the 2026 governorship election approaching, intelligence reports highlight risks of politically motivated thuggery and vandalism. 

Adeleke’s administration has responded by intensifying intelligence gathering, strategic collaboration, and rapid response mechanisms. 

Amotekun and local hunters now form a critical first line of defense, particularly around schools in remote and border areas.

The governor’s hands-on approach reflects his reputation for decisive action. 

By combining modern security agencies with traditional and community structures, Osun aims to create a model of inclusive security governance. 

Residents express cautious optimism, appreciating the transparency even as they demand sustained results on the ground.

As threats from neighboring states persist and election tensions simmer, Adeleke’s proactive campaign sends a clear signal: Osun will not become easy prey for criminals or destabilizing forces. 

The coming weeks will test whether this multi-stakeholder strategy delivers the peace and confidence citizens urgently seek.
