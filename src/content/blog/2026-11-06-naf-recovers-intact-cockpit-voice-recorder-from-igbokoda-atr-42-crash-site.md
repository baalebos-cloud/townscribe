**Abuja, Nigeria—November 6, 2026**—The Nigerian Air Force (NAF) has announced a significant breakthrough in the investigation into the fatal ATR-42 aircraft crash in Igbokoda, Ondo State, with the recovery of the Cockpit Voice Recorder (CVR), confirmed intact by technical teams.

In a statement released on Friday by the Director of Public Relations and Information, Air Commodore Ehimen Ejodame, the NAF said technical teams from the Air Force and the Nigerian Safety Investigation Bureau (NSIB) verified that the CVR was intact. 

Part of the Flight Data Recorder (FDR), commonly known as the black box, has also been retrieved, while efforts continue to locate and recover the remaining portion.

Additional aircraft wreckage, engine components, and human remains have been recovered from the crash site.

 Operations remain ongoing in the challenging swamp terrain as teams work to retrieve outstanding components and facilitate necessary technical examinations.



**Background of the Tragic Incident**

The ATR-42 aircraft, with registration number NAF 931, crashed on October 5, 2026, in a swampy area near Igbokoda in Ilaje Local Government Area of Ondo State. 

The aircraft was on a routine mission from Benin City to Lagos when air traffic controllers lost contact with it at approximately 9:13 a.m.

All 25 people on board — five crew members and 20 passengers — perished in the accident. President Bola Tinubu declared three days of national mourning following the tragedy. 

The victims included Squadron Leader M.I. Aburime (flight captain), Squadron Leader A.P. Man-Ugwueje and Flight Lieutenant I.S. Bako (co-pilots), along with other aircrew and passengers whose identities were released by the NAF shortly after the crash.

The difficult swamp environment has significantly slowed recovery operations over the past month. Specialized equipment, including amphibious excavators and sonar systems deployed by the Nigerian Navy, has been used to navigate the muddy, waterlogged terrain and improve access to submerged wreckage.



**Careful Handling and Ongoing Support**

Ejodame emphasized that the recovery process involves careful attention to the proper handling of human remains. The Directorate of Identification and Classification (DIC) is carrying out the necessary procedures to ensure accurate identification.

The NAF has maintained sustained engagements with the families of the victims, recognizing their need for timely information and support during this difficult period. 

Engagement with relevant authorities on environmental concerns raised by the host community also remains an important part of the broader response.

The investigation is being conducted with due diligence to ensure comprehensive, evidence-based findings. The recovered CVR and FDR are expected to provide critical insights into the final moments of the flight, including cockpit conversations and technical data that will help determine the cause of the accident.

As recovery and analysis continue, the NAF reaffirmed its commitment to transparency and thoroughness in establishing the full circumstances surrounding the loss of its personnel and the civilians on board.
