**IWO, Osun State** — In a tragic turn during what was intended as a routine anti-drug operation, a man was fatally shot at a bustling local market in Osun State, prompting the Nigeria Police Force to launch a full investigation into the circumstances surrounding his death.

The incident occurred on Tuesday, June 9, 2026, at Odo-Ori Market in Iwo Local Government Area. 

Operatives of the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) conducted an enforcement raid targeting suspected drug dealers in the busy trading hub. 

According to security sources, a man identified as Gaji Ibrahim, originally from Ibadan in Oyo State, sustained gunshot injuries during the operation and was later confirmed dead.

Information about the shooting reached the Iwo Divisional Police Headquarters, triggering an immediate deployment of a police team to the scene. 

Officers have begun preliminary investigations to piece together the exact sequence of events. 

Authorities say they are interviewing witnesses and other individuals connected to the incident as part of a *"thorough and professional inquiry."*

A police source emphasized that efforts are underway to establish all relevant facts. 

"The command urged residents to remain calm and allow security agencies to carry out their respective responsibilities while investigations continue," the source added. 

No official statement has yet detailed whether the fatal shot was fired by NDLEA operatives or a suspect or under what conditions the gunfire erupted.

NDLEA operations across Nigeria frequently target drug trafficking networks, which often operate in markets, forests, and urban fringes. 

Osun State has seen previous enforcement actions, including raids on cannabis cultivation sites and arrests of suspects involved in illicit substances. 

Odo-Ori Market, a vibrant commercial center, has occasionally featured in local security reports, though primarily for other incidents like minor disturbances during past lockdowns.

This case highlights the inherent risks and tensions in high-stakes anti-narcotics raids, where split-second decisions can lead to unintended tragedy. 

While NDLEA efforts have yielded significant seizures nationwide, civilian casualties during such operations occasionally raise questions about rules of engagement, coordination with other agencies, and community trust.

For Gaji Ibrahim's family and the people of Iwo, the loss is profoundly personal. 

Markets like Odo-Ori serve not just as economic lifelines but as social hubs where traders, farmers, and residents interact daily. 

A sudden eruption of violence in such a space can send ripples of fear through the community, disrupting livelihoods and eroding confidence in law enforcement operations.

As a veteran journalist covering security matters across Nigeria, one cannot ignore the broader implications: the delicate balance between aggressive drug interdiction and protecting innocent lives.

Families deserve closure, and transparency in the investigation will be crucial to preventing rumors or unrest.

Police have assured the public of their commitment to justice. Senior officers, including those from operations units, have visited the scene and hospitals as part of evidence gathering. 

The outcome of this probe could influence future inter-agency protocols between the NDLEA and police in Osun and beyond.

This story is developing. Updates will depend on official findings from the authorities. 

Residents with information are encouraged to reach out to the Iwo Divisional Police Headquarters or relevant channels to aid the investigation. 

In the meantime, the focus remains on accountability, healing for those affected, and ensuring that the fight against drug-related crime does not come at the cost of public safety.
