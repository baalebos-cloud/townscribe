### **Jos, Plateau State—November 25, 2026—**Barely 24 hours after soldiers died dodging kidnappers on the Jos–Mangu Road, blood has again flowed in Plateau State following the brutal murder of a mining dealer in Jos East Local Government Area.

Security sources told Zagazola Makama, counter-insurgency expert and security analyst, that suspected armed robbers killed Mr. Ahmadu Salmanu, an illegal mining dealer, and carted away mineral products locally known as *kuza* during a midnight raid on his residence.

The attack occurred at about 2:45 a.m. on Monday, November 24, 2026, in Angwan Saki Dauda village, a remote mining settlement notorious for unregulated tin and solid mineral trading.

According to the sources, troops of Sector 1, Sub-Sector 12, Operation Enduring Peace, OPEP, deployed at Kudedu, swiftly responded to distress reports that armed robbers were operating inside the victim’s home.

On arrival, the soldiers met a grim scene: the unidentified gunmen had already shot Salmanu dead at close range before escaping into the darkness with bags of his precious mining products.

Two empty cartridge cases were reportedly recovered at the scene, confirming the use of high-caliber firearms.

The troops, working alongside local hunters who know the rugged terrain, immediately launched a search operation in nearby bushes and hills in an effort to track down the assailants, but no contact was made.

“*The sources said efforts are ongoing to identify and apprehend the perpetrators and recover the stolen mineral products,”* Zagazola reported at 9:18 p.m. on Tuesday, in a post that has drawn 1,587 views.

The killing lays bare the deadly underbelly of illegal mining in Plateau State—a lucrative, organized criminal economy involving local diggers, middlemen, elites, and even foreign buyers, according to ENACT Africa research.

Jos Plateau, home to tin deposits discovered as far back as the 1700s around Kuza river channels, remains Nigeria’s tin capital. Despite a suspension order on illegal activities by the Plateau State Government and repeated shutdowns by OPEP troops, artisanal pits thrive at night, attracting robbers, cultists, and kidnappers.

Only a day earlier, troops shut down another illegal site in the state, during which illegal miners attacked a soldier—a sign of rising tension between security forces and mining communities.

For residents of Angwan Saki Dauda, Salmanu’s death is both a human tragedy and a warning: in Plateau’s shadow, mineral rush, wealth can invite the bullet.

Troops say clearance and manhunt operations continue, while the victim’s family prepares for burial amid calls for stricter regulation of the sector.
