**Abuja—November 9, 2026**—In a significant development in the ongoing criminal trial of human rights activist and African Action Congress (AAC) presidential candidate Omoyele Sowore, the Federal High Court in Abuja has issued a subpoena compelling the Chairman of the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC), Professor Joash Ojo Amupitan, SAN, to produce electoral documents submitted by President Bola Ahmed Tinubu for the 1999 Lagos State governorship election and the 2023 presidential election.

The court, presided over by Justice Mohammed Garba Umar, granted the defence’s request on Monday and directed that a formal ***Subpoena Duces Tecum*** be issued. The order requires Professor Amupitan to appear before the court, produce the specified records, and give testimony on behalf of Sowore, who is the defendant in the case marked FHC/CR/484/2025.

According to court documents made public by Sowore, the INEC chairman must produce two sets of records. 

The first is Tinubu’s INEC Form CF001 (also referred to as Form CF01) submitted when he contested the Lagos State governorship election in 1999, including the accompanying *“Affidavit in Respect of Lost Certificates.”* 

The second is the Form CF001 filed by Tinubu as a presidential candidate in the 2023 general election. A hearing notice was served on Professor Amupitan on Monday, November 9, 2026.

Sowore is being prosecuted by the Department of State Services (DSS) on behalf of the Federal Republic of Nigeria over social media publications in which he described President Tinubu as a “*criminal.”* 

The activist, who is also the publisher of Sahara Reporters, has maintained that his comments were protected speech and has sought to introduce official electoral records as part of his defence strategy.

During Monday’s proceedings, Sowore’s counsel, Ruben Adakole, informed the court of the defence team’s intention to call Professor Amupitan and All Progressives Congress (APC) chieftain Chief Joe Igbokwe as witnesses. Justice Umar granted both requests and ordered that subpoenas be issued. 

The court, however, set aside an earlier attempt by the defence to compel the DSS lead counsel, Akinlolu Kehinde, SAN, to testify, ruling that lawyers cannot be forced to disclose confidential communications with their clients.

Speaking to journalists after the sitting, Sowore confirmed that both the INEC chairman and Igbokwe are expected to appear when the matter resumes. 

He described the development as important to his defence but declined to disclose the full reasons for seeking their testimony at this stage. The case has been adjourned to Wednesday, November 11, 2026, for the appearance of the subpoenaed witnesses.

The request for the 1999 documents is particularly notable because those records have long featured in public debates surrounding Tinubu’s educational and personal history. 

The affidavit relating to lost certificates, submitted more than two decades ago, has been referenced in previous legal and political controversies. 

By placing both the 1999 and 2023 nomination forms before the court, Sowore’s legal team appears to be seeking official verification of the information Tinubu provided to INEC on two separate occasions.

Political observers note that the production of these documents in open court could have wider implications beyond the immediate criminal trial. 

Questions about the consistency of candidate information submitted to INEC have previously featured in election petitions and public discourse. Whether the records will shed new light on those issues remains to be seen, as the court has not yet received or examined the documents.

As of Monday evening, neither INEC nor the Presidency had issued an official response to the subpoena. The Independent National Electoral Commission is expected to comply with the court order in its capacity as the statutory custodian of candidate nomination records.

The trial continues to attract significant public attention, not only because of the high-profile figures involved but also because it touches on the boundaries of free speech, the accountability of public officials, and the transparency of Nigeria’s electoral documentation system. 

For many watching the proceedings, the appearance of the INEC chairman with official records marks a rare moment in which historical candidate filings may be subjected to judicial scrutiny in real time.

Sowore, who is running for president under the AAC platform in 2027, has framed the case as an attempt to silence critical voices. 

The prosecution maintains that the statements in question crossed legal limits. The coming days, with the expected appearance of Professor Amupitan and Joe Igbokwe, are likely to prove pivotal as both sides prepare for the next phase of the trial.



***This report is based on court documents, statements by Omoyele Sowore, and multiple independent news accounts published on November 9, 2026.***
