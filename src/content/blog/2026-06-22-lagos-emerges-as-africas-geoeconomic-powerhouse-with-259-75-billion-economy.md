**Lagos, Nigeria** - In a confident assertion of regional ambition, Lagos State Governor Babajide Sanwo-Olu has positioned Nigeria’s commercial capital as Africa’s preeminent geoeconomic hub, citing its robust $259.75 billion economy — measured on a purchasing power parity (PPP) basis — and a wave of transformative infrastructure investments.

Speaking at the maiden Geo-Economic Optimisation Summit Lagos 2026, organized by the Citadel School of Government in Oregun, Ikeja, Sanwo-Olu painted a vision of Lagos as the “salt and light” of Nigeria’s economy and a dynamic gateway for West Africa and beyond. 

The event, held on Saturday, drew attention to the state’s strategic role in trade, finance, technology, and talent development.

Lagos contributes approximately one-third of Nigeria’s overall Gross Domestic Product, underscoring its outsized influence on the federation. 

On the continental stage, it ranks as Africa’s second-largest metropolitan economy, trailing only Cairo. If treated as an independent nation, Lagos would sit comfortably among Africa’s top economies, ahead of many sovereign states.

Governor Sanwo-Olu highlighted key enablers of this rise: the Lekki Deep Sea Port, the Dangote Refinery, expanding rail networks, and a thriving technology ecosystem. 

These assets, he argued, are positioning Lagos not merely as a Nigerian success story but as a sub-national actor with genuine global ambitions.

The governor, who also chairs the South-West Governors’ Forum, emphasized Lagos’s role in fostering prosperity across Nigeria and the broader West African region through deliberate investments in infrastructure and human capital.

This declaration comes amid Nigeria’s ongoing economic reforms and recovery efforts. Lagos’s projected growth trajectory — with ambitions to push its GDP even higher in coming years — reflects resilience amid national challenges like inflation, infrastructure deficits, and diversification needs away from oil.

Analysts note that Lagos’s success hinges on continued fiscal discipline, public-private partnerships, and addressing urban pressures such as traffic, housing, and inequality. Its status as a magnet for capital, enterprise, and ideas already makes it a bellwether for Africa’s urban economic future.

The summit itself served as a platform for deeper conversations on optimizing Lagos’s geoeconomic potential, with participants exploring opportunities in trade, investment, and regional integration.

Sanwo-Olu’s message carries both pride and a call to action. For Lagos to fully realize its potential as Africa’s geoeconomic powerhouse, sustained investment in education, innovation, security, and inclusive growth will be essential. 

The state’s ability to translate its economic heft into tangible improvements in quality of life for its residents — while serving as an engine for national development — will define its legacy.

As one of Africa’s fastest-evolving megacities, Lagos stands at a pivotal moment. Governor Sanwo-Olu’s optimistic assessment signals confidence that, with the right strategies, the city-state can not only claim its place among global economic hubs but also inspire a new era of African-led prosperity.

Further details from the summit and ongoing economic updates are expected to shed more light on concrete initiatives aimed at cementing this vision. 

For now, Lagos’s momentum offers a compelling narrative of African urban ambition in action.
