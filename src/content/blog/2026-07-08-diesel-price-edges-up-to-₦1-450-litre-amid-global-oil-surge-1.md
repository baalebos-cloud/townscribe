**LAGOS, Nigeria** — In a rapid market response to escalating global energy costs, Nigerian depot owners and petroleum marketers have raised the price of diesel by approximately 3%, pushing the ex-depot price from ₦1,410 per litre on Tuesday to ₦1,450 per litre on Wednesday, July 8, 2026.

The adjustment comes as international benchmark crude prices experienced significant gains. 

Brent crude climbed to $78.36 per barrel (up 5.66%), while West Texas Intermediate (WTI) rose to $74.30 per barrel (up 5.48%), driven by renewed geopolitical uncertainties in the Middle East.

This swift pass-through reflects the deregulated downstream petroleum sector’s sensitivity to global trends. 

Depot owners moved quickly to align with rising import and landing costs, underscoring the interconnected nature of Nigeria’s fuel market even as local refining capacity from facilities like the Dangote Refinery continues to evolve.

For Nigerian businesses and households heavily reliant on diesel for transportation, power generation, and industrial operations, the marginal increase adds to ongoing cost pressures. 

However, analysts note that such adjustments remain relatively moderate compared to previous volatility spikes, and the overall price environment continues to be monitored closely by regulators and consumer groups. 

The development occurs against a backdrop of efforts to stabilize the downstream sector through increased local refining output and policy reforms. 

While global factors dominate short-term pricing, stakeholders continue to advocate for greater self-sufficiency to cushion domestic consumers from international shocks. 

As always, Nigerians are urged to stay informed through official channels and adopt efficient fuel consumption practices amid these fluctuations. 

Market watchers will be tracking whether subsequent global price movements lead to reversals or further adjustments in the coming days.
