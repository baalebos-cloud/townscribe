The Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps on Sunday paraded its suspended Niger State Commandant, Suberu Aniviye, alongside other officers linked to the deaths of 37 suspected illegal miners who died while in the Corps’ custody.

The officers were presented to journalists at the NSCDC National Headquarters in Sauka, Abuja. National Public Relations Officer Assistant Commandant Babawale Afolabi briefed the media on the preliminary findings of the investigation into the incident.

The 37 detainees died in the early hours of Thursday, 17 September 2026. They were among scores of people arrested during anti-illegal mining operations conducted by the NSCDC Niger State Command in the M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto areas of the state on 15 and 16 September. 

Niger State Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago later confirmed that 67 artisanal and suspected illegal miners had been held in the facility before the deaths occurred.

The Corps initially attributed the deaths to a suspected disease outbreak and said medical examinations would establish the exact cause. 

That explanation has faced strong public scepticism. Survivors and relatives have alleged overcrowding, poor ventilation and possible exposure to hazardous substances inside the detention cell. Some accounts have also pointed to possible physical mistreatment.

The Federal Government moved quickly. Interior Minister Olubunmi Tunji-Ojo suspended Aniviye and 20 other officers pending the outcome of a full investigation. 

A 10-member independent committee was also constituted to establish the identities of the deceased, examine the circumstances of their arrest and detention, determine the cause of death, and recommend appropriate action. The panel has two weeks to submit its report.

President Bola Tinubu has directed that any official found to have contributed to the deaths through action, abuse, or negligence must face arrest and prosecution. 

The Niger State Government has also set up its own inquiry and declared a period of mourning.

The parade on Sunday marks a visible step by the NSCDC to demonstrate accountability. Yet many Nigerians, including civil society groups and families of the deceased, continue to demand more than suspensions and briefings. 

They want a transparent process that leads to clear findings, justice for the victims, and reforms that prevent similar tragedies in future detention facilities.

The independent investigation is ongoing. Its conclusions will determine whether the deaths resulted from negligence, overcrowding, disease, or more serious misconduct. 

For now, the images of a suspended commandant and his officers standing before the cameras have become the latest chapter in a case that has already shaken public confidence in the handling of suspects in custody.
