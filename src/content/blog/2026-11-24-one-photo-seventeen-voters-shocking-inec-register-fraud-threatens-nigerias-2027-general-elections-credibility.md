**Abuja, Nigeria—November 24, 2026—Nigeria's** road to the 2027 general elections has been jolted by a startling revelation: 17 different voters, with 17 different names, sharing exactly the same photograph on the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) preliminary voter register.

The explosive discovery, first spotlighted by ARISE NEWS on its flagship programme *What’s Trending With Ojy Okpe*, was made by digital analytics group MundX Analytics. 

In a viral video graphic released on Sunday, the group displayed the duplicated image appearing across different entries, sending shockwaves through the polity and raising a haunting question: who is really on Nigeria’s voter roll?

But the scandal runs even deeper.

According to a detailed investigation by the Foundation for Investigative Journalism (FIJ) published on November 23, 2026, the 17 entries were all found at Community School Ochicha, Polling Unit 16-08-11-003, in Nguogo/Ikembara Ward, Ikeduru Local Government Area of Imo State. The polling unit has 1,460 voters on its preliminary register.

MundX Analytics said it used an automated script to hunt for replicated images and then conducted a pixel-by-pixel comparison. The result: all 17 photos matched exactly.

In a chilling final composite tagged “Same photo, 17 times,” ***the video cycles through Voter 03, 05, 11, 15 and others listed under names like Okoro David N. and Nwosu Chukwuemeka U., before asking: “WHY DO THEY HAVE THE SAME PHOTO?”***

The group admitted it started with the wrong search.

> *“We were looking for the same photo repeated within one polling unit. The bigger problem is the same photo repeated across different polling units and different wards,” Mundus (@mundus01) posted on X.*

Indeed, MundX found 25 additional names linked to three other photographs across 21 distinct polling units. In total, 42 voter entries were attached to just four photographs across 22 polling units in Imo State. In Oru East LGA alone, the group alleged 18,966 voter records share just 7,580 unique photos.

The analysts argue artificial intelligence and machine learning make it possible to flag questionable registrations across all polling units without manually checking each voter.

The revelation comes amid a wider cleanup. INEC Chairman, Professor Joash Amupitan, has confirmed the removal of 1,608,660 invalid registrations, while National Commissioner Mohammed Kudu Haruna disclosed that 54 registration personnel across 10 states have been flagged for possible disciplinary action over irregularities, including AI-generated photographs.

INEC insists the register published online on October 26, 2026 was only preliminary, not the final certified list for the presidential and National Assembly elections scheduled for January 16, 2027. It has now banned external webcam uploads and promised a new AI policy.

Yet Nigerians are asking, if BVAS facial recognition and fingerprint verification could not stop this, what will protect their votes in 2027?
