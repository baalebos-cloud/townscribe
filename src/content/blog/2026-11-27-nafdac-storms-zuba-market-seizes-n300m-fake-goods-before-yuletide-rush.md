**ABUJA, Nigeria — November 27, 2026** — What should have been ordinary trading turned into a day of reckoning at Zuba International Market as the National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control (NAFDAC) shut down the bustling Federal Capital Territory market and seized counterfeit and banned products worth over ₦300 million.

The high-stakes enforcement operation, announced on NAFDAC's official X handle on November 27, 2026, saw officers screen more than 160 shops and warehouses in a sting that began before 5:30 a.m. on Thursday, November 26, following weeks of intelligence gathering and surveillance.

Conducted jointly with the Nigeria Police Force and the Department of State Services (DSS), the dawn raid was designed to prevent resistance, with security operatives securing the market before traders arrived.

According to the Head of Investigation and Enforcement Division, North-Central Zone, Pharm. Babatunji Omoyeni, the operation is part of NAFDAC's intensified war to rid Nigerian markets of counterfeit, substandard, and unwholesome regulated products, particularly ahead of the festive season when demand surges.

Omoyeni said the action followed a direct directive from NAFDAC Director-General, Prof. Mojisola Adeyeye, to the Enforcement Directorate to stop counterfeiters and unscrupulous operators from exploiting increased yuletide consumer demand.

Officials described alarming discoveries. Large quantities of banned alcoholic beverages packaged in sachets of 50 ml, 100 ml, and small PET bottles below 200 ml were found hidden in warehouses, some with metal coverings over windows to conceal stock. Officers had to force entry into several locked warehouses after occupants refused to open.

"We have been asking producers to stop producing alcoholic beverages less than 200 ml. They have been recalcitrant," one enforcement official said during the raid.

The operation also uncovered expired products, expired energy drinks allegedly being repackaged, unregistered medicines, and unwholesome food items. All items were documented, evacuated, and owners identified for statements and possible prosecution.

NAFDAC linked the crackdown to the enforcement of the Federal Government ban on the production, importation, distribution, and sale of alcoholic drinks in sachets and containers below 200 ml, which took effect January 1, 2026, after a transition period. The agency says the ban aims to curb underage drinking and protect children.

*"The moment little children are taking this, they misbehave, and we have so many insecurity issues in Nigeria, which were indirectly or directly being linked to consumption of alcohol by minors,"* the official warned.

The Zuba swoop follows NAFDAC's destruction last Friday of fake, expired, and banned products worth ₦100 billion at Ogwama Dumpsite, Ogere Remo, Ogun State—including codeine syrups, Analgin and Oxytocin injections, counterfeit cosmetics, and banned sachet alcohol recovered nationwide and surrendered by companies and Customs.

Omoyeni commended the Police and DSS for collaboration and urged Nigerians to remain vigilant, report manufacturers and sellers of fake products, and avoid unlicensed drug sellers this Christmas.
