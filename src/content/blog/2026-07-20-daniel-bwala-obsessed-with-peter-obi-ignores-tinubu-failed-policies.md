As Nigeria’s political temperature rises ahead of the 2027 general elections, fresh criticism has emerged targeting one of President Bola Tinubu’s key media voices. 

Social commentator Everest has accused **Daniel Bwala**, Special Adviser to the President on Media and Public Communications, of spending the better part of recent months fixated on attacking Labour Party leader **Peter Obi** rather than articulating or defending the administration’s policies.

In a widely circulated post on X, Everest lamented that Bwala — a publicly funded official — has rarely used his platform to discuss critical government initiatives such as tax reforms, the impacts of fuel subsidy removal, the Nigerian Education Loan Fund (NELFUND), healthcare subsidies, or ongoing security efforts. 

Instead, the commentator claims, Bwala’s public interventions have predominantly centered on criticizing Obi and opposition politics.

### A Question of Priorities

The criticism strikes at the heart of public expectations for government communication. 

Taxpayers, Everest argued, deserve clear explanations of how economic reforms are meant to alleviate widespread challenges, including soaring inflation, unemployment, and rising living costs.

> *“Daniel Bwala has not discussed any of Tinubu’s policies except discussing Peter Obi for months now. Someone paid by Nigerians. Waste of public funds.”* — Everest (@novieverest)

This sentiment resonates with a growing chorus of voices who feel government spokespersons are more focused on political combat than policy advocacy and public enlightenment. 

In an era of economic uncertainty, effective communication is seen by many as essential for rebuilding trust and managing public perception.

### Broader Political Context

Daniel Bwala, a former PDP stalwart who switched allegiance to the APC and Tinubu’s camp, has become one of the administration’s most visible defenders. 

His sharp, often combative style has earned him both praise from supporters and accusations of partisanship from critics.

The latest jab comes as opposition figures, particularly Peter Obi, continue to gain traction with messages centered on good governance, accountability, and citizen welfare. 

With 2027 approaching, the battle for narrative control is intensifying, and government communicators are under increasing scrutiny to move beyond attacks and deliver substantive policy engagement.

### Public Reactions and Implications

Social media responses have been divided. Some users echo Everest’s frustration, calling for more constructive discourse from presidential aides. 

Others defend Bwala, arguing that confronting opposition narratives is part of his role in a competitive political environment.

This episode highlights a recurring challenge in Nigerian politics: the tension between robust defense of government achievements and the need for transparent, policy-focused communication that addresses citizens’ daily realities.

As the countdown to 2027 continues, the effectiveness of the Tinubu administration’s messaging strategy may prove as crucial as its policy outcomes in shaping voter sentiment.
