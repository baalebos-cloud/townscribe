**Abuja, Nigeria—November 7, 2026**—President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has congratulated Nigeria’s U-17 women’s national team, the Flamingos, following their historic 2-1 victory over Japan in the final of the FIFA U-17 Women’s World Cup in Morocco on Saturday.

The triumph at the Mohammed VI Football Complex in Rabat marked a landmark moment for African football. Nigeria became the first African nation to win the FIFA U-17 Women’s World Cup since the competition began in 2008. 

The Flamingos also made history as the first African country to claim titles in both the men’s and women’s U-17 categories and the first team ever to lose their opening match and still lift the trophy.



**How the Final Unfolded**

Goals from Awawu Bashiru in the 39th minute and Oluebube Umejiaku in the 47th minute secured the win. Japan’s Fuki Ito pulled one back in the 80th minute, but the Nigerian defence held firm under intense pressure to preserve the lead until the final whistle.

Bashiru’s long-range effort slipped past Japanese goalkeeper Kou Kadowaki to give Nigeria a 1-0 half-time lead. Just two minutes after the restart, Umejiaku controlled a through ball from Esther Stephen and delicately lofted it over the advancing keeper for 2-0. 

The late Japanese goal set up a tense finish, yet the Flamingos displayed remarkable composure and organization to see the game out.

The victory completed a remarkable turnaround. Nigeria had opened the tournament with a 2-1 group-stage defeat to Poland before winning six consecutive matches, including victories over Puerto Rico, defending champions North Korea, Mexico, and Venezuela, and a commanding 3-1 semi-final win over Brazil.



**Star Performer and Individual Awards**

Sixteen-year-old forward Oluebube Umejiaku, who plays for Heartland Queens, was named Player of the Tournament and received the Golden Ball. 

She scored three goals and provided two assists across the competition, including the decisive second goal in the final. She was also named Player of the Match in the title decider.

Umejiaku’s consistency stood out: she contributed a goal or assist in every match she featured in after coming off the bench to score against Poland in the opening game.



**Presidential and National Praise**

In his message, President Tinubu praised the players, coaching crew led by Akeem Busari, the Nigeria Football Federation and the National Sports Commission. 

He reaffirmed his administration’s commitment to investing in grassroots sports development and expanding opportunities for young Nigerians, particularly girls.

First Lady Oluremi Tinubu was seen cheering enthusiastically from her residence, chanting “*Nigeria! Nigeria!*” as the final unfolded. The National Sports Commission described the victory as a defining moment for Nigerian and African women’s football.

The achievement has sparked widespread celebration across the country. Social media platforms lit up with pride as Nigerians hailed the Flamingos for bringing global glory and inspiring a new generation of female athletes.

This success builds on Nigeria’s strong tradition in youth football and underscores the growing quality of the women’s game at age-grade level. 

The Flamingos had earlier won the 2026 WAFU-B U-17 Girls Cup in Côte d’Ivoire without losing a match, scoring 17 goals and conceding just one.

As the young champions prepare to return home, their victory stands as both a sporting milestone and a powerful symbol of resilience, teamwork, and national pride.
