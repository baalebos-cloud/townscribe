**Jos, Plateau State – November 25, 2026—** Three people, including a serving soldier, have died in a ghastly midnight crash after suspected kidnappers blocked the volatile Jos–Mangu Road in Plateau State.

Security sources told Zagazola Makama, counter-insurgency expert and security analyst, that the tragedy occurred at about 2:00 a.m. on Tuesday, November 25, 2026, around Hawan Kibo in Riyom Local Government Area—a notorious flashpoint for abductions and ambushes.

The victims were soldiers travelling on pass from the Northeast theatre of operations, heading home to reunite with their families after months on the frontline against Boko Haram and ISWAP.

Their hope of a joyful homecoming ended in horror when they ran into a roadblock allegedly mounted by suspected kidnappers who routinely prey on night travelers along the corridor.

One of the survivors reportedly told investigators that the driver, in a desperate bid to manoeuvre around the obstruction and escape a possible abduction, lost control of the vehicle. It somersaulted violently and plunged into a deep ditch.

Troops of Sector 6, Operation Enduring Peace, OPEP, deployed at Sopp, responded to a distress call from the Federal Road Safety Corps, FRSC, at about 9:20 a.m. and rushed to the scene.

On arrival, they found a heartbreaking sight: Corporal Ejikeme and two other occupants had died on the spot, while another Corporal and other passengers sustained varying degrees of injuries.

The suspected kidnappers had already fled the scene before help arrived, leaving their would-be victims to fate.

The injured personnel were swiftly evacuated to the 3 Division Medical Services and Hospital, 3 DMSH, in Jos for intensive care, while other casualties were taken to Riyom General Hospital by the FRSC and Keystone emergency responders.

Military authorities said the unit of the affected personnel has been contacted for further administrative and operational action, including notification of next-of-kin.

The incident, first reported by Zagazola at 9:03 p.m. on Tuesday and tagged to the Nigerian Army headquarters, @HQNigerianArmy, has reignited fears over the Jos–Mangu, Jos–Makurdi, and Jos–Ganawuri highways, where media and EU security reports have documented repeated kidnappings.

Plateau State remains under Operation Enduring Peace, formerly Operation Safe Haven, with troops battling a complex mix of farmer-herder clashes, banditry, and kidnapping-for-ransom.

For now, grieving families who waited excitedly for their heroes’ return are instead preparing for funerals—a painful reminder that Nigeria’s roads can be as deadly as its battlefields.
