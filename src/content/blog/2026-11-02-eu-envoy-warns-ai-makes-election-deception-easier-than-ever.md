**Abuja, Nigeria—November 2, 2026**—European Union Ambassador to Nigeria and ECOWAS, Gautier Mignot, has warned that Artificial Intelligence can make deception easier to produce and harder to detect, posing significant risks to electoral integrity.

Speaking at the *‘Beyond the Ballot’* National Summit on AI, Foreign Information Manipulation and Interference (FIMI), and Electoral Information Integrity in Abuja on Monday, Mignot stressed that technology should not become the entire focus of the conversation.



*“Artificial Intelligence can support translation, accessibility, and communication, but it can also make deception easier to produce and harder to recognise,”* he said. 

*“We should not make technology the whole story. A misleading message does not need sophisticated AI to cause harm.”*

**Threat to Trust and Democracy**

In related remarks at the same event, the ambassador noted that an election can be undermined without altering a single ballot. 

A fabricated announcement could send voters to the wrong location, while a false claim about results could deepen public suspicion before verified information reaches citizens.

*“The immediate target is information; the deeper target is trust,”* Mignot stated.



The summit brought together stakeholders, including electoral officials, civil society groups, technology experts and media representatives, to develop a rights-respecting national response to information threats facing Nigeria’s 2027 general elections.



**Broader Context**

The discussion comes amid growing concern over the potential misuse of AI tools such as deepfakes, cloned voices, and coordinated disinformation campaigns. 

Recent forums, including the AI and Democracy Forum organized earlier this year, have similarly urged stronger safeguards, human oversight and collaboration among INEC, technology platforms, political parties and the media.



Ambassador Mignot’s intervention underscores the European Union’s continued engagement with Nigeria on democratic governance and electoral integrity as the country prepares for the 2027 polls.
