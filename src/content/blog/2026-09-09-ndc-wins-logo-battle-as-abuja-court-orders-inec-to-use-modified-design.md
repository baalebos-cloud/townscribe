**Abuja, Nigeria—September 9, 2026**—The Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) has scored a significant legal victory after a Federal High Court in Abuja ordered the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) to recognize and use the party’s modified logo in its official records and electoral activities.

Justice J. O. Abdulmalik delivered the ruling in a suit filed by the party seeking to compel INEC to adopt the updated design. 

NDC National Leader Senator Seriake Dickson confirmed that the party had earlier approved the modified logo at its first National Executive Council meeting and formally submitted it to the electoral commission.

According to Dickson, INEC initially uploaded the modified version but later removed it without offering any explanation. 

The abrupt reversal forced the party to seek judicial intervention to protect its branding and operational readiness.



**Key Details of the Court Order**

The court granted the NDC’s prayers, effectively directing INEC to reinstate and utilize the modified logo across its systems. 

Both the original and modified designs retain the party’s distinctive two-finger victory sign, a symbol that has featured prominently in its identity.

Dickson described the dispute as one of the administrative hurdles the young party has faced while consolidating its structures. 

He urged NDC candidates and members nationwide to immediately adopt the court-approved modified logo in all campaign materials as preparations intensify for the 2027 general elections.

*“We are democratic fighters. We do not run away from battles,”* Dickson stated, praising the judiciary for upholding the party’s position. 

He added that a formal statement would follow and called on members to remain united.



**Background to the Logo Dispute**

The NDC has navigated several legal and administrative challenges since its emergence as a registered political platform. 

Earlier controversies in 2025 and mid-2026 centered on logo similarities raised by other associations and a subsequent Lokoja court ruling that set aside aspects of the party’s registration process pending fresh hearings involving interested parties.

Party leaders have consistently maintained that the NDC remains a validly registered entity that has conducted congresses and primaries monitored by INEC. 

The latest Abuja judgment focuses specifically on the modified digital logo and does not reopen the broader registration questions.



**Implications Ahead of 2027**

The court’s directive provides the NDC with clearer branding certainty at a critical stage in the electoral calendar. 

With candidate lists, campaign materials, and public visibility increasingly important, a standardized logo helps avoid confusion among supporters and voters.

Political observers note that logo and symbol disputes have periodically disrupted new parties’ momentum in Nigeria. 

Clear judicial resolution, as seen in this case, helps stabilize the playing field and reinforces the role of the courts in electoral administration.

Dickson emphasized continuity and resilience, signalling that the party will continue its organizational work while complying with the latest court directive.

As INEC implements the order, attention will turn to how swiftly the modified logo is restored on the commission’s platforms and whether further administrative issues arise. 

For now, the NDC celebrates a hard-won legal win that strengthens its visual identity heading into the next electoral cycle.
