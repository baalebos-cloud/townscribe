**Abuja, Nigeria—October 30, 2026—Inspector-General** of Police Olatunji Rilwan Disu has advanced a trilateral security partnership involving Nigeria, the United States, and Italy, with a major focus on specialized training for up to 5,000 Nigerian police personnel.

During a working visit to Italy, the IGP engaged Italian law enforcement authorities in leveraging international expertise, advanced training, and technology to strengthen the Nigeria Police Force’s response to terrorism, kidnapping, arms trafficking, and other threats.



**Key Training Offer**

A significant outcome of the visit was an offer to provide specialized training for as many as 5,000 officers. Priority will be given to counter-insurgency operations and policing in challenging rural and forested terrain.

The proposed programme will cover tactical operations, intelligence-led patrols, critical infrastructure protection, digital and scientific forensics, cybercrime, explosives detection, financial investigations, asset tracing, and anti-extortion techniques. 

It will extend beyond the Police Mobile Force to other specialized units, including explosive ordnance disposal and forensic teams.



**Engagements in Italy**

At a meeting with Lieutenant-General Salvatore Luongo, Commander General of the Italian Carabinieri, discussions centred on technical assistance, training and institutional cooperation.

The Nigerian delegation also visited the Carabinieri Centre of Excellence for Stability Police Units (CoESPU) in Vicenza. 

There, they assessed training facilities and explored adaptations suited to Nigeria’s needs. Proposed initiatives include command courses for 40 senior officers, leadership training for 100 junior commanders, and a mobile assistance team to support Police Mobile Force training schools in Gwoza and Ila-Orangun.

Further talks covered the development of a national stability policing doctrine, standard operating procedures, canine operations, forensic science and mounted policing, including local horse-breeding capacity for public order duties.

IGP Disu proposed a memorandum of understanding covering training, technology transfer and operational doctrine. He also invited a Carabinieri assessment team to Abuja to identify priority needs and finalise practical arrangements.

The Inspector-General reaffirmed the Force’s commitment to international partnerships that enhance operational readiness, improve investigative capabilities and better protect lives and property.

The statement was issued by CSP Ani Iniedu, Force Public Relations Officer, Force Headquarters, Abuja.



***This report is based on the official statement released by the Nigeria Police Force on October 30, 2026.***
