The creator economy has officially entered a new era. For the first time in the five-year history of Forbes’ annual ranking, the world’s 50 highest-earning content creators collectively pulled in more than $1 billion — specifically $1.02 billion over the past year. 

That figure marks a 20 percent jump from the previous year’s $853 million and an 80 percent surge from the $570 million total recorded when the list launched in 2022.

At the summit, once again, stands MrBeast. Jimmy Donaldson, the North Carolina YouTuber turned global production powerhouse, earned an estimated $300 million. 

That single figure accounts for nearly 30 percent of the entire top-50 haul and more than four times the earnings of the runner-up. 

Donaldson’s channels now command hundreds of millions of subscribers—he became the first individual creator to surpass 500 million YouTube subscribers — and generate well over five billion views annually. 

Beyond ad revenue, his empire includes snack brand Feastables, meal brand Lunchly, the Amazon series \*Beast Games\*, analytics tool Viewstats, and a recent move into fintech.



**The New Media Moguls**

Second place belongs to Dhar Mann, the producer of uplifting, algorithm-friendly short dramas. Mann’s studio, staffed by roughly 200 people, pumps out content that racks up nearly 300 million views in a typical week, often translated into more than a dozen languages. 

His estimated $65 million earnings reflect both massive digital reach and traditional Hollywood ambitions: he recently signed a deal with Fox Entertainment to produce 40 vertical dramas in 18 months and even served as the NFL’s “Chief Kindness Officer” during Super Bowl LX.

British entrepreneur and podcaster Steven Bartlett rounds out the top three with $52 million. His hit show \*The Diary of a CEO\* has surpassed one billion streams and reaches tens of millions of listeners monthly. 

Bartlett has translated that platform into a broader holdings company, high-profile partnerships, and a regular seat on the UK investment show **Dragons’ Den**.

Further down the list, familiar names continue to dominate. Markiplier (Mark Fischbach) earned about $38 million, while comedy duo Rhett & Link brought in roughly $37 million through their long-running **Good Mythical Morning** franchise and related ventures. 

Other notable earners include IShowSpeed, Mark Rober, Codie Sanchez, Charli D’Amelio, and Druski, whose incomes range from the high teens to low thirties of millions.



**From Influencers to Operators**

What separates this year’s list is not merely higher numbers but a structural shift. The highest earners no longer rely primarily on platform ad revenue or one-off brand deals. 

They run scaled production studios, own consumer product lines, strike multi-year content deals with traditional media companies, and build software or fintech side businesses. 

Total followers across the top 50 reached 3.6 billion, up 7 percent, yet earnings grew nearly three times faster — clear evidence that monetization has moved beyond pure audience size.

Industry observers note that the top 10 percent of creators now capture the overwhelming majority of brand payments, a power-law dynamic that continues to widen. 

While tens of millions of people create content and a smaller professional class earns a living, only a tiny elite operate at the level of true media companies.



**What It Means for the Broader Economy**

The milestone arrives as the global creator economy itself is projected to exceed $300 billion in value this year and could reach well over a trillion by the early 2030s. 

Brands, traditional studios, and investors are increasingly treating the most successful creators as full-fledged partners rather than mere marketing channels.

For aspiring creators, the message is both inspiring and sobering. The pathway to the very top now requires not only viral talent but the ability to build teams, own intellectual property, and diversify revenue streams far beyond any single platform. 

MrBeast, Dhar Mann, and Bartlett have shown that the biggest fortunes in digital media belong to those who treat content as the foundation of a much larger business empire.

As Forbes’ latest ranking makes clear, the richest content creators in the world are no longer just influencers. They are the new media moguls — and their combined balance sheet has finally crossed the billion-dollar threshold.
