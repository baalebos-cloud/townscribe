**Abuja | November 26, 2026—The** long-running $6 billion Mambilla Hydroelectric Power Project fraud trial took a decisive turn on Thursday, November 26, 2026, as a former Minister of Power, Liyel Imoke, testified against former Minister of Power and Steel, Olu Agunloye, with the Federal Capital Territory High Court, Apo, Abuja, admitting crucial Federal Executive Council (FEC) minutes showing non-approval of the contract.

Imoke, testifying as the sixth prosecution witness, PW6, confirmed he attended the FEC meeting of July 23, 2003, where minutes of the previous meeting of May 21, 2003, were adopted. 

The 17th FEC minutes, tendered by the prosecution and admitted by Justice Jude Onwuegbuzie, indicated the Council did not approve Agunloye’s memo for award of the Mambilla contract to Sunrise Power and Transmission Company Limited.

Agunloye is facing prosecution by the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) on an amended seven-count charge bordering on official corruption and fraudulent award of the $6 billion contract while he served as Minister of Power and Steel between 2002 and 2003 under President Olusegun Obasanjo.

#### Legal Fireworks Over Admissibility

At Thursday’s proceedings, prosecution counsel, Abba Mohammed, SAN, while re-examining Imoke, asked if he attended subsequent FEC meetings after May 21, 2003. The witness answered yes, identified the 17th FEC minutes, prompting Mohammed to seek tendering of the document alongside its forwarding letter dated November 12, 2026.

Defence counsel, Adeola Adedipe, SAN, fiercely objected, arguing legal requirements for admission were not met, that the document contained an attachment of an original which could not be tendered from the bar, that prosecution was neither maker nor witness, and only a certified true copy could be admitted without proper foundation through the witness.

Responding, Mohammed argued the document arose from defence cross-examination, entitling prosecution to clarification under Section 215(2) of the Evidence Act, citing Supreme Court decisions in Abdullahi vs FRN 2016 and Joseph vs. State 2021, plus Section 146 presumption of genuineness for certified public documents. 

He said the certifying officer need not appear, and the forwarding letter followed EFCC’s letter to the Secretary to Government of the Federation, SGF, as contained in 13 additional proofs of evidence.

#### Court Prefers Substance Over Technicality

Ruling, Justice Onwuegbuzie held that while defence arguments were reasonable, the court should not be constrained by technicalities in admitting relevant evidence, admitting the minutes in a major boost to EFCC’s case that the controversial Mambilla contract was awarded without FEC approval.

The Mambilla project, Nigeria’s 3,050MW dream stalled for over 40 years, lies at the heart of Nigeria’s power crisis and international arbitration battles.

Trial continues.
