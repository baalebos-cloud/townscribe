A former leader of the Catholic Youth Organization of Nigeria has accused the Bola Ahmed Tinubu administration of circulating a forged press release under the organization's name, allegedly aimed at diluting recent comments by Cardinal John Onaiyekan on the country’s deepening economic hardship and insecurity.

Idris Zekeri Jnr, a former two-term president of the Catholic Youth Organization of Nigeria (CYON) in the Army Chaplaincy, publicly dismissed the document on Tuesday as an obvious fake. In a post on X, he said his experience allowed him to spot the forgery at once.

*“As a former two-term President of the Catholic Youth Organization of Nigeria (CYON) in the Army Chaplaincy, I didn’t need a second look to recognize this as a poorly fabricated forged press release.* 

*The forgers in Aso Rock and their team of failures are now clutching at straws,”* Zekeri wrote.

The disputed statement, dated August 4, 2026, was presented as an open letter from the Catholic Youth Organization of Nigeria. 

It advised Catholic leaders against making partisan political remarks and insisted that Catholic youths do not collectively back any party or candidate ahead of the 2027 general elections.

Zekeri pointed to clear red flags. The document repeatedly used the incorrect name “*Catholic Youth Association of Nigeria”* instead of the official “*Catholic Youth Organization of Nigeria,”* among other inconsistencies that, he argued, proved it did not come from the organization.

The timing appears linked to Cardinal Onaiyekan’s remarks following a meeting between President Tinubu and members of the Catholic Bishops’ Conference of Nigeria. 

During that engagement, the bishops raised concerns about the cost of living, insecurity, and broader governance challenges. 

The circulating letter is widely seen as an attempt to push back against those observations by claiming Catholic youth neutrality.

Online reaction was swift. Many Nigerians questioned the letter’s authenticity and accused political operatives of trying to undercut a senior cleric by putting words in the mouths of young Catholics. 

The episode has added to existing tensions between sections of the Church and the government over the state of the nation.

As of the time of this report, the national leadership of the Catholic Youth Organization of Nigeria had not claimed the document as its own. 

Neither President Tinubu nor the Presidency had issued any response to Zekeri’s allegations.

The claim arrives against a backdrop of earlier controversies in which critics have accused the administration of managing public messaging aggressively. 

Whether the forged letter is confirmed or disowned, the episode underscores how sensitive comments from religious leaders have become in Nigeria’s charged political climate ahead of 2027.

The story continues to develop.
