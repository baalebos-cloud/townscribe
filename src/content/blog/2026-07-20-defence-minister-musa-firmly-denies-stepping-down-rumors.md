**Abuja, Nigeria – July 21, 2026** – In a strongly worded public disclaimer, Nigeria’s Federal Ministry of Information and National Orientation has categorically rejected circulating rumors alleging that Honourable Minister of Defence, General Christopher Gwabin Musa (Rtd) OFR, intends to resign from his pivotal role.

The statement, issued via the ministry’s official X account (@FMINONigeria), describes the claims as “*absolute falsehood borne out of complete mischief.”* 

It emphasizes that the minister remains “fully focused on his national security mandate,” deeply committed to his duties, and actively piloting the nation’s defence strategy with visible results.

General Musa, a highly decorated retired army general born December 25, 1967, in Sokoto State, brings decades of distinguished service to the position. 

He previously served as Chief of Defence Staff from June 2023 to October 2025 before his appointment as Minister of Defence in December 2025.

His tenure has been marked by proactive measures against insecurity, including recent directives to troops to engage terrorists and bandits on sight without hesitation, strengthened partnerships for oil infrastructure protection, calls for the death penalty for kidnappers, and international engagements for technology transfer to boost local defence manufacturing.

The press release, e-signed by Leah Katung-Babatunde, Special Assistant (Media) to the Honourable Minister of Defence, urges the public, media outlets, and social media users to disregard the unverified information and seek clarifications directly from official sources.

Such rumors are not uncommon in Nigeria’s high-stakes political and security environment, where misinformation can spread rapidly on social platforms. 

The timely official rebuttal underscores the government’s commitment to transparency and countering disinformation, especially as the administration under President Bola Ahmed Tinubu advances security and economic reforms.

Observers note that General Musa’s continued leadership is seen as crucial for sustaining momentum in counter-insurgency operations, inter-agency collaboration, and welfare improvements for personnel, including recent salary enhancements for soldiers.

The ministry’s intervention serves as a reminder of the broader challenges posed by fake news in a digital age. 

Nigerians are encouraged to rely on verified channels to avoid unnecessary anxiety and maintain national cohesion during ongoing efforts to tackle insecurity.

As the Renewed Hope Agenda progresses, stability in key appointments like the Defence Ministry remains central to public confidence and operational effectiveness.
