**SOKOTO, Nigeria** — In a bold and uncompromising directive that has resonated strongly with many Nigerians weary of prolonged insecurity, the Honourable Minister of Defence, Major General Christopher Musa (rtd), has instructed security personnel to act decisively against bandits and terrorists. 

“*Once you are deployed, do not wait for any order from anybody to shoot any bandit or any terrorist. Anybody who refuses to shoot or kill any bandit or terrorist in the name of waiting for an order, we will treat you like a bandit*,” the Minister declared during an operational visit to Sokoto where armoured vehicles and equipment were distributed to troops.

The video of the address, widely shared on social media, captures a determined Musa emphasizing immediate action to protect lives and restore peace in troubled regions. 

The statement comes amid ongoing efforts to tackle banditry, terrorism, and other forms of violent crime that have plagued parts of the North-West and North-Central zones for years.

**A New Era of Decisive Security Operations?**

This hardline stance reflects a shift toward more proactive engagement by Nigeria’s security apparatus. 

Minister Musa, a former Chief of Defence Staff known for his straightforward approach, stressed that hesitation on the battlefield would no longer be tolerated. 

The directive aims to empower field commanders and operatives to respond swiftly to threats, potentially reducing response times that have previously cost lives. 

The timing aligns with broader government initiatives to strengthen the military and intelligence capabilities, including equipment procurement and enhanced inter-agency collaboration. 

Many citizens view this as a much-needed morale booster for troops who often face heavily armed criminal elements while operating under restrictive rules of engagement.

**Public Reactions and Hope for Safer Communities**

Social media erupted with a mix of approval and cautious optimism following the Minister’s remarks. 

Supporters praised the no-nonsense tone, arguing that “*a good bandit is a dead bandit*” and calling for sustained implementation. 

Others urged caution to avoid collateral damage to innocent civilians, emphasizing the need for intelligence-driven operations and accountability. 

For communities in Sokoto and beyond that have endured years of kidnapping, killings, and displacement, the message offers renewed hope. 

Parents, farmers, and traders who have lost loved ones or livelihoods to banditry see it as a signal that the government is finally prioritizing citizen safety over bureaucratic delays. 

**Challenges Remain, But Momentum Builds**

While the directive is welcomed, experts note that sustainable success will require more than shoot-on-sight orders. 

Addressing root causes — poverty, unemployment, governance gaps, and arms trafficking — remains essential. 

Complementary efforts in rehabilitation (where appropriate), intelligence gathering, and community engagement will be critical to prevent cycles of violence. 

The Defence Minister’s strong words, delivered in the field alongside tangible support like armoured vehicles, demonstrate a commitment to backing rhetoric with resources.

 As operations continue, Nigerians will be watching closely to see measurable reductions in attacks and restoration of normalcy in affected areas. 

In a country longing for peace and progress, this firm stance against terror and banditry represents a significant step forward — one that could mark a turning point in the nation’s long battle for security and stability.
