In the quiet farming community of Dyein village, Riyom Local Government Area of Plateau State, a profound tragedy unfolded in the early hours of June 3, 2026. 

Mr. Joshua Bulus, the 62-year-old Mai-Anguwa (village head), was discovered lifeless on the outskirts of his community, his body bearing deep machete wounds that spoke of a brutal, up-close assault.

Troops from Operation Safe Haven, responding swiftly to a distress call, arrived at the scene around 8:15 a.m. to find the respected leader's remains. 

Sources indicate Bulus had been performing routine night vigilance duty—a common but increasingly perilous task in this volatile region—when unknown assailants struck. 

Military personnel have intensified patrols and launched a full investigation, vowing to bring the perpetrators to justice while maintaining a visible presence to deter further violence and calm anxious residents.

This killing strikes at the heart of a community already scarred by relentless insecurity. 

Riyom LGA has endured repeated attacks in recent months, with gunmen and suspected militias targeting villages like Jol, Kwi, and Tanjol, claiming lives of vigilantes, farmers, and civilians alike. 

In May 2026 alone, multiple incidents left several dead, including a young vigilante in Tanjol. 

Broader patterns across Plateau—often involving machete attacks, ambushes, and disputes over land and resources—have fueled a cycle of fear, displacement, and reprisals in the Middle Belt.

Bulus was more than a title; as village head, he embodied leadership, continuity, and protection for his people amid farming seasons and communal challenges. 

His death leaves a void not easily filled, raising urgent questions about the safety of those who stand guard while others sleep. 

Families grieve, children lose a pillar of wisdom, and neighbors wonder who will be next.

Authorities emphasize that operations continue to restore stability, but for Dyein and surrounding areas, the human cost mounts. 

This incident underscores the urgent need for sustained intelligence-driven security, community resilience programs, and lasting solutions to the farmer-herder tensions and criminality plaguing Plateau State. 

As troops track the killers, residents hold onto fragile hope that justice and peace may yet prevail in their ancestral lands.
