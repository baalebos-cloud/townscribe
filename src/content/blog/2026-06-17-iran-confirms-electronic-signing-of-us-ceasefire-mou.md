**Tehran/Geneva—June 17, 2026**—Iran has confirmed that a Memorandum of Understanding (MoU) to end the war with the United States and its allies has been signed electronically by both sides, with a formal signing ceremony scheduled for Friday in Switzerland.

The development, announced by Iranian officials and corroborated by US sources, marks a major diplomatic breakthrough in the conflict that escalated dramatically earlier this year. 

President Donald Trump described the agreement as “all signed,” stating that the Strait of Hormuz will be “completely open” following the formalities.

 **Key Terms of the Agreement**

According to a senior US official who briefed reporters on the 14-point document, the MoU includes an immediate and permanent ceasefire across all fronts, including Lebanon. 

It also provides for the immediate lifting of US sanctions waivers related to Iran’s fossil fuel industry and the reopening of key maritime routes.

The agreement does not yet address Iran’s nuclear program in detail. Further negotiations on that issue are expected to begin after the Friday ceremony, potentially involving additional working groups.

Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi emphasized that the deal was reached “from a position of equality” after more than 100 days of conflict. He noted that the lifting of sanctions and opportunities for investment represent key achievements for Tehran.

**Reactions and Caveats**

While both the US and Iran have confirmed the electronic signing, Israel has expressed significant reservations. Israeli officials have voiced concerns about the deal’s implications for regional security, particularly regarding Hezbollah and other proxies. 

Israeli forces continue operations in parts of Lebanon amid ongoing tensions.

The agreement comes after intense mediation efforts involving Pakistan and Qatar. 

A virtual meeting on Sunday finalized the text, paving the way for the electronic signatures by Trump, Vice President JD Vance, and Iranian Parliament Speaker Mohammad Bagher Ghalibaf.



**Broader Implications**

The ceasefire is expected to ease immediate pressures on global energy markets by reopening the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for oil shipments. 

Analysts suggest the deal could stabilize oil prices and reduce the risk of wider regional escalation.

However, questions remain about long-term enforcement and the future of Iran’s nuclear ambitions. 

Hardliners in Tehran have criticized aspects of the negotiations, while US officials maintain that the deal provides a framework for de-escalation without immediate concessions on enrichment activities.

For now, the focus shifts to Friday’s ceremony in Geneva, where Vice President Vance is expected to represent the United States. 

The event will symbolize a potential turning point after months of devastating conflict that has claimed thousands of lives and displaced millions across the region.
