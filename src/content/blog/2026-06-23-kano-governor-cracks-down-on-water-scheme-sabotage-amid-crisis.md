**Kano, Nigeria** - In a bold move signaling zero tolerance for interference with essential services, the Kano State Government has suspended the manager of the Thomas Regional Water Scheme following shocking discoveries of widespread vandalism and alleged sabotage at critical water infrastructure facilities.

Governor Abba Kabir Yusuf’s administration, which has prioritized tackling the state’s long-standing water scarcity challenges, acted swiftly after an on-site inspection revealed extensive damage. 

Commissioner for Water Resources, Environment and Climate Change, Dr. Dahiru Muhammad-Hashim, detailed the troubling findings in a statement issued on Monday, June 22, 2026.

During visits to the Thomas and Ghari Regional Water Schemes — facilities intended to serve multiple local government areas — officials found generators and pumps stripped of nearly all electrical wiring. 

Even more alarming, a power project completed just three months ago at the Thomas scheme, which had not yet been commissioned, had already been looted.

Security personnel assigned to protect the plant were reportedly found farming on the grounds instead of safeguarding the assets, raising serious questions about oversight and accountability.

*“The suspension is pending investigation into alleged sabotage and vandalism of facilities put in place by the government to end the lingering crisis of water scarcity in the state,” t*he commissioner stated. 

He emphasized that where criminal culpability is established, full legal action will be pursued.

Kano, a major commercial hub in northern Nigeria, has grappled with chronic water shortages for years, exacerbated by aging infrastructure, population growth, vandalism, and, in some cases, alleged internal sabotage. 

The Thomas scheme is one of several regional facilities the current administration is actively working to rehabilitate.

Governor Yusuf’s team is already making strides elsewhere, with ongoing rehabilitation works at key sites like Chalawa and Tamburawa. 

The commissioner reaffirmed the governor’s commitment: “*Gov. Abba Kabir-Yusuf is fully committed to restoring Kano’s water infrastructure. We... will ensure that all regional water schemes are fully revitalised.”*

This latest incident underscores a painful reality for many residents: even as public funds are invested in solutions, basic services remain elusive due to decay, theft, and neglect. 

Similar patterns of infrastructure vandalism have plagued water and power projects across parts of Nigeria, often linked to scrap metal scavengers, opportunistic theft, or deeper systemic failures.

Social media reactions to the news reflect a mix of frustration, cautious optimism, and skepticism.

 Many residents welcomed the suspension as a rare display of accountability, with comments highlighting how sabotage of basic amenities like water compounds daily hardships in a region already facing economic pressures. 

Others noted the need for systemic fixes beyond individual suspensions.

The commissioner urged the public to play its part: “*These public assets belong to all of us, and we all must protect them.”*

This development comes against the backdrop of the governor’s proactive stance on governance issues, including recent suspensions in urban planning departments over regulatory violations. 

It sends a strong signal that the administration intends to hold officials responsible while pushing forward with infrastructure renewal.

For Kano’s millions of residents, the real test will be in the speed and effectiveness of repairs, improved security at these sites, and sustained delivery of potable water. 

As investigations proceed, the case could reveal whether this was isolated negligence, criminal enterprise, or symptomatic of deeper challenges in managing public utilities.

In Nigeria’s complex landscape, where access to clean water remains a fundamental yet often unmet need, decisive actions like this offer a glimmer of hope — provided they translate into tangible improvements on the ground. 

The people of Kano, long resilient in the face of scarcity, will be watching closely.
