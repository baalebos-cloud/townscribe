**Lausanne/Rabat—November 24, 2026**— After nearly ten months of uncertainty, legal battles and emotional turmoil, the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) has officially reinstated Senegal as champions of the 2025 Africa Cup of Nations. In a unanimous verdict delivered on Tuesday, the Swiss-based court overturned a controversial Confederation of African Football (CAF) decision that had stripped the Teranga Lions of their title and awarded it to host nation Morocco.

The ruling brings a dramatic close to one of the most chaotic finals in AFCON history. On 18 January 2026 at the Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium in Rabat, Senegal, defeated Morocco 1-0 after extra time. 

Late in normal time, referee Jean-Jacques Ndala awarded a penalty to the hosts. Senegal’s players, furious at the decision, walked off the pitch in protest and remained in the dressing room for about 14 minutes. They eventually returned. 

Morocco’s Brahim Diaz missed the spot-kick, the match went to extra time, and Senegal scored the decisive goal to lift the trophy on the pitch.

Two months later, CAF’s Appeal Board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match under tournament regulations (notably Article 82) by leaving the field without authorization. 

The board awarded Morocco a 3-0 victory and the continental crown. Senegal’s football federation immediately challenged the decision at sport’s highest court.

A three-member CAS panel, chaired by French sports law expert Gérald Simon and including Luigi Fumagalli and Ulrich Haas, heard arguments in Lausanne on 8 October.

In its award published on 24 November, the panel concluded that the referee’s decision to resume the match fell within his sole authority under the “*field of play”* doctrine. Because both teams returned and the game was completed, the forfeit regulations did not apply. The original result therefore stands.

CAS Secretary General Matthieu Reeb confirmed the referee “did not commit any error of law.” The decision reaffirms a fundamental principle of football: the referee’s on-field decisions are final when the match is played to a conclusion.

For Senegal, the ruling restores not only the title but also a second continental championship after their 2021 triumph. 

Players and fans who celebrated wildly in Dakar in January can now do so without the shadow of legal limbo. Morocco, which briefly held the trophy through the CAF process, is confirmed as runners-up.

The verdict ends a long and bitter chapter that tested the integrity of African football’s governance. While CAF and the Moroccan federation had not issued immediate detailed responses at the time of writing, the CAS decision is final on the merits, with only limited procedural recourse possible to the Swiss Federal Tribunal.



Senegal are once again, and definitively, the champion of Africa.
