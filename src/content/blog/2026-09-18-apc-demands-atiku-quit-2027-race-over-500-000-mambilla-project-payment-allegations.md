**Abuja, Nigeria—September 18, 2026**—The All Progressives Congress (APC) Presidential Campaign Council has called on former Vice-President Atiku Abubakar to withdraw from the 2027 presidential race, linking him to a long-running controversy surrounding the Mambilla hydropower project and a $500,000 payment made more than two decades ago.

In a strongly worded statement signed by its spokesperson, Dele Alake, the council accused Atiku, the African Democratic Congress (ADC) candidate, of compromising Nigeria’s national interests in connection with the disputed 3,960-megawatt project in Taraba State. 

The demand follows the recent conclusion of arbitration proceedings at the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC) tribunal in Paris.



**Background to the Mambilla Dispute**

The ICC tribunal ruled in favour of the Federal Government of Nigeria, dismissing claims brought by Sunrise Power and Transmission Company Limited and its promoter, Leno Adesanya. 

The company had sought compensation potentially exceeding $3 billion linked to a purported 2003 Build-Operate-Transfer (BOT) contract for the Mambilla project. 

The tribunal also ordered Sunrise and Adesanya to reimburse Nigeria for 75 percent of its legal costs.

President Bola Tinubu described the victory as clearing the *“single biggest legal hurdle”* that had stalled the project for years, praising the efforts of the Attorney-General’s office and past administrations that defended Nigeria’s position.

Central to the APC’s latest attack is a $500,000 transfer made on January 30, 2003. According to evidence presented before the tribunal, Adesanya moved the funds through China Castle Investments Limited, an offshore company he controlled, into a Citibank account belonging to Jennifer Douglas, who was then married to Atiku. 

The payment occurred less than four months before the purported award of the Mambilla contract on May 22, 2003.

Adesanya told the tribunal the transfer formed part of a foreign-exchange transaction carried out for Atiku. 

The panel examined the timing closely and rejected the explanation that it constituted a genuine foreign-exchange arrangement, noting the “close connection in time” between the payment and the contract award.



**APC’s Allegations and Call for Withdrawal**

Alake argued that the revelations reinforced longstanding concerns about Atiku’s conduct while in public office. 

He accused the former vice-president and then Power Minister Olu Agunloye of facilitating a contract for a company he claimed lacked relevant experience in hydropower generation.

*“The APC Campaign calls on Abubakar Atiku to do himself a rare honour by bowing out of the presidential race, as the $500,000 bribe is just the tip of the iceberg of the revelations to come about his monumental corruption,”* Alake stated. 

The council further claimed Atiku’s actions contributed to the prolonged delay of a project that could have delivered significant economic benefits to Taraba State and the nation.

It is important to note that the ICC tribunal did not make an explicit finding that Atiku personally received a bribe or directed the payment. 

The APC has interpreted the documented transfer, its timing, and the rejected explanation as raising serious questions about his suitability for the highest office.



**Political Context and Reactions**

The Mambilla project has been mired in controversy for over two decades, with successive governments struggling to move it forward amid contractual disputes, financing challenges, and legal battles. 

The latest ICC ruling removes a major obstacle, potentially allowing the project — later rescoped to around 1,500 megawatts — to progress under the current administration.

Atiku has not yet issued a detailed public response to the APC’s latest demand at the time of reporting. 

The former vice-president has previously dismissed similar accusations as politically motivated attacks. 

The call adds fresh intensity to the emerging 2027 contest, where Atiku is positioning himself as a leading opposition figure under the ADC platform.

As Nigeria’s political temperature rises, the exchange underscores how historical infrastructure disputes continue to shape contemporary electoral battles. 

Independent observers note that while the tribunal’s ruling strengthens Nigeria’s legal position, the political weaponization of its findings is likely to dominate headlines in the coming weeks.

The APC campaign council maintains that accountability must begin with those who held high office when the disputed arrangements were made. 

Whether Atiku will heed the call to step aside—or mount a robust defense—remains to be seen.
