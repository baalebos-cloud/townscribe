ABUJA, Nigeria— In a strategic move signaling intensified preparations for the 2027 presidential race, former Vice President and African Democratic Congress (ADC) Presidential Candidate Atiku Abubakar has announced the appointment of two experienced professionals to bolster community engagement and grassroots networks across Nigeria.

Alhaji Aliyu Bello has been named Senior Special Assistant on Community Engagement, while Dr. Victor Moses takes on the role of Special Assistant on Community Engagement. 

The appointments, announced via press release on July 8, 2026, underscore Atiku’s commitment to building inclusive, bottom-up political structures that prioritize youth involvement and local empowerment.

Alhaji Aliyu Bello brings a rich blend of expertise as a journalist, entrepreneur, international trader, and grassroots politician. 

With a Master’s degree in Mass Communication and certifications in leadership and business management, Bello has served two terms as Kaduna State PDP Youth Leader, where he championed greater youth participation in politics. 

He also held the position of Financial Secretary of the Kaduna State PDP chapter before aligning with the ADC. 

His track record in media, business, and community work makes him well-positioned to expand outreach and mentor young leaders.

Dr. Victor Moses, a scholar, public affairs strategist, and entrepreneur with nearly two decades of experience, holds a Ph.D. in Psychology. 

He has served as National Publicity Secretary of the Atiku Support Organisation (ASO) since 2021, playing a pivotal role in stakeholder engagement, behavioral research, and grassroots mobilization. 

His strategic communication skills are expected to enhance Atiku’s connection with diverse communities nationwide.

In the statement from his Media Office, Atiku congratulated the appointees and expressed confidence in their ability to advance the shared vision of a more inclusive and prosperous Nigeria. 

These appointments come as political activities intensify ahead of the 2027 general elections. 

Atiku, who has positioned himself as a bridge-builder with extensive national experience, continues to focus on rebuilding strong community-level networks — a critical component for any successful presidential bid in Nigeria’s diverse political landscape. 

The moves are being viewed positively by supporters as a sign of proactive organization and a renewed emphasis on youth and grassroots development — key themes in Atiku’s long-standing advocacy for good governance, economic reform, and national unity. 

As Nigeria’s political conversation shifts toward the next electoral cycle, appointments like these highlight the importance of experienced, community-focused leadership in shaping the country’s future. 

For Atiku Abubakar and the ADC, the message is clear: building from the ground up remains central to their 2027 ambitions.
