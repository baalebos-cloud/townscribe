A Chicago radio station has broadcast details of Nigerian President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s long-standing U.S. financial records from the early 1990s, linking accounts in his name to an investigation into drug-related activity while he lived in America.

The segment, aired on WBBM Newsradio (780 AM and 105.9 FM), formed part of a morning report and has circulated widely on social media, including through accounts affiliated with opposition figures. 

It draws renewed attention to a civil forfeiture case settled more than three decades ago and to an ongoing Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) lawsuit seeking fuller disclosure of related FBI and DEA files.



**The 1990s Chicago Case in Brief**

U.S. court records from the Northern District of Illinois show that in 1993 authorities pursued civil forfeiture of funds held in bank accounts associated with Tinubu. 

Investigators examined a Chicago-area heroin distribution network active between the late 1980s and early 1990s. 

An Internal Revenue Service special agent’s affidavit described deposits into accounts linked to Tinubu that authorities said did not match his reported legitimate income at the time.

In October 1993 a federal judge ordered the forfeiture of $460,000 from one First Heritage Bank account in Tinubu’s name, stating the funds represented proceeds of narcotics trafficking or were involved in transactions that violated money-laundering statutes. 

Remaining sums exceeding $1 million were released. The proceeding was civil and in rem—directed at the property rather than a criminal prosecution of Tinubu personally. 

He was never arrested, indicted, or convicted of a drug offence in the United States.

Tinubu has consistently denied any wrongdoing. His legal team has long maintained that the matter was a civil settlement only and that he has never faced criminal charges.



**Recent FOIA Developments**

The radio broadcast arrives amid active litigation. American transparency advocate Aaron Greenspan filed multiple FOIA requests between 2022 and 2023 seeking the “entire FBI file” on Tinubu and related interview records from a 1992–1993 investigation. 

In April 2025 a U.S. District Court judge ordered the FBI and DEA to search for and process non-exempt records after rejecting earlier “Glomar” responses that refused to confirm or deny the existence of files.

In a sworn declaration filed on 28 August 2026, the FBI confirmed that Tinubu was a subject of a criminal investigation into drug-trafficking activities in the early 1990s. 

The agency stressed that the confirmation does not establish that he committed any offence. 

It continues to withhold portions of the records, citing privacy, confidential sources, law-enforcement techniques, and potential risks to individuals. 

The FBI has sought permission to submit further justifications to the court in private.



**Political Echoes in Nigeria**

The story has quickly become political fodder in Nigeria. Critics and opposition voices argue that the renewed airing of the records raises questions of transparency and fitness for office ahead of the 2027 elections. 

Supporters counter that the matter was settled civilly more than thirty years ago, that Nigerian courts reviewing the 2023 presidential election found no criminal conviction, and that the focus distracts from governance achievements.

The radio segment itself does not introduce new primary evidence; it restates publicly available historical court documents and the latest FOIA developments. 

Yet its broadcast on a major Chicago news station has amplified the conversation both in the United States and back home.

As the FOIA case continues, the balance between public interest in historical investigative records and individual privacy remains contested. 

For now, the Chicago airwaves have once again placed a decades-old civil forfeiture and the surrounding investigation under the spotlight.
