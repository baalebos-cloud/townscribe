**Abuja, Nigeria – November 20, 2026** — Former Kogi State Governor Yahaya Bello on Friday declared himself the most transparent yet most vilified public figure in the country, expressing firm confidence that he will ultimately be vindicated of the corruption allegations levelled against him by the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC).

Speaking on Channels Television’s **Politics Today**, Bello stated: “I am the most transparent and the most vilified. But at the end of the day, I shall be vindicated.”

The former governor, who served from 2016 to 2024, has consistently denied any wrongdoing. He is currently facing multiple criminal cases filed by the EFCC over alleged misappropriation and money laundering of public funds during his eight-year tenure.



**Ongoing Legal Battles**



One case involves a 16-count charge of criminal breach of trust and money laundering amounting to ₦110.4 billion. Bello is standing trial alongside two former state officials. 

Prosecutors allege that state funds were diverted to acquire properties in highbrow areas of Abuja and Dubai.

In a separate matter before the Federal High Court in Abuja, he faces a 19-count charge involving an alleged ₦80.2 billion fraud and money laundering.

EFCC Chairman Ola Olukoyede had earlier stated that the commission had fulfilled its mandate by investigating and prosecuting the cases, leaving the determination of guilt or innocence to the judiciary.

During the trials, some prosecution witnesses have given evidence that Bello’s name did not appear as a beneficiary, signatory or party to certain disputed transactions involving contracts and property documents. 

In August 2025, the Court of Appeal in Lagos restored an interim forfeiture order covering 14 properties allegedly linked to him.



**Political Ambition Continues**



Despite the ongoing legal challenges, Bello remains the All Progressives Congress (APC) candidate for the Kogi Central Senatorial District in the 2027 general elections. 

A Federal High Court in Abuja recently dismissed a suit challenging his emergence from the party’s primary, allowing his candidacy to stand.

Bello has maintained that the allegations will not survive judicial scrutiny and continues to project confidence as both the court cases and his political ambitions progress.



The matters remain before the courts, and no final determination of guilt has been made.
