**Abuja, Nigeria—November 28, 2026**—The Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) on Saturday declared that widespread public frustration with the current administration has found expression in the organic chant of “Ba ma yi” across parts of the country, particularly in the North. 

The party said Nigerians are increasingly rejecting the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) and looking towards the opposition for relief from what it described as the “Renewed Shege” foisted by inept leadership.

In a statement posted on its official X handle and accompanied by a link to its latest bulletin, the PDP argued that the failed promise of “**Renewed Hope”** has left citizens disillusioned. 

The Hausa phrase “***Ba ma yi,”*** which loosely translates to “***we don’t want you*”** or “*we reject it,”* has in recent months become a familiar expression of political rejection at public events, campaign stops and even religious gatherings involving the President and APC figures.



**Rising Public Sentiment**

The slogan gained wider attention after incidents in which worshippers and residents in northern states chanted it during presidential visits and campaign activities. 

Political observers and media reports have linked the chants to mounting economic pressures, including the high cost of living, inflation, and insecurity. 

While some ruling party voices have suggested the demonstrations are sponsored, others, including opposition figures, maintain they reflect genuine grassroots discontent.

The PDP framed the phenomenon as spontaneous rather than engineered, stating that Nigerians are ***“organically chanting ‘Ba ma yi’ to the ruling party.”*** 

The party used the moment to contrast the current economic realities with the expectations that accompanied the 2023 election of President Bola Tinubu under the Renewed Hope banner.



**Opposition’s Positioning**

By circulating the statement and its party bulletin, the PDP is seeking to capitalize on the prevailing mood and present itself as a credible alternative ahead of the 2027 general elections. 

The opposition has consistently criticized the federal government’s economic reforms, particularly the removal of fuel subsidies and the floating of the naira, arguing that these policies have imposed severe hardship on ordinary citizens.

The party’s message comes at a time when political temperatures are rising across the country. Campaign activities for the next presidential contest are gradually intensifying, and both the ruling party and opposition groups are keenly watching public reactions for signs of electoral mood.

***“Ba ma yi”*** is not entirely new. Versions of the phrase have long been used in northern Nigeria to express disapproval. Its recent political prominence, however, has turned it into a shorthand for rejection of the status quo. Analysts note that such slogans often signal deeper dissatisfaction that can influence voter behaviour if sustained.

The PDP’s intervention is typical of opposition strategy: amplify public grievances, question the government’s delivery on promises, and invite citizens to consider a change of direction. 

Whether the chants will translate into organized political support remains to be seen, but the party is clearly determined to keep the narrative of failed leadership alive.

As Nigeria moves closer to another election cycle, expressions of public discontent such as “Ba ma yi” are likely to feature more prominently in political discourse. 

For now, the PDP has planted its flag firmly on the side of those who feel the promise of Renewed Hope has not been kept.



***This report is based on the official statement released by the Peoples Democratic Party on November 28, 2026, and contextual reporting on the “Ba ma yi” phenomenon.***
