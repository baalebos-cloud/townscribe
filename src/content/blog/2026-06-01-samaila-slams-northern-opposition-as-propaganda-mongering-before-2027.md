**Abuja, Nigeria – June 1, 2026**

Ishaq Samaila, Media Aide to the Special Adviser to the President on Political Matters, declared unwavering support for President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s second-term bid, even if he stands alone among northern voices. 

His fearless statement cuts through mounting opposition criticism and reflects deepening divisions as the 2027 election cycle gathers momentum.

Samaila took to his X handle on Monday to make his position crystal clear. 

*“Even if I become the only Northerner standing openly for President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s second term, I will do it proudly, loudly, and without fear,”* he wrote. 

He dismissed opposition attacks as mere propaganda, insisting that Nigerians already notice tangible differences under Tinubu’s leadership.

Transitioning from criticism to context, Samaila accused past opposition leaders of leaving behind insecurity and hardship during their time in power. He argued they now pose as saviors only because elections loom. 

*“The opposition can keep shouting, spreading propaganda, and sponsoring attacks online,” he stated, “but Nigerians are wiser now.”* 

His words echo a growing narrative among Tinubu loyalists who emphasize continuity over change.

**Strategic Timing Amid National Challenges**

This outspoken endorsement arrives at a critical moment. President Tinubu recently marked his third anniversary in office amid mixed public reactions. 

While macroeconomic indicators show progress in areas like stock market performance and infrastructure projects, many citizens still grapple with high living costs and security concerns. 

Samaila, however, frames the current hardships as necessary sacrifices for long-term stability. 

He insists 2027 must focus on “continuity, courage, and leadership” rather than “empty promises or emotional speeches.”

Political analysts note that northern support remains pivotal for any presidential candidate. Several northern groups have already signaled backing for Tinubu. 

For instance, the Northern Youth Frontiers declared him their candidate early this year, citing zoning principles and reform continuity. 

Similarly, Defence Minister Christopher Musa recently urged continued support for the President’s bold reforms. 

These voices counter pockets of northern discontent fueled by economic pressures and persistent insecurity in parts of the region.

Samaila’s defiance highlights the personal risks some loyalists are willing to take. 

In a polarized environment where online attacks and political intimidation have become common, his declaration of “No intimidation. No pressure. No retreat. 

*"Tinubu 2027 straight”* carries significant weight. It also underscores internal APC efforts to consolidate northern backing ahead of the polls.

**Broader Implications for 2027 Politics**

Observers view this statement as part of a larger strategy to rally core supporters. 

Atiku Abubakar and other opposition figures actively position themselves as alternatives, yet Tinubu’s camp continues to project strength through experienced allies. 

Samaila’s role as a media aide gives his words added institutional resonance, even as independent northern voices express skepticism about re-electing the President.

Nevertheless, the road to 2027 remains complex. 

While some northern governors and youth groups rally behind Tinubu, others point to unresolved challenges like farmer-herder clashes and youth unemployment. 

The administration counters by highlighting reforms in security architecture, agriculture, and infrastructure that aim to deliver lasting benefits.

As political realignments accelerate, statements like Samaila’s humanize the passion driving Nigeria’s democracy. 

They reveal how individual conviction can clash with collective sentiment in a nation hungry for both progress and relief. 

Whether this lone northern voice inspires more supporters or faces isolation will unfold in the coming months.

For now, Ishaq Samaila stands firm. His message sends a clear signal: loyalty to Tinubu’s vision will not be silenced easily, regardless of the political storms ahead.
