**Washington / Maryland – October 6, 2026**—President Donald Trump delivered a blunt assessment of the ongoing U.S. campaign against Iran on Tuesday, praising American service members for eliminating “dozens of Iran’s terrorist leaders” and sending them “straight through to the gates of hell.”

Speaking at a public event, Trump framed the results as a hard-won victory achieved by the men and women of the U.S. Armed Forces. 

*“Thanks to the men and women of America’s Armed Forces, dozens of Iran’s terrorist leaders have been blown out of existence and straight through to the gates of hell,”* he said.

The president went further, claiming successive waves of Iranian leadership had been removed. “*As you know, their leaders are gone. Their second group of leaders are gone. And the biggest problem I have is nobody knows who the hell is running the country. Nobody knows, but maybe that’s a good thing,”* Trump told the audience.



**Military Gains Claimed**

Trump detailed what he described as sweeping damage to Iran’s military capabilities. He said the Iranian navy had been destroyed, with its ships *“lying at the bottom of the sea.”* 

The air force, he claimed, no longer exists. Radar systems and anti-aircraft equipment had been eliminated, while missile and drone manufacturing capacity had been sharply reduced and would soon be gone entirely.

He also pointed to severe economic pressure on Tehran, citing high inflation and reports of officials acknowledging the country’s dire condition.

The remarks come against the backdrop of a prolonged U.S.-Iran conflict that began with major joint U.S.-Israeli strikes in late February 2026. 

Those initial operations killed Iran’s longtime Supreme Leader and numerous senior commanders. Subsequent rounds of strikes have targeted military infrastructure, leadership figures, and assets linked to Tehran’s support for regional proxies.



**Broader Context and Reactions**

Trump has repeatedly used strong language about the campaign, previously describing Iranian leaders as having been *“wiped from the face of the earth”* and framing the effort as necessary to prevent a nuclear-armed Iran and end decades of what he calls state-sponsored terrorism. 

Administration officials have previously claimed significant degradation of Iran’s navy, air force, missile stocks, and command structures.

Supporters of the operations view the president’s latest comments as confirmation that targeted pressure on the regime’s terrorist infrastructure is working. 

Critics, meanwhile, have raised concerns about the human and economic costs of the extended conflict, the risk of further regional escalation, and the challenges of achieving a stable end-state when leadership succession remains unclear.

Trump closed the relevant portion of his remarks by reiterating that Iran would never obtain a nuclear weapon—a goal he has described as non-negotiable throughout the campaign.

As the conflict continues, the president’s stark language underscores both the intensity of the military effort and the uncertainty still surrounding Iran’s internal power structure. 

Whether the reported elimination of multiple leadership tiers will force a negotiated settlement or prolong the fighting remains the central question facing policymakers in Washington and Tehran.
