**Abuja, Nigeria – July 21, 2026** – In a dramatic revelation that has sent shockwaves through Nigeria’s security and political landscape, Senator Ali Ndume (APC, Borno South) has accused an individual identified as Kaigame of attempting to use him to facilitate a massive N5 billion ransom demand supposedly from Boko Haram insurgents.

Speaking candidly in a recent interview, the outspoken senator detailed how Kaigame allegedly contacted him, claiming the terrorist group was demanding the huge sum. 

Ndume firmly distanced himself from any involvement, stating: “*Kaigame wanted to use me for the payment of ransom. I cannot speak for the government if the ransom was paid or not.* 

*I do not and will never support the payment of ransom or negotiations with Boko Haram.”*

Senator Ndume, who represents a region heavily affected by Boko Haram insurgency, has long been vocal on security matters. 

His latest disclosure highlights the complex and often shadowy world of ransom demands that have plagued families, communities, and even high-profile figures in Northeast Nigeria for over a decade.

The senator’s refusal to be drawn into the alleged scheme underscores a principled stance against yielding to terrorists. 

His comments come amid ongoing national debates about how best to tackle insurgency — with many experts and citizens opposing ransom payments that only empower and enrich criminal networks.

Nigeria has faced numerous high-profile kidnapping cases linked to Boko Haram and other armed groups, particularly in the North. 

Ransom demands running into millions — and sometimes billions — of naira have become a disturbing business model for insurgents and bandits alike. 

Families often pay quietly to secure releases, while the government maintains an official policy against negotiating with terrorists.

Ndume’s allegation, if substantiated, raises serious questions about intermediaries who may exploit victims and security situations for personal gain. 

It also draws attention to the immense pressure placed on influential individuals to intervene in such cases.

The revelation adds to the already heavy discourse on insecurity in Nigeria. With Boko Haram and its splinter groups still active despite military campaigns, any claims involving ransom demands fuel public anxiety and demands for greater transparency from authorities.

Senator Ndume’s position reinforces a hardline approach: no ransom, no negotiation. 

This stance aligns with the official government position but contrasts with the painful reality faced by many affected families who feel abandoned when the state cannot secure quick releases.

Throughout my career reporting on Nigeria’s security challenges, I have seen how ransom demands devastate communities and prolong conflicts. 

Senator Ndume’s willingness to speak publicly about personal pressure is commendable — it shines light on a murky underworld that thrives in silence.

His message is clear: yielding to terrorists only breeds more terror. As Nigeria continues its fight against insurgency, greater focus must be placed on dismantling the financial networks that sustain these groups, protecting vulnerable citizens, and ensuring accountability for those who might seek to profit from the suffering of others.

The senator’s courage in calling out the alleged scheme may encourage others in positions of influence to resist similar pressures and speak out. 

Ultimately, defeating terrorism requires not just military might but moral clarity and collective resolve.
