**LONDON** — In a case that has sent shockwaves through British political circles, police in southwest England have arrested a 26-year-old man on suspicion of murdering Ann Widdecombe, the former Conservative minister and prominent Reform UK figure, whose body was discovered with serious injuries at her rural Dartmoor home.

Devon and Cornwall Police confirmed the arrest on Friday, July 10, 2026, just hours after launching a full murder investigation. 

The suspect, described as a white British national, was detained at an address in Newton Abbot — less than 10 miles from Widdecombe’s home in Haytor Vale — and remains in custody. 

Authorities emphasized that the incident is *not being treated as terrorism-related or politically motivated.*

Officers were called to the 78-year-old’s detached bungalow around 11:40 a.m. on Thursday, July 9, by the ambulance service. 

Widdecombe was found dead with serious injuries. Forensic examinations continue at the scene as the murder inquiry advances rapidly.

Assistant Chief Constable Matt Longman described the case as being in its *“early stages but moving at a significant pace.”* Earlier appeals for information about a white male suspect were followed quickly by the arrest.

Ann Widdecombe enjoyed a long and colorful career in public life. She served as a Conservative MP for Maidstone from 1987 to 2010, holding junior ministerial posts under Prime Minister John Major, including as Prisons Minister. 

Known for her staunchly conservative views — particularly her opposition to abortion and support for traditional family values — she later converted to Catholicism and became a vocal Brexiteer.

After leaving Parliament, Widdecombe appeared on popular television shows such as **Strictly Come Dancing** (famously receiving low scores but winning public affection) and **Celebrity Big Brother**. 

She served as an MEP for Nigel Farage’s Brexit Party and most recently acted as immigration spokesperson for Reform UK.

Tributes poured in from across the political spectrum. Prime Minister Keir Starmer described the suspect as *“clearly dangerous.*” 

Former Prime Minister Boris Johnson called her *“a heroic Brexiteer and a great speaker.”* Nigel Farage and others highlighted her fearlessness and authenticity.

This tragedy marks the third murder of a sitting or former British MP in recent years, following the killings of Labour’s Jo Cox in 2016 and Conservative Sir David Amess in 2021. 

While those attacks were politically or ideologically driven, police have stressed no such link here.

The remote Dartmoor location of Widdecombe’s home — a 1970s bungalow named “Widdecombe’s Rest” — has added to the sense of vulnerability. 

As the investigation continues, questions are being raised about safety for public figures, even in retirement.

For many, Widdecombe’s death feels like the loss of a larger-than-life character who never shied away from controversy. 

Whether on the floor of the House of Commons, in reality TV, or championing Brexit, she remained unapologetically herself — a “no-nonsense” voice in an often divided political landscape.

As the suspect remains in custody and forensic work proceeds, the nation mourns a woman whose decades of service left an indelible mark. 

Her family, friends, and colleagues now face the painful task of grieving while the search for answers and justice unfolds. 

This remains a developing story.
