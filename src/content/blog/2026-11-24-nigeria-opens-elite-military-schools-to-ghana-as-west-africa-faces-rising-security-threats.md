**Abuja, November 24, 2026 —** Nigeria has once again asserted itself as the powerhouse of professional military education in West Africa, as the Nigerian Army on Monday approved expanded training opportunities for officers and soldiers of the Ghanaian Armed Forces.

The approval was given by the Chief of Army Staff (COAS), Lieutenant General Waidi Shaibu, when the Defence Attaché of the Republic of Ghana to Nigeria, Commodore Bright Atiayao, paid him a courtesy visit at the Army Headquarters, Abuja, on Tuesday, November 24, 2026.

General Shaibu said the decision reflects Nigeria’s unwavering commitment to strengthening regional defence cooperation, enhancing military professionalism, and promoting collective security across the ECOWAS subregion.

According to the Army Chief, the increasingly complex and evolving security challenges confronting West African countries — from terrorism and insurgency to banditry, piracy, and transnational crime — demand stronger military collaboration, sustained intelligence sharing, and enhanced operational interoperability.

> “
>
> *No single country can secure the region alone,” Shaibu stressed, noting that the Nigerian Army, with its established training institutions, expanding capabilities, and extensive operational experience, remains ready to support the professional development of personnel from sister African armies.*

In furtherance of this commitment, the COAS approved training slots for Ghanaian officers at the prestigious Nigerian Army College of Logistics and Management (NACOLM). 

The slots cover the Junior Leadership and Staff Courses for Captains and the Logistics Management Courses for senior officers.

He equally approved training opportunities for Ghanaian officers and soldiers at the newly established Nigerian Army Airborne School, Nigeria’s elite parachute and air-landing training institution.

In a major show of institutional solidarity, General Shaibu also expressed Nigeria’s readiness to facilitate the exchange of Directing Staff upon the establishment of Ghana’s proposed military college, a move analysts say will fast-track Accra’s military academic development.

The COAS maintained that Nigeria’s sustained investment in military education, specialized training, indigenous defence technology, and force modernization has positioned the Nigerian Army to contribute meaningfully to defence capacity development within the ECOWAS subregion.

He added that strengthening the capabilities of neighbouring armies is essential to building a more resilient, coordinated, and responsive regional security architecture capable of addressing emerging threats.

Earlier, Commodore Atiayao commended General Shaibu for his transformational leadership, particularly the ongoing expansion of the Nigerian Army from 8 to 12 Divisions, advancements in indigenous defence technology, improved personnel accommodation, and sustained modernization efforts.

He acknowledged Nigeria’s growing military capabilities and strategic role in advancing regional peace and stability, while expressing Ghana’s desire to deepen military cooperation with Nigeria, particularly in professional training, institutional development, and knowledge exchange.

Atiayao noted that the new training opportunities would further strengthen the longstanding defence relationship between both countries and enhance their collective capacity to address shared security challenges.

The statement was signed by Colonel Appolonia Anele, Acting Director, Army Public Relations, on November 24, 2026.
