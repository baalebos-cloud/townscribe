**Abuja, Nigeria – October 6, 2026**—The Executive Chairman of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), Ola Olukoyede, has been named a recipient of the Heroes of Democracy Award 2026 in recognition of his leadership in the battle against corruption and his role in national development.

The honour was conferred during a ceremony held in Abuja on Saturday, September 26, 2026, as part of the 66th Nigeria Democracy Lecture. 

The event carried the theme *“Politics, Governance, and the Impact of Leadership in Nigeria’s Democracy.”* Organizers described Olukoyede as one of the major actors shaping the nation’s progress through his determined anti-graft efforts.

Olukoyede was represented at the award presentation by Assistant Commander of the EFCC (ACE II) Aisha Mohammed, Head of the Commission’s Enlightenment and Reorientation Unit. 

In a statement delivered on his behalf, the EFCC chairman emphasized that the fight against corruption is fundamentally a fight for democracy itself.

*“Heroes are builders; they are architects of enduring designs. They are pathfinders and the truest enablers of possibilities,”* the statement noted. “*Today we celebrate those who fought, and those who still fight, to ensure that the voice of the people is not silenced. And that is why the fight against corruption is a fight for democracy.”*

He further linked the Commission’s work to tangible national benefits, stating that recovered funds are channelled into initiatives such as the Nigerian Education Loan Fund. 

The message also stressed the importance of protecting Nigerian talent from cybercrime and using culture as a foundation for integrity, hard work, and national identity.

National Coordinator and organizer of the event, Idakwo Oruma, explained that the Heroes Award was created to publicly acknowledge service, sacrifice, integrity, cultural preservation, and contributions to democratic growth. 

He described the ceremony as an opportunity for national reflection and encouragement of responsible leadership and citizenship.

Among other dignitaries present was the Chairman of the Code of Conduct Tribunal, Dr. Mainasara Umar Kogo.

The recognition comes against the backdrop of significant operational results recorded under Olukoyede’s leadership. 

Official figures released by the EFCC show that between October 2023 and mid-2026 the agency recovered more than ₦1.23 trillion, secured over 10,800 convictions, and handled tens of thousands of petitions and investigations covering advance-fee fraud, cybercrime, money laundering, and other economic offences.

Supporters of the award see it as affirmation of the Commission’s intensified focus on recoveries and prosecutions. 

The honour also aligns with broader conversations about the role of strong institutions in safeguarding Nigeria’s democratic journey more than two decades after the return to civilian rule.

As the EFCC continues its work, the Heroes of Democracy Award places Olukoyede among figures publicly celebrated for efforts aimed at protecting public resources and reinforcing the values that sustain national development.

This report is based on the official statement from the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission and related public records.
