**Lagos, Nigeria – July 24, 2026** — In a significant victory for Nigeria’s anti-narcotics efforts, the Federal High Court in Lagos has ordered the National Drug Law Enforcement Agency (NDLEA) to destroy a sprawling ₦480 billion methamphetamine production facility located deep in the Abidagba Forest, Iloti Village, Ijebu East Local Government Area of Ogun State.

The ruling, delivered by Justice Musa Kakaki, follows a dramatic on-site court inspection earlier this week at the remote industrial-scale laboratory, which authorities describe as one of the largest of its kind ever discovered in the country.



**Inside the Clandestine Operation**

NDLEA operatives uncovered the sophisticated meth lab in May 2026 after months of intelligence gathering. T

he facility, hidden in a dense forest, featured advanced equipment, large quantities of precursor chemicals, and was allegedly capable of producing massive volumes of methamphetamine primarily for export markets.

Prosecutors presented evidence of approximately 2,419 kilograms of methamphetamine and related materials seized from the site, with the total estimated street value exceeding ₦480 billion. 

The operation was reportedly linked to the Anochili Innocent Drug Trafficking Organisation.

Ten suspects, including three Mexicans and seven Nigerians, face an 11-count charge bordering on conspiracy, unlawful production, possession of methamphetamine, and drug trafficking. 

They have pleaded not guilty. The case has drawn international attention due to the involvement of foreign nationals and the lab’s scale.



**Court’s Landmark On-Site Visit**

In a rare move, Justice Kakaki led a special court sitting and inspection tour of the facility this week. Legal teams, including defence counsel and prosecutors, examined the premises, equipment, and chemicals firsthand. 

The visit aimed to verify evidence and address defence arguments that no finished methamphetamine product was directly presented.

NDLEA officials argued strongly for immediate destruction of the lab to prevent any potential reactivation or security risks, emphasizing the facility’s role in a growing trend of international cartels using Nigeria as a production and transit hub, particularly in the Southwest region.

This major bust and subsequent court order underscore ongoing challenges in combating sophisticated transnational drug networks operating in Nigeria’s forests and remote areas. 

The NDLEA chairman highlighted the need for sustained vigilance, as such facilities not only fuel local addiction crises but also position the country unfavorably in the global narcotics trade.

The destruction of the lab will allow authorities to safely dispose of hazardous materials and equipment while preserving necessary evidence for the ongoing trial. Legal proceedings against the suspects continue.

This development sends a strong message that Nigeria is intensifying its fight against large-scale drug manufacturing and trafficking, with the judiciary playing a pivotal role in supporting enforcement agencies.

As the trial progresses, Nigerians and the international community will be watching closely for outcomes that could deter similar operations in the future.
