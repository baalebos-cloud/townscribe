**Minna, Niger State—September 17, 2026**—At least 33 suspected illegal miners died while in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) in Minna, triggering public shock, official investigations, and growing calls for accountability.

The deceased were among scores of suspects arrested during *“burst operations”* conducted by the NSCDC Niger State Command on September 15 and 16 at M.I. Wushishi and Lukoto villages. 

The operations formed part of renewed efforts to curb illegal mining activities in the state and also led to the recovery of various exhibits.

In the early hours of Thursday, September 17, many of the detainees were found dead inside the cell. The state Commandant, Suberu Siyaka Aniviye, said the deaths followed a “*suspected outbreak of disease.”* 

The bodies have been deposited at the General Hospital, Minna, for medical examination to determine the exact cause of death.

Hospital sources cited by multiple outlets indicated the number of dead stood at 33 and suggested diphtheria as a possible cause, though this remains unconfirmed by official autopsy results. 

Six other detainees were reportedly taken to the hospital for treatment.



**Official Response and Investigation**

The NSCDC appealed for calm, stating it is working with sister security agencies to manage the situation. The command urged the public and families of the deceased to refrain from any actions that could lead to a breakdown of law and order.

The Niger State Police Command has opened an investigation. Commissioner of Police Adamu Abdullahi Elleman directed the Deputy Commissioner of Police in charge of the State Criminal Investigation Department (SCID) in Minna to take charge of the probe and establish the circumstances surrounding the deaths. 

The police appealed for credible information from the public.



**Questions Over Detention Conditions**

Security analyst Zagazola Makama described the deaths as “deeply disturbing,” especially against the backdrop of the earlier killing of an NSCDC personnel member shot in the head during an operation at an illegal mining site more than a month ago. 

He questioned how so many people could die in custody without immediate medical intervention and called for a thorough, independent investigation into the condition of the detainees at arrest, detention conditions, food and exposure risks, the medical response, and the exact timeline of events.

Reports of protests and unrest in parts of Minna have emerged, with residents and families seeking clearer answers beyond official assurances.



**Broader Context of Illegal Mining Tensions**

Illegal mining remains a persistent security and economic challenge in Niger State. Clashes between enforcement teams and artisanal miners have previously resulted in fatalities on both sides, including the death of NSCDC officer ASC II Aminu Eshin in July during a confrontation at a mining site in Paikoro Local Government Area.

Authorities maintain that operations against illegal mining are necessary to protect lives, the environment, and the nation’s solid minerals. 

Critics, however, insist that the scale of deaths in state custody demands transparent answers about overcrowding, health screening, and duty of care.

As autopsy results and the police investigation proceed, families of the deceased and the wider public await a full accounting of what happened inside the NSCDC facility in Minna.
