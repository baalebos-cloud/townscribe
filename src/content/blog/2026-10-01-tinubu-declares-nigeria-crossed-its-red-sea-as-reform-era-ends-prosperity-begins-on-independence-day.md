**Abuja, Nigeria – October 1, 2026** — President Bola Ahmed Tinubu on Thursday declared that Nigeria has “passed through our own Red Sea” and corrected its economic course, announcing the end of the reform era and the beginning of a new age of shared prosperity as the nation marked 66 years of independence.

In a nationwide broadcast themed *“From Reforms to Prosperity,”* Tinubu painted a picture of a country that had endured deep structural weaknesses, rising poverty, and declining hope by the time he assumed office in 2023. 

He compared the previous economic state to a patient requiring painful treatment, insisting the difficult reforms of the past three years were necessary medicine rather than the disease itself.

*“Nigeria has corrected its course. We have passed through our own Red Sea. This is not the time to look back,”* the President said. *“Let us go forward together, with faith in ourselves, faith in our country, and faith that the sacrifices we have made will yield their reward.”*



**Key Claims of Progress**

Tinubu pointed to several indicators of recovery. He said the economy had grown by more than 4 percent in 2026, with contributions from both oil and non-oil sectors. 

Inflation had fallen substantially from its peak, foreign reserves had been rebuilt, the foreign exchange market had stabilised, and oil theft had declined. 

Non-oil exports, he noted, hit a record high of more than $6 billion in 2025.

The President described the current moment as a turning point. “*The emergency treatment is over. The foundation has been repaired,*” he stated. 

*“For three years, our overriding purpose was to correct our nation’s course. Now, our purpose is simple: shared and widespread prosperity.”*

He defined that prosperity in practical terms: farmers able to cultivate safely and earn decent returns, factories with reliable power, businesses accessing credit, young people finding productive work, and families able to afford food, transport and education.



**Looking Ahead, Not Backward**

Tinubu urged Nigerians not to reverse the reforms, particularly warning against any return to *“addictive subsidies.”* 

He acknowledged that the journey of nationhood had often been too hard for too many people, citing past experiences of war, military rule, economic crises, insecurity and political upheaval. 

Yet he credited the resilience of ordinary Nigerians — farmers, traders, teachers and young entrepreneurs — for keeping the country going.

*“The age of reform has done its work. Now begins the age of prosperity,*” he declared. The *“Promised Land,”* he said, is a Nigeria of abundance and opportunity where prosperity is broadly shared and every child can dream beyond the circumstances of birth.

The address comes as the country continues to grapple with the lingering effects of high living costs, even as government officials highlight improved macroeconomic numbers. 

It also arrives ahead of the 2027 general elections, with economic policy and the cost of living expected to remain central issues.

Tinubu closed on a note of collective resolve: *“Our destination is in sight. Our foundations are strong. Our direction is clear. So let us go forward. No looking back. Happy Independence Day, my fellow Nigerians.”*
