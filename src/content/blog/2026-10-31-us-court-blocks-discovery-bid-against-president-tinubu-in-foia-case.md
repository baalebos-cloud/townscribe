**Washington, D.C.—October 31, 2026—**A United States District Court for the District of Columbia has blocked an attempt by plaintiff Aaron Greenspan to secure limited discovery from Nigerian President Bola Ahmed Tinubu and political consulting firm DCI Group in an ongoing Freedom of Information Act (FOIA) lawsuit.

In a ruling delivered on October 30, the court found that Greenspan’s requested discovery—which focused on alleged cyberattacks against his website—was not sufficiently relevant to the core issues of the FOIA case. 

The litigation primarily concerns record requests submitted to the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) and the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA).



**Key Rulings in the Decision**

Alongside rejecting the discovery motion against President Tinubu and DCI Group, the court:



\- Denied Greenspan’s motion for summary judgment  

\- Dismissed a request by non-party Karl Von Batten to file an amicus curiae (friend-of-the-court) brief  

\- Rejected Greenspan’s accompanying motion for judicial notice  

Attorney Christopher W. Carmichael noted that the decision reinforces the need to keep the proceedings strictly within their primary legal scope. 

He stated that the court made clear the FOIA litigation cannot be used to pursue unrelated matters.

Attorney Oluwole Afolabi added that while the underlying FOIA claims remain before the court, the rulings establish important procedural boundaries and clarify which issues are appropriate for consideration as the case progresses.



**Background of the Case**

The FOIA suit involves requests for government records. Greenspan had sought to expand the proceedings by seeking discovery related to claimed cyberattacks, bringing President Tinubu and the consulting firm into the matter. The court declined to allow that expansion.

Special Adviser to the President on Media and Public Communication, Sunday Dare, shared the development, describing it as a significant procedural victory that limits the scope of the litigation.

The FOIA claims against the FBI and DEA continue, but the latest rulings prevent the case from branching into broader or unrelated inquiries involving the Nigerian president.

***This report is based on the statement released by Sunday Dare on October 31, 2026, detailing the U.S. District Court ruling of October 30.***
