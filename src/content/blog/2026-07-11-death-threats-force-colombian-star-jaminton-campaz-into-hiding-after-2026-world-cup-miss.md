**BOGOTÁ, Colombia** — In a disturbing reminder of football’s darkest pressures, Colombian midfielder Jaminton Campaz has become the target of death threats after missing a golden scoring opportunity in the 2026 FIFA World Cup Round of 16, forcing him to skip the team’s return flight home amid serious safety concerns.

The Colombia Football Federation (FCF) issued a strong condemnation on July 11 (Korean time), stating it *“strongly condemns the threats to the life and safety of Hamilton Campas (26) \[also known as Jaminton Campaz] and his family.”* 

The federation emphasized that no athlete or their loved ones should face intimidation simply for representing their country.

Colombia’s campaign ended dramatically on July 8 in Vancouver when they lost to Switzerland 0-0 after extra time, falling 4-3 in the penalty shootout. 

Campaz, substituted in during the second half, had a decisive one-on-one chance in extra time but fired his shot wide. 

He later converted his penalty in the shootout, yet the miss became the focal point of intense online backlash.

Social media platforms flooded with abuse, including direct death threats against Campaz and his family.

According to reports, the 26-year-old attacking midfielder, who plays club football for Rosario Central in Argentina, did not board the flight back with his teammates due to these credible risks.

In a heartfelt social media post, Campaz addressed the Colombian public with humility and dignity. 

*“I understand how deeply saddened the Colombian people feel. I sincerely apologize for failing to deliver the joy everyone hoped for,”* he wrote. 

He urged fans to maintain respect: “We may hold different opinions, feel frustration, or sorrow, but no passion can justify hatred or force someone to live in fear.”

The FCF has called on authorities to investigate and punish those responsible for the threatening messages, highlighting the need to protect players from toxic fan behavior.

This incident tragically revives memories of one of football’s most infamous tragedies. 

During the 1994 FIFA World Cup in the United States, Colombian defender Andrés Escobar scored a heartbreaking own goal against the host nation, contributing to Colombia’s group-stage exit. 

Upon returning home to Medellín, Escobar was murdered outside a bar, shot multiple times in an act widely linked to fan rage and the dangerous intersection of football, betting, and organized crime in Colombia at the time.

Escobar’s killing remains a painful scar on Colombian football history, symbolizing the extreme pressures players face when national expectations collide with harsh realities on the pitch.

The case of Jaminton Campaz underscores ongoing challenges in global football: the toxic blend of intense passion, social media amplification, and unrealistic expectations placed on athletes. 

While fans invest emotionally in their teams, especially during high-stakes tournaments like the FIFA World Cup 2026, crossing into threats and violence crosses a fundamental line of human decency.

As Colombia reflects on another early exit and the personal toll on one of its rising talents, the incident serves as a call for better mental health support for players, stronger platform accountability on social media, and a cultural shift toward more constructive criticism in sports.

Football federations worldwide, FIFA included, continue to grapple with fan behavior issues, but cases like this highlight the urgent need for proactive measures to protect athletes and their families from real-world harm stemming from on-field disappointments.

For Jaminton Campaz, a young player with significant promise, the focus now shifts from the missed opportunity on the pitch to ensuring his safety and well-being off it. In the beautiful game, passion should inspire — never terrorize.
