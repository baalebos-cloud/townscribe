**Abuja / Ibadan / Enugu – August 4, 2026** — A viral social media claim is intensifying public debate over Nigeria’s security crisis, with a netizen asserting that recent high-profile kidnappings in Oyo and Enugu states form a deliberate pattern targeting political and religious voices critical of the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) and President Bola Tinubu.

The X user, @ibiyemiibro, wrote: ***“Seyi declared to run… They kidnapped children from his State. The Cardinal said the truth to power… They kidnapped members of his faith. The people that are sponsoring and enabling the terrorists in Nigeria are in plain sight.”***

The post, amplified by Parallel Facts and others, has sparked widespread discussion, with many Nigerians expressing frustration over persistent insecurity and questioning official narratives.

**The Oyo School Abductions**

On May 15, 2026, armed gunmen attacked three schools in Oriire Local Government Area of Oyo State — Community Grammar School, Baptist Nursery and Primary School, and L.A. Primary School in the Esiele and Yawota communities. 

Dozens of pupils and teachers were abducted.

The incident occurred around the period Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde publicly declared his intention to contest the 2027 presidential election, initially under platforms linked to the Peoples Democratic Party and Allied Peoples Movement.

After 56 days in captivity, the victims—reported as at least 39 pupils and several teachers—were rescued by security forces on July 10, 2026. 

President Tinubu welcomed the development, stating his administration does not negotiate with or pay ransom to criminals while praising the security agencies involved.



**The Enugu Church Attack**

Just days ago, on Sunday, August 2, 2026, gunmen stormed St. Joseph Catholic Church in Inoyi, Affa, Udi Local Government Area of Enugu State during Mass. 

They abducted a seminarian (who had returned home for his father’s burial), a catechist, and several worshippers before fleeing into the bush. Local youths attempted a chase but were forced to retreat under fire.

The attack came after Cardinal John Onaiyekan, former Archbishop of Abuja, publicly challenged the Tinubu administration over its handling of insecurity. 

In mid-July interviews, the Cardinal stated the government had “no excuse” for failing to end kidnappings and questioned why criminal camps continued to operate.



**Claims, Context and Public Reaction**

The netizen’s post frames these incidents as more than coincidence, suggesting a strategy of intimidation against those who challenge the status quo. 

Supporters of the theory point to a broader pattern of violence in opposition strongholds or against critical voices. 

Critics of the claim dismiss it as speculative and dangerous, arguing that insecurity is a nationwide crisis driven by complex factors, including banditry, poverty, and weak security architecture, rather than direct political sponsorship.

Security agencies have not publicly linked the attacks to any political motivation. 

The Federal Government continues to attribute the violence to criminal and terrorist elements and insists on its commitment to restoring peace.

On social media, reactions range from outrage and calls for mass action to demands for deeper investigations into the sponsors of terrorism. 

Some users argue that repeated high-profile attacks after political or moral challenges erode public trust and fuel conspiracy theories in the absence of transparent accountability.



**A Nation Under Pressure**

Nigeria continues to grapple with one of its most severe insecurity challenges in recent years, with school abductions, church attacks, and rural banditry claiming lives and disrupting communities across multiple regions. 

The rescue of the Oyo schoolchildren was welcomed as a rare success, yet the subsequent Enugu church abduction has reinforced a sense of vulnerability even in places of worship.

As the 2027 election cycle gathers momentum, incidents like these risk deepening political polarization. 

For ordinary citizens, the priority remains safety: parents want their children protected in school, worshippers want to attend Mass without fear, and communities want assurance that the state can protect them regardless of political affiliation.

Whether the netizen’s stark conclusion holds water or not, the underlying demand is clear — Nigerians are tired of living in fear and are calling for decisive, transparent action against the architects and enablers of terror, whoever they may be.
