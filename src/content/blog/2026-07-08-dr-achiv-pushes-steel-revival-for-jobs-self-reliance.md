**ABUJA, Nigeria** — In a timely and optimistic push for national development, the National President of the Nigerian Metallurgical Society, Dr. Fidelis Achiv, has strongly lobbied the Federal Government to prioritize the revitalization of Nigeria’s steel sector as a cornerstone for achieving long-term industrial ambitions.

Speaking on NTA’s flagship morning program **Good Morning Nigeria**, Dr. Achiv emphasized the need for “*adequate parameters*” — including policy consistency, infrastructure upgrades, investment incentives, and public-private partnerships — to transform the sector from a symbol of unrealized potential into a driver of economic growth. 

The discussion, themed “The Steel Sector & Nigeria’s Industrial Future,” highlighted steel’s critical role in construction, manufacturing, defense, and technology.

**A Sector Poised for Comeback**

Nigeria is blessed with abundant iron ore deposits, yet the steel industry has historically underperformed despite massive past investments, most notably the iconic but long-dormant Ajaokuta Steel Complex. 

Decades of challenges — including policy inconsistencies, funding gaps, technical issues, and legal disputes — have kept production far below capacity. 

However, recent government commitments signal renewed hope.

The Federal Government has outlined ambitious plans, including a 10-year roadmap for the steel and non-ferrous metals industry, targets to produce millions of tonnes annually, and strategic partnerships aimed at reviving key assets like Ajaokuta. 

Initiatives such as MoUs with international investors and integration with defense production underscore a multifaceted approach to industrialization.

Dr. Achiv’s intervention comes at a pivotal moment. Experts agree that a vibrant steel sector could create thousands of direct and indirect jobs, reduce import dependency (Nigeria currently imports significant quantities of steel products), boost GDP, and support infrastructure projects across the country. 

It aligns with broader goals of economic diversification away from oil reliance.

**Path Forward: Practical Steps and Optimism**

Stakeholders, including the Nigerian Metallurgical Society, advocate for declaring a state of emergency in the metals sector, accelerating modernization of facilities, addressing power supply challenges for energy-intensive operations, and fostering skills development in metallurgy and engineering. 

Dr. Achiv’s message carries a tone of cautious optimism: with the right frameworks in place, Nigeria can move from importing finished steel to becoming a regional exporter, fostering innovation and self-reliance. 

As the government pushes forward with reforms under the current administration, voices like Dr. Achiv’s serve as important reminders that industrial dreams require sustained commitment, transparency, and collaboration. 

For millions of Nigerians yearning for economic opportunities and national pride, revitalizing steel could represent a tangible step toward a more prosperous future.
