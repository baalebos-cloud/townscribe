The Executive Chairman of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), Ola Olukoyede, on Friday, November 6, 2026, charged retired officers of the anti-graft agency to remain good ambassadors of the Commission and continue protecting its hard-earned reputation long after leaving active service.

Olukoyede gave the charge when members of the Association of Retired Officers of the EFCC paid him a courtesy visit at the Commission’s headquarters in Abuja. 

He described the retirees’ past efforts as foundational to the EFCC’s growth and achievements in the fight against economic and financial crimes.

*“Permit me to thank you for your contributions to building the EFCC. Your names will be written in gold. Without your legacy and efforts, the EFCC would not exist today,”* the Chairman told the gathering. 

He stressed that the way retired officers conduct themselves outside the Commission continues to shape public perception of the institution.

*“The way you portray yourselves outside is very important. Let us come together and uphold the standard of this Commission. Respect this brand, EFCC. Do not tarnish this image. Let us join hands together to build this brand, EFCC. Let people know you were staff members of integrity and transparency,”* Olukoyede urged.

The EFCC boss, who assumed office in October 2023, highlighted the internal reforms his administration has pursued. 

He noted that upon taking charge, there was an urgent need for reorganisation and restructuring to cleanse the Commission of corrupt practices. 

As part of these efforts, the agency has dismissed more than 40 staff members for gross misconduct and indiscipline.

Olukoyede acknowledged that the Commission’s successes in securing convictions and recovering assets would not have been possible without the support of both serving and retired personnel. 

He assured the retirees of continued commitment to their welfare, confirming that his administration has strengthened arrangements for the payment of their severance packages.

Looking ahead, the Chairman disclosed plans to reintegrate retired officers into the EFCC’s leadership development programmes as resource persons. 

He also revealed ongoing work toward establishing an EFCC Estate for both serving and retired personnel on a subscription basis, aimed at providing lasting support for those who dedicated their careers to the anti-corruption mandate.

Earlier, the President of the Association of Retired Officers of the EFCC, Dr. Ayo Olowonihi, a former Commandant of the EFCC Academy, described the visit as the group’s second engagement with the Commission’s leadership. 

He expressed gratitude for the facilitation of severance packages and allowances and for the Chairman’s role in securing the Association’s registration with the Corporate Affairs Commission (CAC). In recognition of this support, the Association appointed Olukoyede as its branch patron.

Olowonihi appealed for a dedicated office for the Association and a special retirees’ welfare desk within the Commission. 

He also called for greater recognition of retired officers’ expertise through opportunities to contribute to training programmes, describing a strong retiree association as a valuable asset to the EFCC.

The meeting underscored the Commission’s emphasis on institutional continuity, discipline, and the enduring responsibility of all who have served under its banner. 

By calling on retirees to remain standard-bearers of integrity, Olukoyede reinforced the EFCC’s message that the fight against corruption extends beyond official tenure and into the lifelong conduct of its personnel.
