**Yenagoa, Bayelsa State – August 15, 2026** — The Bayelsa State Government has reaffirmed its zero-tolerance policy towards corruption and pledged robust collaboration with the Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC) to strengthen preventive measures, public enlightenment, and ethical standards in governance.

The commitment was sealed during a courtesy visit by a delegation from the ICPC Rivers State Office, led by Resident Anti-Corruption Commissioner (RACC) Dr. (Mrs) Ekere Usiere, to Deputy Governor Hon. Peter Pereotubo Akpe at the Government House in Yenagoa.



**Focus on Prevention and Public Education**

Dr. Usiere emphasized that sustainable anti-corruption efforts require more than enforcement. 

She highlighted the Commission’s National Ethics and Integrity Policy as a key tool for building ethical consciousness across society and stressed the need for continuous public sensitization.

*“We must move beyond enforcement to entrench a culture of accountability. The people of Bayelsa must be fully educated on the detrimental impact of corruption and the virtues of transparency,”* she said.

The visit formed part of ICPC’s broader engagement with state executives to build multi-stakeholder partnerships that support the Commission’s preventive mandate.



**Bayelsa’s Clear Commitment**



Deputy Governor Akpe welcomed the ICPC team and restated the state government’s uncompromising position on integrity under Governor Douye Diri’s administration.

*“Our administration operates a zero-tolerance policy towards corruption. We are wholly prepared to collaborate with the ICPC in this collective endeavor, and we shall leverage our Ministry of Information, Orientation, and Strategy to amplify public enlightenment campaigns,”* Akpe declared.

He also noted the high volume of petitions originating from Bayelsa that reach the ICPC, describing many as frivolous, and warned residents that lodging unfounded petitions carries legal consequences while reiterating the government’s openness to genuine partnership with anti-graft agencies.

The meeting, held in the Deputy Governor’s Conference Room, was attended by senior state officials, including Secretary to the State Government Prof. Nimbofa Ayawei.

Both sides agreed to intensify grassroots orientation and institutionalize ethical governance practices.



**Why the Partnership Matters**

Bayelsa’s renewed engagement with ICPC comes amid ongoing national efforts to strengthen integrity systems at the sub-national level. By committing to joint sensitization campaigns and closer institutional cooperation, the state government signals an intention to match strong anti-corruption rhetoric with practical action.

The collaboration is expected to enhance public awareness, reduce the culture of frivolous petitions, and support a more transparent public service environment in the oil-rich state.

As Nigeria continues to grapple with the long-term challenge of corruption, partnerships of this nature between federal anti-graft agencies and state governments remain critical to building lasting institutional resilience and public trust.
