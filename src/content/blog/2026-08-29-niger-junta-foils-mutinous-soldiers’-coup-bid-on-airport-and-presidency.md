**Niamey, Niger – August 29, 2026** — Niger’s ruling military junta said Saturday it had thwarted an attempted coup after mutinous soldiers launched coordinated attacks on the capital’s international airport and the presidential area, unleashing hours of intense gunfire and explosions across Niamey.

The National Council for the Safeguarding of the Homeland (CNSP) and Defence Minister General Salifou Mody confirmed that a group of soldiers, acting *“in breach of discipline and military regulations,”* seized fellow officers as hostages at Base 101 — the air force facility located inside Diori Hamani International Airport — and opened fire on several sensitive sites in the city.



**Pre-Dawn Assault Shakes the Capital**

Witnesses reported the first bursts of heavy weapons fire and explosions beginning around 1 a.m. local time near the airport, roughly 10 kilometres from the presidential palace in the Plateau administrative district. 

The violence continued into the morning, briefly knocking state television and radio off the air.

Security sources indicated that the mutineers also attempted to breach the perimeter around the presidency but were repelled by the presidential guard. 

Some reports suggested involvement of troops from southwestern localities including Ouallam, Tera and Dosso — areas that have suffered heavy losses in ongoing battles against jihadist groups.

By late morning and into the afternoon, authorities stated that defence and security forces had “*gradually regained control”* of the targeted sites. 

Residents were urged to remain calm and resume normal activities, though security deployments remained heavy around the airport and key government zones.



**Strategic Stakes at Base 101**

The airport complex is one of Niger’s most sensitive military locations. Beyond the national air force base, it hosts the headquarters of Russia’s Africa Corps contingent and the command centre of the joint force operated by the Alliance of Sahel States (AES) — the regional bloc uniting Niger, Mali and Burkina Faso.

This was the third major security incident at the site this year. Jihadist groups previously attacked the base in January and June 2026, underscoring the persistent pressure on the junta’s security apparatus.



**Context of Growing Pressure on the Junta**

General Abdourahamane Tiani has led the CNSP since the July 2023 coup that ousted elected President Mohamed Bazoum. 

The military government has shifted away from traditional Western security partners toward closer ties with Russia while struggling to contain a decade-long jihadist insurgency and mounting economic hardships.

Saturday’s internal mutiny represents a significant challenge from within the armed forces themselves. 

Officials framed the episode as a limited breach of discipline rather than a broad rebellion, yet the scale of the fighting and the choice of high-value targets have raised questions about cohesion inside the military.

No official casualty figures were immediately released. Authorities continued operations to fully secure the capital and restore public confidence as the day progressed.

The events in Niamey add another layer of uncertainty to the Sahel region, where military-led governments in Niger, Mali and Burkina Faso face simultaneous threats from jihadist groups and the internal strains of prolonged transitions.
