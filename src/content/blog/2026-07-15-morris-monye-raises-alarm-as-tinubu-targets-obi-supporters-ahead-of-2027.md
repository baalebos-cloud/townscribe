**ABUJA, Nigeria** — Fresh tensions are erupting in Nigeria’s polarized political landscape as Morris Monye, a prominent former Director of Mobilisation for the Obidient Movement, has sounded a loud alarm, claiming that the Bola Tinubu-led federal government is now actively targeting and harassing key supporters of Peter Obi and the broader opposition movement.

In a short but explosive post on X (formerly Twitter) on July 15, 2026, Monye declared: “*The government is going after Peter Obi’s people and harassing us. I will share more later in the evening. It’s really unfortunate.”* 

The statement quickly gained traction, sparking intense debate among political observers and Obi’s vast supporter base, popularly known as the Obidient Movement.

Although Monye did not immediately provide specific details, names of agencies, or concrete incidents in his initial post, his warning has ignited widespread speculation and concern. 

The timing is particularly sensitive, coming as informal discussions and positioning for the 2027 general elections continue to intensify across the country. 

Peter Obi, the 2023 Labour Party presidential candidate and a leading opposition voice, continues to command significant grassroots support, especially among young Nigerians disillusioned with the current administration’s economic policies.

The allegation fits into a broader pattern of accusations and counter-accusations that have defined Nigeria’s politics since the contentious 2023 elections. 

Obi’s supporters have frequently claimed marginalization, surveillance, and selective persecution, while government officials have often dismissed such claims as unsubstantiated or politically motivated. 

Monye’s intervention comes against the backdrop of ongoing national challenges — economic hardship, inflation, and public frustration — which have kept opposition voices active and vocal. 

The Obidient Movement remains a potent force online and in public discourse, often mobilizing effectively on issues of governance, accountability, and youth inclusion.

No official response from the Presidency or security agencies had been issued at the time of this report regarding Monye’s specific claims. 

Such allegations, whether proven or not, highlight the deep divisions and trust deficit that continue to plague Nigeria’s democratic journey.

True democratic strength lies in protecting the rights of all citizens — including vocal critics and opposition figures — to express dissent without fear. 

Suppressing opposition voices risks further eroding public confidence and fueling cynicism ahead of 2027.

For now, Nigerians await further details from Monye and any rebuttal from the government. 

In the meantime, the episode serves as a reminder that political tolerance, rule of law, and protection of fundamental rights must remain non-negotiable pillars if the country is to move toward genuine national cohesion and progress.
