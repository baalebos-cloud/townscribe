**Abuja, Nigeria – September 15, 2026** — Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate Peter Obi has thrown down a transparency gauntlet to all aspirants for the 2027 elections, declaring that anyone seeking public office must willingly submit to full public scrutiny.

Speaking in an interview with **90MinutesAfrica**, Obi responded to recent claims by Nigeria’s Ambassador-designate to Mexico, Reno Omokri, that leading presidential contenders—including himself—have records with the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI).



**“I Have Never Met the FBI”**

Obi said he was unaware of any FBI file concerning him and insisted he had never met or dealt with the American agency.

*“If you should seek public office, you must submit yourself to public scrutiny,”* he stated firmly. 

He challenged anyone in possession of such records to release them publicly rather than rely on unverified allegations.

The former Anambra State governor maintained he had nothing to hide and urged that the same standard of openness should apply to every candidate seeking the trust of Nigerians.



**Call for Full Academic Transparency**

Going further, Obi called on the University of Nigeria, Nsukka (UNN), where he obtained his degree, to release every document in his academic file—including admission records and results from first year through graduation.

He extended the invitation to other institutions where he undertook executive education programs, among them Lagos Business School, the London School of Economics, and Harvard Business School. 

Obi said these institutions should make his records available for public examination if questions arise about his educational background.



**Broader Context Ahead of 2027**

The remarks come amid intensifying debate over the personal, academic, financial and legal records of major political figures as the 2027 general elections draw closer. 

Omokri had earlier claimed that all major candidates have some form of FBI record, while carefully distinguishing between an investigative file and a criminal conviction.

Obi’s position frames the issue as one of principle: those who ask Nigerians for their votes must accept the highest level of accountability. 

His challenge effectively places other contenders under pressure to match the same standard of openness.

As political temperature rises, the conversation around candidates’ backgrounds is expected to remain a central feature of the campaign season. 

Obi’s intervention reinforces his long-standing message that transparency and public scrutiny are non-negotiable for anyone seeking to lead Nigeria.
