**Benin City, Edo State – November 25, 2026–** Tension gripped Benin City on Wednesday as the Nigeria Democratic Congress, NDC, alleged a violent attack on its supporters during the visit of its presidential candidate, Mr. Peter Obi, to Edo State.

Obi, who recently dumped the ADC to contest the 2027 presidential election under the NDC with former Kano governor Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso as running mate, arrived in the Edo capital for a town hall meeting and campaign activities, including a homage visit to the Oba of Benin.

But celebration quickly turned to chaos along Airport Road.

In a statement on Wednesday, the party’s National Publicity Secretary, Osa Director, said suspected political thugs attacked NDC members and supporters as Obi’s convoy arrived, opening fire, smashing campaign vehicles and buses, and leaving several people injured.

“*Reports indicate that the assailants opened fire, damaged campaign vehicles and buses, and left several people injured. Videos circulating online show the aftermath of this brazen assault,*” Director said.

Disturbing videos attached to the statement showed blood stains on one of the party’s campaign buses, while bullet exhibits littered the floor of its campaign office. A damaged NDC campaign vehicle photographed after the incident has since gone viral.

Director described the incident as part of a worrying pattern of violence, intimidation and attacks on NDC members, candidates and structures across Edo and other parts of the country.

*“Politics is not war. No Nigerian should be attacked, shot at, or terrorised simply for supporting a political party or exercising their democratic rights,”* he declared.

The NDC called on the Edo State Police Command, Inspector-General of Police Tunji Disu, and other security agencies to immediately secure the area, protect Obi, his supporters and candidates, and order a swift, transparent and impartial investigation.

*“The NDC remains committed to peaceful, issue-based politics. We will not be intimidated or driven out of any part of Nigeria,”* Director added. “Our supporters are law-abiding citizens, and their safety is non-negotiable.”

He also urged civil society organisations, the media and the international community to take note of what he called escalating political violence and hold authorities accountable for protecting citizens’ right to political participation.

The Benin attack comes barely 48 hours after six people were killed during an attack on the campaign team of Ovie Omo-Agege, the NDC senatorial candidate for Delta Central, in Uduere on Monday – an incident in which one survivor chillingly recounted: “They shot me five times.”

It also follows a venue dispute, with the NDC accusing managers of Crown Bowl Event Centre of blocking its town hall, and the party’s boycott of the IGP’s security meeting over alleged police complicity.

Despite the tension, Obi went ahead with the Benin town hall, promising a united and secure Nigeria.

As of press time, the police were yet to react to the latest allegation.
