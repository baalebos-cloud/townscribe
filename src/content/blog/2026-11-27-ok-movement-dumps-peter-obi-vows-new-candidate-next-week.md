**ABUJA, Nigeria—November 27, 2026—Nigeria's** opposition landscape suffered a fresh tremor on Friday as a faction of the Obi-Kwankwaso Movement, popularly known as the OK Movement, announced it has withdrawn campaign mobilization for Peter Obi, 2026—Nigeria's candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), and his running mate, Senator Rabiu Musa Kwankwaso.

The announcement, first reported by TheCable, was contained in a statement issued on Friday by the factional Director-General, John Ughulu, who said the group will unveil its preferred political party and presidential candidate next week ahead of the January 16, 2027 general elections.

> "The national leadership of the OK Movement has formally been made to understand that the presidential candidate of the NDC, his excellency Peter Obi, and the party he represents have decided to discontinue their political relationship with the OK Movement," Ughulu said.

He described the development as regrettable but said the movement respected the decision, adding that all campaign, mobilization, and operational support for the NDC ceases immediately.

*"We have already commenced engagements with other political parties and potential presidential candidates whose political vision, leadership capacity, and ideological commitments align with our established principles and values,"* he added.

The rupture had been brewing since September, when the movement constituted a 59-member presidential campaign council for the 2027 election. The move angered NDC leaders.

NDC National Leader, Seriake Dickson, lambasted the decision as "nonsensical," insisting support groups must not compete with the party. 

Obi himself, in an interview on Arise Television, distanced himself from the council, saying it was formed without his approval and directing all support groups—including the OK Movement, Obidient Movement, and Kwankwasiyya—to operate strictly as support structures, not parallel councils.

Reacting swiftly, the NDC dismissed the withdrawal as belated and self-serving.

Director-General of the NDC National Campaign Council, Ibrahim Hussain Abdulkarim, said Ughulu's announcement was a reaction to Obi's earlier directive and a calculated attempt to slow the momentum of the Obi-Kwankwaso ticket.

*"It bears the unmistakable hallmarks of a sponsored distraction, one designed by interests aligned with the ruling party to manufacture factions, sow confusion, and divert public attention,"* Abdulkarim said.

He insisted the authentic grassroots structures of the Obidient Movement and Kwankwasiyya remain firmly with the NDC, challenging Ughulu to prove his representation claim.

Despite the drama, the NDC says its campaign continues with polling-unit agent verification, market mobilization, town halls and youth engagements.
