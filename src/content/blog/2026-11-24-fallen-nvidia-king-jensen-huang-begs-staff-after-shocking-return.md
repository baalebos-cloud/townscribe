**Santa Clara, USA – November 25, 2026 –** A day after his stunning return as chief executive at Nvidia, Jensen Huang stood before his entire company on Tuesday and apologized for violating its code of conduct—in an hour-long meeting that began on a somber note before turning into a defiant airing of grievances.

*“I made some terrible mistakes,”* Huang said, according to livestreams reviewed by The New York Times, CNN and WIRED. “***I was unfaithful to Lori, and I hurt my kids and, of course, I hurt all of you.”***

Huang, who founded the AI-chip giant three decades ago, resigned on November 12 following reports of multiple extramarital affairs—including with a subordinate and with Alice Huang, a woman US officials feared was a Chinese intelligence asset formerly running Singapore-based Megaspeed, accused of diverting Nvidia semiconductors to China in breach of export controls.

On Monday, in a surprise reversal, Nvidia’s board reinstated him after an unprecedented White House pressure campaign orchestrated by President Donald Trump, who took credit on CNBC, saying he directly appealed to the board. Semafor reported Trump even dialed into Sunday’s board meeting.

But Huang dismissed that narrative on Tuesday.

*“It is completely untrue,"* Trump forced his return, he insisted. The board *“evaluated the entire situation” and “gathered facts”* over the weekend, concluding he was not under federal criminal scrutiny and posed no national security risk. “*He is my president... We are the most apolitical company*,” Huang added.

The 62-year-old billionaire revealed he is in therapy *“every day for an hour and a half,"* and it is helping “a lot.” He said the town hall was his hardest company meeting ever—"*the hardest meeting I’ve ever done was with my wife.”* 

He also apologized to wife Lori, children Madison and Spencer Huang, both Nvidia executives, and son-in-law Nico Caprez, thanking them for a second chance.

Defiantly, Huang rejected claims he is a “tech bro” above the law, arguing breaking the code “*does not mean, the moment you violate, we walk you out the door.”* 

He defended Nvidia’s flat structure with 60 direct reports, called succession planning “flawed,” admitted his *“insanely intense” s*tyle disempowers staff, and promised monthly 360-degree reviews.

CFO Colette Kress had served as interim CEO. Huang took no questions.

He still faces two Congressional hearings next week before House Foreign Affairs and Senate Banking committees.
