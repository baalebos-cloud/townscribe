**Kaduna, Nigeria—September 26, 2026**—In a clear call that cuts through potential religious hesitation, the Chairman of the Northern Christian Association (NCA), Rev. Joseph Hayab, has urged Christians across Northern Nigeria to treat the ongoing Dangote Petroleum Refinery Initial Public Offering as a straightforward business opportunity rather than a matter of faith.

Speaking at a media parley in Kaduna on Saturday, Hayab addressed reports that some northern Christians were reluctant to participate because of the Muslim identity of the promoter, Africa’s richest man, Aliko Dangote.

*“Business has no religion. What Dangote is selling is not Islam; Dangote is selling shares. Shares bring money,”* he declared.



**A Call for Investment Culture Over Religious Lenses**

Hayab stressed that Christian leaders have a duty to encourage their members to build a stronger culture of investment and long-term wealth creation instead of relying solely on salaries. 

He said he had already instructed NCA members to create awareness about the offer in churches across the region, beginning the previous Sunday and continuing until the IPO closes.

*“If I have my way, just as they are advertising, every Christian should have 10 shares,*” he added, illustrating the potential power of collective participation. 

He also noted that Dangote is a northern Nigerian entrepreneur and that supporting viable businesses from the region benefits everyone regardless of faith.



**The People’s IPO at a Glance**

The Dangote Petroleum Refinery and Petrochemicals FZE is offering up to **4.1 billion ordinary shares** at **₦525 each**. 

The minimum subscription is just 10 shares, or **₦5,250**, deliberately designed to allow ordinary Nigerians to participate. If fully subscribed, the offer will raise approximately \*\*₦2.15 trillion\*\*.

The public offer opened on 14 September 2026 and is scheduled to close on 13 October 2026. Shares are expected to list on the Nigerian Exchange later in the year. 

Proceeds will partly fund the refinery’s expansion from its current capacity of about 700,000 barrels per day toward 1.4 million barrels per day.

Marketed as a *“People’s IPO,*” the offer has attracted strong interest from both retail and institutional investors, coming after a highly oversubscribed private placement earlier in the year that helped push the refinery’s valuation into the tens of billions of dollars.

**Why the Message Matters**

Hayab’s intervention comes at a sensitive moment in Nigeria’s interfaith landscape. By framing share ownership as a neutral economic act that can create intergenerational wealth, the cleric seeks to lower barriers that sometimes keep communities from participating fully in major national economic opportunities.

He reminded listeners that political leaders come and go, but productive businesses endure: “*Today we have Tinubu as president, tomorrow we’ll have another person as president, but we will be having Dangote business all through.”*

Investment experts continue to caution that buying shares always carries risk. Prospective investors are advised to study the company’s valuation, financial performance, expansion plans, and the full prospectus before committing funds.

As the Dangote Refinery IPO moves into its final weeks, Hayab’s message underscores a broader national conversation: whether Nigerians can look beyond identity and seize ownership stakes in the country’s most ambitious industrial projects.
