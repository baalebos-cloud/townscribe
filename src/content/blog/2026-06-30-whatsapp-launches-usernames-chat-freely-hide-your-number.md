**Lagos, Nigeria – June 30, 2026** – In a significant privacy upgrade that millions of users have long awaited, WhatsApp has begun rolling out unique usernames, enabling people worldwide to connect and converse without ever exchanging their personal phone numbers.

The Meta-owned messaging giant, which boasts over three billion monthly users, announced the feature on Monday, describing it as a deliberate step “*designed to protect the privacy of your phone number.”* 

Instead of relying on the phone number that has been central to WhatsApp’s identity since its inception, users will now be able to select and share a personalized username – much like on platforms such as Telegram, Signal, or Instagram.

Users can choose usernames between 3 and 35 characters long, incorporating lowercase letters, numbers, periods, and underscores, but they must include at least one letter. 

Certain restrictions apply: usernames cannot start with “www.” or end with a domain extension like .com, to avoid confusion with websites.

There is no public directory or search suggestions within the app. To contact someone, others must know the exact username – a safeguard that prevents unsolicited outreach. 

For even greater control, users can optionally set a “username key” (such as a short code) that recipients must enter alongside the username to initiate the first chat.

Phone numbers remain required for account registration and core functionality, but they will no longer be automatically shared in scenarios like joining large group chats or messaging a new contact for the first time. 

Existing contacts who already have your number will continue to see it as before, unless privacy settings are adjusted.

Alice Newton-Rex, Vice President and Head of Product at WhatsApp, explained the human motivation behind the change: “*When you meet someone new, whether it’s a classmate, a neighbour, or someone you met at an event, sharing your phone number can feel like a big step.* 

*Your phone number is personal, and it’s tied to so many other parts of your life. So usernames are designed to give you control of who gets to see your phone number in the first place.”*

The feature is being introduced gradually. Users will receive a notification when username reservation becomes available in their country. 

They can then navigate to **Settings > Account > Username** to claim their handle.

To manage the massive user base and prevent conflicts, WhatsApp is prioritizing reservations for celebrities, VIPs, organizations, and creators. 

Businesses and individuals can claim the same username they already use on Facebook or Instagram by verifying ownership through Meta’s Accounts Center. 

This cross-platform consistency offers branding opportunities but comes with a privacy trade-off: linking accounts may make it easier for others to connect your profiles across Meta services.

Experts note that popular or common usernames will likely be snapped up quickly, so users eager for a specific handle should act promptly once the option appears in their region.

This update addresses long-standing user feedback and brings WhatsApp in line with competitors. 

For years, privacy advocates and ordinary users have expressed discomfort with the mandatory exposure of phone numbers – information that can lead to spam, harassment, doxxing, or unwanted business solicitations.

Privacy benefits are clear: safer networking for students, professionals, activists, or anyone wary of sharing contact details. 

It could reduce “*WhatsApp bombing*” in groups and make casual or professional outreach less intrusive.

Business and creator advantages include easier discoverability and consistent branding without forcing customers to share personal numbers. Small businesses, influencers, and organizations stand to benefit significantly.

However, nuances remain. End-to-end encryption continues to protect message content. Usernames do not replace phone numbers entirely – the underlying account infrastructure stays number-based. 

Some users worry about username squatting or impersonation, though Meta says it will enforce policies against violations.
The full rollout will continue through the coming months and into late 2026, with phased availability by country. Early testers in beta versions have reported a smooth, intuitive experience.

For the hundreds of millions who rely on WhatsApp daily – from family chats across continents to customer service interactions – this feels like a mature evolution. 

It acknowledges that in 2026, not every conversation needs to begin with sharing one of the most personal pieces of information we carry.

As one veteran user put it in online discussions: “*Finally, I can give someone a way to reach me without handing over the keys to my entire digital life.”*

WhatsApp continues to iterate on privacy while maintaining its position as the world’s dominant messaging platform. 

This username era marks another step toward making secure, low-friction communication the default – without unnecessary personal exposure.
