ARLINGTON, Texas — In a tense, hard-fought Round of 32 clash at the air-conditioned AT&T Stadium (home of the Dallas Cowboys) on Tuesday, June 30, 2026, Erling Haaland delivered yet another moment of clinical brilliance. 

The Manchester City striker pounced in the 86th minute to secure a dramatic 2-1 victory for Norway over a resilient Côte d'Ivoire side, sending the Scandinavians into the World Cup last 16 for the first time in their history.

It was a match that encapsulated the raw physicality and tactical battle expected from two strong, athletic teams. Norway, runners-up in their group behind France, faced an energetic, youthful Ivorian outfit making their knockout debut. 

The game started cautiously, with few clear chances in a tepid opening period. Côte d'Ivoire threatened early through defender Ghislain Konan, who burst into the box but could only find the side netting.

The breakthrough came six minutes before halftime, courtesy of a moment of genuine magic from 21-year-old Antonio Nusa. Fed by Captain Martin Ødegaard, ,the winger cut inside and unleashed a stunning curled effort into the top corner—one of the goals of the tournament. Ødegaard could only watch in disbelief as Norway took a deserved lead into the break.

Côte d'Ivoire, one of the youngest teams at the tournament, refused to wilt. Substitutions injected fresh energy, notably the introduction of Manchester United's Amad Diallo after the hour mark. 

The lively forward immediately made his presence felt, first producing a heroic goal-line clearance to deny Norway a second, then dancing past defenders to fire home a spectacular equalizer in the 74th minute. With extra time looming, the momentum had shifted toward the Elephants.

But Haaland, the predator supreme, had other ideas. As the clock ticked toward 86 minutes, Patrick Berg whipped in a dangerous cross. 

The Norwegian striker got on the end of it, prodding it home from close range — his fifth goal of the tournament. 

Goalkeeper Ørjan Nyland then produced a crucial flying save from a Diallo free-kick in stoppage time to preserve the lead.

For Norway, this victory marks a historic milestone: their first-ever knockout-stage win at a World Cup. 

The last time they appeared in the tournament was 1998; now, powered by Haaland's relentless scoring form (already a Golden Boot contender) and a well-organized squad featuring stars like Ødegaard, they face five-time champions Brazil in the last 16 on Sunday in New Jersey.

Côte d'Ivoire can hold their heads high. They progressed from the group stage with memorable performances, including a key contribution from Nicolas Pepe, and showed tremendous fight here. 

Diallo's equalizer was a highlight, and their young talents like Yan Diomande (linked with a move to PSG) displayed promise on the biggest stage. Their elimination stings, but they've left a positive mark.

As the World Cup intensifies, Norway's blend of youthful energy, tactical discipline under coach Ståle Solbakken, and the unstoppable force of Haaland makes them a dangerous proposition. 

Brazil awaits — but for now, the Vikings are marching on, proving that knockout football at this level rewards resilience and star quality in equal measure. Fans in the stands, famous for their "*rowing"* celebrations, will be dreaming bigger dreams tonight.
