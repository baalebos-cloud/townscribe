The highly anticipated Iberian derby lived up to its billing as a cagey, tactical battle for much of the evening. Both sides created chances, but it was Spain’s super-sub Merino who delivered the decisive blow in the 91st minute. 

Coming off the bench in the 85th minute, the Arsenal midfielder capitalized on a quick free-kick and a clever assist from fellow substitute Ferran Torres, ghosting into the box to fire a low, powerful shot past a helpless Diogo Costa.

For Cristiano Ronaldo, widely regarded as one of football’s greatest-ever players, the match marked a heartbreaking end to his World Cup career. 

The Portuguese icon, playing what many believed could be his final tournament appearance, could not inspire his side to victory despite Portugal’s resilient defensive display. 

Spain’s solid backline, which has yet to concede in the tournament, proved impenetrable.

The goal not only propelled Spain toward a potential deep run but also highlighted the depth and tactical flexibility of coach Luis de la Fuente’s squad, which continues to impress as defending European champions.

The encounter was billed as a clash of styles and generations. 

Portugal relied on experience and Ronaldo’s enduring threat, while Spain showcased youthful energy, technical brilliance (with stars like Lamine Yamal shining), and midfield control anchored by Rodri, who earned Man of the Match honors.

Merino’s winner was no fluke — the 30-year-old has built a reputation for delivering in big moments. His composure under pressure and clinical finish encapsulated Spain’s clinical edge in a match defined by fine margins.

Spain advances to face the winner of USA vs Belgium in the quarter-finals, carrying strong momentum and an unbeaten defensive record. 

For Portugal and Ronaldo, the focus shifts to national rebuilding and reflection on a storied international career that brought immense pride to a football-mad nation, even if the ultimate World Cup glory remained elusive.
