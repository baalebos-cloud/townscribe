**ABUJA, Nigeria** — In a significant move for Nigeria’s evolving advertising and outdoor media landscape, the Senate on Thursday passed the Chartered Out-of-Home Media Practitioners of Nigeria (Establishment) Bill 2026. 

The legislation aims to create a dedicated regulatory body for practitioners in the out-of-home (OOH) media sector, which includes billboards, digital displays, transit advertising, and other outdoor formats.

Sponsored by Senator Enyinnaya Abaribe, representing Abia South, the bill sailed through after the upper chamber considered and adopted the report of a joint committee on establishment and public service matters, information and national orientation, and media and public affairs.

Proponents argue the new charter will bring much-needed structure, professionalism, and ethical standards to an industry that has grown rapidly with urbanization, digital advancements, and increased marketing spend across Nigeria. 

A regulatory framework could help standardize practices, protect consumers, ensure quality control, and foster industry growth while addressing issues like illegal placements, environmental impact, and revenue leakages.

The development comes at a time when Nigeria’s advertising sector is expanding, driven by economic activities, political campaigns, and corporate branding. 

Out-of-home media remains a vital channel for reaching mass audiences in a country with high mobile penetration but varying digital access levels.

However, the bill has previously faced resistance from key stakeholders. 

Groups such as the Association of Advertising Agencies of Nigeria (AAAN), Broadcasting Organisation of Nigeria (BON), and others have expressed reservations, fearing it could lead to fragmentation, additional bureaucracy, or overlap with existing regulators like the Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria (ARCON). 

Critics worry about potential censorship risks or undue control over media practice in a democracy.

Senator Abaribe, a vocal lawmaker known for independent positions on national issues, has positioned the bill as a step toward elevating standards rather than restricting freedom. 

The image circulating with the news shows the senator actively engaging in legislative proceedings, underscoring his sponsorship role.

With Senate approval secured, the bill now advances to the House of Representatives for concurrence before presidential assent. 

If signed into law, it will establish the Chartered Out-of-Home Media Practitioners of Nigeria as a professional body, potentially including provisions for membership, certification, ethics, and dispute resolution.

This legislative development reflects broader efforts to formalize various professional sectors in Nigeria, from engineering to healthcare. For the OOH industry, it could mean better coordination, investment attraction, and contribution to the creative economy — a key pillar in national diversification away from oil.

As stakeholders digest the passage, questions remain on implementation details, stakeholder inclusion, and balancing regulation with innovation and press freedom. 

Industry watchers will closely monitor the next steps in the National Assembly and any potential amendments to address earlier concerns.

In a nation where public communication shapes opinions, policies, and markets, effective yet non-intrusive regulation of outdoor media could set a positive precedent for other emerging sectors. 

The coming months will determine whether this bill strengthens Nigeria’s advertising ecosystem or sparks further debate on media governance.
