Fresh controversy has erupted over President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s educational credentials after the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) published the personal particulars of candidates for the 2027 presidential election. 

Documents show that Tinubu listed only his Bachelor of Science degree in Business Administration from Chicago State University (1979) and his National Youth Service Corps certificate, leaving the sections for primary and secondary school qualifications blank.

The omission has drawn sharp reactions online and in traditional media, with critics comparing Tinubu’s submission to those of major rivals like Peter Obi and Atiku Abubakar, who provided fuller educational timelines. 

Some have questioned how a university degree could be obtained without documented foundational schooling and revived long-standing debates about the authenticity of the President’s academic records.

Appearing on ARISE News, public policy analyst and PR consultant Deyemi Saka pushed back against the uproar. 

*“I don’t see the big deal in the President only uploading his university credentials to INEC,”* Saka said. 

He noted that the certificate has previously been questioned, thoroughly examined, and confirmed as genuine. 

*“He has fulfilled all constitutional requirements and I think we should move on. There are better topics to pick up with the president.”*

Saka’s intervention highlights a recurring tension in Nigerian politics: the gap between legal compliance and public expectations of transparency. 

Nigeria’s 1999 Constitution (as amended) requires a candidate for president to have at least a School Certificate or its equivalent. 

A university degree clearly exceeds this minimum threshold. Supporters argue that focusing on the absence of primary and secondary details distracts from governance performance, economic challenges, and security concerns.

Critics, however, insist that incomplete disclosures erode public trust, especially given past controversies surrounding Tinubu’s educational and professional history. 

Chicago State University has previously confirmed that Tinubu attended and graduated in 1979, though discrepancies in documentation have fueled prolonged legal and political battles.

As INEC’s publication window allows for public scrutiny and potential legal challenges, the debate is unlikely to fade quickly. 

For many Nigerians, the issue goes beyond technical compliance — it touches on questions of integrity, accountability, and the standards expected of the nation’s highest office. 

Whether the conversation shifts, as Saka hopes, to “better topics” remains to be seen in the months leading to the 2027 polls.
