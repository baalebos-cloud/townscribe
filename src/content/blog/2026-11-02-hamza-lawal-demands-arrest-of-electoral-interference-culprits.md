**Abuja, Nigeria—November 2, 2026**—Hamza Lawal, founder of Connected Development (CODE), has called on security and intelligence agencies to immediately investigate, arrest, and prosecute individuals involved in the alleged manipulation of Nigeria’s electoral process.

Lawal made the strong demand while appearing on **Good Morning Nigeria**, a programme on the Nigerian Television Authority (NTA), during a discussion on safeguards against electoral interference.



**Call for Accountability**

The civil society leader stressed that those behind attempts to undermine the integrity of elections must face the full weight of the law. 

He emphasized the need for decisive action by relevant agencies to protect the credibility of the democratic process as the country prepares for the 2027 general elections.

His comments came amid growing public concern over reported irregularities in the voter register, including the discovery of AI-generated photographs and other anomalies that INEC has acknowledged and is currently cleaning up.



**Broader Context of Electoral Safeguards**

The NTA discussion also featured other experts who raised related concerns. Elections analyst Paul James called on INEC to strengthen cybersecurity measures and ensure greater transparency. 

Cybersecurity expert Hanniel Jafaru warned about risks associated with the increasing use of artificial intelligence in the electoral process without adequate regulatory frameworks. 

Former INEC Director of Legal Services, Oluwole Uzzi, cautioned against both political and digital interference in the voter register.

Lawal’s intervention adds to the chorus of voices demanding accountability and stronger protections to prevent any form of electoral manipulation.

Connected Development, the organization he founded, is known for its work on transparency, accountability, and citizen engagement, particularly through the “Follow the Money” initiative.



**This report is based on statements made by Hamza Lawal on NTA’s Good Morning Nigeria programme on November 2, 2026.**
