**Abuja, Nigeria—November 6, 2026**—A chieftain of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), Barrister Kenneth Imasuagbon, has boldly declared that President Bola Ahmed Tinubu will lose the 2027 presidential election, accusing the incumbent of being completely clueless about Nigeria’s worsening security challenges.

Speaking on Channels Television’s **Politics Today** programme on Friday evening. Imasuagbon did not mince words as he assessed the Tinubu administration’s performance. “Tinubu is going to lose this election… He is clueless about security,” the Edo-based lawyer and former governorship aspirant stated emphatically.

His comments came against the backdrop of a major legal setback for the opposition party. On Thursday, Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court in Abuja sacked the David Mark-led National Working Committee of the ADC and nullified the presidential candidacy of former Vice President Atiku Abubakar. 

The court also voided all candidates presented by the party for the 2027 general elections, ruling that the Mark-led caretaker committee, which organized the primaries, was illegal.

Imasuagbon strongly condemned the judgment, describing it as “despicable” and “unacceptable.” He argued that the ruling violated the principles of the Nigerian Constitution and represented a deliberate attempt to cripple the opposition.

 *“The judgment yesterday was despicable; it is unacceptable. It is against the principles of our constitution… Atiku will be on the ballot, and he will win,”* he insisted.

The ADC chieftain further claimed that no Nigerian, both at home and abroad, is happy with the current administration. 

He positioned Atiku Abubakar as the only candidate capable of rescuing the country from what he called its “*downward trajectory”* under President Tinubu. 

Imasuagbon warned that attempts to prevent the ADC from participating in the 2027 elections could plunge Nigeria into chaos and anarchy, urging the international community to intervene to safeguard democracy.

Security remains one of the most pressing issues facing Nigerians. Persistent banditry, kidnapping, farmer-herder clashes, and insurgency in different parts of the country have continued to claim lives and disrupt economic activities. 

Imasuagbon’s sharp criticism reflects growing public frustration with the government’s handling of these challenges, even as the administration insists it is making progress through various security operations and reforms.

The ruling APC has dismissed the ADC’s claims, accusing the opposition of failing to resolve its internal leadership crisis and blaming the judiciary and the ruling party unfairly. 

APC National Publicity Secretary Felix Morka advised the ADC to put its house in order rather than politicize court judgments.

Despite the legal hurdle, the ADC has vowed to appeal the Federal High Court ruling at the Court of Appeal. A

tiku Abubakar has also maintained that he will continue his campaign, describing the judgment as a calculated attempt to weaken the opposition ahead of the January 2027 elections.

As the political temperature rises less than three months to the polls, Imasuagbon’s fiery remarks on national television have further intensified the debate over security, democracy, and the future of opposition politics in Nigeria.
