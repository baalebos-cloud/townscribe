**Abuja, Nigeria – July 3, 2026** — In a move welcomed by many as a step toward greater electoral inclusion, Nigeria’s Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has extended the nationwide Continuous Voter Registration (CVR) exercise by two weeks and introduced a pioneering self-service online registration option for first-time voters.

The announcement, made via official press release on Friday, comes after reviewing ongoing operations and gathering input from state offices, political parties, civil society groups, and other stakeholders. 

Originally set to end on July 10, 2026, the CVR — which began on August 18, 2025 — will now run until Friday, July 24, 2026.

This extension provides additional time for eligible Nigerians who have not yet registered, transferred their details, replaced lost or damaged Permanent Voter Cards (PVCs), or corrected their biodata. 

INEC emphasized that the decision underscores its commitment to ensuring every eligible citizen has a fair opportunity to join the National Register of Voters ahead of future elections, particularly the 2027 general polls.

In a significant leap toward modernization and convenience, INEC is rolling out a self-service registration option starting Wednesday, July 8, 2026. 

Eligible first-time voters can now initiate and largely complete their registration process online through the dedicated CVR portal (cvr.inecnigeria.org), including biometric capture using personal devices — without needing to visit a physical INEC office.

The system incorporates robust identity verification, biometric validation, and backend integrity checks to maintain the credibility and accuracy of the voter register. A step-by-step guide is available on the portal.

This development addresses long-standing complaints about lengthy queues, limited office hours, and logistical challenges at registration centers. 

Public reactions on social media have been largely positive, with users praising the innovation while raising practical questions about biometric capture via personal devices, PVC collection, and full functionality across states.

The CVR has operated in phases since mid-2025, with the final phase running from May 11 to the original July 10 deadline. 

It allows new registrations for those aged 18 and above, updates, transfers, and replacements. 

Despite progress, reports highlighted issues such as portal glitches, insufficient slots at centers (especially in urban areas like the FCT), inconsistent office operations, and difficulties with transfers or updates in some states.

Civil society groups had recently urged even longer extensions — up to three months — to open more centers, stabilize systems, and accommodate weekend or evening sessions. INEC’s two-week extension and digital innovation represent a measured response to such calls.

National Commissioner and Chair of the Information & Voter Education Committee, Mohammed Kudu Haruna, signed the release, reaffirming INEC’s dedication to a “*credible, inclusive, and accessible”* process through technology.

 **Eligibility**: Nigerian citizens aged 18 and above who are yet to register, or those needing updates/transfers.

\- **Action Steps**: Visit cvr.inecnigeria.org for self-service (from July 8) or locate nearby centers. Take advantage of the extended window.

\- **Implications**: A more complete voter register could enhance participation and trust in the 2027 elections.

 However, success depends on seamless portal performance, public awareness, and clear guidance on obtaining physical PVCs post-registration.

As Nigeria prepares for its next electoral cycle, this initiative signals INEC’s willingness to adapt and innovate. 

Citizens are urged to act promptly — the deadline is firm, and every vote counts in building a more representative democracy. For the latest details and portal access, check official INEC channels.
