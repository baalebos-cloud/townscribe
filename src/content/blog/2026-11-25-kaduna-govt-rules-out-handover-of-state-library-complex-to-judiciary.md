**Kaduna—November 25, 2026—Relief** swept through Kaduna’s academic and literary community on Wednesday after the state government finally ruled out plans to hand over the Kaduna State Library Board headquarters to the State High Court.

The clarification, which ends weeks of anxiety and speculation, was conveyed by the Acting Director of the Library Board, Hajiya Amina Yahaya, in a statement issued in Kaduna.

Yahaya anchored the government’s position on a circular issued by the Secretary to the State Government, Dr. AbdulKadir Mu’azu Meyere, stressing that the controversial proposal was never approved by Governor Uba Sani.

She explained that all government proposals undergo wide consultations and reviews before a final decision is reached, noting that reports of the planned handover were unfortunately published before the governor gave any approval.

“The proposal to hand over the Library Board complex to the judiciary was never approved by His Excellency, Senator Uba Sani,” she declared, putting to rest fears of an imminent eviction of books, researchers, and staff.

According to the statement, the government deeply recognizes the judiciary’s pivotal role in upholding the rule of law and administering justice and has demonstrated support through the massive renovation of judicial facilities and plans to provide additional court accommodation across the state.

However, Yahaya insisted such support would not come at the expense of other critical public institutions, particularly the library headquarters.

She described the complex as an irreplaceable centre for preserving and sharing knowledge, adding that its continued existence was essential to research, learning and the preservation of information for future generations.

In a Solomonic move, she revealed that the Ministry of Justice has now been directed to work closely with the judiciary to identify alternative land for additional courtrooms and office accommodation.

The decision has already drawn applause from the Nigerian Library Association (NLA), which commended Governor Sani for listening to concerned citizens, stakeholders, and professionals who warned against relocating the historic library to Zaria or ceding it to judges.

For students, researchers, and book lovers who troop daily to the headquarters, Wednesday’s announcement is more than administrative clarification—it is the survival of Kaduna’s memory house.

As one librarian told TheCable, *"You don’t bury books to build courtrooms. Justice itself needs knowledge to stand.”*

The government assured that both the temple of justice and the temple of knowledge will now grow side by side.
