**IBADAN, Oyo State** — In a triumphant display of coordinated security action, Nigerian forces have successfully rescued all schoolchildren and teachers abducted from three schools in Oriire Local Government Area of Oyo State, ending a harrowing 56-day ordeal that gripped the nation with fear and outrage.

Intelligence sources confirmed that a joint team from the Nigerian Army, Nigeria Police Force, and Department of State Services (DSS) stormed the bandits’ hideout in the early hours of Friday, July 10, 2026. 

The intelligence-driven raid triggered a fierce gun battle, resulting in at least nine kidnappers neutralized, with others fleeing injured into surrounding bushes.

All victims — 39 pupils and seven teachers kidnapped on May 15, 2026, from schools in Yawota and Ahoro-Esiele communities — were rescued alive and are being evacuated to Ibadan for medical checks, debriefing, and a formal reception by Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde.

Tragically, one teacher, Mr. Michael Oyedokun, a mathematics instructor at Community High School, Ahoro-Esiele, was murdered by the abductors during captivity. His death added profound sorrow to the families’ long wait.

The abduction on May 15 sparked widespread condemnation and prompted sustained multi-agency efforts. 

Heavily armed assailants had stormed the schools in a coordinated attack, shattering the relative calm in the Southwest and highlighting the spread of banditry beyond traditional hotspots in the North.

Video footage circulating from the rescue scene captures raw emotion: rescued children and teachers, some visibly exhausted yet overjoyed, surrounded by relieved adults clapping, waving, and offering comfort. 

The images of young pupils clutching snacks or being embraced offer a powerful testament to the human cost of insecurity — and the relief of safe return.

Security operatives continue exploitation operations to prevent the gang from regrouping, with efforts underway to apprehend fleeing suspects.

This successful rescue comes against a backdrop of escalating kidnapping incidents targeting schools across Nigeria. 

The Oyo case, one of the largest in the Southwest in recent memory, exposed vulnerabilities in rural educational institutions and strained community trust in security architecture.

Governor Seyi Makinde’s administration, alongside federal forces, faced intense public pressure during the prolonged search. 

The operation’s success is being hailed as a significant morale booster for security agencies battling multifaceted threats, including banditry, insurgency, and opportunistic crime.

Parents, educators, and civil society groups have welcomed the news but called for sustained vigilance, improved rural security infrastructure, and stronger deterrence against abductors. 

Many are urging the government to address root causes — poverty, unemployment, and ungoverned spaces — that fuel such criminality.

As the rescued children and teachers reunite with loved ones, their story serves as both a beacon of hope and a sobering reminder of the ongoing battle to secure Nigeria’s schools and communities. 

In a country where every safe return feels like a hard-won victory, today’s events reaffirm the potential of intelligence-led, joint operations when agencies collaborate effectively.

Further details on the victims’ condition and post-rescue support are expected as authorities conclude assessments. For now, Oyo State — and indeed Nigeria — breathes a collective sigh of relief.
