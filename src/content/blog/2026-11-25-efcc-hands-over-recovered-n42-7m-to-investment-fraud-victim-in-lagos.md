**Lagos, Nigeria – November 25, 2026 –** Hope was restored on Tuesday for a Lagos-based trader when the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission, EFCC, handed over bank drafts valued at N42,700,000 to a victim of a brazen investment fraud that shattered his business dreams five years ago.

The restitution, carried out by the Lagos Zonal Directorate 2 of the anti-graft agency, saw the Acting Zonal Director, Assistant Commander of the EFCC, ACE I Bawa Usman Kaltungo, present the recovered funds to Mr. Chibuzor Emeh in Lagos.

Emeh’s ordeal began sometime in 2020 when one Karis Olaniyi allegedly walked into his shop and marketed the products of her company, Lite House Maritime Services, persuading him to stake a staggering minimum of N180 million.

According to the EFCC, Olaniyi dangled an irresistible package: after just 13 months, Emeh would receive a Samsung Galaxy tablet, the latest iPhone at the time, two free trips to any country of his choice, and a return of three times his investment.

Convinced, Emeh allegedly issued a cheque to Olaniyi, which was cleared on presentation and subsequently transferred into a bank account belonging to Lite House Maritime Services. Months later, the suspect vanished, and every effort to recover the money failed.

Desperate for justice, Emeh briefed M.O. Ubani SAN & Co. Chambers, which petitioned the EFCC on June 2, 2025, alleging obtaining money under false pretence against Olaniyi and her company.

Following diligent investigation, EFCC operatives traced and recovered N42.7 million as part of what the Commission described as an ongoing recovery process.

Kaltungo, while handing over the drafts, reiterated the Commission’s commitment under Executive Chairman Ola Olukoyede to not only prosecute fraudsters but also restore proceeds of crime to victims. He urged Nigerians to verify investment schemes before committing funds.

The development adds to the EFCC’s growing restitution profile. The Commission recently disclosed it recovered over N1.23 trillion, $684 million, and secured more than 10,872 convictions in recent years, with Lagos remaining a hotspot for investment, forex, and Ponzi-related fraud.

For Emeh, who witnessed the handover with relief, it was an emotional end to years of pain. EFCC officials say efforts are still ongoing to recover the outstanding balance, while investigation into Olaniyi and Lite House Maritime Services continues.

The EFCC again warned the public: if an investment promise sounds too sweet—triple returns, luxury gifts, and foreign trips—think twice, verify, and report early.
