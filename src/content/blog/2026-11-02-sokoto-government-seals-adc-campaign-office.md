**Sokoto, Nigeria—November 2, 2026**—The Sokoto State Government has sealed one of the campaign offices of the African Democratic Congress (ADC), citing an unresolved ownership dispute and the need to protect lives and property after a recent disturbance.

Commissioner for Lands and Housing, Barrister Nasiru Dantsoho, announced the action on Monday. 

He said the decision followed a review of the property’s certificate of occupancy and other documents, which showed the building originally belonged to the defunct Savannah Bank, with no official record of a change in ownership.



**Official Explanation**

Dantsoho stated that the government had written to the Nigeria Deposit Insurance Corporation (NDIC), described as the custodian of the property, seeking clarification on its current ownership status. No response had been received at the time of the announcement.

*“Following a review of the certificate of occupancy and other relevant documents, we discovered that this property belongs to the Savannah Bank and that ownership has not changed as of today. We have written to the NDIC… but we are yet to receive a response,”* he said.

The commissioner explained that the premises would remain sealed pending the NDIC’s reply to prevent further disturbances and safeguard public safety. 

He invited anyone claiming ownership to present relevant documents for verification, after which the property could be released to its rightful owner. 

He also warned that any attempt to forcefully enter the sealed building would attract legal action, including prosecution.



**Rising Political Tension**

The sealing comes amid heightened political tension in Sokoto State. The ADC has previously alleged attacks on its supporters, including women at a rally, damage to its campaign office, and an assault on the convoy of its governorship candidate, Manir Muhammad Dan’iya. 

The party has also accused the state government of attempts to frustrate its presidential campaign flag-off, now scheduled for November 4.

The government maintained that the sealing was a precautionary measure focused on property ownership and security rather than partisan targeting.
