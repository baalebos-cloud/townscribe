**Abuja, Nigeria – August 3, 2026** — Nigerian news platform Parallel Facts has released an opinion commentary casting doubt on President Bola Tinubu’s recent assertion that the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) will deliver a free and fair election in 2027.

In a video posted on Monday, the outlet stated: “*Tinubu says INEC will conduct a free and fair 2027 election. Based on the patterns we’ve seen in recent years, we’re not convinced.”* 

The commentary examines the President’s statements alongside developments that have shaped public skepticism among many Nigerians.

President Tinubu recently told leaders of the Catholic Bishops’ Conference of Nigeria that INEC remains neutral and has never intimidated any political actor. 

He dismissed opposition concerns, saying those “crying wolf” may simply be “*afraid of their own shadows.”* 

The President also declared he is prepared to “wrestle to the finishing point” in the 2027 contest.

The remarks came as political tension continues to rise less than a year before the general elections.

The video highlights several issues it says undermine confidence in the electoral process. These include questions around the neutrality of security agencies, the timing of high-level engagements with the new INEC leadership, recent police redeployments, and the wave of defections by sitting governors into the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC).

The commentary argues that such patterns create an uneven political environment and discourage broader participation, even as official assurances of impartiality continue.

The exchange reflects a wider national conversation. Opposition figures and some civil society voices have repeatedly expressed concern that the combination of institutional control and political realignments could tilt the playing field. 

Government officials and APC supporters counter that INEC is independent by law and that defections simply reflect the popularity of the ruling party’s policies.

Cardinal John Onaiyekan and other voices have also warned that even a technically free election may not fully reflect the will of the people if structural imbalances persist.

With the 2027 presidential election approaching, public trust in INEC remains a central issue. 

Whether official assurances will translate into broad confidence on election day remains one of the defining questions of the coming months.
