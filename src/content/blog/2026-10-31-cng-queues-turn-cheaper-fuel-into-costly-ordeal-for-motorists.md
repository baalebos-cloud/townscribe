**Lagos, Nigeria—October 31, 2026**—Motorists who embraced compressed natural gas (CNG) to escape high petrol prices are now facing a new headache: long queues and limited refueling stations that turn the cheaper fuel into a costly ordeal of time and frustration.

The issue featured prominently in Saturday’s newspaper headlines, according to a roundup by \*TheCable\*. Reports from **Punch** highlighted how drivers in Lagos, Ogun, and other states who spent hundreds of thousands of naira converting their vehicles are spending hours—sometimes overnight—waiting to refill.



**Long Queues and Limited Stations**

At stations such as NIPCO in Magboro, Ogun State, and IOGC in Ojota, Lagos, queues of 30 to over 50 vehicles are common, stretching into the night. 

Commercial drivers and private motorists complain that the time lost cancels out the savings from CNG, which is significantly cheaper than petrol, priced between ₦1,330 and ₦1,405 per litre.

The Presidential Initiative on Compressed Natural Gas and Electric Vehicles (Pi-CNG) has acknowledged the challenge, attributing it to rising demand as more people convert their vehicles. 

Officials say additional stations are expected to open before year-end in places like Kano and Abuja, following President Bola Tinubu’s directive to expand the network toward 1,000 facilities.



**Other Front-Page Stories**

While CNG queues led many reports, the 100-point manifesto of Obafemi Hamzat, the APC governorship candidate in Lagos, also dominated front pages. 

Hamzat, the current deputy governor, unveiled “*The Lagos Promise”* on Friday, pledging single-digit mortgages, expansion of public buses from 1,000 to 4,000, improved healthcare, inner-road construction, and better security.

Other headlines covered former President Olusegun Obasanjo’s criticism of Tinubu’s comments on debt, ongoing counterfeiting of drugs and food products, and the federal government’s new monthly ₦50,000 cash transfers to vulnerable households.

Motorists continue to call for faster expansion of CNG infrastructure to match the growing number of converted vehicles and ease the daily struggle at the pumps.
