**Abuja, Nigeria—November 25, 2026**—In a decisive move to tackle the growing menace of financial fraud, the Nigeria Police Force on Wednesday convened a high-level stakeholders’ meeting aimed at forging tighter collaboration between law enforcement, banks, fintech companies, telecommunications operators, and regulators.

The NPF VIGILANT Stakeholders’ Deliberation Meeting, held at the NPF Peacekeeping Hall, Force Headquarters, Abuja, carried the theme “**Bridging the Gap: Accelerating Inter-Agency Synergy, Financial Fraud Prevention, and Rapid Funds Recovery.”** 

**I**t was hosted by the Assistant Inspector-General of Police in charge of the Department of Information and Communication Technology.

Representatives from commercial and microfinance banks, financial technology firms, telecommunications operators, regulatory agencies, law enforcement bodies, and the Judiciary gathered to confront persistent gaps in fraud reporting, information sharing, and coordinated response. 

Participants agreed that existing systems often move too slowly once money has left a victim’s account, allowing fraudsters valuable time to dissipate funds across multiple platforms.

Key outcomes from the deliberations included a consensus to harmonize fraud incident reporting protocols so that intelligence can be shared in near real time. 

Stakeholders also stressed the need to streamline account-freezing procedures and clarify inter-bank escalation channels, always within the bounds of existing laws and judicial processes. 

Stronger standards for handling digital evidence and closer working relationships between investigators, regulators, and the courts were identified as critical to successful prosecution of financial crimes.

The Nigeria Police Force used the occasion to issue a clear call to action: Deposit Money Banks, Microfinance Banks and fintech companies must prioritize participation in the NPF VIGILANT framework. 

The platform is designed to enable real-time fraud reporting, coordinated response, and faster recovery of stolen funds. Institutions interested in onboarding were directed to contact npf@vigilant.ng or call +234 806 090 7855 and +234 704 551 3679.

Force Public Relations Officer CSP Ani Iniedu, who signed the official statement, reaffirmed the Force’s commitment to technology-driven policing. 

*“The Nigeria Police Force remains committed to technology-driven policing and sustained inter-agency collaboration to protect Nigerians, combat financial crime, and safeguard the financial system,”* the statement read.



The meeting reflects a broader recognition that financial fraud has evolved beyond the capacity of any single agency. 

As digital transactions continue to expand across Nigeria, the ability of criminals to move money at speed has outpaced traditional investigative methods. 

By bringing banks, fintechs, telecom operators and the police into a shared operational framework, the Force hopes to close the window that fraudsters currently exploit.

For ordinary Nigerians who have lost life savings to phishing, romance scams or unauthorised transfers, the message is one of cautious optimism. 

Faster freezing of accounts and clearer recovery pathways could mean the difference between permanent loss and eventual restitution. 

Whether the VIGILANT framework delivers on that promise will depend on how quickly financial institutions respond to the Police invitation and how effectively the agreed protocols are implemented on the ground.



***This report is based on the official statement issued by the Nigeria Police Force on November 25, 2026.***
