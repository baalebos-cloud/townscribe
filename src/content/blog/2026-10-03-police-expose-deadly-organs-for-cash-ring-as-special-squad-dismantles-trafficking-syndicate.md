**Abuja, Nigeria—October 3, 2026**—In a stark reminder of the dark underbelly of Nigeria’s growing black market in human organs, the Nigeria Police Force has released a detailed documentary titled “*Organs for Cash: Inside the Investigation.”* 

The film offers a rare, behind-the-scenes look at how operatives of the Force Intelligence Department’s Special Tactical Squad (FID-STS) tracked, penetrated and dismantled a sophisticated organ-trafficking syndicate.

The official police account @PoliceNG announced the release on Saturday, urging the public to watch the full documentary on YouTube. 

The production walks viewers through months of intelligence gathering, undercover work and the eventual sting that brought the alleged ring to its knees.



**How the Syndicate Operated**

According to police findings, the network specialized in recruiting desperate, often poorly educated young men from satellite communities in Nasarawa State and neighboring areas. 

Recruiters allegedly used false promises of jobs or financial relief to lure victims to hospitals in Abuja, where their kidneys were surgically removed.

One of the key suspects, 23-year-old Emmanuel Ode of Auta Balifi, confessed during interrogation to acting as a recruiter. Police say on April 24, 2026, he persuaded 22-year-old Samuel Ezekiel to travel to Wellington Hospital in Life Camp, Abuja. 

There, Ezekiel’s kidney was allegedly harvested in exchange for just $1,250—roughly ₦1.7 million at the prevailing exchange rate.

A second alleged victim claimed he had been similarly recruited by the same agent in 2022 and paid ₦7 million after his kidney was removed. 

Investigators further allege that official documents—including National Identification Numbers, birth certificates, age declarations, affidavits, and consent letters—were forged to legitimize the procedures.



**The Arrests That Broke the Ring**

Acting on credible intelligence, FID-STS operatives launched a sting operation on the evening of August 19, 2026, in Auta Balifi, Karu Local Government Area of Nasarawa State. Four suspects were arrested:



\- Emmanuel Ode, 23  

\- Dr Benjamin Oyime, 48, a nephrologist (kidney consultant) linked to Wellington Hospital  

\- Dr Daniel Otukpa, 53, also a nephrologist and Acting Director of Clinical Services at the same hospital  

\- David Idoko, 25  

Clothes and designer items allegedly purchased with proceeds of the crime were recovered as exhibits. The suspects were later paraded before the media.



**A Wider Crisis**

The case has thrown a harsh spotlight on Nigeria’s underground organ trade. Medical experts and investigators note that vulnerable young people in low-income communities around Abuja and Nasarawa have become soft targets. 

Prices paid to *“donors”* are often a fraction of what the organs later fetch on the black market, sometimes running into tens of millions of naira when sold to wealthy recipients.

Wellington Hospital has publicly denied direct involvement in illegal transactions, stating that its facilities were merely rented to specialist teams under commercial arrangements. The matter remains under investigation.



**Police Message and Public Appeal**

By releasing the documentary, the Nigeria Police Force aims to both celebrate the operational success of its Special Tactical Squad and raise public awareness about the dangers of organ trafficking. 

Force authorities have repeatedly stressed that organ harvesting remains a serious crime under Nigerian law and that investigations into wider networks continue.

As the documentary makes clear, the battle against this form of human trafficking is far from over. For many young Nigerians struggling under economic pressure, the promise of quick cash can carry a permanent and devastating price.
