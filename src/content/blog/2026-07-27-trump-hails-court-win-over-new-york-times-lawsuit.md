President Donald Trump declared a significant legal victory Monday against The New York Times, announcing that a federal judge rejected the newspaper’s latest effort to dismiss his high-stakes defamation lawsuit and directed the filing of an updated complaint.

In a statement posted on Truth Social and widely shared by accounts including Breaking911, Trump framed the ruling as another setback for the outlet after what he described as “*10 years of harassment, slander, and libel.”* 

He said the *“Highly Respected Judge” g*ranted his side’s request for a revised complaint that will detail, “*in extreme detail, all the ways in which The Times has repeatedly and consistently acted, with actual malice in defaming me, my family, our Great MAGA Movement, and America, itself.”*

Trump vowed to keep pressing the case, writing that he and his legal team “*will continue to hold The Times and their ‘comrades’ in the Mainstream Media accountable for publishing, promoting, and dealing in Fake News.”* He closed with his familiar sign-off: “*Thank you for your attention to this matter!”*

The $15 billion lawsuit, originally filed in September 2025 in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Florida before Judge Steven Merryday, accuses The New York Times, several of its reporters, and publisher Penguin Random House of defaming Trump through articles and a book published in the run-up to the 2024 election. 

Trump has long claimed the coverage was designed to damage his reputation as a businessman and undermine his campaign.

The case has already seen procedural twists. Judge Merryday struck the original 85-page complaint in September 2025, calling it overly long, repetitive, and filled with *“tedious and burdensome*” language that violated federal rules requiring a short and plain statement of the claims. 

He ordered a more focused amended complaint limited to 40 pages, which Trump’s lawyers filed in October 2025. 

The Times later moved to dismiss the revised suit, arguing it failed to meet the high “*actual malice*” standard required for public figures under First Amendment precedent.

Trump’s Monday announcement indicates the judge declined to throw out the case entirely and instead directed further refinement of the pleadings. 

The development keeps the litigation alive and positions the next phase around specific allegations of knowing or reckless falsehoods.

The New York Times has consistently rejected the claims, describing the suit as meritless and an attempt to intimidate independent journalism. 

In earlier statements, the paper said the action “lacks any legitimate legal claims and instead is an attempt to stifle and discourage independent reporting.”

Legal observers note that defamation cases against major news organizations face a steep climb under the actual-malice standard established in **New York Times v. Sullivan**. Trump has pursued multiple high-profile media lawsuits in recent years, some resulting in settlements or dismissals, as part of a broader campaign against outlets he labels “*fake news.”*

The updated complaint, once filed, will give both sides clearer battle lines. Trump’s legal team is expected to expand on alleged patterns of bias and factual errors, while the Times is likely to reassert constitutional protections for reporting on public figures and political candidates.

As the case proceeds in Florida federal court, it remains one of the most closely watched media-defamation battles involving a sitting president, with potential implications for press freedom, journalistic accountability, and the limits of aggressive coverage of political leaders.
