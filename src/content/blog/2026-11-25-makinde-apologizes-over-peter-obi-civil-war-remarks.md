**Ibadan—November 25, 2026—Oyo** State Governor, Engr. Seyi Makinde has publicly apologized over the misinterpretation of his remarks in a recent national television interview that triggered outrage, especially among supporters of Mr. Peter Obi and people of the South-East.

The apology, captured in a video posted by Oyo Affairs at 8:45 p.m. on Monday, November 24, 2026, which has already garnered over 286,700 views, was delivered at the public presentation of *Monday Lines* and eight other books authored by Dr. Lasisi Olagunju in Ibadan.

*“It was a pleasure to be part of the public presentation... I also apologized for any statements in my recent interview that could have been misunderstood by anyone. I will endeavor to speak my thoughts more clearly going forward to leave little room for misinterpretation,”* Makinde wrote in a follow-up post on X.

The controversy erupted after his appearance on *The Rooftop* programme hosted by Kayode Okikiolu on Channels Television, where he discussed Nigeria’s political history, national unity, and the place of the South-East ahead of the 2027 presidential race.

While marketing his proposed presidential ticket with former DSS Director-General Lawal Daura against the proposed Peter Obi–Rabiu Kwankwaso ticket of the Nigeria Democratic Congress, NDC, Makinde argued that the historical North and South-West alliance had preserved Nigeria’s stability during crises.

He then referenced the January 1966 military coup and its consequences—remarks critics said invoked one of Nigeria’s most painful episodes in a manner that disparaged the South-East and questioned the electability of an Obi-led ticket.

Obi’s supporters, popularly called Obidients, and Igbo groups accused the governor—whom Obi once called *“my friend”*—of insulting Ndigbo on national TV, displaying shallow knowledge of history, and veiling bigotry. 

Some commentators demanded he name the ethnic groups targeted and withdraw the “truth” claim.

Makinde’s latest statement neither identified the specific comments nor withdrew his earlier arguments, promising only clearer communication.

At the Ibadan book event, he used the moment to preach his Reset Agenda, particularly a socio-political reset built on inclusion, transparency, and accountability, urging a return to the ideals of the founding fathers who named the country the Federal Republic of Nigeria.

He also condemned the fatal attacks on NDC members in Delta State, where six supporters of senatorial hopeful Ovie Omo-Agege were killed in Uduere.

*“No politician’s ambition is worth the blood of any Nigerian,”* he warned, urging youths not to become tools of destruction in 2027.

Reactions remain split—supporters hail accountability as leadership, critics call it a non-apology.
