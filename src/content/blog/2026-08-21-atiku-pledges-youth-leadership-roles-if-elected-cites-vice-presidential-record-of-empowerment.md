**Abuja, August 21, 2026** — Former Vice President and African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has renewed his appeal to young Nigerians, pointing to his record of opening doors for them during his time in office and promising the same approach if elected president in 2027.

In a widely shared statement, Atiku said: *“When I was Vice President, I gave many young Nigerians the opportunity to serve in government. Today, some of them are governors, ministers, directors and hold other important positions. Therefore, if I become President, young Nigerians will continue to be given opportunities to serve and lead.”*

The remark forms part of Atiku’s broader outreach to the youth demographic as he positions himself for what he has described as his final presidential contest. 

The 2027 election, he has repeatedly stated, will mark the end of his personal bids for the nation’s highest office regardless of the outcome.



**Track Record and Fresh Commitments**

During his tenure as Vice President between 1999 and 2007 under President Olusegun Obasanjo, Atiku was widely credited with assembling a relatively young and technocratic economic team. 

Several of those appointees later rose to prominent positions, including governorships and senior federal roles—a point he has long used to counter claims that he sidelined younger Nigerians.

In recent weeks, Atiku has sharpened this message at youth-focused gatherings. At the ADC National Youth Leadership Summit and other platforms, he urged young people to move beyond complaint and prepare to take power. 

He has pledged that at least 30 per cent of legislative candidates under the ADC platform would be under 40, with a significant number below 30. 

His recurring charge to the youth remains simple and direct: “REGISTER. ORGANISE. LEAD.”



**Context of the 2027 Race**

Atiku’s comments come as the 2027 presidential campaign season intensifies. He has framed his candidacy around experience, economic reform, education, and deliberate inclusion of younger voices. 

Critics have questioned the age of leading contenders and called for generational change; Atiku has responded by stressing both his health and his proven willingness to elevate capable young talent.

Supporters see the latest statement as consistent with a long-held philosophy of mentoring the next generation while leveraging institutional knowledge. 

Detractors argue that past appointments, however well-intentioned, did not sufficiently transform youth opportunities at scale.



**Looking Ahead**

As the ADC continues mobilizing, Atiku’s message to young Nigerians is clear: the doors he opened two decades ago will remain open—and wider—if he returns to Aso Rock. 

Whether that promise resonates enough to reshape the 2027 electoral landscape will depend on how effectively the party converts rhetoric into visible tickets, policies and grassroots organization in the months ahead.

For now, the former Vice President is betting that his record of empowerment, combined with fresh guarantees of inclusion, will persuade a generation hungry for both opportunity and results.
