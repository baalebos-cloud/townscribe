Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar stood before a surging crowd in Lokoja on Tuesday and declared that the long-abandoned Ajaokuta Steel Company would finally roar back to life under his watch.  

Addressing thousands of African Democratic Congress (ADC) supporters at Muhammadu Buhari Square, the presidential candidate framed the decades-old industrial dream as both an economic necessity and a moral debt owed to the people of Kogi Central. 

“*Ajaokuta must finally become a working steel mill. I want its furnaces working, its gates open to skilled workers and its promise felt in the homes of the people who have waited so long,”* he said. 

Atiku insisted the project is “*very doable”* through foreign investment that would require no direct government funding. He linked the steel complex’s revival to broader plans that would leave “*no part of Kogi behind.”* 

For Kogi East he pledged to develop the Idah River Port, fix roads, and improve water supply so farmers can move produce and families can access clean water. 

For Kogi West he promised to complete the long-delayed Kabba–Egbe road. He also committed to dredging the River Niger to upgrade Baro Port in neighboring Niger State, turning it into a northern gateway to rival Lagos’ Apapa.  

The rally carried a second, equally pointed message. Atiku renewed his call for a targeted subsidy on fuel refined inside Nigeria, arguing it would lower transport and production costs and restore an “*affordable life”* for ordinary families. 

*“I have already promised Nigerians that I will return the subsidy. I want to let you know that I mean it. I am serious about it, and I am sincere about it,”* he told the gathering.  

Supporters responded with sustained chants of “***Tinubu Must Go,***” a spontaneous outburst Atiku interpreted as exhaustion with prolonged hardship rather than mere partisan noise. 

He arrived by road from Abuja, saying the journey allowed him to assess the state of the Abuja–Lokoja highway, which he vowed to fix within two years if elected.  

In a statement issued by his Director of Strategic Communication, Phrank Shaibu, Atiku tied every promise to a single goal: giving families breathing room and young people real opportunities. “*Kogi’s strength lies in its people,*” he said.

“*You deserve a government that turns your state’s potential into jobs, working infrastructure and a life your families can afford.”*  

The Ajaokuta complex, conceived as the centrepiece of Nigeria’s industrialization, has remained incomplete for nearly half a century despite repeated government pledges. 

Tuesday’s commitment, delivered in the steel company’s backyard and coupled with concrete regional infrastructure plans, places the long-dormant plant at the centre of Atiku’s 2027 economic pitch. 

Whether foreign capital and political will can finally finish what successive administrations left unfinished remains the question hanging over the furnaces of Kogi.
