**BENIN CITY/ ASABA, Nigeria** — In a decisive move to protect the integrity of the national currency, the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) Benin Zonal Directorate has secured the conviction and sentencing of ten individuals for naira mutilation and abuse in Delta and Edo States.

The convicts — Lucky Onoberhie, Happy Joseph Isioma, Friday Ehiedu, Collins Ndudi Albert, Okorie Builder, Ernest Achieme, Isicheli Desmond Chijioke, Nwosu Davison, Enadegh Agbonmware, and Lucky Osaro — were found guilty of tampering with the naira by spraying it during parties and celebrations, an offence punishable under Section 21(3) of the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) Act, 2007.

Mugshots released by the EFCC show the defendants holding signage detailing their offences, underscoring the agency’s commitment to public awareness and deterrence. 

Justice F. A. Olubanjo of the Federal High Court in Asaba convicted and sentenced eight of the convicts to 12 months imprisonment each or a fine of N100,000. 

In a parallel ruling, Justice (Professor) C. A. Obiozor of the Federal High Court in Benin City sentenced two others to six months imprisonment or similar fines. All convicts reportedly undertook in writing to be of good conduct moving forward.

The cases stemmed from incidents where the defendants were alleged to have sprayed naira notes, particularly N200 denominations, while dancing at social events such as weddings. 

Prosecution counsel urged the courts to convict and sentence them accordingly after guilty pleas.

The CBN has long campaigned against the abuse, mutilation, and spraying of the naira, practices that damage currency, increase production costs, and undermine national symbols. 

The EFCC’s sustained enforcement in the South-South region sends a clear message that cultural celebrations must respect legal boundaries.

Public reactions to the convictions have been mixed: some praise the EFCC for focusing on “small” offences while calling for stronger action against high-profile corruption, while others commend the agency for upholding the law without fear or favour.

This latest success adds to the EFCC’s growing list of convictions for economic and financial crimes, reinforcing its mandate to sanitise Nigeria’s financial environment. 

As the agency continues operations nationwide, officials urge citizens to report violations and promote responsible handling of the national currency.

In a country where respect for institutions and symbols is often tested, these convictions serve as both punishment and reminder: the naira belongs to all Nigerians and deserves dignity.
