Prominent Peoples Democratic Party chieftain and former university lecturer Dr. Adetokunbo Pearse has delivered a sharp rebuke of President Bola Tinubu, declaring that the Nigerian leader “does not deserve to be our leader” over the country’s deepening electricity challenges.

Speaking on the Political Lens programme, Pearse argued that electricity supply has worsened under the current administration. 

He specifically criticised the decision to power the Presidential Villa largely with solar energy while ordinary Nigerians continue to endure unreliable national grid supply. 

Pearse also took issue with the President’s reported encouragement of state governors to generate their own electricity, framing it as an admission of failure to fix the country’s power infrastructure.

The comments come against the backdrop of the Federal Government’s multi-billion-naira solar mini-grid project at Aso Rock. 

Officials have said the Villa’s solar installation, funded with about ₦10 billion in the 2025 budget and an additional ₦7 billion in 2026, was designed to cut unsustainable electricity costs previously estimated in the tens of billions of naira annually and to reduce pressure on the national grid. The State House has indicated plans for a full transition away from the grid.

Government supporters have defended the move as practical cost-saving that frees up power for the public. Critics, including Pearse, see it differently: a privileged off-grid solution for the seat of power while millions of citizens remain trapped in darkness and high generator costs.

Pearse, a long-time critic of the President who has also raised questions about Tinubu’s educational records, used the platform to press the broader point that leadership requires solving national problems rather than insulating the elite from them. 

His remarks add to ongoing public debate about the pace and effectiveness of power sector reforms under the Tinubu administration.

Nigeria’s electricity challenges remain one of the country’s most persistent developmental bottlenecks, affecting households, businesses and overall economic productivity. 

As the conversation around grid reliability, renewable energy and state-level generation continues, voices like Pearse’s ensure the pressure on the Federal Government stays firmly in the public eye.
