**Lagos/Washington – September 5, 2026—A** Washington-based lobbying firm retained by former Vice President Atiku Abubakar has deleted its social media announcement claiming that its managing partner, Karl Von Batten, was appointed by US President Donald Trump to a White House presidential commission.

The firm, Von Batten-Montague-York, L.C., had posted on X earlier this week that Trump appointed Von Batten as a commissioner, expressing gratitude and describing the role as a tremendous honour reflecting his commitment to public service. 

The post did not name the specific commission or provide an official appointment letter. It has since been removed, with the White House offering no confirmation or denial.



**Clarification Emerges from Tinubu’s Camp**

The deletion followed comments by Sunday Dare, a media aide to President Bola Tinubu. Dare stated that the appointment was to the Commission on Presidential Scholars, an education-focused panel administered by the US Department of Education. 

The commission selects outstanding high school graduates for national recognition and holds no authority over foreign policy, national security, diplomacy, or US-Nigeria relations.

Senior US administration sources familiar with the matter indicated discomfort with the original framing, which created the impression of a White House policymaking role. 

The appointment does not make Von Batten a White House staffer or grant him influence over administration policy.



**Context of the $1.2 Million Lobbying Contract**

Von Batten, a Nigerian-born lobbyist and Republican, heads the firm that Atiku engaged in March 2026 under a 12-month, $1.2 million agreement registered with the US Department of Justice under the Foreign Agents Registration Act (FARA). 

The contract aims to strengthen Atiku’s reputational standing in the United States, facilitate engagements with US officials and Congress, and counter narratives associated with the Nigerian government ahead of the 2027 elections.



The firm has been active in sharing US Department of Justice records related to past civil forfeiture proceedings involving Tinubu with members of the Trump administration and congressional staff. These efforts form part of a broader pattern of Nigerian political actors intensifying lobbying in Washington as the 2027 presidential race gathers momentum.



**Political Sensitivities and Unanswered Questions**

The original announcement drew significant attention in Nigeria because of Von Batten’s client relationship with Atiku, the African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential candidate. 

Critics viewed the claim as an attempt to signal influence within the Trump orbit, while supporters of the lobbyist described any appointment as recognition of individual service.

As of Saturday evening, neither the White House nor official federal channels had issued a statement verifying the appointment or clarifying its terms. 

The firm’s X account remains active, but the specific post is no longer accessible.

The episode underscores the growing role of US-based lobbying in Nigeria’s domestic political contests. 

With both the ruling All Progressives Congress and opposition figures seeking favorable positioning in Washington, questions persist about the boundaries between legitimate advocacy and the optics of access.

Observers note that while the Commission on Presidential Scholars is a legitimate honor, its limited scope stands in contrast to the broader implications initially suggested by the firm’s language. Whether the deletion was voluntary or prompted by external pressure remains unclear.
