**Abuja/Port Harcourt—September 2, 2026**—Federal Capital Territory Minister Nyesom Wike has openly acknowledged the personal political danger he faces if President Bola Tinubu fails to win re-election in 2027, warning that opposition figures Peter Obi and Atiku Abubakar would come after him first.

Speaking during a media parley on Wednesday, the former Rivers State governor and still-PDP member left little room for ambiguity about the stakes of his high-profile support for the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) president.

*“I will be the first politician that I think they will come after. I will be the first politician. I know they will come for my head,”* Wike stated.



**Unwavering Loyalty Amid Clear Risks**

Wike framed his comments as a realistic assessment rather than a complaint. 

He said he understands that his vocal and consistent backing of Tinubu—despite remaining in the opposition Peoples Democratic Party—has made him a prime target for political reprisals should power change hands.

Yet the minister insisted the possibility of consequences has not shaken his resolve. 

*“Each day I sleep, I am thinking about the President’s reelection. When I am eating, I think about the President’s reelection. Everything I am doing now is about the President’s reelection,”* he added.

Wike has faced sustained criticism from both former allies and opponents for his decision to stand with Tinubu after the 2023 election. 

He maintained that the vilification does not deter him and that he has no regrets about the path he has chosen.



**Political Context and Broader Implications**

Wike’s unusual position—operating as a senior minister in a PDP-affiliated capacity while campaigning for an APC incumbent—has become one of the more closely watched storylines ahead of the 2027 presidential contest. 

He has repeatedly predicted strong performances for Tinubu across regions and has vowed not to allow opposition candidates to gain ground in key areas such as Rivers State and the FCT.

While some reports and headlines have referenced the prospect of jail terms or severe repercussions, any such outcome would depend on specific allegations, investigations, or legal processes rather than political rhetoric alone. 

Wike himself focused on the political rather than purely legal dimensions of the risk.



**A High-Stakes Personal Bet**

By publicly naming Obi and Atiku as the figures he expects to pursue him, Wike has elevated the personal drama surrounding his political gamble. 

Supporters of the minister view the statement as evidence of courage and conviction. Critics see it as further confirmation of deep internal divisions within the PDP and a willingness to prioritise personal and presidential loyalty over party solidarity.

As Nigeria’s political temperature rises more than a year before the next general election, Wike’s remarks underscore how high the personal costs of alignment have already become for some of the country’s most visible players. Whether his prediction of being the first target materializes will ultimately depend on the outcome of the 2027 vote—and on what follows it.

For now, the FCT Minister remains firmly on message: the only outcome he is working toward is another term for President Tinubu.
