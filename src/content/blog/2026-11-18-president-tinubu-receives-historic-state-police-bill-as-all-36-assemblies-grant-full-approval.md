**Abuja, Nigeria—November 18, 2026**— In a major step toward reforming Nigeria’s security architecture, President Bola Ahmed Tinubu on Wednesday received the Constitution (Sixth Alteration) Bill, 2026, which seeks to establish State Police Services across the country. 

The bill arrived at the Presidency after securing the unanimous approval of all 36 State Houses of Assembly, far exceeding the constitutional threshold.

The formal transmission was handled by the President’s Special Advisers on Senate and House of Representatives Matters, Senator Basheer Lado and Dr. Wasiu Olanrewaju-Smart. 

Chief of Staff to the President and Chairman of the Presidential Working Group on State Police, Femi Gbajabiamila, confirmed the development in an official statement.

Gbajabiamila noted that the endorsement by every state legislature surpasses the requirement under Section 9(2) of the 1999 Constitution, which mandates approval by at least two-thirds of the 36 states—equivalent to 24 assemblies. 

*“President Tinubu will review the Constitution Alteration Bill in consultation with the Attorney-General of the Federation before assenting to it at a formal signing ceremony,”* he stated.



**Path to Constitutional Change**



The journey of the bill has been deliberate and broad-based. The Senate passed the amendment on June 24, 2026, while the House of Representatives followed on July 23, 2026, both with the required two-thirds majority. It was then transmitted to the state assemblies in September for consideration. 

By mid-November, the Clerk to the National Assembly, Kamoru Ogunlana, confirmed that all 36 states had given their approval—an unprecedented show of consensus on a security reform of this magnitude.

The proposed alteration amends key provisions of the Constitution, particularly Section 214, to replace the exclusive Nigeria Police Force with a dual structure comprising a Federal Police Service and State Police Services. 

This framework is designed to allow states to establish and manage their own police formations while maintaining coordination with the federal level, addressing long-standing concerns about over-centralization of policing in a diverse federation.



**Parallel Work on Operational Framework**



Alongside the constitutional process, the Presidential Working Group on State Police, chaired by Gbajabiamila and supported by the Policy Advisory Committee, has completed extensive stakeholder engagements and nationwide consultations. 

These efforts produced a draft National Policing Bill intended to provide the detailed operational, administrative, and regulatory guidelines for the new system.

The draft has been submitted to the Federal Ministry of Justice for legislative drafting validation. Once validated, the Working Group will review it and present both the bill and its accompanying report to the President for consideration. 

Officials emphasize that the constitutional amendment alone does not activate state police; the National Policing Bill will define modalities, jurisdiction, funding, training standards, and inter-agency relationships to prevent conflicts between federal and state forces.



**Presidential Appreciation and National Context**



President Tinubu has expressed appreciation to the National Assembly, state governors, State Houses of Assembly and ordinary Nigerians for their commitment to tackling the country’s security challenges. 

The push for state police has gained momentum amid persistent insecurity in various regions, with advocates arguing that decentralized policing will improve response times, local intelligence, and community trust.

Critics have historically raised concerns about potential misuse by state governors, funding sustainability and the risk of political interference. The thorough consultations and the separate operational bill are intended to address these issues through clear legal safeguards.



With the bill now on the President’s desk and the Attorney-General of the Federation, Lateef Fagbemi, SAN, expected to provide legal advice, the formal assent ceremony is anticipated in the coming weeks. 

Once signed, Nigeria will take a historic step toward a more flexible and responsive national policing system, marking one of the most significant constitutional reforms in recent years.

The development reflects a rare alignment across federal and state institutions on a matter of national security, signalling that the long debate over state police may finally be moving from discussion to implementation.
