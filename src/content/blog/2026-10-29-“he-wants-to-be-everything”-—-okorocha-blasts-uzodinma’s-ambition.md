**Abuja, Nigeria—October 29, 2026**—Former Imo State Governor and Senator Rochas Okorocha has accused incumbent Governor Hope Uzodinma of harboring an insatiable ambition to occupy every available political position, including the office of Vice President.

In a video clip shared widely on social media on Thursday, Okorocha stated, *"Hope Uzodinma wants to be even vice president; he wants to be everything. In the last election, he wanted to be vice president to Ahmed Lawan.”*



**Context of the Comments**

The remarks came shortly after a Federal High Court in Abuja, presided over by Justice Obiora Egwuatu, nullified Uzodinma’s nomination as the All Progressives Congress (APC) candidate for the Imo West Senatorial District in the 2027 elections.

The court held that Uzodinma did not win the APC primary election conducted on May 18, 2026, which was monitored by the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC). 

It ruled that Okorocha secured the majority of votes and ordered the APC to forward his name to INEC as the authentic candidate. The judge also restrained Uzodinma from parading himself as the party’s flag bearer.

Okorocha, who governed Imo from 2011 to 2019, had challenged the recognition of Uzodinma, arguing that official results and INEC’s monitoring reports showed he was the rightful winner.



**Broader Political Friction**

The long-running rivalry between the two APC leaders intensified after the judgment. Okorocha has further described Uzodinma as arrogant for declaring he would be on the ballot while the matter was still in court. 

He also questioned how the governor could continue holding office until January 2028 while seeking a senatorial seat that begins in May 2027, urging him to resign if serious about the contest.

Okorocha expressed confidence that with his name now on the ballot, President Bola Tinubu’s victory in Imo State is assured, and he pledged to mobilize a “***Rainbow Coalition***” involving APC, PDP and other parties to deliver the state for the President.

The APC in Imo has, however, insisted that Uzodinma remains its candidate despite the ruling, signaling that further legal or political developments may follow.
