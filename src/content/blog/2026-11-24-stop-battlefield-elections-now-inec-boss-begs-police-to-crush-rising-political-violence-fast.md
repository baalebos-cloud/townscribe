**Abuja, Nigeria – November 24, 2026** – With just 53 days to the 2027 general elections, the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) has raised the alarm over rising campaign violence, urging the Nigeria Police Force to speed up arrests and investigations before bloodshed derails democracy.

INEC Chairman, Prof. Joash Amupitan, made the passionate plea on Tuesday in Abuja at a high-level interactive session convened by Inspector-General of Police Olatunji Disu with national chairmen, secretaries and leaders of registered political parties.

“*The police should expedite investigations and transmit completed case files to INEC to enable the Commission to prosecute electoral offenders under the Electoral Act 2026,”* Amupitan declared.

He warned that impunity must be checked before January, expressing deep worry over reported cases of campaign violence, intimidation and attacks on political opponents, journalists and electoral facilities.

*“Political competition should not be turned into a battlefield,”* he said. “*When participants cannot assemble, campaign or express views freely, the process is compromised long before election day.”*

Amupitan, who disclosed INEC temporarily suspended its own meeting to attend the security talks, cited Sections 96 and 97 of the Electoral Act 2026 which prohibit inciting statements, thuggery, private armed guards and masquerades at rallies. Section 95 mandates Commissioners of Police to provide security for rallies, while Section 148 empowers INEC to prosecute offenders after receiving police files.

*“INEC is not a security agency and has no power to arrest. Security at rallies rests with the Police,”* he stressed, urging adequate protection for polling units, collation centres and INEC facilities.

IGP Disu assured neutrality is non-negotiable, declaring zero tolerance for violence regardless of party. *“No ambition is worth the life of any Nigerian. We will not protect political interests; we will protect the Nigerian people,” h*e said, warning sponsors and financiers of thugs will be held accountable.

The warning comes amid chilling realities: the National Peace Committee recorded 109 breaches of the Peace Accord across 26 states and FCT between August 19 and October 27, with 33 cases of violence and thuggery. On Monday, gunfire in Uduere, Delta State during an NDC campaign left six dead.

Former PDP Secretary Senator Samuel Anyanwu even accused some local government chairmen of fuelling violence for governors.

Amupitan announced a security conference on November 26 with service chiefs and a nationwide mock BVAS accreditation on November 28, begging parties to participate and keep peace.
