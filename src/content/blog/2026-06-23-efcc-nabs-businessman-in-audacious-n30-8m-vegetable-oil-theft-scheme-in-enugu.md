**Enugu, Nigeria –** In a case that highlights the persistent ingenuity of economic saboteurs even in everyday commerce, operatives of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC) have arrested a 30-year-old businessman, Onwughalu Chikodi Godwin, for allegedly masterminding the theft of vegetable oil worth N30,853,800 from Bro-kenn Agro Allied Industries Limited.

The arrest, announced by the EFCC’s Enugu Zonal Directorate on Tuesday, June 23, 2026, underscores the agency’s ongoing vigilance against fraud that preys on legitimate businesses, erodes trust in commercial relationships, and disrupts local industries critical to Nigeria’s food security and economy.

According to the EFCC statement and details provided by the company’s counsel, Dr. Mazi Udegbulem, Godwin, described as a major customer of the oil refining and processing firm, allegedly conspired with others still at large to systematically siphon off oil between January 9, 2024, and May 26, 2025.

The modus operandi was deceptively simple yet technically clever: manipulating the company’s weighbridge system. 

Vehicles entering to lift paid-for oil are inspected and weighed empty, loaded, and reweighed. The difference determines the net weight and value of the oil loaded.

Investigators allege that Godwin and his accomplices filled extra jerry cans with water to inflate the initial *“empty” weight readings.* 

*After passing the weighbridge, they would drain the water, refill the cans with stolen vegetable oil, and exit with significantly more product than paid for.* 

This scheme reportedly allowed them to extract approximately 960kg of oil—valued at around N2.496 million per trip—on multiple occasions.

A pivotal incident in May 2025 exposed the plot. Godwin arrived with a Sienna bus carrying 115 gallons of jerry cans, having paid only for one ton (roughly 63 gallons of 18 liters each). 

Alerted company officials inspected the vehicle and discovered 40 gallons filled with water. The weight discrepancy matched exactly the amount needed to facilitate the theft, raising red flags that had been building for months.

Preliminary investigations suggest the suspect had gained the company’s confidence over time, exploiting that trust to perpetrate what the EFCC describes as a calculated betrayal. 

The total loss to Bro-kenn Agro Allied Industries is put at N30.8 million.

This arrest comes amid the EFCC’s sustained efforts to combat various forms of economic and financial crimes across Nigeria. 

While high-profile cyber-fraud (“Yahoo Yahoo") and political corruption cases often dominate headlines, incidents like this reveal how fraud can infiltrate supply chains in critical sectors such as agro-processing. 

Vegetable oil is a staple in Nigerian households; disruptions and losses at the production level can contribute to price volatility and reduced availability.

Bro-kenn Agro-Allied Industries, with operations tied to refining and processing, represents the kind of local enterprise that governments and agencies aim to protect to bolster food production and reduce import dependency. 

The company’s decision to involve the EFCC signals growing corporate willingness to push back against insider threats rather than absorbing losses quietly.

Public reactions on social media to the EFCC announcement were mixed. Some praised the agency’s swift action *(“Thank for your good job”)*, while others expressed skepticism about the scale—questioning focus on a N30 million case amid larger alleged graft—or wondered about accountability for bigger players.

The EFCC has stated that Godwin will be charged in court as soon as investigations are concluded. This typically involves gathering forensic evidence on the weighbridge manipulations, tracing any proceeds, identifying accomplices, and preparing a robust case for prosecution.

For Nigerian businesses, the case serves as a cautionary tale: robust internal controls, regular audits of weighing systems, and verification protocols for high-volume customers are essential. 

It also illustrates how seemingly mundane operational vulnerabilities—such as weighbridge procedures—can be exploited with devastating financial impact.

As Nigeria grapples with economic pressures, including inflation and the need for stronger local production, stories like this remind us that the fight against corruption is not abstract. 

It plays out in factories, markets, and boardrooms, affecting jobs, livelihoods, and national resilience.

The EFCC’s Enugu team continues to appeal for credible information from the public to aid ongoing probes. In the words of anti-graft advocates, every successful prosecution, regardless of scale, chips away at the culture of impunity that has long plagued Nigeria’s economic landscape.
