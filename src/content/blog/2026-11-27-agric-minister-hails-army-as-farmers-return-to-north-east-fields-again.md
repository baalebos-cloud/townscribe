**MAIDUGURI, Nigeria—November 27, 2026—From** battlefields to bountiful harvests—the story of Nigeria's North-East is taking a hopeful turn as farmers once driven from their lands by insurgency return in large numbers, thanks to the guns shielding their hoes.

The Honourable Minister of Agriculture and Food Security, Distinguished Senator Abubakar Kyari, CON, gave this glowing verdict on November 26, 2026, during a visit to Headquarters Joint Task Force (North East), Operation HADIN KAI, at Maimalari Cantonment, Maiduguri.

According to a statement by Capt. Mohammed Goni, Acting Media Information Officer, the Minister commended the Theatre Commander, Major General Ibikunle Ajose, officers, and men of the Joint Task Force for sustained efforts in restoring agricultural activities, protecting farming communities, and contributing to national food security.

Kyari observed that prolonged insecurity had severely disrupted agricultural production, restricted farmers' access to farmlands, and undermined rural livelihoods across Borno, Adamawa and Yobe States for over a decade.

But that narrative, he said, is changing.

*"The improved security environment created by ongoing military operations is gradually reversing this trend, with farmers returning to their farmlands, resuming cultivation and moving harvested produce to markets in Maiduguri, Kano and other parts of the country,"* the Minister noted.

He particularly praised the Theatre Commander for grasping the link between security, agricultural productivity, and economic recovery, noting that securing farming communities, guaranteeing access to land, and protecting movement of produce were yielding measurable results.

The impact is already visible. Military authorities cited bumper harvests including increased watermelon production in Monguno and the Kukawa general area, as well as improved beans harvests around Molai and Delwa in Borno State—communities once synonymous with fear. 

Kyari stressed that sustainable food security demands more than seeds and subsidies. It requires a secure enabling environment where farmers can plant without fear, harvest safely and transport produce without ambush.

*"These interventions are not only revitalizing agricultural production and stimulating local economies but also complementing the resettlement and recovery initiatives of the Borno State Government, as well as the Federal Government's declaration of a state of emergency on food security,*" he added.

He paid glowing tribute to officers and soldiers who paid the supreme price, saying their courage remains fundamental to peace and rural recovery.

In his response, Major General Ajose said Operation HADIN KAI has deliberately integrated food security into its operational priorities. 

Troops now conduct routine clearance of Improvised Explosive Devices (IEDs) along farm routes, regular patrols around farming communities, and armed escorts for harvested produce along major supply routes outside the theatre.

He reaffirmed commitment to sustain the tempo against Boko Haram and ISWAP terrorists, insisting that protecting lives, livelihoods, and harvests remains central to restoring stability.

For thousands of families, the message is simple: the farm road is open again.
