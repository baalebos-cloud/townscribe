In a series of pointed remarks that blended faith, personal testimony, and political urgency, Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde on Sunday told Nigerians that the country is simply not working for the majority—and invited those who feel the same to join him in resetting it.

Speaking after worship at Dunamis International Gospel Centre in Abuja and later at the Universal Reformed Christian Church (NKST) in Mabushi, the Allied Peoples Movement (APM) presidential candidate for 2027 framed his message with unusual clarity. 

*“If Nigeria is not working for you, then join us to Reset Nigeria,*” he said, adding that those for whom the system still functions could continue as before. *“But I know Nigeria is not working.”*

Makinde used the dual church appearances to ground his call in both spiritual and personal terms. He thanked Dr. Pastor Paul Enenche and the Dunamis congregation for their warm reception, then moved to NKST, where he recalled a longstanding relationship with the church that stretches back to his time in Oyo State. 

Addressing the congregation, he acknowledged the security challenges facing Benue State and said the people there *“can hold us accountable to our plan to reset security.”*

*The governor’s most resonant line came when he flipped a common Nigerian complaint on its head. “Nigeria happened to me,”* he declared—not as a story of hardship, but of opportunity. 

Free education, access to university, and the chance to build a career and business outside his home state had shaped his life. Now, he said, he wants to serve at the highest level so that Nigeria can “happen” positively to other citizens rather than become a source of frustration and failure.

His proposed Reset Agenda rests on four pillars: expanding the economy to lift more people out of poverty, overhauling the security architecture so farmers and ordinary travellers can move without fear, reforming education to produce skills that actually earn a living, and resetting the socio-political environment to make the system more inclusive. He pointed to progress already achieved in Oyo State as evidence that the approach can work.

Makinde also stressed the spiritual dimension, urging Nigerians to pray for divine mercy on the nation and for God’s will—rather than any individual’s ambition—to prevail. 

The visits formed part of a broader mobilisation drive in the Federal Capital Territory as the 2027 election cycle intensifies.

The message lands at a moment when many citizens continue to grapple with insecurity, economic pressure, and a sense that the promise of the Nigerian project has slipped out of reach for ordinary people. 

Whether Makinde’s call to “press the reset button” resonates beyond the pews and into the wider electorate remains the central political question of the coming months.
