**Abuja, Nigeria – September 1, 2026** — Tension is rising in Nigeria’s opposition ranks as the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) intensifies its claim that the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) published a candidate list that differs sharply from the one the party officially signed and uploaded.

Speaking on ARISE News’ **Prime Time**, Senator Victor Umeh, who represents Anambra Central and is a prominent NDC figure, delivered a pointed challenge to the electoral body. 

He stated that the NDC National Chairman has confirmed the list he personally signed and uploaded through the official portal is not the version that later appeared in the public domain.

*“Instead of INEC asking NDC to show evidence to prove its case, it is the commission that has to show the trail of how they got the list of candidates they published; if it was received through the portal, they should show it,*” Umeh said.



**The Heart of the Dispute**

The controversy centres largely on several State House of Assembly constituencies in Anambra State — the political base of NDC presidential candidate Peter Obi. 

The party has identified multiple names it says were never nominated, authorised, or submitted by its leadership. 

National Chairman Moses Cleopas Zuwoghe has publicly insisted that, as the custodian of the party’s portal access code, he never uploaded those names.

NDC leaders describe the published list as containing “*strange”* or “*unknown”* individuals and have called the development a deliberate attempt to undermine the party’s candidates and create internal disaffection, particularly in a state critical to its 2027 ambitions.

INEC has responded by opening an investigation into the source of the disputed entries. The commission maintains it did not unilaterally replace candidates and says its electronic and documentary records will establish the history and origin of every name that appeared.



**Why This Matters Now**

For a relatively new party still consolidating after a turbulent registration process and court battles earlier in 2026, the integrity of its candidate list is existential. 

Any perception that the electoral umpire altered submissions risks eroding public confidence ahead of the 2027 general elections and could trigger legal challenges that further delay clarity for voters and aspirants.

Umeh’s appearance on national television framed the issue not merely as a technical glitch but as a question of transparency and burden of proof. 

In his view, the digital audit trail of portal submissions should settle the matter quickly and objectively — placing the responsibility squarely on INEC to demonstrate the path each name took from upload to publication.

As the probe continues, both the party and the commission face pressure to resolve the discrepancies without further damaging trust in the electoral process. 

For ordinary Nigerians watching the exchanges, the episode is a reminder of how fragile the mechanics of candidate nomination remain, even in an increasingly digitised system.

The coming days will show whether the digital records can restore clarity — or whether the dispute will deepen into another protracted legal and political battle.
