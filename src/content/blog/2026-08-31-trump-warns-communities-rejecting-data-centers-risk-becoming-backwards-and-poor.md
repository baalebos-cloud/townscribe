**Washington, August 31, 2026** — President Donald Trump issued a blunt warning Monday to American communities opposing new data centers, declaring that those that turn them away risk ending up “backwards and poor” while places that welcome the facilities stand to gain jobs, lower taxes, and lasting prosperity.

In a Truth Social post that quickly circulated widely, Trump wrote: *“The only reason that communities throughout the U.S.A. should not want Data Centers is if they want to end up being backwards and poor. If they want to be successful and rich, with far lower taxes and jobs all over the place, let Data Reign.”*

He framed data centers as a national ***“Golden Goose,”*** cautioning that rejecting them would be self-inflicted damage. 

*“The good news is that there are plenty of other places that want them. If we kill the Golden Goose, you will only have yourselves to blame,”* Trump added. 

He closed by noting that China “could not be happier with this anti Data Center movement. Actually, they can’t believe it is happening!”



**Rising Local Resistance Meets National AI Ambitions**

The president’s comments arrive amid intensifying grassroots pushback against the rapid expansion of data centers needed to power artificial intelligence. Residents and local officials in multiple states have cited concerns over higher electricity bills, water consumption, land use, and environmental effects. 

Hundreds of counties and municipalities have imposed bans, moratoria, or delays, and several high-profile projects have been blocked or slowed.

Public opposition has surged, with recent polls showing a majority of Americans now opposed to new data centers in their areas. 

The issue has become a flashpoint in some midterm races, forcing candidates in both parties to recalibrate messaging even as the Trump administration continues to prioritize domestic AI infrastructure as a strategic priority.

Trump has consistently positioned large-scale data center construction as essential to keeping the United States ahead of China in the global AI race. 

His administration has pursued faster permitting, incentives, and other measures to accelerate the buildout, arguing that the facilities generate construction jobs, long-term employment, and significant local tax revenue.



**Strategic Stakes and Competing Priorities**

Supporters of the buildout echo Trump’s view that data centers are foundational infrastructure for the AI era, comparable to earlier industrial expansions. 

Critics counter that the benefits often flow more to tech companies than to host communities, while the costs — especially energy demand — are felt locally. 

Some Republican governors and candidates have also grown more cautious, seeking stricter local protections even as they broadly support technology investment.

Trump’s latest message leaves little room for nuance. 

By casting rejection of data centers as a path to economic stagnation and Chinese advantage, he is doubling down on a pro-development stance at a moment when many voters are prioritizing affordability and local control.

As the debate intensifies heading into the November elections, communities across the country face a practical choice: embrace the facilities and the economic activity they bring, or hold the line on concerns about power, resources, and quality of life. 

Trump has made clear which path he believes leads to success — and which one, in his words, risks leaving America behind.
