**Lagos, Nigeria—July 31, 2026** - In a sharp critique that underscores growing political restlessness ahead of Nigeria’s 2027 general elections, former All Progressives Congress (APC) National Vice Chairman (North-West) and political strategist **Salihu Lukman** has declared that President Bola Ahmed Tinubu and the ruling APC have disappointed Nigerians, insisting the country urgently needs a fundamental change of direction.

Appearing on Channels Television’s **Politics Today**, Lukman stated plainly: *“Nigeria desires a change for the better by every standard of measurement. APC and President Asiwaju Tinubu have disappointed Nigerians.”* 

The remarks capture the frustration of a politician who once held a senior position within the ruling party but has since become one of its most consistent internal and external critics.



**From Insider to Outspoken Critic**

Lukman’s journey reflects the turbulence within Nigeria’s political class. After rising through APC ranks, he later resigned from the party, citing a departure from progressive ideals and poor governance outcomes under the Tinubu administration. In recent months, he joined efforts to build an opposition platform under the African Democratic Congress (ADC) but has now expressed deep frustration with the pace and direction of those efforts.

In related interviews, including on ARISE News, Lukman revealed he had suspended his ADC membership out of exasperation. 

He warned that the opposition risks repeating the mistakes of 2023 by remaining divided and driven by personal ambitions rather than a collective national purpose. 

*“I don’t have any restraint. I want APC and President Asiwaju defeated and out of government in 2027. But the way we are going about it, I am frustrated,”* he said.



**A Call for Unity and Accountability**

Lukman argues that many Nigerians feel boxed in by a government that, in his view, has failed to deliver on accountability and improved living standards. 

He maintains that genuine political competition is essential for better leadership to emerge, but fears the opposition’s current trajectory may hand the APC another victory.

His comments highlight a broader tension in Nigerian politics: the gap between public desire for change and the ability of opposition forces to organize effectively. 

While Lukman is clear about his goal of removing the current administration, he simultaneously challenges fellow opposition figures to prioritise national interest over individual ambitions.

As the 2027 election cycle gathers momentum, voices like Lukman’s are likely to shape the emerging debate — one that pits the ruling party’s record against calls for a fresh political alternative. 

Whether that alternative materialises will depend on how seriously opposition leaders take the warnings of one of their own most experienced strategists.
