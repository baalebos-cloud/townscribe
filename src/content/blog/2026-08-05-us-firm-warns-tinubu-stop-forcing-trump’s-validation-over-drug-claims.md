**ABUJA/WASHINGTON** — A United States communications and lobbying firm has sharply rebuked the Nigerian government for what it described as attempts to project American presidential endorsement for President Bola Tinubu, warning that U.S. President Donald Trump “***does not associate with drug traffickers.***”

In a statement released on its official X account and widely circulated on Wednesday, Von Batten-Montague-York, L.C., reacted to remarks by Nigeria’s Permanent Representative to the United Nations, Jimoh Ibrahim. 

During a television interview, Ibrahim reportedly said Tinubu would meet Trump during the upcoming United Nations General Assembly in September.

The firm accused Tinubu and his associates of trying to convince Nigerians and the international community that Trump condones longstanding drug trafficking allegations that have trailed the Nigerian president for decades. 

It said it had already forwarded a video of Ibrahim’s interview to senior members of the Trump administration and planned to share it with members of the U.S. Congress and the State Department.

“*As we have stated before, Trump does not tolerate drug trafficking. We will continue briefing the White House on these developments, and Dr. Von Batten fully intends to brief Trump directly regarding the drug trafficking allegations against Bola Tinubu*,” the firm stated.

**Background of the Allegations**

The claims center on a 1993 U.S. Department of Justice civil forfeiture case involving funds allegedly linked to a heroin trafficking network in Chicago. 

Court documents from that period named Tinubu alongside others in connection with bank accounts and money transfers that authorities believed were tied to drug proceeds. Nearly $2 million was seized at the time.

Importantly, Tinubu was never criminally charged or convicted in the matter. He has consistently denied any involvement in criminal activity, and the case remained a civil proceeding without a finding of criminal guilt. 

The Nigerian presidency has previously dismissed renewed attention to the decades-old files as a politically motivated campaign, particularly noting the firm’s reported links to former Vice President Atiku Abubakar.

Von Batten-Montague-York has in recent weeks circulated more than 60 pages of related Department of Justice records to Trump administration officials and congressional staff, arguing that the information should inform any high-level engagement with the Nigerian leader.

**Diplomatic Tensions and Public Reaction**

The exchange underscores the complex and often tense undercurrents in Nigeria–U.S. relations under the current administrations. 

Tinubu’s government has faced criticism at home and abroad over security challenges, economic reforms, and governance issues, while opposition figures have sought to leverage international platforms to raise questions about the president’s past.

Supporters of the Nigerian leader view the lobbying efforts as an affront to national sovereignty and an attempt to undermine a democratically elected president through foreign channels. 

Critics, however, argue that transparency around historical legal records remains essential, especially when high-level diplomatic meetings are being floated.

As the United Nations General Assembly approaches, both sides appear locked in a public battle of narratives. 

Whether a Trump–Tinubu meeting materializes remains uncertain, but the firm’s intervention ensures the old allegations will stay in the spotlight of American political and congressional circles.

For ordinary Nigerians, the episode is another reminder of how domestic politics continues to spill onto the global stage — with reputations, alliances, and national image hanging in the balance.
