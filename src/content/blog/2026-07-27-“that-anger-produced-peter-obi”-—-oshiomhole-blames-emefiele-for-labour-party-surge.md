**Abuja, Nigeria – July 27, 2026** — Senator Adams Oshiomhole has stirred fresh debate over the roots of Peter Obi’s unexpected 2023 presidential surge, linking it directly to the severe cash shortages triggered by the Central Bank of Nigeria’s naira redesign under then-Governor Godwin Emefiele.

Appearing on **TVC News’ Beyond the Headlines** with Nifemi Oguntoye on Monday, the Edo North lawmaker and former APC national chairman delivered a pointed assessment of the events that shaped the last general election. 

He argued that Emefiele’s frustration after being blocked from contesting the presidency led to policies that *“choked and suffocated the system,”* generating widespread public anger that ultimately benefited Obi and the Labour Party.

*“Emefiele’s crazy attempt to choke and suffocate the system once he wasn’t able to contest for president, it was that anger that produced Peter Obi,”* Oshiomhole said in the widely circulated clip from the interview.

**Revisiting the 2022–2023 Cash Crisis**

The naira redesign policy, announced in late 2022, required Nigerians to exchange old notes for redesigned ones within a tight deadline. Implementation failures led to acute cash scarcity across the country. 

Long queues at banks, closed ATMs, and disrupted commerce became daily realities for millions in the months leading up to the February 2023 presidential election.

Many analysts and ordinary citizens at the time blamed the hardship on the Buhari administration and Emefiele’s leadership at the CBN. 

The resulting frustration fuelled anti-establishment sentiment. Obi, running on a platform of fiscal discipline, anti-corruption, and “*Obidient”* youth mobilization, capitalized on that mood and recorded strong performances in several states, particularly in the South-East and urban centres.

Oshiomhole, a long-time APC figure who has been critical of Emefiele in the past, framed the episode as personal score-settling by the former CBN boss rather than pure economic policy. 

In earlier comments during the crisis, the senator had accused the CBN of misleading then-President Muhammadu Buhari on the redesign’s purpose and impact.



**Broader Political Context**

The remarks come as political conversations intensify ahead of 2027. Oshiomhole has also described Obi as a **“trader”** whose mindset is short-term, contrasting it with the long-term thinking required for national leadership. 

In the same TVC appearance, he discussed other issues including Senate oversight powers, the “phantom agency” controversy, and his focus on securing support for President Bola Tinubu and his own re-election bid.



Supporters of Obi have long rejected any single-factor explanation for his 2023 performance, pointing instead to years of grassroots organizing, social media mobilization, dissatisfaction with the major parties, and Obi’s personal brand of frugality and competence. 

Critics of Oshiomhole’s narrative argue it oversimplifies a complex election driven by multiple grievances, including insecurity, economic hardship, and governance failures that predated the naira redesign.



**Public Reaction and Lingering Questions**

The video clip of Oshiomhole’s comments quickly circulated on social media, drawing both agreement and sharp pushback. 

Some users saw it as a candid admission of how policy failures can reshape electoral politics. Others accused the senator of rewriting history to diminish Obi’s organic appeal.

What remains undisputed is the scale of hardship caused by the cash scarcity and its proximity to the election. 

Whether Emefiele’s personal ambitions played the decisive role Oshiomhole claims continues to divide opinion. 

The former CBN governor has faced multiple legal battles since leaving office, including charges related to his tenure, though those proceedings are separate from the political analysis offered by the senator.

As Nigeria prepares for another election cycle, Oshiomhole’s comments serve as a reminder of how economic pain and institutional decisions can rapidly alter the political landscape—sometimes in ways no one fully anticipates.
