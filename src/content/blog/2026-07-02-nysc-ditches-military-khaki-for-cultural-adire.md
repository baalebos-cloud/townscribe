**Abuja, Nigeria – July 2, 2026** – In a bold cultural and economic statement, the Federal Government has confirmed that the traditional khaki uniform of the National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) will be replaced with Adire fabric as part of sweeping reforms aimed at modernizing the 53-year-old scheme and boosting local industries.

Minister of Youth Development Ayodele Olawande made the announcement while confirming the Federal Executive Council’s approval of the changes, declaring: “*It’s Adire... Let’s put our money back into the country.”* 

The move replaces the military-style khaki and heavy combat boots with professionally designed Adire-inspired attire and lighter footwear like Crocs, prioritizing comfort, national pride, and cultural identity.

Established in 1973 after the Nigerian Civil War to promote national integration, the NYSC has long symbolized unity through its iconic khaki uniform. 

This marks the most significant reform since its inception. The changes stem from a comprehensive multi-stakeholder review involving the Ministries of Youth Development and Education, alongside the Office of the Special Adviser to the President on Policy Coordination.

**Key elements of the reform package include:**

\- Transition to civilian leadership with a civilian Director-General.

\- Digitization of the call-up process.

\- Restructuring into specialized skills-based streams focused on entrepreneurship and job creation.

\- Improved orientation camp standards with national grading.

\- Replacement of the Passing Out Parade with a formal graduation ceremony.



Olawande emphasized that the reforms reposition the NYSC as more than a mobilization exercise, transforming it into a productivity pipeline aligned with Nigeria’s $1 trillion economy ambition through skills development and stronger public-private partnerships.

Producing the new uniforms locally is a central goal. Adire, a traditional indigo-dyed textile with roots in Yoruba culture but now widely produced across states like Ogun and Kwara, is expected to create jobs for local textile industries and artisans. 

*“Adire is being produced in Nigeria,*” the minister noted, highlighting its potential to circulate money within the economy rather than on imports.

While the khaki uniform represented neutrality and discipline, the shift to Adire celebrates Nigerian creativity and heritage. 

Officials believe a thoughtfully designed version can foster pride without favoring any single ethnic identity, though some online discussions have raised questions about cultural symbolism.

Reactions have been swift and varied. Supporters hail it as a patriotic move that empowers local businesses and modernizes the corps members’ experience. 

Critics wonder about practicality in fieldwork or potential costs, with some nostalgic for the old khaki’s simplicity. 

Many former corps members express relief at ditching heavy boots for more comfortable options.

As Nigeria’s youth continue to play vital roles in national development, these reforms reflect a government intent on evolving the NYSC from a post-war reconciliation tool into a dynamic force for economic growth and innovation in the 21st century.

The full implementation timeline, including amendments to the NYSC Act, will be closely watched by millions of young Nigerians and stakeholders invested in the scheme’s future. 

For now, the Adire era signals a fresh chapter — one woven with threads of heritage, comfort, and economic self-reliance.
