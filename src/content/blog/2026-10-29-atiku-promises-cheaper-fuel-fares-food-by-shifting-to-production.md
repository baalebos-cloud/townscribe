\*\*Subheadline:\*\* 

**Abuja, Nigeria—October 29, 2026**—Former Vice President and African Democratic Congress (ADC) presidential candidate Atiku Abubakar has reiterated his commitment to making life affordable for Nigerians by prioritizing local production over importation.

In a post on X (formerly Twitter) on Thursday, Atiku thanked volunteers of the Rise Up Naija movement in Zamfara State for taking his message of affordable living from street to street.



*“Thank you to every volunteer in Zamfara carrying this message from street to street. Affordable living is not a slogan; it is cheaper fuel, cheaper fares, and cheaper food, and I will deliver it by moving support from importation to production. Keep going; the people are listening,”* he wrote.



**Focus on Production Over Imports**

Atiku’s statement comes as part of his ongoing campaign messaging for the 2027 elections. He has consistently argued that supporting Nigerian production—particularly in the energy and agricultural sectors—would bring down the cost of fuel, transportation, and food prices that have burdened households under the current administration.

The former vice president has previously linked high living costs to heavy dependence on imports and middlemen, promising that under his leadership support would shift directly to local refineries and producers so that “the subsidy will follow the barrel.”



**Grassroots Momentum**

The Rise Up Naija volunteers in Zamfara were photographed distributing campaign materials and engaging residents with the message that life would become more affordable under an Atiku presidency. 

The movement has been active across several states, urging voters to reject the ruling All Progressives Congress (APC) in 2027.

Atiku, who is the ADC’s leading presidential aspirant, has positioned affordable living as a central pillar of his campaign, alongside security and economic revival. 

His latest remarks reinforce earlier pledges to reverse policies he says have driven up the cost of essential goods.

Supporters of the former vice president view the Zamfara mobilization as evidence of growing grassroots enthusiasm, while the campaign continues to emphasize practical solutions over slogans.
