**Abuja, Nigeria—November 19, 2026 -** The Independent Corrupt Practices and Other Related Offences Commission (ICPC) has urged an Abuja court to deny bail to Adeniyi Adeyemi, the man accused of posing as Director-General of the non-existent Presidential Foreign Investment Promotion Council (PFIPC), arguing that his continued detention is necessary for his own safety.

Appearing before Justice Halilu Yusuf of the Federal Capital Territory High Court on Wednesday, the ICPC told the court that many of those allegedly defrauded by Adeyemi—some of whom have filed formal petitions—could physically harm him if he were released. 

Prosecution counsel also described the defendant as a flight risk who possessed the means and influence to interfere with potential witnesses.

The commission equally opposed bail applications by four civil servants standing trial alongside Adeyemi: Aminu Abdullahi, Ogaba Harry, Florence Omameh and Patricia Akhigbe. 

All five defendants pleaded not guilty to a 36-count amended charge bordering on forgery, impersonation, obtaining money by false pretence and related offences.

According to the ICPC, Adeyemi allegedly presented himself as a public servant and secured more than ₦27 billion to establish the fictitious agency, plus an additional ₦3.8 billion purportedly for personnel costs. 

Prosecutors claim he used forged documents—including one purportedly signed by Chief of Staff to the President Femi Gbajabiamila—to occupy office space at the Federal Secretariat Complex in Abuja, request self-accounting status from the Accountant-General, and obtain a domain name from NITDA.

Further allegations include the unauthorised gifting of cash to co-defendants to facilitate office allocation and the release of official vehicles, as well as collecting ₦20 million from a private company under the pretence of a project facilitation loan for a non-existent contract.

Defence lawyers, led by Senior Advocate of Nigeria Ibrahim Angulu for Adeyemi, argued that the defendants should be granted bail on liberal terms. 

They cited constitutional provisions, the Administration of Criminal Justice Act, claims of ill health, and the fact that Adeyemi’s bank accounts had already been frozen, removing any realistic flight risk.

Justice Yusuf adjourned the matter to 30 November for a ruling on the bail applications and the commencement of trial. 

He ordered the male defendants remanded at Kuje Correctional Facility and the two female defendants at Suleja Correctional Facility.

Adeyemi has been in custody since mid-July. The case has drawn widespread attention after the Presidency earlier declared that the PFIPC never existed as a legally created government body, despite the agency having secured a place in the national budget and operated openly for a period.
