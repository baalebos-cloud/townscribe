The Police Service Commission has dismissed three senior officers of the Nigeria Police Force after establishing cases of serious misconduct and conduct unbecoming of law enforcement personnel. 

The decisions, reached at a plenary meeting in Abuja, also saw 16 officers reduced in rank, 21 given severe reprimands, and two issued ordinary reprimands, bringing the total number of officers sanctioned to 42. Three other officers were fully exonerated while some previously suspended personnel were reinstated.

PSC Head of Protocol and Public Relations Torty Njoku Kalu announced the outcomes in a statement following the meeting presided over by Commission Chairman Hashimu Salihu Argungu, a retired Deputy Inspector-General of Police. 

The session took place at the Solomon Arase Conference Hall at the Commission’s headquarters. Argungu used the occasion to restate the Commission’s zero tolerance for corruption, human rights abuses, interference in land and civil disputes, and other forms of professional misconduct within the Force.

*“The Police Service Commission has approved the dismissal of three senior police officers following established cases of serious misconduct and conduct unbecoming of officers of the law,*” the statement said. 

It added that the disciplinary process reflected careful review at departmental, committee and plenary stages. 

The Chairman specifically praised the Standing Committee on Discipline and Legal Matters, led by retired Justice Paul Adamu Galumje, along with other board members including DIG Taiwo Lakanu (rtd), Justice Christine Dabup (rtd), Alhaji Abdulfatah Mohammed, Commission Secretary Chief Onyemuche Nnamani, and Director of the Police Discipline Department Ferdinand U. Ekpe for their thorough work.

In a separate announcement the following day, the Commission also redeployed four Commissioners of Police after interviews that confirmed their suitability for new responsibilities. 

Sunday Okoebor, previously CP Police Accounts and Budget at Force Headquarters, takes charge in Plateau State. Fidelis Ogarabe moves from CP INTERPOL Annex Lagos to become CP Enugu. 

Sheikh Mohammed Danko, former CP Force Intelligence Annex Lagos, assumes leadership in Nasarawa State. Johnson Ayodeji Babalola, who served as CP Special Inquiry at Force Headquarters Abuja, is now CP Ondo State.

These simultaneous actions—firm sanctions on the one hand and strategic leadership postings on the other—underscore the Commission’s dual focus on accountability and operational effectiveness. 

By removing officers found to have breached professional standards while placing experienced hands in key state commands, the PSC signals that discipline remains non-negotiable even as the Force continues to adapt to security challenges across the country.

The Commission has repeatedly emphasized that no officer is above the rules. Argungu assured the public that disciplinary cases would continue to be handled with speed and fairness. 

For ordinary Nigerians who interact daily with the police, the latest moves offer a measure of reassurance that mechanisms for internal correction are active. 

Whether the dismissals and demotions translate into improved conduct on the streets will depend on consistent follow-through, but the message from Abuja is clear: misconduct carries real consequences.
