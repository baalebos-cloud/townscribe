**Abuja, Nigeria – July 24, 2026—In** a pointed rebuke to American Senator Ted Cruz, Nigerian lawmaker Senator Kawu Sumaila (Kano South) has urged international actors to avoid reducing the country’s multifaceted insecurity crisis to a religious conflict, warning that such framing risks inflaming tensions and complicating peace efforts.

Sumaila’s statement comes amid ongoing debates in Washington over Nigeria’s security situation and the government’s engagement of a US lobbying firm to highlight its counter-insurgency and protection efforts for all citizens, including Christians.

Senator Cruz recently criticized Nigeria’s decision to retain DCI Group, a public affairs firm, under a reported $9 million contract. 

He accused Nigerian officials of prioritizing public relations over addressing concerns about violence against Christians and religious minorities, while referencing blasphemy laws and Sharia-related issues. 

Cruz has long advocated for Nigeria’s redesignation as a ***“Country of Particular Concern”*** (CPC) regarding religious freedom.

In response, Sumaila argued that portraying Nigeria’s security challenges — encompassing terrorism, banditry, kidnapping, and resource-based conflicts — primarily as persecution of one faith oversimplifies a crisis that impacts Nigerians across religious lines.

*“Insecurity affects Muslims, Christians, and adherents of other faiths alike,”* the senator emphasized, noting that criminals and insurgents often target communities indiscriminately for ransom, control, or ideological reasons.

Nigeria continues to grapple with violence from groups like Boko Haram and bandits, alongside farmer-herder clashes exacerbated by climate change, weak governance, and socio-economic factors. 

Security forces, composed of personnel from diverse faiths, routinely combat these threats while protecting places of worship, schools, and markets for all citizens.

Sumaila cautioned that divisive external narratives could undermine interfaith cooperation and national unity at a time when collective efforts are needed. 

He called on genuine international partners to support holistic solutions focused on poverty alleviation, good governance, and sustainable development rather than politicized religious lenses.

The Kano senator urged Christians in Nigeria and the diaspora to reject narratives that promote polarization, stressing that shared sacrifices by security operatives deserve recognition over selective interpretations. 

He appealed to the global community, diplomats, and media to prioritize facts that reflect Nigeria’s complex realities.

This latest exchange highlights ongoing tensions between Nigerian officials and some US lawmakers over how best to characterize and address the country’s security issues. 

While concerns about religious freedom and minority protections remain valid discussion points, many Nigerian voices insist on a nuanced understanding that accounts for the broader socio-economic drivers of violence.

As Nigeria works to strengthen its security architecture and foster national cohesion, balanced international engagement could prove more constructive than confrontational rhetoric, according to local stakeholders.
