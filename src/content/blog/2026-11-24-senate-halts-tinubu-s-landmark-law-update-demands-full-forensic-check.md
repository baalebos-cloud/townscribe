**Abuja, Nigeria—November 24, 2026—**The Nigerian Senate on Tuesday slowed the approval process for President Bola Tinubu’s proposed revised Laws of the Federation of Nigeria (LFN) 2025, ordering a thorough forensic and volume-by-volume verification before any legislative green light. 

Lawmakers warned that even minor errors in the massive compilation could undermine the integrity of the country’s legal system.

The decision followed a formal communication from President Tinubu seeking the Senate’s consideration and approval of the updated compendium. 

In a letter read by Senate President Godswill Akpabio, the President explained that the last comprehensive consolidation of federal laws took place in 2004. 

More than two decades of new legislation, amendments, and subsidiary rules had made a fresh review long overdue.

According to the President, a specialized committee inaugurated on 24 October 2024 under the Attorney-General of the Federation examined 932 federal enactments and 731 subsidiary legislations. 

Of these, 664 principal enactments were validated for inclusion in the 2025 edition, while 268 were identified as obsolete, spent or repealed. 

Tinubu described the revised LFN as a vital, authoritative reference for the judiciary, lawyers, government agencies, investors and ordinary citizens, and urged expeditious consideration.

Senators, however, refused to rush. Senator Yahaya Abdullahi (Kebbi North) described the exercise as a fundamental national assignment with far-reaching implications. 

He recalled controversies that arose after the 1999 return to democracy when military-era decrees found their way into earlier consolidations, eventually necessitating the 2004 edition. 

Abdullahi called for the documents to be referred to the Committee on Judiciary, Human Rights and Legal Matters and for a technical team of competent lawyers and experts to dissect the contents for lawmakers.

Deputy Senate President Barau Jibrin supported the call for detailed scrutiny, noting that it aligned with the Senate’s traditional approach to highly technical matters. 

He also endorsed the engagement of external consultants. Senate President Akpabio agreed that the chamber must not hurry the process. 

He directed that the committee compare the proposed compilation volume by volume against the original Acts passed by the National Assembly and assented to by the President to guarantee accuracy and completeness.

The Senate subsequently referred the proposed LFN 2025 to the Committee on Rules and Business to work jointly with the Judiciary Committee, National Assembly management, and legal consultants. 

The verification exercise is expected to confirm that the new edition accurately reflects only those federal laws that were validly enacted.

This cautious stance underscores the Senate’s determination to safeguard the reliability of Nigeria’s statute books. 

Once completed, the 2025 Laws of the Federation would replace a two-decade-old compilation and provide a clearer, more accessible legal framework for the nation.
