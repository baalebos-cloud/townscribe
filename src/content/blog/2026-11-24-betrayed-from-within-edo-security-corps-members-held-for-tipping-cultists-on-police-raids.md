In a startling breach of trust that has rattled security circles in Edo State, police have arrested four members of the Edo State Security Corps for allegedly leaking operational details to suspected cultists, enabling them to evade a major raid in Benin City.  

The Edo State Police Command confirmed the arrests of Isaac Iruvwe (45), Gideon Ache (32), Tony Okhiale (49) and Culture Uzo (45) following investigations into how intelligence about police movements reached members of the Black Axe (Aye) confraternity. The men were picked up in Evbuomore Community, Ovia North-East Local Government Area.  

According to Police Public Relations Officer ASP Eno Ikoedem, operatives of the anti-cultism unit acted on credible intelligence about an initiation exercise at Osakpolor Street, off Adolo College Road. When they arrived, the suspects had already fled. 

*“Someone was giving out information on our movement to these cultists who escaped arrest,*” Ikoedem stated during a parade of suspects. Further probes pointed directly to the four corps members.  

One of the arrested men, Iruvwe, said to be a chief security officer in the area, reportedly confessed but claimed the cultists had threatened to kill him and his family if he refused to cooperate. Police say investigations continue to determine the full extent of each man’s role.  

The same operations netted ten suspected Black Axe members caught during the aborted initiation, four alleged kidnappers, an armed robbery suspect, and others. 

Two kidnapping victims, Yakubu Ibrahim and Abdullahi Mohammed, were rescued unhurt after an intensive search of the Ikakumoh/Ayaran forest. A Mercedes-Benz GLK 350 was also recovered in related activity.  

Commissioner of Police Patrick Daaor, through his spokesperson, reiterated the command’s resolve to dismantle criminal networks. 

*“These arrests underscore the Command’s determination to sustain pressure on criminal elements and ensure the safety of residents,”* Ikoedem said. 

She praised officers for their professionalism and urged the public to remain vigilant and report suspicious activity promptly.  

The episode has raised uncomfortable questions about the integrity of community-based security outfits meant to complement formal policing. 

Residents and security analysts note that insider leaks can undermine hard-won gains against cult violence, kidnapping, and armed robbery that have long troubled parts of Edo State.  

All suspects remain in custody as investigations proceed. Police have assured that those found culpable will face the full weight of the law, stressing that the safety of Edo remains a shared responsibility between security agencies and the public.
