**Minna, Niger State—September 24, 2026**—Families of 37 young men who died while in the custody of the Nigeria Security and Civil Defence Corps (NSCDC) in Minna have rejected ₦100,000 offered by the Niger State Government, dismissing the sum as “blood money” and insisting on justice over the mysterious deaths.

The rejection comes amid growing public outrage and ongoing investigations into the incident that has rocked the state capital.

On September 15 and 16, 2026, NSCDC operatives arrested scores of people during operations targeting suspected illegal mining activities around the M.I. Wushishi Estate and Lukoto areas near Minna. Reports indicate that about 67 suspects were detained.

In the early hours of September 17, 37 of them were found dead in a cell. The NSCDC initially attributed the deaths to a suspected disease outbreak. 

Governor Mohammed Umaru Bago later confirmed the figure and ordered the suspension of the state NSCDC commandant, Suberu Siyaka Aniviye, along with other officers.

Investigators are examining possible causes, including severe overcrowding, poor ventilation, extreme heat and suffocation, as well as potential exposure to hazardous substances linked to mining activities. 

Autopsies, forensic tests, and multiple probes by the state government, police, DSS, and NSCDC remain underway. The definitive cause of death has not yet been publicly established.



**Families’ Pain and Rejection of the Offer**

Survivors and relatives have described harrowing conditions inside the cell, alleging extreme overcrowding and that pleas for air, water, and basic needs went unanswered. 

Some families have also disputed the ***“illegal miners”*** label, saying certain of those arrested were engaged in other activities at the time of their detention.

When the Niger State Government reportedly offered ₦100,000 to each affected family, relatives in areas such as Tunga Sabon Titi in Minna rejected the money. 

They described it as an inadequate and insensitive gesture that cannot replace the lives lost or answer questions about how their loved ones died in official custody.

Governor Bago has stated that the NSCDC is a federal agency and that the state government was not informed prior to the arrests. 

He has also argued that formal compensation, including any applicable diyah under Islamic law, falls under the responsibility of the Federal Government.



**Public Reaction and Official Response**

The deaths triggered protests in Minna, prompting the imposition of a curfew and three days of mourning declared by the governor. 

Rights groups and residents continue to call for a transparent, independent investigation and accountability for those responsible.

Lists of the deceased, compiled with the help of groups such as the International Human Rights Commission, have been published, putting names and faces to the tragedy and intensifying pressure for answers.

As forensic results and official reports are awaited, the families’ firm rejection of the ₦100,000 offer has sharpened the focus on one central demand: justice, not token compensation, for the 37 young lives lost behind the walls of an NSCDC cell.
