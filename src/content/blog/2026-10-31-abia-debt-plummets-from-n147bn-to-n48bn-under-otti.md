**Umuahia, Nigeria—October 31, 2026**—Abia State has significantly reduced its debt profile from ₦147.2 billion in the second quarter of 2023 to ₦48.6 billion in the corresponding period of 2026, Governor Alex Otti has disclosed.

The governor made the announcement during the October edition of his monthly media chat, attributing the over ₦90 billion reduction to prudent management of resources, greater accountability, and a deliberate cut in the cost of governance.



**How the Reduction Was Achieved**

Otti explained that the drop was possible through the drawdown of funds from existing multilateral loan arrangements, including the Abia State Integrated Infrastructure Development Project (ABSIID) and the Rural Access and Agricultural Marketing Project (RAAMP). He emphasized that his administration had undertaken no direct borrowing so far.

*“So far, there’s no direct borrowing by the government. But that doesn’t mean we won’t borrow if it becomes absolutely necessary. We will borrow for regenerative projects, at the right time,”* the governor stated.

He noted that the official Debt Management Office figures did not initially capture certain liabilities such as pension and salary arrears, meaning the actual inherited burden was higher.



**Broader Achievements Highlighted**

Beyond debt reduction, Otti highlighted other gains under his administration. Over 5,000 Certificates of Occupancy have been issued in three years, while a pocket housing project in Umuahia is oversubscribed. 

The CKC Aba water project is ready for commissioning, with Ariaria and Ubakala schemes expected to follow soon.

On energy, the solar mini-grid at the Nnenna Bus Terminal has been energized to recharge electric buses. In healthcare, the state has activated an isolation centre at the Mother and Child Hospital in Umuahia as a precaution against Ebola, even though no case has been recorded in the state. 

The Abia State Eye Health Management Bureau has conducted more than 31,000 screenings and performed 1,811 surgeries, including 304 free cataract operations.

Abia also ranked second in the 2025/2026 Climate Governance Ranking.

The governor’s update underscores a continued focus on fiscal discipline while advancing infrastructure, housing, water supply, and public health priorities across the state.
