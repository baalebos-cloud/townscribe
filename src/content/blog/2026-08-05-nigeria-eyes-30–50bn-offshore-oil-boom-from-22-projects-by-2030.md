**LAGOS, August 5, 2026** — Nigeria’s upstream oil and gas sector is on the cusp of a significant investment wave, with the Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission (NUPRC) projecting that 22 major offshore projects could attract between $30 billion and $50 billion between 2026 and 2030.

The announcement came Wednesday during the Society of Petroleum Engineers’ Nigeria Annual International Conference and Exhibition (NAICE 2026) in Lagos. 

Commission Chief Executive Mrs. Oritsemeyiwa Eyesan, represented by Executive Commissioner for Development and Production Engineer Enorense Amadasu, delivered the keynote address outlining the scale of expected capital inflow.

Since 2024, the NUPRC has approved more than $57 billion in Field Development Plans (FDPs). Several of these approvals have already progressed to Final Investment Decisions, laying the groundwork for the coming offshore projects.

*“Beyond increasing production, these investments will create jobs, expand infrastructure, strengthen energy security, and reinforce Nigeria’s position as a leading global upstream investment destination,”* Eyesan stated.

The regulator emphasized that the projects form part of a broader strategy to develop proven reserves while maintaining a strong pipeline of exploration opportunities. 

This dual approach aims to support long-term growth and energy security for Africa’s largest oil producer.

Industry observers note the timing is significant. Nigeria has been working to reverse years of underinvestment, boost crude output, and position itself more competitively amid global energy transitions. 

The projected offshore investments are expected to lift production volumes, generate employment across the value chain, and improve the country’s energy infrastructure.

NAICE 2026, themed ***“Thriving in the Evolving Global Energy Landscape: Collaborative Growth and Resilience,”*** has drawn regulators, operators, engineers, and policymakers to Lagos this week. 

The conference continues to serve as one of Africa’s premier platforms for technical exchange and industry strategy in the upstream sector.

As these 22 projects move from planning toward execution over the next four years, attention will turn to how quickly operators convert approvals into actual drilling, development, and production. 

For now, the NUPRC’s figures offer a clear signal of renewed confidence in Nigeria’s offshore potential.
