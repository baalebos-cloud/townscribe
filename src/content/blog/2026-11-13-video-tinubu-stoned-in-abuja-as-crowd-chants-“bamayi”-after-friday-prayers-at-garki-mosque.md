**Abuja—November 13, 2026**—President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s convoy came under a hostile reception on Friday afternoon shortly after he observed Juma’at prayers at the renovated Shehu Shagari Central Mosque in Area 1, Garki, Abuja. 

Viral videos captured sections of the crowd chanting the Hausa phrase ***“Bamayi”*** (also heard as ***“Ba ma yi” or “Ba ma so”*)**, meaning ***“we don’t want you” or “we refuse,”*** while some individuals threw stones and other objects towards the departing vehicles and security personnel.

The President had attended the Friday congregational prayers at the historic mosque, widely regarded as the first central mosque established in the Federal Capital Territory. 

The visit followed the completion of renovation works facilitated by the Presidency. 

A large number of worshippers gathered for the service. In his sermon, Chief Imam Malam Rabiu Suleiman reflected on Surah Al-Fatiha, urging steadfast faith and trust in Allah. 

He also called on the congregation to pray for Nigeria’s peace, unity and progress, and specifically asked for divine wisdom, good health and strength for President Tinubu.

According to multiple accounts and circulating footage, the atmosphere shifted as the presidential convoy prepared to leave the premises. Crowds gathered behind metal barriers near the mosque gates and along the exit route. 

Voices could be heard repeatedly shouting rejection slogans. In several clips, people are seen throwing objects in the direction of the black SUVs and the motorcycle outriders that form part of the presidential security detail. 

Security operatives, including police in camouflage uniforms, moved quickly to create space and escort the vehicles away from the immediate area. An ambulance and additional support vehicles were also visible at the scene.

Eyewitnesses who spoke to reporters described the chants as an expression of public frustration. Some linked the reaction partly to heavy security restrictions that prevented late-arriving worshippers from entering the mosque once the President had arrived. 

Others pointed to broader economic pressures felt by many Nigerians. Premium Times reported that slogans including “***Tinubu Must Go”*** were also heard. 

Independent verification has not confirmed that the President himself was struck by any object or sustained injury. No official figures on injuries, arrests or vehicle damage have been released by the police or the Presidency as of Friday evening.

Presidential aides have strongly disputed the characterisation of the incident as an attack on the President. Senior Special Adviser on Information and Strategy Bayo Onanuga described claims that the President was stoned as “**not true.”** 

Other aides released separate footage showing the President greeting some worshippers and departing, describing the overall reception as positive and attributing the hostile videos to opposition elements or ***“paid thugs.”*** 

Official state media, including Voice of Nigeria and the News Agency of Nigeria, focused on the President’s attendance at the prayers and the Imam’s supportive message without mentioning the post-prayer disturbance.

The episode adds to a series of public expressions of discontent that have occasionally interrupted high-profile appearances by the President in recent months. 

It occurs against the backdrop of intensifying political activity ahead of the 2027 general elections. Security agencies have not issued a detailed public statement on the specific events outside the Garki mosque.

As videos from multiple angles continue to circulate on social media, the incident has fuelled sharp online debate. 

Supporters of the government emphasize the President’s right to worship freely and the presence of welcoming worshippers inside the mosque. 

Critics present the chants and stone-throwing as evidence of growing grassroots rejection. 

What remains clear from the available footage and reporting is that a section of the crowd outside the Shehu Shagari Central Mosque openly voiced opposition as the presidential convoy left the premises on Friday afternoon.

***This report is based on verified video footage, eyewitness accounts published by independent news organizations, official statements from the Presidency, and contemporaneous coverage by Premium Times, ICIR, Voice of Nigeria, and other outlets on November 13, 2026.***
