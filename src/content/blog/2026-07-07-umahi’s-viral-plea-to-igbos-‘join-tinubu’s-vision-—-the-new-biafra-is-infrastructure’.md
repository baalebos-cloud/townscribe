**ABUJA/LAGOS, Nigeria** — In a passionate and widely shared video message, Senator Engr. David Umahi, CON, Honourable Minister of Works, has delivered a direct appeal to every Igbo person living in Lagos and across Nigeria. 

The clip, which has been circulating rapidly on social media, emphasizes themes of unity, development, and full participation in national progress under President Bola Tinubu’s administration.

Speaking at what appears to be an on-site inspection or public event, Umahi — flanked by aides and wearing a high-visibility vest — addresses longstanding sentiments of marginalization while spotlighting tangible achievements in infrastructure. 

He positions the current government’s efforts as delivering the “Biafra” of inclusion and development that many in the South-East have long sought through national integration rather than separation.

Umahi stresses that President Tinubu has opened doors for Igbo sons and daughters in national politics and governance. 

He points to ongoing federal road projects, flyovers, and connectivity initiatives in the South-East and beyond as evidence of commitment. 

Projects such as major highways, bridges, and urban interventions are presented as dividends of supporting the APC-led government.

The Minister, a former Ebonyi State Governor known for his infrastructure focus, frames 2026 as a pivotal year of restoration. 

He encourages Igbos, particularly those in commercial hubs like Lagos, to recognize opportunities in the current administration’s policies and prepare for greater participation ahead of future elections.

David Umahi has earned praise from some quarters as one of the most active ministers in the Tinubu cabinet, with significant road rehabilitation and new construction projects nationwide. 

His message aligns with ongoing efforts to bridge regional divides and promote APC’s vision of renewed hope.

However, such appeals often spark debate. While supporters see it as a pragmatic call for collaboration and development, critics within Igbo political circles sometimes view it as overly partisan or dismissive of historical grievances. 

The video’s timing, amid preparations for 2027 politics, adds layers of electoral significance.

In a diverse nation like Nigeria, public communication that directly addresses ethnic communities carries weight. 

Umahi’s message seeks to shift focus from past alienation narratives toward present and future gains in infrastructure, economic integration, and political relevance. 

For Igbo traders, professionals, and residents in Lagos — a key economic and political battleground — the appeal underscores the benefits of national unity.

As Nigeria continues its journey of healing regional divides while tackling infrastructure deficits, voices like Umahi’s highlight both progress and the persistent challenge of building trust across ethnic lines. 

The full impact of such messages will be measured not just in views and shares, but in improved livelihoods and stronger national cohesion.

This ***“must-watch”*** video serves as a timely reminder: in politics and development, inclusion is not just rhetoric — it is roads built, opportunities opened, and futures shared.
