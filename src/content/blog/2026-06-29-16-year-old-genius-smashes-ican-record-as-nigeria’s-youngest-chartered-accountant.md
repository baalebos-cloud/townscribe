**Lagos, Nigeria – June 29, 2026** — In a stunning display of youthful brilliance that has electrified the nation, 16-year-old Osasere Okundaye has etched her name into Nigeria’s history books as the youngest person to qualify as a chartered accountant through the Institute of Chartered Accountants of Nigeria (ICAN).

This remarkable feat not only eclipses the previous national record but also underscores the extraordinary potential of determined young Nigerians in a field long dominated by seasoned professionals. 

Okundaye’s achievement comes at an age when many peers are still navigating secondary school final exams or early university life.

### A New Benchmark in Professional Excellence

Okundaye’s success breaks the record set in 2022 by Jonathan Adewale, who qualified at age 17. The ICAN qualification is one of Nigeria’s most rigorous professional certifications, typically demanding years of intensive study, multiple levels of examinations, and practical experience. 

Candidates often include university graduates and mid-career professionals who invest significant time and resources to master complex accounting principles, auditing standards, taxation, and financial reporting.

While full ICAN membership and the formal “chartered accountant” designation generally require additional post-qualification experience (often three years), passing the professional examinations at such a tender age is an extraordinary academic and personal triumph. It signals not just intellectual prowess but also exceptional discipline, resilience, and time management.

### National Recognition and Inspiration

Nigeria’s Minister of Youth Development, Ayodele Olawande, was quick to celebrate the milestone in an official statement issued Monday. 

He praised Okundaye’s accomplishment as “a remarkable demonstration of hard work, discipline, resilience, and an unwavering commitment to excellence.”

*“I heartily congratulate Miss Osasere Okundaye on her outstanding achievement of becoming Nigeria’s youngest Chartered Accountant at just 16 years of age,”* Olawande said. “*Osasere has distinguished herself as a shining example of the limitless potential of Nigerian youth. Her accomplishment shows that with determination, dedication, and the right support, young Nigerians can break barriers and set new records.”*

The minister extended congratulations to Okundaye’s parents, teachers, and mentors, highlighting the critical role of family and educational support systems in nurturing such talent. 

He reaffirmed the Federal Government’s commitment to youth empowerment programs that help young people realize their aspirations.

### Broader Context and Implications

Okundaye’s story arrives amid growing national conversations about education, youth potential, and human capital development in Nigeria. 

It follows closely on the heels of other young achievers, such as Temilola Blossom Arise, who qualified at 18. These milestones challenge stereotypes about age and capability while spotlighting the rewards of early focus on STEM-related professional paths.

For context, ICAN exams are notoriously demanding, with low pass rates that test candidates’ depth of knowledge and endurance. 

Succeeding at 16 suggests Okundaye likely began preparing years earlier—possibly through accelerated learning, strong foundational education, and unwavering personal drive. 

Though specific details about her school, family background, or study routine remain limited in initial reports, her journey exemplifies how targeted support and individual grit can overcome systemic challenges in Nigeria’s education landscape.

### A Beacon for the Next Generation

This achievement carries profound implications. In a country where youth unemployment and skill gaps often dominate headlines, Okundaye’s success offers a powerful counter-narrative: excellence knows no age limit when passion and opportunity align. 

It inspires students across Nigeria to dream ambitiously, reassures parents and educators of the impact of their investment, and signals to policymakers the value of investing in gifted learners.

As Osasere Okundaye steps into this new chapter, her path will likely be watched closely. 

Whether she pursues further studies, gains practical experience for full membership, or becomes a mentor herself, her story already serves as a rallying cry for Nigerian youth: barriers are meant to be broken.

In the words of Minister Olawande, Okundaye’s triumph reinforces a hopeful truth—that “*the future of Nigeria is bright in the hands of focused, determined, and talented young citizens.”*

Nigeria celebrates not just a record, but a rising star whose light promises to illuminate paths for many more.
