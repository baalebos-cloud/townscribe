**DURBAN, South Africa** —South African police announced on Friday the arrest of 69 individuals after a protest against undocumented immigrants in the country’s southeast coast turned violent, involving looting and leaving at least one person hospitalized.

The incident is the latest flare-up in weeks of growing unrest targeting foreigners, particularly those without legal documentation. 

Thousands of migrants, many from other African nations, have fled their homes and businesses in fear, with reports of widespread intimidation, property vandalism, and displacement.

Police described most of the recent marches as largely peaceful, but isolated pockets of criminality—including looting of foreign-owned shops (known locally as spaza shops)—prompted swift intervention. 

In broader operations tied to the protests around June 30, authorities reported over 900 arrests nationwide, covering charges ranging from public violence and immigration violations to robbery.

Anti-immigrant sentiment has simmered for years in South Africa, fueled by high unemployment (around 32%), crime concerns, and competition for scarce resources. 

Protest groups such as Operation Dudula, March and March, and Progressive Forces have organized marches demanding stricter enforcement of immigration laws and the departure of undocumented foreigners. 

Some set deadlines that heightened fears among migrant communities.

President Cyril Ramaphosa and government officials have condemned violence while acknowledging legitimate public concerns about illegal immigration. 

Police and the army were heavily deployed in hotspots like Johannesburg, Durban, and other provinces to maintain order. 

The African Union and international observers have urged restraint and proper legal processes.

For many migrants — traders, workers, and families who have lived in South Africa for years — the unrest has been traumatic. Shops have been looted, homes attacked, and lives upended. Some have chosen voluntary repatriation under police escort to neighboring countries.

Behind the statistics lie human stories of fear, anger, and desperation. South African protesters often voice genuine frustrations about economic marginalization and perceived failures in governance. 

At the same time, migrants — many fleeing hardship in their own countries — find themselves scapegoated for problems they did not create.

This cycle of xenophobic tension is not new; deadly outbreaks in 2008 and subsequent years left scars. Today’s events test South Africa’s commitment to its *“Rainbow Nation”* ideals and its role as a regional economic powerhouse.

As investigations continue into the latest arrests and incidents, authorities face the dual challenge of upholding the rule of law while addressing root causes: unemployment, service delivery failures, and effective immigration management.

For a nation still healing from its apartheid past, how it handles diversity and migration will define its future. Peaceful dialogue, not violence, offers the only sustainable path forward — for citizens and newcomers alike.
