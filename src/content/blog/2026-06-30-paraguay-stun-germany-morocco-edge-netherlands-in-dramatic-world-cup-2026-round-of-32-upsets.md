**Boston and Monterrey, June 30, 2026** — In a day that will be remembered as one of the most seismic in modern World Cup history, two footballing titans from Europe were sent packing in the Round of 32 as underdogs Paraguay and Morocco delivered heart-stopping victories on penalties following tense 1-1 draws after extra time.

**Paraguay’s Historic Shock: Germany Out After Penalties Heartbreak**

Four-time world champions Germany, who entered the tournament among the favorites, found themselves eliminated by Paraguay in a match that defied every expectation. 

The Germans, ranked more than 30 places higher in the FIFA standings, were held to a 1-1 draw through 120 minutes before falling 4-3 in a nerve-shredding penalty shootout.

Kai Havertz gave Germany the lead in the 54th minute, but Paraguay’s dynamic midfielder Julio Enciso leveled the score in the 42nd minute with a strike that encapsulated the South Americans’ resilience and tactical discipline. 

Extra time was a cagey affair, marked by a controversial disallowed German goal following a VAR review involving a foul on the Paraguayan goalkeeper.

In the shootout, Paraguay held their nerve while Germany faltered, missing key kicks—including one from Jonathan Tah—allowing José Canale and his teammates to clinch the victory. 

Celebrations erupted across Paraguay as the nation, often overshadowed on the global stage, produced one of the greatest upsets in knockout-stage history.

For Germany, the early exit echoes painful memories of group-stage eliminations in recent tournaments but feels even more profound. 

A squad boasting world-class talent across the pitch could not convert dominance into progression. Manager Julian Nagelsmann and his players now face intense scrutiny back home, while questions swirl about the future direction of German football.

Paraguay, by contrast, advance to the Round of 16 with belief surging through the squad. Their defensive organization, counter-attacking threat, and unbreakable spirit under pressure have earned them a place in football folklore.


**Morocco Repeat Magic: Netherlands Fall in Penalty Drama**

In Monterrey, Morocco once again proved their mettle as giant-killers. Four years after their memorable run in Qatar that included a penalty victory over Spain, the Atlas Lions overcame the Netherlands 3-2 on penalties following another 1-1 stalemate.

Cody Gakpo opened the scoring for the Dutch in the 72nd minute with what appeared to be a decisive, emotional strike. 

Yet, in stoppage time of regular play, Issa Diop rose highest to head home a dramatic equalizer, sending the match into extra time. 

Goalkeeper Bart Verbruggen produced heroic saves to keep the Netherlands alive, but the Dutch ultimately missed three penalties, allowing Ismael Saibari to slot home the winner for Morocco.

This result marks another landmark achievement for African football. Morocco’s blend of tactical intelligence, physical intensity, and clinical finishing in the shootout showcased a team that has grown in confidence since their quarter-final appearance in 2022. 

They now prepare to face co-hosts Canada in the last 16, carrying the hopes of a continent.

For the Netherlands, the disappointment is acute. A side with rich attacking pedigree and experienced leadership exits prematurely, prompting soul-searching about squad cohesion and big-game temperament.

These back-to-back upsets on June 29-30 underscore the beautiful unpredictability of the expanded 48-team World Cup format. 

With more teams from diverse confederations, the traditional power balance is shifting. Lower-ranked nations are arriving better prepared, better coached, and more tactically astute than ever before.

The human stories stand out: Paraguayan players, many of whom ply their trade in modest leagues, outshining multimillion-dollar stars; Moroccan athletes channeling national pride and diaspora support into extraordinary performances.
