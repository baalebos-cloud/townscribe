**ADO-EKITI, EKITI STATE** – The leadership and prominent stakeholders of Ward 9 in the African Democratic Congress (ADC), Ado Local Government Area of Ekiti State, have firmly rejected reports of the purported suspension of Chief Ayodeji Babatola Olora, insisting that he remains a recognised, respected and influential member of the party.

The Ward Chairman, Hon. Idris Lukman, who addressed the controversy, categorically dismissed the purported suspension, maintaining that Ward 9 stands firmly with Chief Ayodeji Babatola Olora and does not recognise the individuals reportedly responsible for announcing the disciplinary action.

Lukman insisted that those who purportedly announced Olora’s suspension were not recognised members of the ward and therefore lacked the legitimacy to speak or take decisions on behalf of its recognised leadership and membership.

He maintained that the ward remains united in its support for Olora, stressing that no legitimate decision had been taken by the recognised ward leadership to suspend him from the African Democratic Congress.

The Ward Chairman’s position was reinforced by Hon. Mrs. Bolanle Owolabi, a prominent stakeholder in the ward, who spoke passionately about Olora’s contributions to the growth and development of the ADC in Ward 9.

Owolabi observed that Ward 9 had not enjoyed the level of political organisation and visibility it currently commands before Olora joined the party.

According to her, Olora’s arrival brought renewed energy, mobilisation and organisation to the ward, transforming its political structure into a formidable force within the party.

She maintained that his contributions to the growth of the ADC in the area cannot be overlooked, describing him as a significant political figure whose commitment and influence have helped strengthen the party’s grassroots structure.

She questioned the rationale behind attempts to discredit a leader whose involvement, according to her, has contributed substantially to building the ward’s political relevance.

Also reacting, another respected ward leader, Hon. Akomolede Oludare, dismissed the purported suspension in strong terms, questioning the credibility and understanding of those behind the announcement.

Oludare described the individuals responsible for the action as people who did not understand the political implications of their pronouncements, insisting that their claims should not be taken seriously.

He maintained that the purported suspension was not a reflection of the position of the ward’s legitimate leadership or its committed members.

Similarly, the Hausa/non-indigene representative in the ward, Hamzat Mohammed, reaffirmed his unwavering support for Chief Ayodeji Babatola Olora, declaring that the ward’s commitment to the former deputy governorship candidate remains intact.

Mohammed insisted that nobody could unilaterally suspend Olora under the circumstances, reiterating that he and other committed members of the ward remain firmly behind him.

The collective position of these ward stakeholders comes against the backdrop of a November 10 report by Roundoff News alleging that Ward 9 executives had suspended Olora over purported anti-party activities, including alleged interference with party mobilisation, absence from meetings and other internal disagreements.

The report attributed the announcement to individuals identified as Ward Chairman Abdul Jimoh and Ward Secretary Abdul Latifa Motunrayo.

However, the account has been challenged by the ward stakeholders identified above, particularly over the legitimacy of those claiming to have exercised disciplinary authority.

**ADC STATE CARETAKER COMMITTEE ALSO REJECTS PURPORTED SUSPENSION**

Significantly, the controversy took another turn on November 11, following a separate position published by Roundoff News, in which the ADC Ekiti State Caretaker Committee officially refuted the purported suspension.

In the statement issued through the State Publicity Secretary, Hon. Oluwole Akindele, the committee described the purported suspension as illegal, unconstitutional, null and void.

The committee explained that the former ward, local government and state executive committees had been dissolved following the constitution of the Ekiti State Caretaker Committee by the National Chairman of the party, Senator David Mark.

It further maintained that the dissolved executives no longer possessed the authority to suspend, query, discipline or issue official statements on behalf of the party.

According to the committee, disciplinary proceedings against a member of Olora’s standing must follow the party’s constitutional provisions, including fair hearing and the involvement of the appropriate recognised party organs.

It consequently reaffirmed Olora’s standing as a bona fide, loyal and respected member and leader of the ADC in Ekiti State, while passing a vote of confidence in him.

The development provides an important institutional dimension to the disagreement, as the state caretaker leadership has independently rejected the purported suspension.

For Ward 9 stakeholders, however, the issue goes beyond party procedures. They insist that the contributions of individuals who have invested their time, resources and political energy in building the party should not be undermined by what they consider unauthorised actions.

With the ward leadership and the state caretaker committee both rejecting the purported suspension, attention is now on maintaining internal cohesion, strengthening grassroots mobilisation and preventing the disagreement from escalating into a wider division within the party.

For now, the position expressed by the Ward 9 stakeholders is unequivocal: they stand with Chief Ayodeji Babatola Olora, and they do not recognise the purported suspension as a legitimate decision of their ward.
