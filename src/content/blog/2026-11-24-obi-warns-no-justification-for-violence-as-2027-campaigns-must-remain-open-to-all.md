Peter Obi, the presidential candidate of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC), has called for unrestricted campaigning in the lead-up to the 2027 general elections, declaring that there is simply no justification for subjecting Nigerians to violence during political activities.

In remarks highlighted by Channels Television on Tuesday, the former Anambra State governor stressed that every citizen and candidate must be allowed to campaign freely across the country. 

He urged political actors and their supporters to uphold peaceful democratic practices, warning that violence has no place in a mature democracy.

Obi’s statement comes against a backdrop of rising concerns over political intolerance. Recent months have seen reports of disrupted party meetings, restricted access for opposition figures in certain states, and attacks on campaign convoys. 

The NDC candidate has repeatedly positioned himself as an advocate for issue-based politics rather than intimidation or thuggery.

*“There is no justification for subjecting Nigerians to violence during political campaigns,”* Obi said, according to the Channels Television report. 

He emphasized that the country must protect the constitutional rights of citizens to assemble, associate, and move freely while participating in the democratic process.

Political observers note that Obi’s message aligns with earlier calls he made after signing the National Peace Accord and in various public appearances. 

He has consistently urged leaders to reject violent rhetoric, rein in their supporters, and focus campaigns on governance issues such as insecurity, poverty, education, and national unity.

As the 2027 race intensifies, with major candidates including President Bola Tinubu (APC), Atiku Abubakar (ADC), and Obi (NDC) already mobilizing, the demand for a level playing field has grown louder. 

Civil society groups, editorial boards, and some lawmakers have also condemned incidents of political thuggery, arguing that democracy thrives only when all voices can be heard without fear.

Obi’s latest intervention serves as both a reminder and a challenge: Nigeria’s democracy will be measured not only by the eventual outcome of the polls but also by whether candidates and ordinary citizens can campaign, canvass, and choose freely without violence. The coming months will test whether political leaders heed that call.
