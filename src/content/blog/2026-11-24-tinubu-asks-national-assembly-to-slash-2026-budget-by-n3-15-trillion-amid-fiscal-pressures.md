**Abuja, Nigeria—November 24, 2026**—President Bola Ahmed Tinubu has formally requested the National Assembly to approve a significant downward revision of the 2026 federal budget, proposing a cut from the earlier approved N68.323 trillion to N65.176 trillion.

The request, contained in a letter dated November 20, 2026, was read on the floor of the House of Representatives on Tuesday by Speaker Tajudeen Abbas during plenary. 

The President also transmitted a revised 2026 Federal Government Fiscal Framework alongside a bill seeking to repeal and reenact the 2026 Appropriation Act.



**Details of the Proposed Revision**



Under the new framework, aggregate expenditure drops by approximately N3.147 trillion. Projected federal government revenue, including earnings from government-owned enterprises, stands at N33.720 trillion, while aggregate capital expenditure is fixed at N26.232 trillion.

Despite the spending reduction, the fiscal deficit remains unchanged at N31.457 trillion—equivalent to 5.64 percent of GDP. 

Planned new borrowing is also retained at N29.218 trillion, comprising N18.428 trillion in domestic borrowing and N10.790 trillion in foreign borrowing. 

The proposed US$7.7 billion external loan is intended only to finance the existing foreign borrowing provision and does not expand the overall borrowing envelope.

President Tinubu explained that the adjustments are designed to align the budget with existing obligations and available resources. 

The revision will regularize certain expenditures incurred by the Nigerian National Petroleum Company Limited (NNPCL) on behalf of the Federation, address under-provided debt-service obligations, recognize issued capital warrants and supplementation commitments, and provide for refunds to ministries, departments, agencies, and special accounts.

He noted that most MDA capital provisions already covered by warrants form the bulk of the capital budget, with limited new interventions prioritized for critical road projects, agricultural support, and water-related vulnerabilities.



**Call for Expeditious Passage**



Tinubu urged lawmakers to give the bill “expeditious consideration and passage” to enable orderly implementation of the revised budget and the settlement of outstanding obligations before the end of the fiscal year.

The original N68.323 trillion budget was signed into law in April 2026 after the National Assembly increased the initial proposal presented by the President in late 2025.

The development comes as the administration continues to navigate fiscal constraints, with the revised plan framed as a measure to improve budget credibility and execution while maintaining fiscal restraint.



***This report is based on the official letter presented to the House of Representatives and multiple independent confirmations of its contents on November 24, 2026.***
