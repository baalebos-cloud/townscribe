**Asaba, Delta State—November 7, 2026**—A campaign rally organized by supporters of the Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) in Asaba, the Delta State capital, descended into violence on Saturday after suspected political thugs attacked participants, damaging vehicles and injuring several people. 

In a striking turn of events captured on video, members of the crowd confronted and severely beat at least one of the alleged attackers before security operatives arrived.

The incident occurred during what organizers described as a peaceful one-million-man march and mobilization exercise in support of NDC candidates, including Delta North senatorial aspirant Rt. Hon. Victor Ochei and the party’s presidential candidate, Peter Obi. 

Supporters were marching along major routes such as Summit Road towards Interbau Roundabout when the violence erupted.



**Details of the Attack**

According to multiple accounts and viral videos, a group of suspected thugs, some reportedly arriving on commercial tricycles and armed with sticks, knives, and other weapons, disrupted the gathering. 

Campaign vehicles bearing NDC and Ochei posters were vandalized, with windscreens smashed. 

Several supporters sustained injuries, including knife and machete wounds. The Delta State Deputy Coordinator of the Obidient Movement, identified as Comrade Noah, was reportedly stabbed and is receiving treatment at the Federal Medical Centre in Asaba.

Footage shared widely on social media showed chaos as people fled the scene. One particularly graphic video captured a man in a red top crawling on the ground as an angry crowd beat him severely. 

The clip, posted by users including @ChuksEricE, carried a viewer warning and noted that the beating happened shortly before security operatives arrived.

Reacting to the video, popular commentator @AfamDeluxo wrote: “In the past, people would have fled at the sight of these thugs. 

It’s encouraging to see Nigerians beginning to stand their ground.” The post quickly gained thousands of likes and reposts, reflecting a broader sentiment among some citizens that political intimidation is no longer being tolerated in silence.



**Official and Party Reactions**

The Delta State Police Command confirmed that the clash resulted in the death of one person and injuries to five others. 

In a statement, the police said they had received prior intelligence about the likelihood of violence and had advised the organizers against holding an open road procession, recommending a designated venue with security. 

The organizers reportedly proceeded anyway. Police operatives responded to a distress call, took the injured to hospital, and reinforced security in the area. An investigation is ongoing, and the identities of the victims remain under verification.

NDC officials and Obidient Movement leaders strongly condemned the attack, describing it as a premeditated assault on democracy rather than a spontaneous clash. 

They alleged that the attackers were linked to APC supporters and specifically named one individual, Mr. Okafor, said to be connected to a local government task force, as a leader of the group. 

The party demanded a thorough investigation by the police, DSS and other agencies, and called on Governor Sheriff Oborevwori to ensure justice.

Peter Obi, the NDC presidential candidate, also condemned the violence, calling it a threat to Nigeria’s democracy and urging authorities to protect the right of citizens to peaceful political participation.

**Broader Context**

The incident comes amid rising political temperatures as parties intensify campaigns ahead of the 2027 general elections. Similar reports of disruptions, convoy attacks and clashes have been recorded in other states, raising concerns about the safety of opposition activities.

As videos of both the initial attack and the crowd’s response continue to circulate, the events in Asaba have sparked intense debate online. 

While some celebrate what they see as ordinary citizens refusing to be intimidated, others warn against mob justice and call for strict adherence to the rule of law.

Security agencies have urged political parties to prioritize peaceful engagement and coordinate with the police for adequate protection during campaigns. 

For many Nigerians watching the footage, however, the images of citizens standing their ground against alleged thugs mark a noticeable shift in the country’s political culture.





***This report is based on official police statements, party releases, eyewitness videos and multiple independent news accounts published on November 7, 2026.***
