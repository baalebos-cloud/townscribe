**Ibadan, Nigeria—October 15, 2026**—Governor Seyi Makinde of Oyo State on Thursday commissioned the permanent headquarters of the Oyo State Rule of Law Enforcement Authority (OYRLEA) in Ibadan, describing the agency as a fulfillment of his administration’s promise to enforce environmental, road traffic, and related laws for a safer and more orderly state.

In a post shared after the event, the governor said the institution was set up to create a clear framework for enforcing existing laws, with the overall goal of supporting sustainable development through a secure environment.

*“Earlier today, we commissioned the Oyo State Rule of Law Enforcement Authority (OYRLEA) office in Ibadan. This institution was established in fulfillment of our promise to create a framework to enforce existing environmental and road traffic laws, amongst others. Our goal is to ensure a safe and secure environment that will support sustainable development in our dear State,”* Makinde stated.

He expressed appreciation to the Chairperson, Honourable Justice Aderonke Aderemi (retd), members of the OYRLEA team, and security agencies for their collaborative efforts toward a safer Oyo State.



**Strong Institutions Beyond Tenure**

During the ceremony, Makinde emphasized that his administration would be remembered for building lasting institutions that did not exist before 2019. He listed OYRLEA alongside the Oyo State Security Network Agency (Amotekun), Oyo State Agribusiness Development Agency (OYSADA), Project Performance Monitoring Agency, Oyo State Mobilization Agency for Socioeconomic Development (OYMASED), and the Oyo State Anti-Corruption Agency (OYACA) as bodies deliberately created to strengthen governance.

The governor challenged any future administration interested in the state’s progress not to dismantle these agencies, arguing that they form the foundation for order, investor confidence, and long-term prosperity.

*“At the heart of good governance is the rule of law. When people know their rights and security agencies enforce the law without bias, you have order, investors come in, and the economy improves. That is what we have experienced in Oyo State,*” he said.



**Background of OYRLEA**

OYRLEA was established after Makinde signed the enabling bill into law in April 2024. The agency is charged with enforcing state laws on environmental protection, sanitation, waste control, road traffic management, and open rearing and grazing, among other matters. It works with mobile courts and collaborates with security agencies to ensure swift enforcement.

Speaker of the Oyo State House of Assembly, Rt. Hon. Adebo Ogundoyin, who attended the event, described the commissioning as a significant milestone that shows how legislation can translate into stronger institutions that benefit citizens.

The unveiling of OYRLEA’s maiden magazine also formed part of the day’s activities, highlighting the agency’s early enforcement efforts and public enlightenment campaigns.

As Makinde’s second term approaches its final months, the commissioning of the permanent OYRLEA headquarters stands as a visible symbol of his focus on institutional reforms aimed at embedding the rule of law in everyday governance across Oyo State.
