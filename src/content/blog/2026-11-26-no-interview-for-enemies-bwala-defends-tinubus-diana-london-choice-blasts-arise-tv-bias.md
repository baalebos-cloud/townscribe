**Abuja | November 26, 2026—The** Presidency has finally broken its silence over the firestorm triggered by President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s interview with American lobbyist and media personality Diana London, insisting the President is under no obligation to grant interviews to journalists he considers hostile to his administration.

Daniel Bwala, Special Adviser to the President on Media and Policy Communications, made the blunt declaration during an appearance on the *Niger Unfiltered* podcast released on Thursday, November 26, 2026, while defending Tinubu’s controversial decision.

Bwala dismissed claims that the President was neglecting local journalists, arguing that the loudest criticism came from presenters who themselves desperately wanted the opportunity to interview Tinubu.

*“I think those who led the charge against her and against the government are members of Arise TV, and they’re biased. If you listen to their bias, you don’t need to talk further,”* Bwala said.

#### Arise Show Attacked, Abati Praised

Asked why the President has not granted local media an interview, Bwala said, 

"When *they say local media, they’re inferring 'themselves." They’ve been wanting to interview the president. And so, they have an exaggerated opinion about their self-worth.”*

He singled out only two professionals on the Arise morning show for praise—veteran journalist Dr. Reuben Abati and Ojy Okpe, who he said had indicated interest in interviewing the President and had remained professional.

*“Apart from Dr. Reuben Abati on that morning show, veteran journalist, and then maybe Ojy Okpe... Apart from the two of them, the rest of the people on the morning show are content creators surrogating for Peter Obi. And I’ve never hidden the fact of their hatred for the president,”* Bwala fired.

*“We are human beings. If you hate me and you are not rational in the way you do things, why would I grant you an interview?”*

He argued even if it were CNN’s Christiane Amanpour, critics would still complain, noting Tinubu had already granted an interview in Nigeria moderated by Abati, without reading from notes or needing briefing.

#### More Local Engagements Coming

Bwala assured of greater openness: “*I expect us to do more. And the second time, you’ll see more of that. There is a willingness to grant interviews on the part of the president.”*

He stressed that the State House press corps—mainstream, new media, social and print—covers the President daily, adding, "We *control our narrative. We decide who we grant interviews to. Nobody can come and tell us, grant to this one and not the other one.”*

The row follows London’s November 19 denial that she received $4.5 million from Nigeria, though an American firm disclosed $75,000 and $375,000 payments to her London Global Strategies for retainership. 

Reports had linked NSA Nuhu Ribadu to an alleged $4.5m lobbying contract after London posted photos on the US-Nigeria security partnership and a documentary interview with Tinubu.
