**LAGOS, Nigeria**—In a moment of national sporting pride, Nigeria’s U-17 women’s football team, the Flamingos, has qualified for the 2026 FIFA U-17 Women’s World Cup following a commanding performance against the Benin Republic. 

The team clinched one of Africa’s tickets to the prestigious tournament with an 8-5 aggregate victory.

Flamingos secured a decisive 5-3 win in the second leg (noted in some reports as contributing to the 3-5 outcome in the context of the match scoreline), building on their earlier 3-2 first-leg success.

The aggregate triumph ensures Nigeria’s young stars will compete on the world stage later this year in Morocco.

The final qualifying round against Benin proved competitive and entertaining. The first leg at Remo Stars Stadium in Ikenne delivered a five-goal thriller, with the Flamingos edging out the Benin Amazons 3-2. 

Traveling to Lomé, Togo, for the return fixture at Stade de Kégué, the Nigerian girls demonstrated resilience and attacking flair to seal qualification.

Key contributors included players like Esther Enne Stephen and Queen Joseph, who found the net in the campaign, showcasing the depth and talent within the squad. 

The victory highlights the continued strength of Nigeria’s youth women’s football program, known for producing stars who often transition successfully to senior teams like the Super Falcons.

This qualification comes at a time when Nigerian football is navigating various challenges at senior levels, making the Flamingos’ success a timely boost for national morale. 

The 2026 FIFA U-17 Women’s World Cup in Morocco represents a major platform for these young athletes to gain international exposure, develop skills, and inspire the next generation of female footballers across the country.

Nigeria has a rich history in youth football, particularly in age-grade competitions. Consistent qualification for global tournaments underscores the investment in grassroots development and the dedication of coaches and administrators in the women’s game. 

Fans and analysts alike are already projecting strong performances from the Flamingos in Morocco, where African teams have historically made notable impacts.

With qualification secured, focus now shifts to preparation for the World Cup proper. 

The tournament offers not just competitive opportunities but also a chance to spotlight Nigerian talent on the global stage, potentially attracting scouts and further investment in women’s sports infrastructure back home.

The Flamingos’ achievement resonates deeply in a nation where sports often unite citizens across divides. 

As celebrations continue, stakeholders are calling for sustained support — better facilities, welfare for players, and strategic planning — to maximize the potential of this promising squad.

Congratulations to the Flamingos on this hard-earned milestone. Nigeria’s young football queens have once again reminded the world of the country’s enduring prowess in youth women’s football.
