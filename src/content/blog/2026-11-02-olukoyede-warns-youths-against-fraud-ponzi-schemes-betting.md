**Abuja, Nigeria—November 2, 2026**—The Executive Chairman of the Economic and Financial Crimes Commission (EFCC), Ola Olukoyede, has warned Nigerian youths against internet fraud, Ponzi schemes, betting and other get-rich-quick practices, urging them instead to embrace integrity, financial discipline, and legitimate means of livelihood.

Olukoyede delivered the message in a goodwill address on Saturday, October 31, 2026, at an event organised by the Great Achievers Initiative for Youth and Community Development in Abuja. 

The event carried the theme “Raising Responsible Leaders, Building Sustainable Nations.”

He was represented by the Head of the Commission’s Enlightenment and Reorientation Unit, Assistant Commander of EFCC, ACE II Aisha Mohammed.



**Leadership Failure and Corruption Linked**

The EFCC boss stressed that responsible leadership is fundamental to the fight against economic and financial crimes, noting that leadership failure and corruption are closely linked.

*“No nation can build a sustainable future on the quicksand of corruption, fraud, and financial indiscipline,”* he said. *“Sustainable nations are not built by natural resources alone; they are built by responsible people, leaders who understand that public trust is not for private gain, that integrity is not negotiable, and that accountability is not optional.”*

He observed that the root of most financial crimes is a failure of values. “We arrest Yahoo boys who want quick wealth without work, we investigate corrupt officials who divert resources meant for communities, and we prosecute those who see leadership as an opportunity for enrichment, not service,” he added.



**Focus on Enlightenment and Value Reorientation**

Olukoyede explained that the EFCC’s mandate extends beyond arrests and prosecutions to public enlightenment and value reorientation, especially among young people. 

Through its Public Affairs Department, the Commission has established Integrity Clubs in primary and secondary schools and Zero Tolerance Clubs in tertiary institutions to instil integrity and discourage financial crimes.

He identified financial responsibility as another key pillar, urging youths to embrace legitimate earnings, savings, budgeting and delayed gratification. 

He warned that the country cannot achieve sustainable development if its young population continues to chase Ponzi schemes, betting and get-rich-quick schemes.



**Deterrence and Leadership Responsibility**

The Chairman stressed the need for deterrence, reminding young Nigerians that a criminal record for internet fraud, money laundering or forgery can permanently close doors to global opportunities.

*“A criminal record for internet fraud, money laundering, or forgery will close doors to global opportunities forever. No responsible future is built with a compromised past,*” he said.

He reminded the audience that leadership is not limited to public office. “*The youth are not just the leaders of tomorrow; they are the leaders of today,”* he declared, noting that they already exercise leadership in their homes, schools, social media spaces, and communities.

Olukoyede charged the youths: “*Be the responsible leader Nigeria is waiting for. Choose integrity over popularity. Choose diligence over Yahoo-Yahoo. Choose service over selfishness. A sustainable nation is simply a collection of responsible citizens doing the right thing daily.”*



He also commended the Great Achievers Initiative for its work and assured that the EFCC remains willing to partner with organizations promoting integrity, civic responsibility, and responsible leadership among young Nigerians.
