**Bauchi, Nigeria – August 12, 2026** — Former Anambra State Governor and Nigeria Democratic Congress (NDC) presidential candidate Peter Obi on Tuesday issued a stark warning that Nigeria’s democracy is deteriorating to the point where it may soon cease to meaningfully exist.

Obi spoke at the 2026 Goodluck Jonathan Foundation Democracy Dialogue held in Bauchi, themed “***Beyond Elections: Can Political Parties and the Judiciary Save African Democracy?”*** 

The event was chaired by former President Olusegun Obasanjo and attended by former President Goodluck Jonathan, Bauchi State Governor Bala Mohammed, and other prominent figures.

Drawing from personal experience, Obi recalled how he spent years in court after believing his mandate as Anambra governor had been stolen. At that time, he said, the judiciary demonstrated genuine independence.

*“I went to the Court of Appeal; five judges I had never met gave judgment in my favour,”* Obi stated. 

He contrasted that era with the present, alleging that institutions once trusted to uphold the rule of law are now enabling its erosion.

Obi listed what he described as alarming developments: professors allegedly aiding electoral malpractice, lawmakers removing forgery as an electoral offence, Senior Advocates of Nigeria defending certificate forgery, religious leaders praying for politicians accused of corruption, and some judges appearing politically aligned by chanting campaign slogans.

*“When the very institutions established to protect democracy begin to accommodate its destruction, we must stop pretending that everything is normal,”* he said in a statement shared after the event.

He warned that the abuse being tolerated today would eventually take its revenge on future generations.

Obi invoked Socrates’ famous line that “the unexamined life is not worth living,” urging Nigerians to examine the direction of the nation and halt the drift towards institutional decay.

Former President Obasanjo, who chaired the dialogue, questioned the suitability of the Western liberal democratic model for Africa and called for a more authentic African approach. 

Goodluck Jonathan highlighted the excessive reliance on the judiciary to resolve political and electoral disputes, advocating structural reforms that reduce the concentration of power.

The Bauchi gathering forms part of a broader conversation about the health of Nigeria’s democracy ahead of the 2027 general elections. 

Obi’s remarks, delivered in the presence of two former presidents, have intensified debate over electoral integrity, judicial independence, and the role of political elites.

As Nigeria continues to grapple with questions of trust in its institutions, the message from Bauchi was unambiguous: without urgent course correction, the democratic experiment itself risks losing its meaning for ordinary citizens.
