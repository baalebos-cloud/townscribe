The Independent National Electoral Commission has issued a strong public appeal for Nigerians to turn out in large numbers for a nationwide mock accreditation exercise scheduled for Saturday, November 28, 2026. 

The exercise forms a critical part of preparations for the 2027 general elections and is designed to stress-test the Bimodal Voter Accreditation System and electronic results transmission under real field conditions.

Speaking on Channels Television’s **Sunrise Daily** on Tuesday, Victoria Meci, INEC’s Director of Voter Education and Publicity, made it clear that the commission will not wait until election day to discover whether its technology works. 

*“We don’t want to wait till the elections before we test the ruggedness, readiness, and functionality of the system,”* she said. 

The mock exercise will run from 8:30 a.m. to 2:30 p.m. across 17,676 carefully selected polling units — two in each of the country’s 8,838 Registration Areas.

The decision comes against the backdrop of widespread technical challenges experienced during the 2023 general elections, when many BVAS machines malfunctioned, partly due to extreme heat and poor storage conditions. 

In response, INEC has procured additional devices, reduced the maximum number of voters assigned to each machine from 1,250 to 850, and is constructing climate-controlled storage facilities across the states. Polling units with higher voter populations will receive multiple machines to prevent bottlenecks.

INEC Chairman Professor Joash Amupitan had earlier described the mock exercise as a simulation of the presidential election. 

It will also test new technical safeguards meant to stop inaccurate results or figures showing over-voting from being uploaded to the INEC Result Viewing Portal. 

The list of selected polling units has been published on the commission’s official website, and eligible voters in those locations are being urged to participate with their Permanent Voter Cards.

For many Nigerians still scarred by the frustrations of 2023, the exercise represents more than a routine drill. It is an early opportunity to judge whether the electoral umpire has truly learned from past failures or is simply repeating familiar promises. 

High turnout this Saturday could provide the commission with the real-world data it needs to deliver a more credible process in 2027. 

Low participation, on the other hand, risks leaving critical weaknesses undetected until it is too late.

As the clock ticks toward the next general elections, the mock accreditation has become an early test of public trust—and of INEC’s determination to get the technology right this time.
