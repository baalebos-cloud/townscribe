**Abuja, Nigeria – June 23, 2026** — As the rainy season intensifies across Nigeria, the military’s Operation WHIRL STROKE has issued a stern safety advisory to mariners, boat operators, passengers, and riverine communities, highlighting the heightened dangers posed by rising water levels and strong currents in inland waterways.

The Maritime Component of the operation, under the directive of Force Commander Major General Moses Gara, warned that the surge in water volume is accompanied by turbulence that significantly increases the risk of boat mishaps, potentially leading to loss of lives and property. 

Lieutenant Commander Dusse Christopher Zakari, the Maritime Component Commander, emphasized the need for immediate compliance with safety protocols in the Joint Operations Area.

The comprehensive advisory outlines mandatory precautions designed to protect those reliant on Nigeria’s vital inland waterways for transportation, fishing, and commerce:

1. **Life Jackets Mandatory**: All passengers and mariners must wear life jackets at all times while on board.

2. **Life Buoy Requirement**: Boat operators and owners must ensure at least one life buoy is available on every vessel.

3. **No Overloading:** Boats must not exceed approved carrying capacities.

4. **Essential Equipment**: Functional torchlights, extra spark plugs, and tarpaulins must be carried for emergencies.

5. **Anti-Pollution Measures**: Mariners are prohibited from dumping non-biodegradable materials and waste into waterways.

6. **Daylight Operations Only**: Night sailing is banned; operations are restricted to 6:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m. daily for security and safety reasons.

7. **Registration and ID**: All boat operators must register with maritime unions and carry valid identification.

8. **Vigilance Against Crime**: Riverine residents are encouraged to report suspicious or criminal activities promptly to security agencies.

Lieutenant Commander Zakari stressed that defaulters will face sanctions under existing regulations, while calling on maritime unions to enforce compliance strictly. 

The statement underscores the operation’s commitment to safeguarding lives, property, and the security of Nigeria’s waterways.

Launched to tackle insecurity in states including Benue, Taraba, Nasarawa, and parts of surrounding areas, Operation WHIRL STROKE has focused on countering armed herders, bandits, kidnappers, and other threats through coordinated land and maritime efforts. 

Recent activities have included neutralizing criminals, rescuing kidnap victims, and recovering weapons in Benue and Taraba states.

The maritime advisory comes amid recurring boat accidents during Nigeria’s rainy season, when rivers like the Benue often swell dramatically, overwhelming vessels ill-equipped for the conditions. 

Past incidents have claimed dozens of lives, underscoring the life-saving potential of these measures.

For riverine communities where waterways serve as lifelines for trade, agriculture, and daily mobility, these warnings carry profound weight. 

A fisherman or trader overloaded in turbulent waters risks not just their livelihood but the future of entire families. 

The military’s dual focus on safety and security reflects the complex challenges in these regions, where environmental hazards intersect with criminal threats.

Members of the public are urged to cooperate fully with security agencies, remain vigilant, and prioritize these precautions. 

As Lieutenant Ahmad Zubairu Zubairu, Acting Media Information Officer for HQ Operation WHIRL STROKE, signed off in today’s release: adherence today could prevent tragedies tomorrow.

This proactive step by the Armed Forces of Nigeria demonstrates a commitment not only to combat operations but also to community protection during vulnerable seasonal transitions. 

Observers note that sustained enforcement and public education will be key to its effectiveness.
