Oyo State Governor Seyi Makinde has issued a direct appeal to President Bola Tinubu, urging him to protect Nigeria’s democratic institutions following a Federal High Court ruling that nullified former Vice President Atiku Abubakar’s presidential candidacy under the African Democratic Congress (ADC).

Speaking during a live radio interview on Bauchi Radio Corporation on Friday, Makinde said: “Court nullification of Atiku’s candidacy: I want to appeal to President Bola Tinubu. 

Governments will come and go, but our country will remain. Please, do not destroy our institutions, be it the Judiciary or INEC. Stop trying to shut out the opposition.”

The remarks come just a day after Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court in Abuja delivered a judgment that declared the David Mark-led caretaker leadership of the ADC illegal. 

The court voided all decisions taken under that leadership, including the primary that produced Atiku as the party’s flag bearer for the 2027 presidential election. 

It also nullified the nominations of all ADC candidates for the general elections and directed the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) not to recognize related decisions from the party’s July 2025 National Executive Committee meeting.

The suit was filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former ADC Deputy National Chairman (North-East), who challenged the process through which Mark and other officers, including former Osun Governor Rauf Aregbesola as National Secretary, assumed control after the resignation of previous leadership. 

The judgment has thrown the ADC’s participation in the January 2027 elections into uncertainty, though the party and Atiku have vowed to appeal.

Makinde, who is himself the presidential candidate of the Allied Peoples Movement (APM) and was in Bauchi ahead of his campaign flag-off, framed his intervention as a defence of democratic norms rather than support for any individual. 

His comments highlight growing concerns among some opposition figures that repeated legal challenges risk undermining public confidence in the judiciary and the electoral body at a critical stage of the election cycle.

Atiku has rejected the ruling as a calculated attempt to weaken the opposition and frustrate his campaign. He has continued holding rallies, including in Kebbi State, and insists the judgment will be challenged up to the highest levels. 

The ADC has described the decision as politically motivated and overtaken by events, pointing to an earlier conflicting ruling from the same Federal High Court that had affirmed aspects of the Mark-led structure.

The timing is sensitive. With general elections less than three months away, the nullification of a major opposition ticket raises questions about fairness, institutional independence, and the space available for genuine multiparty competition. 

Nigeria’s political history is marked by high-stakes legal battles over party leadership and candidacy, often stretching into the appellate courts and the Supreme Court close to election day.

Makinde’s appeal underscores a broader anxiety shared by some stakeholders: that the pursuit of short-term political advantage could leave lasting damage to the credibility of the judiciary and INEC. 

***“Governments will come and go, but our country will remain,”*** he stressed, positioning institutional integrity as more important than any single electoral contest.

As the legal process unfolds and campaigns intensify, the response from the presidency, the courts, and the electoral commission will shape public perception of the 2027 race. 

Makinde’s intervention adds a prominent voice calling for restraint and the preservation of democratic guardrails at a moment when tensions are rising.
