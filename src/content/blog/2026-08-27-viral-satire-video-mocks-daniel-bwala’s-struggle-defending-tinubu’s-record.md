A sharply edited satirical video circulating widely on X has reignited fierce debate over the credibility of Daniel Bwala, Special Adviser to President Bola Tinubu on Policy Communication, as he continues to defend the administration amid lingering questions about the president’s personal and educational background.

Posted by popular commentator and ARISETV OAP Oseni Rufai (@ruffydfire) in the early hours of Thursday, the roughly 85-second clip shows a suited man in a black fedora — widely interpreted as a stand-in for Bwala or a similar government spokesperson — seated in an office, gesturing emphatically while reviewing documents. 

Overlaid footage of President Tinubu and other officials appears above, while a visible form detailing school qualifications, working experience, and employment history sits prominently on the desk.

The montage, which has already racked up tens of thousands of views and hundreds of engagements, plays on long-standing public curiosity about gaps in Tinubu’s documented educational and career history — questions that have surfaced repeatedly since the 2023 campaign. 

Critics online have framed the clip as a visual metaphor for the difficulty of “*defending the indefensible,*” with several replies accusing Bwala of repeatedly walking back or denying earlier opposition-era criticisms of the same president he now serves.

Bwala, who previously served as a prominent spokesperson for the Peoples Democratic Party and Atiku Abubakar’s 2023 campaign, has faced sustained scrutiny for shifting positions. In a high-profile March 2026 Al Jazeera interview and subsequent media appearances, he dismissed past critical remarks as “*all politics,*” arguing that many current office holders once opposed the leaders they later served. 

More recently, on August 26, he again denied ever describing Tinubu as an “illegitimate president,” attributing the phrase instead to activist Aisha Yesufu and challenging critics to produce evidence.

Public reaction to the latest video has been predictably polarised. Opposition-leaning users have shared it as proof that defending the administration requires increasingly strained efforts, with comments such as *“Anybody defending Tinubu will look stupid at the end” and calls to “retire these set of spokes persons.”* 

Supporters, meanwhile, have dismissed the clip as cheap political theatre typical of the run-up to 2027.

The timing is sensitive. With the All Progressives Congress already assembling its 2027 campaign structures and the opposition still consolidating, every viral moment carries amplified weight. 

Bwala has repeatedly insisted he remains ready to defend government policies *“anywhere in the world,”* yet the persistent recirculation of his earlier statements continues to supply ammunition to critics.

As Nigeria’s political temperature rises, videos like this one serve as both entertainment and low-level political warfare — reminding citizens that in the age of social media, every past word and every present defence can be edited, amplified, and weaponised within hours. 

Whether the satire lands as fair commentary or unfair character assassination remains a matter of perspective. 

What is clear is that the conversation around authenticity, consistency, and political loyalty is far from over.
