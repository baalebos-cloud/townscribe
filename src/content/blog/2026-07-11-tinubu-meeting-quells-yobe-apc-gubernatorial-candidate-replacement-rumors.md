**DAMATURU, Yobe State** — Political tension in Yobe State eased somewhat this week following a high-profile meeting between Governor Mai Mala Buni and President Bola Ahmed Tinubu at the Presidential Villa in Abuja. 

For several days, persistent rumors had swirled across the state suggesting that the All Progressives Congress (APC) gubernatorial candidate for the 2027 election might be replaced by Bashir Machina, who placed third in the party’s primary.

The speculation gained traction online and within political circles, fueled by the competitive nature of the APC primaries earlier this year. 

However, sources close to the development indicate that yesterday’s closed-door engagement between Governor Buni—the declared winner of the primary—and President Tinubu has effectively put those rumors to rest, at least for now.

Photographs circulating from the meeting show warm handshakes and cordial moments involving key Yobe figures, including former Governor and current Minister of Police Affairs, Senator Ibrahim Gaidam, signaling continued alignment within the APC structure in the state.

Yobe State has been a stronghold for the APC, with Governor Mai Mala Buni enjoying significant influence since his election in 2019. 

The 2027 succession battle has been closely watched, especially after the party’s internal processes produced Buni as the preferred candidate while other aspirants, including Bashir Machina, contested vigorously.

Machina, a notable political figure in the state, secured a respectable number of votes in the primaries but ultimately fell short. 

Rumors of a potential substitution reflected broader tensions common in Nigerian politics, where party hierarchies, godfatherism, and federal influence often shape candidate selection. 

The meeting with President Tinubu — whose administration has been cultivating strong ties with state governors ahead of the next electoral cycle — appears to have reinforced party unity and Buni’s position.

This development comes at a critical time as parties gear up for the 2027 general elections. 

For Yobe residents, the focus remains on continuity of governance, infrastructure development, security improvements, and economic opportunities in a state that has seen notable progress under Buni’s leadership but still faces challenges typical of the Northeast region.

Political analysts note that such high-level interventions by the presidency often serve to stabilize party structures and prevent internal crises that could weaken the APC’s chances in key states. 

Whether this meeting fully resolves underlying ambitions within the Yobe APC remains to be seen, but it has clearly dampened immediate talk of a candidate swap.

Governor Buni’s team has emphasized unity and collective support for the party’s candidates, while calls for transparent processes continue from some quarters. 

As the political temperature rises nationally, eyes will remain on Yobe to see how these intra-party dynamics play out in the months ahead.

For now, the message from the Presidential Villa meeting seems clear: stability and loyalty within the ruling party take precedence as Nigeria marches toward 2027. 

Ordinary citizens in Yobe, meanwhile, will be watching to ensure that whatever unfolds serves the broader interest of development and good governance in the state.
