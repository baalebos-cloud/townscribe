**Abuja, Nigeria—November 6, 2026**—The African Democratic Congress (ADC) has rejected a Federal High Court judgment that sacked its David Mark-led national executive and voided the presidential candidacy of former Vice President Atiku Abubakar, insisting that Senator David Mark remains the party’s authentic leader.

Speaking on Channels Television’s **Morning Brief** on Friday, Kenneth Okonkwo, spokesperson for Atiku Abubakar’s presidential campaign, declared that no court order could change the party’s internal reality.

*“Our (ADC) leader is David Mark, and no amount of judgment from Justice Peter Lifu can stop that,”* Okonkwo stated firmly.



**The Court Judgment**

On Thursday, November 5, 2026, Justice Peter Lifu of the Federal High Court in Abuja delivered a far-reaching ruling in a suit filed by Nafiu Bala Gombe, a former National Deputy Chairman (North-East) of the ADC. 

The court declared the Mark-led Caretaker Committee illegal and nullified all actions taken under its leadership.

Those actions included the presidential primary that produced Atiku as the party’s flag bearer for the January 16, 2027, general election, the appointment of former Osun State Governor Rauf Aregbesola as National Secretary, and the nomination of all other ADC candidates for elective positions.

Justice Lifu ordered the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) not to recognize decisions taken at the party’s 99th National Executive Committee meeting of July 27, 2025, and subsequent steps. 

Atiku had already been listed by INEC as the ADC presidential candidate and had chosen former Rivers State Governor Rotimi Amaechi as his running mate.



**ADC’s Strong Pushback**

Okonkwo linked the timing of the judgment to the party’s successful campaign flag-off rally in Sokoto State, which he said *“shook the APC to the bone marrow.”*

He described the gathering not merely as a political event but as “*a historic journey to rescue our people from the incompetence and corruption of the APC, hunger and unemployment.”*

The ADC has vowed to appeal the judgment, arguing that it contradicts an earlier ruling by Justice Musa Liman of the same Federal High Court on July 2, 2026, which had affirmed the legitimacy of the Mark-led leadership. 

Party officials maintain that a court of coordinate jurisdiction cannot overrule a subsisting judgment of another judge of the same court.

Okonkwo further accused the ruling All Progressives Congress of using the judiciary to weaken the opposition and create a pathway for President Bola Tinubu to run virtually unopposed in 2027. 

He recalled that Justice Lifu had previously ordered the deregistration of the ADC and four other parties—a decision later overturned by the Court of Appeal, which described the lower court’s action as “judicial rascality.”



**Broader Political Implications**

The ruling has thrown the ADC’s participation in the 2027 elections into uncertainty. Legal experts note that if the judgment is upheld on appeal, the party could find itself without candidates from the presidency down to state legislative seats. 

The ADC’s National Welfare Secretary, Nkemakolam Ukandu, confirmed that the leadership would challenge the decision at the appellate court.

Atiku himself has continued campaigning, condemning the judgment as a calculated attempt to frustrate his bid and weaken the opposition. 

The presidential election is scheduled for January 16, 2027, leaving the party with limited time to resolve the legal crisis.

For now, Okonkwo’s defiant stance signals that the Mark-led faction remains determined to press forward, treating the court ruling as a temporary setback rather than a final defeat.
