**LYNNFIELD, Massachusetts—The** Catholic community on Boston’s North Shore is in mourning following the tragic death of Rev. Benjamin Okwy Madu, a 54-year-old Nigerian priest who reportedly took his own life on July 2, 2026, just days before his religious worker visa was set to expire.

Known affectionately as Father Ben, Madu had served diligently in the Archdiocese of Boston since 2021 as a hospital chaplain at Salem Hospital and as a weekend Mass celebrant for the Catholic Community of Gloucester and Rockport on Cape Ann. 

His sudden passing has left parishioners, colleagues, and friends devastated.

According to reports, Madu’s home diocese in Abakaliki, Nigeria, had directed him to return earlier than planned ahead of his R-1 visa expiration on July 29. 

Friends and acquaintances described him as deeply distressed by the impending departure and the recent death of his bishop back home.

Archbishop Richard G. Henning of Boston extended condolences, noting the “devastating loss” and offering prayers for Madu’s family, brother priests, and the many people he ministered to in the United States. 

Local parishioners remembered him as a dedicated, compassionate shepherd who touched countless lives through his chaplaincy and parish work.

Madu, who would have marked 25 years of priestly ordination on July 7, had built a meaningful ministry in the U.S., balancing hospital pastoral care with weekend liturgical duties. 

His death has prompted an outpouring of tributes from the communities he served, highlighting the personal connections he forged despite the temporary nature of his assignment.

The incident has also reignited broader conversations about the challenges faced by international religious workers in the U.S., including visa uncertainties, cultural transitions, and the emotional toll of mandatory returns. 

For many African priests serving in American dioceses facing clergy shortages, such assignments provide vital ministry opportunities but come with the constant shadow of expiration dates and family obligations back home.

While authorities have not released an official cause of death, an internal archdiocesan communication confirmed the tragic circumstances. 

Investigations remain ongoing, but the focus for now is on supporting those grieving and honoring Madu’s legacy of quiet, faithful service.

In a statement, the Archdiocese urged prayers for his soul and his loved ones in Nigeria. 

Community members in Gloucester, Rockport, and Lynnfield have organized vigils and shared memories of a priest who brought warmth, spiritual guidance, and a deep commitment to the sick and marginalized.

This heartbreaking loss serves as a poignant reminder of the human stories behind immigration policies, visa regimes, and the global movement of clergy. 

Father Benjamin Madu’s ministry, though cut short, left an indelible mark on the North Shore — a testament to the bridges built through faith across continents.

May he rest in peace.
