**Ogun, Nigeria -** Ogun State motorists are being put on notice: the police have begun a determined statewide operation to impound vehicles running with covered, obscured, defaced, fake, or missing number plates. 

The move, ordered by Commissioner of Police Bode Ojajuni, follows a clear directive from the Inspector-General of Police and aims to strip criminals of one of their simplest tools — anonymity on the road.

The enforcement, which kicked off across Ogun’s Area Commands and divisions, targets any vehicle whose registration marks are deliberately hidden, altered, or absent. Only vehicles officially authorised to fly flags under established protocols are exempt. 

Officers have been instructed to stop offending vehicles, impound them where necessary, and conduct proper investigations in line with the law.

**Why the sudden push?**

The Ogun State Police Command says the exercise is designed to strengthen security and public safety. 

Criminals have long exploited vehicles with hidden or fake plates to carry out kidnappings, armed robberies, and other offences, knowing they are harder to trace. 

By forcing every vehicle to display clear, valid registration, the police hope to improve identification, speed up investigations, and make it far more difficult for suspects to blend into everyday traffic on Ogun’s busy highways and inner roads.

This is not an isolated Ogun initiative. It forms part of a broader nationwide enforcement drive ordered by the Inspector-General of Police, IGP Olatunji Rilwan Disu, who has directed all commands to tackle the growing problem of unregistered and deliberately concealed plates as a threat to national security.

**What drivers need to know**

Law-abiding citizens are not the target, the Command stresses. The focus is on those who choose to obscure their plates — whether for “**aesthetic**” reasons, to dodge traffic enforcement, or for more sinister purposes. 

However, anyone whose vehicle currently falls short of the rules is advised to regularise their registration immediately to avoid the inconvenience of impoundment and investigation.

Newly purchased vehicles or those still awaiting proper plates may face practical questions in the early days of enforcement. 

While the police have not announced a formal grace period in the current release, officers have been told to act professionally and within the law. 

Motorists in genuine transitional situations are encouraged to carry all relevant documents and approach officers calmly.

**Professional conduct promised**

The Command has specifically directed all personnel involved in the operation to remain courteous and professional. 

This is meant to prevent the kind of overzealous or extortionate encounters that sometimes accompany such exercises. Residents who witness misconduct are urged to report it through official channels.

**The bigger picture**

Vehicle identification has become a quiet but critical front in Nigeria’s security architecture. 

When plates are hidden or fake, it becomes significantly harder for security agencies to track movements, respond to incidents, or build cases against repeat offenders. 

Ogun State, as a major transit corridor linking Lagos with the rest of the Southwest and beyond, feels this challenge acutely.

The current enforcement represents a shift toward more visible, technology-friendly policing — making it easier to match vehicles to owners and incidents in real time. 

While some drivers may initially grumble about the sudden checks, the long-term goal is fewer “ghost” vehicles on the roads and greater peace of mind for residents and commuters.

Motorists who value both their convenience and their safety are being asked to do one simple thing: make sure their number plates are valid, clearly visible, and properly fixed. 

In a state that has seen its share of security challenges, that small act of compliance could make a meaningful difference.
