**ABUJA, Nigeria** —In a significant judicial blow to one faction of Nigeria’s main opposition party, the Federal High Court in Abuja has struck out a lawsuit filed by the Board of Trustees and founding leaders of the Peoples Democratic Party (PDP) seeking formal recognition for the Kabiru Turaki-led Interim National Working Committee.

Justice Salim Olasupo Ibrahim, in a ruling delivered on Friday, upheld the preliminary objection of the defendants. 

The court held that the matter constitutes an internal affair of the party, over which it lacks jurisdiction.

The suit, which aimed to compel the Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC) to recognize the Turaki-led interim body, has been at the heart of the PDP’s prolonged leadership crisis. 

The plaintiffs — including figures like former Senate President Adolphus Wabara — had argued for the legitimacy of the interim structure established following earlier court rulings invalidating certain party conventions.

In a strongly worded press statement signed by Comrade Ini Ememobong, National Publicity Secretary of the Interim National Working Committee, the PDP described the judgment as contrary to extant decisions of higher courts. 

The party expressed respect for the ruling but announced plans to appeal immediately, stating: “*Victory postponed, the struggle continues.”*

The statement emphasized confidence that higher courts would ultimately affirm “*truth over lies, principles over compromise,”* and the survival of genuine opposition politics in Nigeria. 

It ended with the rallying cry: *“Aluta continua, Victoria ascerta!”* (The struggle continues, victory is certain!).

This latest court episode is only one chapter in the PDP’s long-running internal turmoil, which has seen factional battles, multiple court cases, and defections ahead of the 2027 general elections. 

The party, once Nigeria’s dominant political force, has been grappling with leadership disputes since losing power in 2015. 

The current rift pits different blocs — including factions associated with Nyesom Wike and others — against one another, complicating efforts to present a united front against the ruling APC.

With INEC’s election timetable looming, the unresolved leadership question poses a serious challenge for the PDP’s ability to field candidates effectively and rebuild public confidence. 

Many Nigerians watching the drama unfold see it as emblematic of broader issues plaguing the country’s political class: power struggles, legal battles, and a focus on internal control rather than delivering solutions to citizens’ pressing needs.

As the appeal process begins, the PDP’s ability to resolve its differences will be closely watched. 

For a party that once symbolized stability and opposition strength, the coming months represent a critical test of resilience and relevance.

The struggle, as the party itself declares, indeed continues—both in the courts and in the court of public opinion.
