**Lagos, Nigeria—November 20, 2026**—Prominent political analyst and former Daily Trust editorial board chairman, Mahmud Jega, has squarely blamed the incumbent government for the rising wave of political violence across Nigeria, arguing that the abuse of power by state and local authorities is the root cause.

Speaking as an ARISE News analyst, Jega said the pattern of violence begins when state governments deny opposition parties access to stadiums and other public rally grounds. He described such actions as a form of violence in themselves.

*“The beginning of these violence is the abuse of the incumbent power by state and local authorities. It is violence when a state government denies opposition parties access to stadiums and rally grounds. Things like this will enrage supporters of those candidates,”* Jega stated.

His comments come amid growing concern over politically linked attacks as campaigns intensify ahead of the January 2027 general elections.

Independent monitors have documented dozens of incidents involving assaults on candidates’ convoys, disruption of rallies, and clashes between supporters in several states since the official campaign period began.

Jega, a respected voice in Nigerian political commentary, framed the denial of venue access not merely as administrative inconvenience but as a deliberate exercise of incumbency advantage that inflames tensions and invites retaliation. 

He suggested that such practices undermine the level playing field required for credible elections and contribute to the cycle of unrest now visible on the campaign trail.

Observers note that political violence has become a recurring feature of Nigeria’s electoral cycles, with civil society groups and security trackers reporting fatalities, injuries, and destruction of campaign materials in multiple regions. 

The National Peace Committee and other monitoring bodies have repeatedly called on all parties and governments to uphold the National Peace Accord and ensure equal access to public facilities.

Jega’s intervention adds a sharp analytical lens to the debate, shifting focus from isolated thuggery to structural issues of power abuse at the state and local levels. 

As the country moves closer to the polls, his warning underscores the need for stricter enforcement of electoral guidelines and genuine neutrality by public office holders to prevent further escalation.
