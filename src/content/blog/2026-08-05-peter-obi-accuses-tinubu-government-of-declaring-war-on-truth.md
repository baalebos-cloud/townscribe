**ABUJA, Nigeria** — Former Anambra State Governor and 2027 presidential hopeful Peter Obi has strongly criticised the Tinubu administration for what he describes as a growing intolerance of truth and hostility toward those who highlight the suffering of ordinary Nigerians.

In a detailed statement posted on X on Wednesday, Obi referenced U.S. President Donald Trump’s recent description of Nigeria as “*a now disgraced country*,” arguing that the nation’s deepening challenges are made worse when the government turns hostile to honest criticism.

Obi’s intervention comes amid a fierce public dispute that erupted after members of the Catholic Bishops’ Conference of Nigeria (CBCN) met President Bola Tinubu at the State House on July 28, 2026. 

During the closed-door session, the bishops presented concerns over worsening insecurity, economic hardship, inflation, poverty, and the health of Nigeria’s democracy.

Cardinal John Onaiyekan, Archbishop Emeritus of Abuja, later revealed in television interviews that the bishops told the President the nation was “*bleeding*” and that the economy was failing the poor. 

According to Onaiyekan, Tinubu rejected their assessment, insisting the economy was performing well.

Presidential aides responded sharply. Special Adviser Daniel Bwala accused Onaiyekan of speaking for Catholics “*rooting for Peter Obi*” rather than for Nigerian Christians as a whole, describing the cardinal as “*more political than spiritual.”* 

Senior Special Assistant Temitope Ajayi called Onaiyekan’s public comments an “*abuse of clerical privilege*.”


Obi rejected the attacks as deeply troubling. He reminded Nigerians that the Catholic Church has never acted as an opposition party, consistently speaking across military and civilian governments as a moral conscience. 

He noted that Muslim organisations such as Jama’atu Nasril Islam and the Nigerian Supreme Council for Islamic Affairs have also raised similar concerns about hardship and insecurity.

The former governor paid tribute to Cardinal Onaiyekan’s distinguished record, recalling that the cardinal achieved the best Higher School Certificate result in the old Northern Region in 1962 and rose to a position that places him among those eligible for the papacy. 

Obi argued that personal attacks on such a figure weaken public discourse and reveal an unhealthy intolerance of criticism.

Drawing from his own experience as governor, Obi said he welcomed criticism from traditional rulers and religious bodies because it improved governance, particularly in education and health. 

He warned that surrounding leaders only with praise-singers disconnects them from reality and harms the nation.

*“Good governance depends on truth,” Obi wrote. “When governments punish truth-tellers and reward sycophants with appointments, honest advisers withdraw, sycophancy thrives, and leaders become disconnected from reality. This is our situation today*.”

The controversy has drawn reactions from other political figures. Former Vice President Atiku Abubakar urged the presidency to address the bishops’ concerns rather than attack Onaiyekan, while former Transport Minister Rotimi Amaechi accused the government of attempting to intimidate the cardinal.

Obi concluded by affirming that religious leaders have a moral duty to speak when the people are suffering, and that silence would betray that responsibility. 

He called on those around President Tinubu to offer honest appraisals instead of flattery, arguing that only truth can guide effective leadership.

As Nigeria grapples with persistent economic pressures and security challenges, the exchange between the Catholic hierarchy, the presidency, and opposition voices has once again placed the right to speak truth to power at the centre of national conversation.
