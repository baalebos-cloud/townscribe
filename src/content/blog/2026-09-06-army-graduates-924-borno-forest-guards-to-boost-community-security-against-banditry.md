**Abuja, Nigeria – September 6, 2026**—In a significant boost to grassroots security in the North-East, the Nigerian Army has graduated 924 Forest Guards from Borno State after a rigorous six-week intensive training programme designed to strengthen forest security and protect surrounding communities.

The Federal Ministry of Information and National Orientation (FMINO) highlighted the development in its Sunday Brief Issue 045, released on September 6, 2026. 

The training focused on equipping the guards to serve as a critical first line of defence against banditry, terrorism, and other criminal activities that have long plagued forested areas in the region.



**Key Details of the Training**

According to the official brief, the newly graduated Forest Guards underwent instruction in five core areas:



\- Discipline  

\- Physical fitness  

\- Tactical awareness  

\- Weapon handling  

\- Field operations  



The programme, lasting six weeks, prepared the 924 personnel to protect forests, report threats promptly, and work closely with local communities. 

Officials describe the initiative as a practical step toward building layered security architecture that combines military support with community-based protection.



**Government’s Perspective**

Honorable Minister of Information and National Orientation, Mohammed Idris, FNIPR, welcomed the graduation, stating that it demonstrates the Federal Government’s commitment to strengthening security at the community level.

*“The training of 924 forest guards demonstrates the Federal Government’s commitment to strengthening security at the community level.* 

*By equipping local personnel to protect forests, report threats and work with communities, Nigeria is building another layer of defence while safeguarding valuable national resources,*” the Minister said in the official note accompanying the brief.

The initiative aligns with broader efforts under President Bola Ahmed Tinubu’s Renewed Hope Agenda to improve security, protect natural resources, and empower communities to play an active role in their own safety.



**Broader Context and Impact**

Borno State has faced persistent challenges from insurgency and criminal groups operating in forested and remote areas. 

By training and deploying local forest guards, authorities aim to improve early warning systems, reduce the operational space available to criminal elements, and foster greater trust between security forces and residents.

The Forest Guards are expected to complement the work of regular security agencies, focusing on forest protection while supporting wider efforts against banditry and terrorism. 

Officials emphasise that community involvement remains central to sustainable security outcomes.

The graduation marks another milestone in the Federal Government’s multi-layered approach to national security. 

As more trained personnel are deployed, stakeholders anticipate improved coordination between military units, forest guards, and local communities in Borno and potentially other affected states.

The Federal Ministry of Information and National Orientation continues to communicate government policies and progress through its regular Sunday Brief series, aimed at keeping citizens informed and engaged.
